# 16.3 · Company organs and shareholders' rights

By the end of this lesson you will know who decides what inside an Israeli company, how a general meeting is called and run in a private company, and what rights a shareholder has against the company and the other shareholders. You will also write the demand that a shareholder with 10% sends when the board refuses to call a meeting.

## Why it matters

A company acts only through people. The law decides which group of people (the general meeting, the board, the general manager) speaks for the company on each matter, and a decision taken by the wrong organ can be void or open to challenge. In small private companies, the founders are usually shareholders, directors and managers at once, and it is easy to forget which hat they are wearing: a "decision" made over coffee by two of three founders may be a valid board resolution, an invalid general-meeting resolution, or nothing. Knowing the rules on meetings and organs is also the precondition for every remedy in lesson 16.6.

## The law

### Organs and their powers

> האורגנים של החברה הם האסיפה הכללית, הדירקטוריון, המנהל הכללי וכל מי שעל פי דין, או מכוח התקנון רואים את פעולתו בענין פלוני כפעולת החברה לאותו ענין.

*Gist:* The organs are the general meeting, the board, the general manager and anyone whose act the law or the articles treat as the company's act. [Companies Law s. 46](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_46) An organ's acts and intentions are the company's (s. 47), so the company is directly liable in tort for a wrong committed by its organ (s. 53(א)), without removing the personal liability of the individuals (s. 54(א)).

The law divides the work:

- **General meeting.** Amends the articles, appoints the auditor, appoints outside directors, approves interested-party transactions where required, increases or reduces the registered capital, approves mergers and resolves on winding-up (s. 57). The company cannot contract out of this list (s. 58(א)). It also appoints directors unless the articles say otherwise (s. 59).
- **Board.** Sets policy and supervises the general manager: plans of action, financial position and credit limits, organisational structure and pay policy, financial statements, appointment and dismissal of the general manager, allotment of shares within the registered capital, distributions (s. 92(א)). These powers cannot be delegated to the general manager (s. 92(ב)).
- **General manager.** Runs day-to-day affairs within the board's policy (s. 120) and has every management and executive power not given to another organ (s. 121(א)). A private company need not appoint one; then the board manages (s. 119(ב)).
- **Residual authority.** A power not given to any organ belongs to the board (s. 49). The articles may let the general meeting take over another organ's powers for a specific matter or period (s. 50(א)); shareholders who do so carry directors' duties for that matter (s. 50(ב)). If the board cannot act and the act is essential, the general meeting may act instead (s. 52).

### General meetings in a private company

| Rule | Default | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Annual meeting | Every year, within 15 months of the last; agenda includes financial statements and the board's report | s. 60 |
| Skipping the annual meeting | A private company's articles may say it need not hold one (except to appoint the auditor) unless a shareholder or director demands it; financial statements are then sent to shareholders yearly | s. 61 |
| Special meeting on demand | The board must call one at the demand of one director, or shareholders holding 10% of issued capital and 1% of votes, or 10% of votes | s. 63(א) |
| Timetable | The board convenes within 21 days of the demand | s. 63(ג) |
| If the board refuses | The demanding shareholders (or those with more than half their votes) may convene it themselves within three months, at the company's cost; or ask the court | ss. 64, 65 |
| Notice | At least 7 and no more than 45 days, unless the articles say otherwise; date, place, agenda and reasonable detail, and the text of any proposed amendment of articles | ss. 67–68 |
| Agenda item | A shareholder with 1% of votes may ask the board to add an item | s. 66(ב) |
| Only agenda items | Resolutions only on items on the agenda | s. 66(ג) |
| Quorum | Two shareholders with 25% of the votes within half an hour; adjourned one week; not for a one-shareholder company | s. 78 |
| Without a meeting | Unanimous resolution of all shareholders entitled to vote, without notice or meeting | s. 76 |
| By video or phone | Allowed unless the articles exclude it | s. 77 |
| Votes | One vote per share unless the articles set classes with different votes | s. 82 |
| Majority | Simple majority unless the law or the articles say otherwise | s. 85 |

The quorum rules (s. 81) and most notice rules can be changed in the articles; s. 57 cannot.

### The board in a private company

The board meets as the company needs, at least once a year (s. 97). Two directors (one, if the board has up to five) may demand a meeting on a specified subject, and if the chairman does not call it within 14 days they may call it themselves (s. 98). Quorum is a majority of directors (s. 104); one vote per director (s. 105). The board may meet by any means of communication (s. 101) and decide without meeting if all directors agree not to meet (s. 103). Every director must exercise independent judgment and may not be party to a voting agreement (s. 106(א)); a person who acts as a director without being appointed carries a director's duties (s. 106(ב)–(ג)).

### Shareholders' rights

> זכויותיו וחובותיו של בעל מניה הן כקבוע בחוק זה, בתקנון החברה ולפי כל דין.

*Gist:* A shareholder's rights and duties come from the law, the articles and the general law. [Companies Law s. 183](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_183)

- **Information** (ss. 184–187): lesson 16.6.
- **Vote** (s. 188): every shareholder may attend and vote, subject to the votes attached to his shares.
- **Voting agreements** (s. 189): shareholders (unlike directors) may agree how to vote, subject to their duties (s. 192).
- **Dividend** (s. 190): only once the company decides on a distribution (s. 306); there is no right to force one, except through the oppression remedy (lesson 16.6).
- **Pre-emption** (s. 290(א)): in a private company with one class of shares, new shares are offered to every shareholder in proportion to his holding, unless the articles say otherwise.
- **Register** (ss. 127, 129, 130): the company keeps a shareholders' register open to everyone, listing each holder's name, ID, address, shares and dates.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 817/79 קוסוי נ׳ בנק י.ל. פויכטוונגר בע״מ, פ״ד לח(3) 253 (9.7.1984)

The controlling shareholders of a bank sold control to buyers who, as they knew, would pay for the shares with the bank's own money; the directors approved the financing, and the bank collapsed. The Supreme Court described the director as the company's "brain" and "nerve centre", an organ for some purposes and an agent for others, and held that directors who approved the financing in breach of the law, knowing it would lead to insolvency, breached their duty of loyalty and were jointly and severally liable for the money lost; the controlling shareholders who sold knowing the buyer would strip the bank breached a duty of loyalty too. Why it matters: it is the founding case for the organ theory and for duties owed by those who hold power in a company; ss. 47, 106, 193 and 254 of the Companies Law build on it (lessons 16.4–16.5). Read it in English at [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/kossoy-and-filco-v-bank-feuchtwanger-ltd). Hebrew citation as given by Fiegenbaum and Licht; Hebrew party names ⚠️ unconfirmed.

### ע״א 5663/90 הספקה חברה מרכזית לחקלאים בע״מ (בפירוק) נ׳ גרוס, פ״ד מח(3) 86 (1994)

Cited by Fiegenbaum and Licht for the general manager's role as the person responsible for day-to-day management within the board's policy, the rule now in s. 120. Why it matters: when a company is run by one active founder, the question "who decided this?" usually ends with the general manager's powers and the board's duty to supervise him. Summary only: [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 26](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

## How it works in practice

### Which hat? A quick test

1. **Is it in s. 57?** Only the general meeting.
2. **Is it in s. 92 (policy, financial statements, GM appointment, allotment, distribution)?** The board, not delegable to the GM.
3. **Is it ordinary management?** The GM (or the board if there is no GM).
4. **Do the articles move it?** Check s. 50 clauses.
5. **Was the right procedure used?** Notice, agenda, quorum, majority, minutes (ss. 90, 108).

### Minutes and registers

The company keeps at its registered office the articles, general-meeting and board minutes, financial statements, the shareholders' register and the directors' register (s. 124); copies may be kept electronically (s. 125). Shareholders may inspect the general-meeting minutes and the shareholders' register (s. 184); the directors' register is open to everyone (s. 224).

## Worked example

Kfir Hi-Tech Ltd, in Yokneam, has three shareholders: Adnan Khatib (40%), Lior Peretz (40%) and Galit Marom (20%). Adnan and Lior are the two directors; Lior is also the general manager. The articles follow the defaults and allow skipping the annual meeting. Galit has received no financial statements for two years and hears that Lior gave himself a large raise.

1. **Annual meeting.** The articles let the company skip it, but Galit, as a shareholder, may demand one (s. 61(א)); and the company must anyway send financial statements yearly (s. 61(ב)). If it is not held, she can ask the court to order it (s. 62).
2. **Special meeting.** Galit holds 20% of capital and votes, above the 10% threshold (s. 63(א)(2)). She sends a written demand with the subjects: presentation of the financial statements and approval of officers' pay. The board must convene within 21 days (s. 63(ג)).
3. **The raise.** Lior's pay as general manager who is also a director is a transaction with a director on his terms of office, which needs board approval and then the general meeting (s. 273(א)); Lior has a personal interest and may not take part in the board vote (s. 278), which needs Adnan alone. If it was not approved this way it has no force against the company (s. 280).
4. **If the board does not convene.** Galit may convene the meeting herself within three months of her demand, at the company's expense (s. 64), or apply to the court (s. 65).
5. **Next steps.** At the meeting she asks for the documents relating to the pay transaction (s. 185(א)(1), lesson 16.6) and, if it was never approved, reserves her rights to a derivative claim (s. 194).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The articles of a private company you hold shares in, or the default rules of the Companies Law, or the Kfir Hi-Tech case.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Map the organs (10 min).** Who are the shareholders, directors and general manager? Draw a one-page chart.
2. **Classify five decisions (10 min).** For example: amending the articles, hiring a CFO, approving the financial statements, signing a lease, issuing new shares. Name the competent organ and section.
3. **Read the meeting rules (10 min).** Note what your articles change: annual meeting, notice, quorum, written resolutions, votes per share.
4. **Draft a demand (15 min).** In Hebrew, to the board: the demanding shareholder and his holding, the s. 63 basis, the subjects for the agenda (with proposed resolutions), the demand to convene within 21 days, and a reservation of the rights under ss. 64–65.

### Template

None specific. Use the letter structure of the [shareholder information request (draft)](../../templates/shareholder-information-request.md), part ג (דרישה לכינוס אסיפה כללית מיוחדת).

### How you know it worked

- Each decision is matched to an organ with a section number.
- Your demand states a holding that meets s. 63(א)(2) and lists the subjects clearly.
- You noted the 21-day and three-month periods.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can say which general-meeting powers cannot be taken away (s. 57).
- [ ] I know that board powers under s. 92 cannot be delegated to the general manager.
- [ ] I know the default quorum and when a written resolution is valid without a meeting.
- [ ] I know that shareholders may make voting agreements but directors may not (ss. 106, 189).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Demanding a meeting, attending and voting, and asking for documents are things a shareholder does in his own name. Applying to the court to convene a meeting (ss. 62, 65, 72) is a court proceeding: an individual may file it himself (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170; [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)), but a company in court generally acts through a lawyer.

### Where and how

- The demand goes to the board at the registered office (s. 123(ג)); send it by registered post and email to the digital address.
- Court applications under the Companies Law go to the district court, and matters of shareholders' rights to its Economic Department where it has jurisdiction (Courts Law ss. 40(5), 42ב(3); ⚠️ check the department's territorial scope). Fees: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The board refuses and you plan to convene the meeting yourself or apply to court.
- The dispute concerns control of the company or a large transaction.
- You are a director as well as a shareholder and may face duties of your own.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two founders "decide" an amendment of articles in a chat | No general-meeting resolution: no amendment | Hold a meeting or a unanimous written resolution (s. 76) | Use the right organ and keep minutes |
| Calling a meeting with 3 days' notice | The resolution can be challenged as defectively convened (ss. 67, 91) | Re-convene with proper notice | Check the articles' notice period |
| Resolving on a subject not on the agenda | The resolution is open to challenge (s. 66(ג)) | Re-convene with the item | Put every item and draft resolution on the agenda |
| A director signs a voting agreement with an investor | Breach of the duty of loyalty (s. 106(א)) | Cancel it; disclose | Voting agreements only at shareholder level (s. 189) |
| Ignoring a 10% shareholder's demand | The shareholder convenes at the company's cost; directors repay (s. 64(ב)) | Convene within 21 days | Diary every demand |

## Review

- The organs are the general meeting, the board and the general manager; their acts are the company's (ss. 46–47).
- The general meeting's s. 57 powers are mandatory; the board holds s. 92 powers and the residual authority (s. 49); the GM runs daily affairs (ss. 120–121).
- A 10% shareholder can force a special meeting within 21 days and convene it himself if the board refuses (ss. 63–64).
- Default quorum: two shareholders with 25%; unanimous written resolutions are valid in a private company (ss. 76, 78).
- Shareholders may make voting agreements; directors must use independent judgment (ss. 106, 189).
