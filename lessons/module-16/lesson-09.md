# 16.9 · Practice lab: a founders' agreement checklist and a shareholder's information request

In this lab you do the two documents that matter most for people who own part of a small company: the checklist of what founders must agree before they start, and the letter a shareholder sends when he wants to know what is going on. You work on your own company, a planned one, or the supplied case. It is a dry run; nothing is signed or sent unless you decide to, in your own name.

## Why it matters

Most disputes in lessons 16.3–16.6 start in the same place: founders who never wrote down who owns what, who decides, who is paid, and what happens when one wants out. Courts then have to reconstruct "reasonable expectations" from emails (lesson 16.6). A two-hour checklist at the start avoids most of that. And when trouble comes, the first move is almost always an information request: it is cheap, it is your statutory right, and the company's answer (or refusal) shapes every later step.

## The law

- **Articles v. agreement.** The articles bind the company and every shareholder, present and future (s. 17); a shareholders' agreement binds only those who signed it. Shareholders may make voting agreements (s. 189); directors may not (s. 106(א)). Entrenching a clause in the articles protects it against amendment by majority (ss. 20(ב), 22). [Companies Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות)
- **Pre-emption.** In a private company with one class of shares, new shares go to existing shareholders in proportion, unless the articles say otherwise (s. 290(א)).
- **Information.** As of right: minutes, register, articles, financial statements, public filings (ss. 184, 187); with a stated purpose: documents on interested-party transactions or agenda items (s. 185); 10% holders: auditor's statement of directors' pay (s. 186).
- **Escalation.** Special meeting on a 10% demand within 21 days (s. 63); self-convening within three months (s. 64); derivative-claim demand with a 45-day answer (ss. 194–196); court approval (s. 198); oppression (s. 191).

## Leading cases

### Key text: Hollander and the gap between articles and side agreements

As summarised by Fiegenbaum and Licht, in ע״א 54/96 הולנדר the Supreme Court refused to use a shareholders' agreement to interpret a listed company's articles ([n. 14](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf); ⚠️ full text not opened). The practical lesson for founders of any company: if a term must bind the company and future shareholders (transfer restrictions, a veto, a special majority), put it in the articles, not only in a side agreement. Lessons [16.2](lesson-02.md) and [16.6](lesson-06.md) give the rules.

## How it works in practice

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Where each term belongs

| Term | Articles | Founders' agreement | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share classes, votes, pre-emption | Yes | Summary | Binds the company and future holders (ss. 17, 82, 290) |
| Transfer restrictions (first refusal, tag, drag) | Yes | Yes | Enforceable against transferees only if in the articles |
| Reserved matters (veto list) | Yes, with entrenchment (s. 22) | Yes | A majority could otherwise amend them (s. 20) |
| Board composition and appointment rights | Yes | Yes | Appointment is a shareholder matter (s. 59) |
| Vesting and leaver terms | Usually agreement (with a buy-back mechanism) | Yes | Personal to the founders |
| Roles, time commitment, salaries | No | Yes | Employment and personal terms |
| IP assignment, confidentiality, non-compete | No | Yes (and in employment contracts) | Personal undertakings |
| Deadlock and exit (BMBY, valuation) | Possibly | Yes | Needs detailed mechanics |
| Dispute resolution | Possibly | Yes | Mediation then arbitration is common |

### The information request: making it hard to refuse

1. Ask first for what is yours as of right (ss. 184, 187); the company has no discretion there.
2. For other documents, state the purpose and tie each document to a transaction needing general-meeting approval or to an agenda item (s. 185(א)).
3. Offer confidentiality to pre-empt the trade-secret refusal (s. 185(ב)).
4. Give a reasonable date (14 days is common) and say what you will do next.
5. Send to the registered office and digital address; keep proof.

## Worked example

Maya Levi (30%), Daniel Cohen (40%) and Ahmad Mansour (30%) are founding "Haifa Robotics Ltd". Daniel will be CEO full time; Maya and Ahmad will work part time for the first year. An angel investor may come in.

1. **Checklist decisions.** Shares: 4,000 / 3,000 / 3,000 ordinary shares. Vesting over three years with a one-year cliff for all three. Reserved matters needing 75%: new share issues, sale of the company, related-party transactions, CEO pay. Board: three seats, one per founder, until the investment. First refusal and tag-along in the articles, entrenched. BMBY after two years of deadlock. IP assigned to the company from day one. Disputes: mediation, then arbitration.
2. **Where.** Classes, pre-emption, transfer restrictions and reserved matters go in the articles with an entrenchment clause (s. 22); vesting, roles, salaries, IP and non-compete go in the founders' agreement.
3. **Two years later.** Maya receives no financial statements and hears that Daniel signed a contract with his wife's company. She sends the information request (below): part א׳ as of right; part ב׳ for the contract documents, stating that they concern an extraordinary transaction with a director's relative needing general-meeting approval (ss. 185(א)(1), 270, 272); part ד׳ for directors' pay (she holds 30%, above 10%, s. 186). She diaries 14 days; if refused, she will demand a special meeting (s. 63) and consider a s. 194 demand.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your own company or planned company, or the Haifa Robotics case.
- The [founders' agreement checklist (draft)](../../templates/founders-agreement-checklist.md) and the [shareholder information request (draft)](../../templates/shareholder-information-request.md).
- About two and a half hours.

### Part A: founders' agreement checklist (80 min)

1. **Facts (10 min).** Founders, contributions (money, work, IP), expected time, the plan for investors.
2. **Fill the checklist (50 min).** Every section: shares and classes, vesting and leavers, roles and pay, decisions and reserved matters, board, transfers, new shares and dilution, deadlock and exit, conflicts of interest, IP and confidentiality, non-compete, death and incapacity, disputes.
3. **Allocate (20 min).** Mark each term "articles", "agreement" or "both", and write the entrenchment clause for the articles in Hebrew.

### Part B: information request and escalation plan (70 min)

1. **Facts (10 min).** Your holding, what you know, what you suspect.
2. **Draft the request (40 min)** in Hebrew on the template: parts א׳ (as of right), ב׳ (with purpose), ג׳ (meeting demand, if you hold 10%), ד׳ (directors' pay, if 10% and the company has an auditor); a confidentiality offer; a deadline.
3. **Escalation plan (20 min).** A table with dates: request, answer due, special meeting demand and the 21-day and three-month periods, s. 194 demand and the 45- and 75-day periods, court.

### Template

### How you know it worked

- Part A: every checklist section has a decision, and the terms that must bind future shareholders are in the articles.
- Part B: each document asked for is tied to s. 184, 185, 186 or 187; the s. 185 items state a purpose; the escalation plan has correct periods.

<details><summary>Reference answer for the Haifa Robotics request (gist)</summary>

To the board, at the registered office and digital address. Maya Levi, holder of 3,000 ordinary shares (30%). (א) Under ss. 184 and 187: copies of the articles and amendments, the financial statements for 2026 and 2027, minutes of all general meetings, the shareholders' register. (ב) Under s. 185(א)(1): all documents on the contract between the company and [wife's company], including the contract, board minutes and any approvals; purpose: to decide how to vote on its approval, which requires the general meeting because it is a transaction with a director's relative (ss. 270(1), 272(ב)); she undertakes confidentiality. (ד) Under s. 186: an auditor-approved statement of all payments to each director for the last three years. Answer within 14 days; otherwise she will demand a special meeting under s. 63 and reserve her rights under ss. 191 and 194.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may write your own information requests, meeting demands and s. 194 demands, and you may fill the checklist and negotiate with your co-founders. The founders' agreement and articles are legal documents: you may draft your own, but drafting them for other founders as a service is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20), and with investors or unequal contributions a lawyer is strongly advisable.

### Where and how

- Letters go to the company's registered office and digital address (ss. 123, 123א).
- Amended articles are filed with the Registrar within 14 days (s. 21(ב)), online by a reporting officer (reg. 16ג).
- Free help: Ma'of small-business centres; university law clinics on entrepreneurship (⚠️ manual check of current clinics); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- An investor, an employee option plan or foreign founders are involved.
- A founder wants to leave, or the company refuses information and you are considering court.
- The agreement will include non-compete or IP terms that may be tested in court.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer restrictions only in a side agreement | A buyer of shares is not bound | Amend the articles and entrench | Put binding terms in the articles |
| No vesting | A founder who leaves after three months keeps a third | Negotiate a buy-back; amend | Vesting with a cliff from day one |
| No deadlock clause in a 50/50 company | Paralysis; oppression or winding-up litigation | Mediation; agree BMBY now | Agree an exit mechanism at the start |
| Information request with no purpose | Lawful refusal under s. 185 | Re-send with purpose and confidentiality | Tie each item to a section |
| Waiting years before asking | Evidence lost; limitation | Ask now; diary | Ask for statements every year |

## Review

- Terms that must bind the company and future shareholders go in the articles, entrenched where needed; personal terms go in the founders' agreement.
- Vesting, reserved matters, transfer restrictions, deadlock and IP assignment are the core founders' terms.
- An information request asks first for what is yours as of right, then for documents tied to s. 185's grounds, with a purpose and a confidentiality offer.
- Escalation follows fixed periods: 21 days, three months, 45 days, 75 days.
