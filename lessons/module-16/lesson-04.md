# 16.4 · Officers' duties of care and loyalty

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say whether a director or manager of an Israeli company breached a duty to the company, which duty, and what follows. You will also know how a conflicted transaction is approved properly, and you will produce the disclosure-and-approval record that keeps a small company's founders out of trouble.

## Why it matters

Directors and managers control other people's money. Corporate law answers the risk with two duties: care (do the job competently and on an informed basis) and loyalty (act for the company, not for yourself). In small private companies the duties come up constantly: a founder who pays his own consulting firm, a director who takes a business opportunity for a side company, a manager who signs a large contract without reading it. The same founder may later be on the receiving end, as the minority shareholder whose partner did these things. Duties of officers are a classic Bar-exam topic.

## The law

### Who is an officer

> ”נושא משרה“ – מנהל כללי, מנהל עסקים ראשי, משנה למנהל כללי, סגן מנהל כללי, כל ממלא תפקיד כאמור בחברה אף אם תוארו שונה, וכן דירקטור או מנהל הכפוף במישרין למנהל הכללי;

*Gist:* Officers are the general manager and his deputies under any title, directors, and managers reporting directly to the general manager. [Companies Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_1) A person who performs a director's functions without being appointed carries a director's duties (s. 106(ב)–(ג)).

### The duty of care

> נושא משרה חב כלפי החברה חובת זהירות כאמור בסעיפים 35 ו־36 לפקודת הנזיקין [נוסח חדש].

*Gist:* An officer owes the company the tort-law duty of care. [Companies Law s. 252(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_252) It may also owe care to others (s. 252(ב)).

> נושא משרה יפעל ברמת מיומנות שבה היה פועל נושא משרה סביר, באותה עמדה ובאותן נסיבות, ובכלל זה ינקוט [...] באמצעים סבירים לקבלת מידע הנוגע לכדאיות העסקית של פעולה המובאת לאישורו [...]

*Gist:* An officer must act with the skill of a reasonable officer in the same position and circumstances, including taking reasonable steps to obtain information on the business merits of what he approves or does. [Companies Law s. 253](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_253) The standard links to negligence ([lesson 04.2](../module-04/lesson-02.md)): duty, breach measured by the reasonable officer, causation and damage.

### The duty of loyalty

> נושא משרה חב חובת אמונים לחברה, ינהג בתום לב ויפעל לטובתה, ובכלל זה – (1) יימנע מכל פעולה שיש בה ניגוד ענינים [...]; (2) יימנע מכל פעולה שיש בה תחרות עם עסקי החברה; (3) יימנע מניצול הזדמנות עסקית של החברה [...]; (4) יגלה לחברה כל ידיעה וימסור לה כל מסמך הנוגעים לעניניה [...]

*Gist:* An officer must act in good faith for the company's benefit: avoid conflicts of interest, competition with the company and taking its business opportunities, and disclose to it every relevant information and document. [Companies Law s. 254(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_254)

The company may approve an act within s. 254(א) only if the officer acts in good faith, the act does not harm the company, and he disclosed his personal interest, including every material fact and document, a reasonable time before the approval (s. 255(א)); approval follows the interested-party procedures of ch. 5 (s. 255(ב)). Remedies are those for breach of contract, with the officer treated as having breached his contract with the company (s. 256(א)–(ב)); the company may cancel the act against a third party who knew of the breach (s. 256(ג)). A director who learns of an apparent breach of law or of proper business practice must have the board convened without delay (s. 257).

### Interested-party transactions in a private company

| Transaction | Approval | Section |
|---|---|---|
| With an officer, or in which an officer has a personal interest, not extraordinary | Board, unless the articles set another way | ss. 270(1), 271 |
| Same, extraordinary (not in ordinary course, not on market terms, or materially affecting profits, assets or liabilities) | Audit committee then board; in a private company without an audit committee, the board, and the general meeting if the officer is a director | ss. 1, 272(א)–(ב) |
| Terms of office of an officer who is not a director | Board (private company without audit committee) | s. 272(ב) |
| Terms of office of a director | Board, then general meeting | s. 273(א) |

A person with a personal interest may not attend or vote in the board's discussion, except to present the transaction; if most directors have a personal interest, they may vote and the general meeting must also approve (s. 278). An officer must disclose his personal interest without delay, no later than the board meeting that first discusses it (s. 269(א)). A transaction not approved as required has no force against the company or the officer, and against a third party who knew (s. 280).

### Exemption, indemnity and insurance

> חברה אינה רשאית לפטור נושא משרה מאחריותו בשל הפרת חובת האמונים כלפיה.

*Gist:* A company may not exempt an officer from liability for breach of loyalty. [Companies Law s. 258(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_258) It may exempt in advance for breach of care if its articles allow, but not a director for care in a distribution (s. 259). It may indemnify (s. 260) and insure (s. 261) under the articles; insurance may cover breach of loyalty only where the officer acted in good faith with reasonable grounds to think the act would not harm the company (s. 261(2)). Nothing may cover breach of loyalty beyond that, a breach of care done intentionally or recklessly, an act meant to make an unlawful personal profit, or a fine or administrative penalty (s. 263).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 817/79 קוסוי נ׳ בנק י.ל. פויכטוונגר בע״מ, פ״ד לח(3) 253 (1984)

The directors approved the bank's financing of the purchase of its own shares, in breach of the law and knowing it would lead to insolvency. The Supreme Court held that the duty of loyalty is a general duty of anyone holding power over another, that a director may not be in a conflict between the company's good and his own or another's, that the burden of disproving bad faith shifts to him once there is a substantive suspicion, and that it is a personal duty that includes preventing other directors' breaches. Directors who breached were liable jointly and severally for money the company lost as a foreseeable result, even if a third party received it. Why it matters: it is the root of s. 254 and of s. 311 (directors' liability for a prohibited distribution, lesson 16.5). [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/kossoy-and-filco-v-bank-feuchtwanger-ltd)

### ע״א 267/55 טוקטלי נ׳ שמשון בע״מ, פ״ד יא(2) 1569 (1957)

As summarised by Fiegenbaum and Licht, the Court adopted the strict English "no further inquiry" rule (citing *Aberdeen Railway v. Blaikie*): a conflicted fiduciary's transaction is not saved by showing that it was fair. Why it matters: it explains why the Companies Law turns on disclosure and approval by disinterested organs (ss. 255, 270–280) rather than on a court's view of fairness. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 44](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### ע״א 7735/14 ורדניקוב נ׳ אלוביץ׳ (28.12.2016)

As summarised by Fiegenbaum and Licht, the Supreme Court adopted the business judgment rule with an Israeli-specific caveat: courts will not second-guess an informed, good-faith, unconflicted business decision, but where there is concern about officers' potential conflict (here, huge cash dividends after a leveraged change of control), "enhanced scrutiny" applies and the burden shifts to show a reasonable business logic. Why it matters: it is the current frame for duty-of-care claims; a director's best defence is a documented, informed process. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, nn. 45, 59](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### ע״א 610/94 בוכבינדר נ׳ כונס הנכסים הרשמי, פ״ד נז(4) 281 (2003)

Cited by Fiegenbaum and Licht for the baseline competence expected of every director, who must at least understand the company's business and its financial position as shown in its statements; a director who lacks the expertise may rely on advisers reasonably but may not abandon oversight. Why it matters: "I was only a figurehead director" is not a defence. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, nn. 53–54](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### The debate

Scholars and judges disagree on how far courts should review the *content* of business decisions. One view, influenced by Delaware, says courts should check only process (information, good faith, no conflict) and leave risk-taking to managers; otherwise directors become timid. The other says s. 253 speaks of a reasonable officer and asks for substantive reasonableness, especially where minority shareholders or creditors bear the risk. Fiegenbaum and Licht describe recent Supreme Court decisions as pointing both ways; the law is still settling.

## How it works in practice

### A four-question test for any officer's act

1. **Was there a personal interest?** (His own, a relative's, or a company he or a relative holds an interest in; s. 1 "עניין אישי".) If yes, loyalty and the approval procedure govern.
2. **Was it disclosed and approved correctly?** Timing (s. 269), organ (ss. 271–275), who abstained (s. 278).
3. **If no conflict, was the decision informed?** Board papers, professional advice, time to consider (s. 253).
4. **What is the remedy?** Account of profits and damages as for breach of contract (s. 256), cancellation against a knowing third party, or a derivative claim when the board will not sue (lesson 16.6).

### Corporate opportunity in small companies

The classic small-company case: a director hears of a deal in the company's line of business and does it through his own company. Section 254(א)(3) forbids exploiting the company's opportunity; s. 255 allows approval after full disclosure and without harm to the company. The safe course is to offer the opportunity to the company first, in writing, and let the disinterested directors (or shareholders) decline it on the record.

## Worked example

Bnei Brak Print Ltd has two equal shareholders and directors: Yaakov Rosenberg and Moshe Halevi. Yaakov's brother-in-law owns a paper supplier. In 2026 Yaakov signs a two-year supply contract with that supplier, at prices 15% above the market, without telling Moshe. Moshe discovers it when he compares invoices.

1. **Personal interest.** A brother-in-law is a "relative" (s. 1: the spouse's sibling, or the sibling's spouse, are listed), so Yaakov has a personal interest in the transaction (s. 270(1)).
2. **Extraordinary?** Prices above market: not on market terms, so it is an extraordinary transaction (s. 1). In a private company without an audit committee it needed board approval, and, since Yaakov is a director, general-meeting approval too (s. 272(ב)). Yaakov could not vote on the board (s. 278(א)).
3. **Disclosure.** Yaakov had to disclose the interest no later than the first board discussion (s. 269(א)). He did not.
4. **Consequence.** The contract has no force against the company or Yaakov, and none against the supplier if the brother-in-law knew of Yaakov's interest and should have known approval was missing (s. 280). Yaakov is liable as for breach of contract for the excess paid (s. 256). No exemption is available for a breach of loyalty (s. 258(א)).
5. **What Moshe does.** He calls a board meeting on the subject (s. 98(ב)(1)), demands the documents, and proposes that the company cancel the contract and claim the excess from Yaakov. If Yaakov blocks the company from suing, Moshe considers a derivative claim (lesson 16.6). As a director, Moshe must himself act once he knows (s. 257).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A company in which you are or could be an officer, or the Bnei Brak Print case.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **List personal interests (10 min).** Write every business you, your relatives (s. 1 definition) or companies you hold interests in have with the company or in its field.
2. **Classify one transaction (10 min).** Ordinary or extraordinary? Which organs approve? Who must abstain?
3. **Draft a disclosure (10 min).** In Hebrew, to the chairman: the transaction, the nature of the interest, all material facts and documents, the date, and a statement that you will not take part in the vote.
4. **Draft the approval minute (10 min).** The disinterested directors' finding that the transaction is for the company's benefit (s. 270 opening words), the information they reviewed, and the vote; plus the general meeting if required.
5. **Check the articles (5 min).** Do they allow exemption, indemnity and insurance? What would not be covered (s. 263)?

### Template

None specific. A clause list for the founders' agreement is in the [founders' agreement checklist (draft)](../../templates/founders-agreement-checklist.md), part ו (עניין אישי וניגוד עניינים).

### How you know it worked

- Your disclosure names the relative or company and the interest in concrete terms.
- The approval path matches s. 271, 272 or 273 and records who abstained.
- You can say which part of your conduct no clause in the articles can exempt.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know the four limbs of s. 254(א).
- [ ] I can state the conditions for approving a conflicted act (s. 255).
- [ ] I know that loyalty cannot be exempted but care can be, within limits (ss. 258–259, 263).
- [ ] I can explain the business judgment rule and enhanced scrutiny in two sentences.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for complying with your own duties as an officer: disclosures, abstentions, minutes. A claim against an officer is the company's claim; if it sues, a company generally appears in court through a lawyer, and a shareholder's derivative claim needs court approval (lesson 16.6). You cannot act in court for the company yourself.

### Where and how

- Inside the company: disclosure to the chairman, a board meeting, minutes kept at the registered office (ss. 108, 124).
- Court: claims under the Companies Law are in the district court; derivative claims in its Economic Department where it has jurisdiction (Courts Law ss. 40(5), 42ב(2)).
- D&O insurance: ask an insurance broker; check that the articles allow it (s. 261).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are an officer facing a claim or a demand under s. 194, or a criminal or regulatory investigation.
- The transaction involves a controlling shareholder of a public company or bondholders.
- You are asked to sign as a director of a company you do not understand ("figurehead director").

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not disclosing a relative's interest | Transaction without force (s. 280); liability (s. 256) | Disclose now and seek approval (s. 255), unwinding if needed | Keep a written list of interests and update it |
| Voting on your own transaction | Defective approval (s. 278) | Re-approve with disinterested directors | Leave the room; record it |
| Approving without reading the papers | Breach of care (s. 253) | Re-examine with advice; document | Ask for papers in advance and minute the discussion |
| Taking a deal for your side company | Breach of loyalty, account of profits | Offer it to the company; seek approval | Offer opportunities first, in writing |
| Relying on an exemption clause for disloyalty | No protection (ss. 258(א), 263) | Settle; insurance only if s. 261(2) applies | Know what clauses can and cannot cover |

## Review

- Officers owe care (ss. 252–253, the reasonable officer, an informed process) and loyalty (s. 254: conflict, competition, opportunity, disclosure).
- A conflicted act can be approved only with good faith, no harm to the company and full prior disclosure (s. 255), by the organs in ss. 270–275, without interested votes (s. 278).
- Unapproved interested transactions have no force (s. 280); remedies follow breach of contract (s. 256).
- Exemption covers care only; insurance and indemnity have limits; disloyalty, intent, recklessness and fines are never covered (ss. 258–263).
- Kossoy founded the duty of loyalty; Vrednikov frames the business judgment rule and enhanced scrutiny.
