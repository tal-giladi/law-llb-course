# 16.5 · Lifting the corporate veil, distributions and creditor protection

By the end of this lesson you will know when an Israeli court may make a shareholder pay a company's debt, when a company may pay its shareholders a dividend or buy back their shares, and what happens when it pays out money it should have kept for creditors. You will then advise a creditor of a small company how to protect himself before giving credit.

## Why it matters

Limited liability shifts risk from shareholders to creditors. The law tolerates that only if the company's capital is not drained away and the corporate form is not abused. Two sets of rules police the line: lifting the veil (הרמת מסך) when the separate personality is used to defraud or to take unreasonable risks at creditors' expense, and the distribution rules, which forbid paying shareholders out of money needed to pay debts. For a small business owner these rules decide whether taking money out of the company is safe; for a supplier, employee or landlord of an insolvent company they are often the only route to recovery. Both are Bar-exam favourites.

## The law

### Lifting the veil

> בית משפט רשאי לייחס חוב של חברה לבעל מניה בה, אם מצא כי בנסיבות הענין צודק ונכון לעשות כן, במקרים החריגים שבהם השימוש באישיות המשפטית הנפרדת נעשה באחד מאלה: (א) באופן שיש בו כדי להונות אדם או לקפח נושה של החברה; (ב) באופן הפוגע בתכלית החברה ותוך נטילת סיכון בלתי סביר באשר ליכולתה לפרוע את חובותיה, ובלבד שבעל המניה היה מודע לשימוש כאמור [...]

*Gist:* A court may attribute a company's debt to a shareholder, if just and proper, only in exceptional cases where the separate personality was used to defraud a person or to deprive a creditor, or in a way that harms the company's purpose while taking an unreasonable risk to its ability to pay its debts, and only if the shareholder was aware of that use, having regard to his holdings, his duties under ss. 192–193 and the company's ability to pay. [Companies Law s. 6(א)(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_6)

A shareholder who suspected the conduct but chose not to check is treated as aware, unless he was merely negligent (s. 6(א)(2)). The court may also attribute a shareholder's characteristic, right or duty to the company, or the company's right to a shareholder, in light of the purpose of the law or contract involved (s. 6(ב)), and may subordinate a shareholder's loan to the company until all other creditors are paid in full, if the conditions for attribution exist (s. 6(ג)). After lifting the veil the court may bar the shareholder from being a director or general manager, or founding or managing a company, for up to five years (s. 7).

The 2005 amendment (Amendment 3) narrowed the original 1999 text: "just and proper" is not enough alone; one of the two abuse grounds and the shareholder's awareness must be shown.

### Distributions

> חברה רשאית לבצע חלוקה מתוך רווחיה (להלן – מבחן הרווח), ובלבד שלא קיים חשש סביר שהחלוקה תמנע מן החברה את היכולת לעמוד בחבויותיה הקיימות והצפויות, בהגיע מועד קיומן (להלן – מבחן יכולת הפרעון).

*Gist:* A company may distribute out of its profits, provided there is no reasonable concern that the distribution will prevent it from meeting its existing and expected obligations when due. [Companies Law s. 302(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_302)

- **"Distribution"** is a dividend or an undertaking to give one, directly or indirectly, and a purchase of the company's own shares (including financing someone else's purchase of them) (s. 1). **"Dividend"** includes any asset given to a shareholder by virtue of his shares, including a transfer without equivalent consideration (s. 1).
- **"Profits"** for the profit test are the retained earnings, or the earnings accumulated over the last two years, whichever is higher, in the latest adjusted, audited or reviewed financial statements, less earlier distributions, provided the statements are not more than six months old at the distribution (s. 302(ב)).
- **Court approval.** A company that fails the profit test may ask the court to approve a distribution if it passes the solvency test; creditors are notified and may object (s. 303).
- **Who decides.** The board, unless the articles give it to the general meeting on the board's recommendation, or another way that lets the board check that it is not prohibited (s. 307).
- **No contracting out.** A company may distribute only under these rules, though it may agree to stricter limits (s. 301(א)); a distribution in breach is prohibited (s. 301(ב)).

### Consequences of a prohibited distribution

> ביצעה חברה חלוקה אסורה, יהיה על בעל מניה להשיב לחברה את שקיבל, זולת אם לא ידע ולא היה עליו לדעת כי החלוקה שבוצעה אסורה.

*Gist:* A shareholder must return what he received in a prohibited distribution unless he did not and should not have known it was prohibited (a public-company shareholder who is not a director, GM or controlling shareholder is presumed not to know). [Companies Law s. 310](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_310)

Every director at the time is deemed to have breached his duties (ss. 252–254) unless he proves he opposed it and took all reasonable steps to stop it, relied reasonably and in good faith on misleading information, or did not and should not have known of it (s. 311). A director cannot be exempted in advance for breach of care in a distribution (s. 259(ב)). A creditor may bring a derivative claim on the company's behalf for a prohibited distribution (s. 204).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 4263/04 קיבוץ משמר העמק נ׳ מנור, עו״ד, מפרק אפרוחי הצפון בע״מ, פ״ד סג(1) 458 (2009)

As summarised by Fiegenbaum and Licht, the liquidator of a poultry company asked to subordinate its shareholders' loans. The Supreme Court majority held that in charting the company's course the interests of all stakeholders, especially creditors, must be balanced, and that thin capitalisation by itself could justify subordination, reasoning from creditors' property rights and their weaker access to information. Why it matters: it is the leading case on s. 6(ג) and on financing a company with shareholder loans instead of equity; Fiegenbaum and Licht criticise it for giving no guidance on when a risk is "unreasonable". [Fiegenbaum & Licht, nn. 41, 79](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### ע״א 817/79 קוסוי נ׳ בנק י.ל. פויכטוונגר בע״מ, פ״ד לח(3) 253 (1984)

The bank financed the buyers' purchase of its own shares, which stripped it of assets. Under today's law that financing is a "purchase", and so a distribution (s. 1), that fails the solvency test; directors who approved it are liable under s. 311 unless one of its defences applies. Why it matters: it shows the policy behind the distribution rules, protecting creditors from a drain of capital to insiders. [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/kossoy-and-filco-v-bank-feuchtwanger-ltd)

### ע״א 7735/14 ורדניקוב נ׳ אלוביץ׳ (28.12.2016)

As summarised by Fiegenbaum and Licht, the case concerned huge cash dividends approved after a leveraged change of control; the Court applied enhanced scrutiny to the board's decision because of potential conflicts. Why it matters: passing the arithmetic of the profit test does not end the inquiry; the board must also show a real, informed solvency assessment and a reasonable business logic. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 45](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### Key text: Ofir, Pelach and Procaccia, "Lifting the Veil: A Theoretical and Empirical Inquiry" (2019)

The authors' central argument is that the veil should not be lifted where the creditors charged, when they gave credit, a premium for the extra risk of lending to a limited-liability company, and should be lifted more readily where no such premium was charged, because limited liability then lets shareholders push costs onto creditors without justification. They analyse creditor types separately (public bondholders, involuntary creditors such as tort victims, the state treasury, junior employees, private voluntary creditors) and company types (public companies with controlling shareholders, small private companies, groups), and study about 200 Israeli rulings from 2011–2016, finding that judges sometimes rely on the wrong parameters. Why it matters: it gives a principled way to read the open words "צודק ונכון" and "סיכון בלתי סביר". [Iyunei Mishpat 42 (2019) 199, free PDF](https://www.taulawreview.sites.tau.ac.il/_files/ugd/e74fa2_9723bedb2be34d9fa437d5c75e849d6c.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### Lifting the veil: a checklist

1. **Who is the creditor?** Contract creditor who could price risk, or involuntary creditor (tort, wages, tax)?
2. **Which ground?** Fraud or deprivation of a creditor (asset stripping, moving the business to a new company, false statements), or unreasonable risk contrary to the company's purpose (trading on while insolvent with no capital).
3. **Which shareholder?** His control, his knowledge or wilful blindness (s. 6(א)(2)), his own conduct.
4. **Which court?** The court competent for the claim itself, not necessarily the district court (Courts Law s. 40(5) excludes ss. 6–7 from the district court's exclusive list); labour courts apply s. 6 in wage claims.
5. **Remedy.** Attribution of the specific debt; subordination of the shareholder's loans in liquidation; a s. 7 restriction order.

### The distribution test in a small company

Before taking a dividend, the owner of a small company asks the accountant three questions: what are the retained earnings in statements no older than six months (profit test); what are the company's expected obligations in the coming period and can it meet them (solvency test); and is the money being taken in another form (a "loan" to the shareholder never repaid, a car, a below-market sale of an asset), which is also a dividend under s. 1. The board's resolution should record both tests.

### How creditors protect themselves

- Personal guarantees from shareholders (the practical answer for banks and landlords).
- Security over company assets (charges registered with the Registrar).
- Check the company's extract and filings before giving credit (lesson 16.8): a "violating company", unpaid fees, recent transfers of shares to an unknown person, a new company with the same name.
- Shorter credit terms, retention of title on goods.

## Worked example

Hassan Abu-Ghosh supplied ₪180,000 of stone to "Golan Build Ltd", a building contractor owned by Eitan Ben-David (100%) and managed by him. Golan Build stopped paying in March 2026. In April, Eitan formed "Golan Build 2026 Ltd", moved the equipment, staff and current projects to it, and Golan Build was left with debts and no assets. In January 2026, with sales already falling, Golan Build paid Eitan a ₪400,000 dividend.

1. **Veil (s. 6(א)(1)(א)).** Moving the business to a new company to leave creditors behind is a classic use of the separate personality "to deprive a creditor". Eitan, as sole shareholder and manager, was obviously aware. Hassan can ask the court to attribute the debt to Eitan, and also claim against the new company (on a separate theory of continuity, depending on the facts).
2. **The dividend (ss. 302, 310).** If, in January, there was a reasonable concern that Golan Build could not meet its existing and expected obligations, the dividend failed the solvency test even if the books showed profits. Eitan, as shareholder who knew, must return it (s. 310); as the director who approved it, he is deemed to have breached his duties (s. 311).
3. **Who can claim the dividend back?** It is the company's claim. A creditor may bring a derivative claim for a prohibited distribution (s. 204), and in insolvency the trustee will pursue it (s. 205 bars derivative claims once a trustee is appointed).
4. **Forum.** Hassan's claim for ₪180,000 and attribution goes to the magistrates' court (its civil ceiling covers it; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), which may decide s. 6 (Courts Law s. 40(5)).
5. **Restriction (s. 7).** The court may also bar Eitan from managing companies for up to five years.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A company you own or deal with as a creditor (a supplier, landlord or employer that owes you money), or the Golan Build case.
- The company's extract and, if you can get them, its financial statements (lesson 16.8).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Map the money (10 min).** List payments from the company to shareholders in the last two years: dividends, "loans", salaries above market, assets transferred.
2. **Apply the distribution tests (15 min).** For each, would it pass the profit test (s. 302(ב)) and the solvency test at the date paid? Is it a "dividend" by s. 1 although called something else?
3. **Apply s. 6 (10 min).** Is there a ground (fraud or deprivation; unreasonable risk), awareness, and a just and proper reason? What would the shareholder argue?
4. **Write a creditor's protection memo (10 min).** One page: what you would ask for next time (guarantee, security, information), and what claim you have now and against whom.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- You classified every payment correctly as a distribution or not, with s. 1.
- You applied both distribution tests, not only profits.
- Your s. 6 analysis names the specific ground and the evidence of awareness.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can state the two grounds of s. 6(א)(1) and the awareness requirement.
- [ ] I know the difference between attribution, reverse attribution and subordination.
- [ ] I can apply the profit test and the solvency test.
- [ ] I know who must repay a prohibited distribution and the directors' three defences.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. An individual creditor may sue a company and its shareholder in his own name, including a request to lift the veil (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). Small claims are available for claims within the ceiling (only an individual may file there); but a veil-lifting argument against a determined shareholder is complex. A derivative claim for a prohibited distribution needs court approval (lesson 16.6).

### Where and how

- Magistrates' or district court by the amount; labour court for wage claims; the court competent for the claim decides s. 6 (Courts Law s. 40(5)).
- Fees and ceilings: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Execution against the shareholder after judgment: [lesson 08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md).
- Free help: court information centres; Legal Aid for eligible individuals; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The company is insolvent or in insolvency proceedings (a trustee controls the claims).
- The shareholder has moved assets abroad or into other companies.
- You are the shareholder and a creditor alleges abuse or asks for a restriction order.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taking a dividend because the books show profit | Prohibited distribution if solvency fails; repayment (s. 310) and director liability (s. 311) | Return the money; document a solvency assessment | Apply both tests and minute them |
| Calling withdrawals "shareholder loans" | Treated as a dividend or subordinated (ss. 1, 6(ג)) | Formal loan terms and repayment | Pay yourself salary or a lawful dividend |
| Moving the business to a new company when debts pile up | Veil lifted; restriction order (ss. 6–7) | Settle with creditors; insolvency advice | Never strip a company with unpaid creditors |
| A supplier extends large credit with no guarantee | Unsecured claim against an empty company | Seek attribution if there was abuse | Personal guarantee or security before credit |
| Assuming the veil is never lifted | Surprise personal liability for wages or taxes | Pay employees and taxes first | Treat involuntary creditors as top priority |

## Review

- The veil is lifted only exceptionally: fraud or deprivation of a creditor, or unreasonable risk against the company's purpose, with the shareholder's awareness (s. 6(א)); subordination and reverse attribution also exist (s. 6(ב)–(ג)).
- A company may distribute only out of profits and only if it stays able to pay its debts (s. 302); the court may approve a distribution that fails only the profit test (s. 303).
- "Distribution" includes buy-backs and financing purchases of the company's shares; "dividend" includes any asset given without equivalent value (s. 1).
- Recipients who knew must repay; directors are deemed in breach unless they prove a defence (ss. 310–311); creditors may sue derivatively (s. 204).
- Contract creditors usually protect themselves with guarantees and security.
