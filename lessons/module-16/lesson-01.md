# 16.1 · Legal persons and business forms: company, partnership, sole trader, amuta, cooperative

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any Israeli business or project, say which legal form it uses and what follows from that, and choose a form for something you plan to start. You will see what a legal person is, why the company is the dominant form, and why a sole trader, a partnership, an amuta and a cooperative society still exist alongside it.

## Why it matters

Every time you sign with a supplier, hire a contractor, lend to a friend's start-up or join a neighbourhood association, the first legal question is *who* is on the other side. A sole trader answers with everything he owns. A general partner answers for the whole partnership's debts. A company answers only with its own assets, and its shareholders usually lose no more than they put in. An amuta cannot distribute profits at all. Getting this wrong has real consequences: you sue the wrong party, you accept a company's signature where you needed a personal guarantee, or you start a business as a "partnership" without noticing that you have just become liable for your partner's debts.

Corporate law (דיני תאגידים) is also a Bar-exam subject and a core second-year course at every Israeli law faculty (TAU 1411-9222, HUJI 62611, Bar-Ilan 99205). This module follows the university course and adds the practical side: registering a company yourself, reading company filings, and using your rights as a shareholder.

## The law

### The company is a legal person

> חברה היא אישיות משפטית כשרה לכל זכות, חובה ופעולה המתיישבת עם אופייה וטבעה כגוף מואגד.

*Gist:* A company is a legal person capable of any right, duty and act consistent with its nature as a body corporate. [Companies Law s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_4)

It exists from the date of incorporation shown on the certificate until it is dissolved (s. 5). Anyone may found a company for a lawful purpose (s. 2), and a company may have a single shareholder (s. 3: "לחברה יכול שיהיה בעל מניה אחד").

### Limited liability is a choice made in the articles

> אחריות בעלי המניות לחובות החברה יכול שתהיה לא מוגבלת, והדבר יצוין בתקנון; היתה אחריות בעלי המניות מוגבלת, יפורט אופן ההגבלה בתקנון.

*Gist:* Shareholders' liability may be unlimited or limited; the articles say which and how. [Companies Law s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_35) A limited company must end its name with "בערבון מוגבל" or "בע״מ" (s. 26). A company whose shares are listed or offered to the public is a public company; every other company is private (s. 1).

### The company's purpose

> תכלית חברה היא לפעול על פי שיקולים עסקיים להשאת רווחיה, וניתן להביא בחשבון במסגרת שיקולים אלה, בין השאר, את עניניהם של נושיה, עובדיה ואת ענינו של הציבור [...]

*Gist:* A company aims to maximise its profits by business considerations, which may take into account creditors, employees and the public; it may donate a reasonable sum to a worthy cause if the articles allow. [Companies Law s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_11) A public-benefit company (Part 9, ch. 1א) is the exception.

### Partnerships

> ”קשרי שותָפות“ – הקשרים שבין בני אדם המנהלים יחד עסק לשם הפקת רווחים [...]

*Gist:* A partnership is the relation between persons who carry on a business together to make profits. [Partnerships Ordinance s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_השותפויות#סעיף_1) It must be registered within a month of being formed (s. 4), but failing to register does not mean the partnership does not exist (s. 6). Each partner is liable jointly and severally for the partnership's obligations (s. 20(א)), though execution against a partner personally generally waits until the partnership has been sued and has not paid (s. 20(ב)). A limited partnership needs at least one general partner with full liability and at least one limited partner liable only up to his contribution (s. 57). Sharing co-owned property or its gross income does not by itself make people partners (s. 2). A general partnership may have no more than twenty general partners (s. 3).

### Amutot and cooperative societies (brief)

> שני בני אדם או יותר, שהם בגירים, החפצים להתאגד כתאגיד למטרה חוקית שאינה מכוונת לחלוקת רווחים בין חבריו [...] רשאים לייסד עמותה; העמותה תיכון עם רישומה בפנקס העמותות.

*Gist:* Two or more adults may found an amuta for a lawful purpose not aimed at distributing profits to members; it comes into being on registration. [Amutot Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העמותות#סעיף_1) The application is filed online by the founders (s. 2); an amuta is refused registration if its purposes deny the existence of the State of Israel or its democratic character, or if it is likely to be a cover for illegal activity (s. 3). Its articles bind it and its members like a contract, and without its own articles the model articles apply (ss. 9–10).

A cooperative society (אגודה שיתופית) is registered under the Cooperative Societies Ordinance for thrift, self-help and mutual aid among people with common economic interests (s. 4); no member other than another society or body corporate may hold more than a fifth of the share capital (s. 5), and on registration it becomes a body corporate that can own property, contract and sue (s. 21). Kibbutzim, moshavim and many community settlements are cooperative societies; lesson 16.7 returns to them briefly.

## Leading cases

### Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1896] UKHL 1, [1897] AC 22

Aron Salomon turned his boot business into a company in which he held 20,001 of 20,007 shares and secured debentures. When it failed, the liquidator argued that the company was only his agent, so he should pay its creditors. The House of Lords unanimously held that a duly incorporated company is a different person from its members, even when one person holds almost all the shares, and the unsecured creditors could not reach Salomon. Why it matters: the Israeli companies law came out of the English Companies Acts, and Israeli courts still treat Salomon as the starting point. Section 3 (one-person company) and s. 4 state the same idea, and s. 6 (lesson 16.5) is the exception. [Summary and BAILII citation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salomon_v_A_Salomon_%26_Co_Ltd)

### Key text: Fiegenbaum and Licht, *Corporate Law of Israel* (2017)

A short English survey of the Companies Law by two Israeli scholars: legal personality, founding documents, organs, duties, related-party transactions, the veil and derivative claims. It shows how much of the Israeli law is codified and where the case law is still unsettled, and it is the best free overview to read alongside this module. [ECGI Working Paper 372/2017](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### Comparing the forms

| | Sole trader (עוסק) | General partnership | Limited partnership | Private company (בע״מ) | Amuta | Cooperative society |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Separate legal person? | No | Not a full legal person; registered under the Ordinance | As general partnership | Yes (s. 4) | Yes, on registration | Yes (s. 21) |
| Owners' liability | Unlimited, personal | Each partner jointly and severally (s. 20) | General partner unlimited; limited partner up to his contribution (s. 57) | Usually up to the unpaid price of the shares (s. 35) | Members not liable for its debts | By its bylaws (limited or unlimited, s. 4) |
| Formation | Open files at VAT, Income Tax and National Insurance (lesson 16.7) | Notice to the Registrar of Partnerships within a month (ss. 4, 7) | Notice signed by partners with the limited partners' contributions (s. 58) | Online application to the Companies Registrar (lesson 16.2) | Online application by two or more adult founders (Amutot Law s. 2) | Application to the Registrar of Cooperative Societies |
| Profits | Owner's income | Shared as agreed (s. 34) | As agreed | Distributed only under the profit and solvency tests (lesson 16.5) | Not distributable | By bylaws |
| Typical use | Freelancer, small shop | Two professionals, family business | Investment funds, real-estate projects | Most businesses and start-ups | Charities, community groups, sports clubs | Kibbutz, moshav, consumer or credit cooperative |

### Costs and running burden

A sole trader's main costs are bookkeeping and tax compliance. A company adds a registration fee, an annual fee to the Registrar, audited annual financial statements, an annual report to the Registrar, and in practice an accountant for corporate tax returns (amounts on [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). The extra cost buys limited liability, an easy way to bring in partners and investors, and a separate tax regime. Banks and landlords often ask a small company's shareholders for personal guarantees, so the protection in practice is mainly against trade creditors, tort claimants and tax authorities, not against the bank.

### Where to look a body up

- Companies, partnerships and public-benefit companies: the Corporations Authority's online registry (רשם החברות, רשם השותפויות), where an extract (נסח חברה) costs a small fee (lesson 16.8).
- Amutot: the Registrar of Amutot (רשם העמותות) and GuideStar Israel.
- Cooperative societies: the Registrar of Cooperative Societies at the Ministry of Economy (⚠️ manual check of the current search service).

## Worked example

Yonatan Tesfaye, from Netanya, wants to open a small catering business with his cousin Mazal, serving Ethiopian-Israeli weddings and community events. They plan to rent a kitchen for ₪6,000 a month, buy equipment for ₪80,000 with a bank loan, and hire two cooks. A friend tells them "just open a partnership, it's simpler".

1. **What a "simple partnership" means.** If they run the business together for profit, they are partners whether or not they sign anything (Ordinance s. 1) and must register within a month (s. 4). Each of them is liable jointly and severally for the whole loan, the rent and any tort claim by a guest who gets food poisoning (s. 20). Mazal owns a flat; Yonatan does not. Mazal is the one really at risk.
2. **The company alternative.** A private company with both of them as shareholders and directors would be the debtor for the rent, the loan and the wages. Their personal exposure would be limited to what they pay for their shares (s. 35), subject to any personal guarantees the bank or landlord demands and to lifting the veil in exceptional cases (lesson 16.5).
3. **What does not change.** The bank will almost certainly demand personal guarantees. Food-safety and business-licensing duties apply either way. Tort victims can also sue an individual who was personally negligent (lesson 16.4).
4. **Not an amuta.** The business aims to make profits for its owners, so an amuta is not available (Amutot Law s. 1). If the community later sets up a fund to subsidise weddings for low-income families, that could be an amuta.
5. **Decision.** They choose a private company with two equal shareholders and a founders' agreement (lesson 16.9). Mazal limits her personal guarantee to the bank loan and refuses one for the kitchen lease.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One real business, project or organisation you are involved in or plan to start (your own freelance work, a family shop, a sports club, a start-up idea), or the Tesfaye case.
- The comparison table above.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Describe the activity (5 min).** Who are the people, what will they do, who will they contract with, what could go wrong (debts, injuries, tax)?
2. **Identify the current form (10 min).** If it already exists, find out what it is: check the business's invoices (a VAT number of a sole trader is the owner's ID number; a company number usually starts with 51; an amuta's with 58), and look it up in the relevant registry.
3. **List the risks (10 min).** Write the three largest liabilities the activity could create and who would bear each under the current form.
4. **Compare two forms (10 min).** For the two most realistic forms, fill a short table: liability, formation steps, annual costs, how profits reach the owners, how a new partner joins.
5. **Decide (5 min).** One paragraph: which form and why, and what the form does not solve (personal guarantees, your own negligence, licensing).

### Template

None. Use the comparison table in this lesson.

### How you know it worked

- You named the form correctly and supported it with a document or registry entry.
- Your risk list puts each liability on a specific person or entity.
- Your decision names at least one risk that limited liability does not remove.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for choosing a form for your own business and looking bodies up in the public registries. Registering your own company online as a shareholder is possible without a lawyer (lesson 16.2); opening your own sole-trader files is routine (lesson 16.7). Drafting incorporation documents or agreements *for other people* as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20; [lesson 01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)).

### Where and how

- Corporations Authority (רשות התאגידים) at the Ministry of Justice: companies, partnerships, public-benefit companies (⚠️ manual check of the current online services on gov.il).
- Registrar of Amutot: online registration of an amuta (Amutot Law s. 2).
- Tax Authority and National Insurance for sole traders (lesson 16.7).
- Free help: Ma'of (מעו״ף) small-business centres of the Ministry of Economy offer low-cost advice to new businesses (⚠️ manual check of current services); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Several founders will invest unequal amounts or work unequal hours, or an investor is involved.
- The activity involves regulated work (food, finance, health, real estate) or significant public liability.
- You are asked to sign a personal guarantee for a large or open-ended amount.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting a joint business without any structure | An unregistered partnership: each partner liable for all its debts (s. 20) | Register the partnership or move the business into a company; agree in writing who owns what | Decide on the form before signing the first lease or loan |
| Signing contracts in your own name although the company exists | You, not the company, are the contracting party | Ask the other party to novate the contract to the company | Always sign as "X בע״מ, by Y, director", with the company number |
| Assuming a company protects you from your own negligence | Personal tort liability (lesson 16.4) | Insurance; correct the practice | Keep personal insurance and good practices |
| Using an amuta to run a profit-making business for its founders | Refusal or deregistration; tax problems | Separate the commercial activity into a company | Choose the amuta only for a non-profit purpose |
| Treating co-owners of a flat as partners | Wrong rules applied (s. 2(1)) | Apply the Land Law co-ownership rules ([lesson 13.5](../module-13/lesson-05.md)) | Ask whether there is a business carried on together for profit |

## Review

- A company is a separate legal person (s. 4); it can have one shareholder (s. 3); limited liability is set in its articles (s. 35).
- Partners carry on a business together for profit and are jointly and severally liable (Ordinance ss. 1, 20); registration is required within a month but does not create the partnership (ss. 4, 6).
- A limited partnership needs at least one general partner; a limited partner who manages becomes liable like a general partner (ss. 57, 63(ג)).
- An amuta is a non-profit legal person founded by two or more adults; a cooperative society serves mutual economic aid.
- Limited liability protects mainly against trade and tort creditors; banks usually take personal guarantees.
