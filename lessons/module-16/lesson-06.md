# 16.6 · Shareholder remedies: oppression, derivative claims and inspection rights

By the end of this lesson you will know what a minority shareholder can do when the majority shuts him out, takes the profits through salaries, or lets a director harm the company: ask for documents, ask the court to remove oppression, or sue on the company's behalf. You will also be able to tell which of these fits a given complaint, and what the first step of each looks like.

## Why it matters

In a private company the minority has no market to exit through. If the majority stops sharing information, pays itself instead of paying dividends, or diverts business, the minority is locked in. The Companies Law gives three tools. Information rights let the shareholder see what is going on. The oppression remedy (קיפוח) lets the court reshape the company's affairs or order a buy-out. The derivative claim (תביעה נגזרת) lets a shareholder or director sue in the company's name when the people who control it will not sue themselves. Choosing the wrong tool is costly: a derivative claim filed as a personal claim will be struck out, and an oppression claim without proof of reasonable expectations will fail. These remedies are a core Bar-exam topic.

## The law

### Information

> לבעלי מניות זכות עיון במסמכי החברה המפורטים להלן: (1) פרוטוקולים של האסיפות הכלליות [...]; (2) מרשם בעלי המניות [...]; (3) מסמך שברשות החברה, כאמור בסעיף 185; (4) תקנון ודוחות כספיים כאמור בסעיף 187; (5) כל מסמך שעל החברה להגיש [...] לרשם החברות [...]

*Gist:* Shareholders may inspect general-meeting minutes, the shareholders' register, certain company documents under s. 185, the articles and financial statements, and every document filed publicly with the Registrar. [Companies Law s. 184](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_184)

> בעל מניה זכאי לדרוש מהחברה, תוך ציון מטרת הדרישה, לעיין בכל מסמך הנמצא ברשות החברה בכל אחד מאלה: (1) המסמך נוגע לפעולה או לעסקה הטעונה אישור האסיפה הכללית לפי הוראות סעיפים 255 ו־268 עד 275; (2) בחברה פרטית – אם הדבר נדרש לצורך קבלת החלטה בנושא שעל סדר יומה של האסיפה הכללית של החברה.

*Gist:* A shareholder may demand, stating his purpose, to inspect any document in the company's possession that concerns an act or transaction needing general-meeting approval under ss. 255 and 268–275, or, in a private company, that he needs to decide on an item on the general meeting's agenda. [Companies Law s. 185(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_185) The company may refuse if it thinks the request is in bad faith, or the documents contain a trade secret or patent, or disclosure may otherwise harm the company's interest (s. 185(ב)).

In a non-reporting company that must have an auditor, the board must, at the demand of a holder of 10% of the votes, give him an auditor-approved statement of all payments to each director in the last three years of financial statements, including retirement terms and payments as an officer of a subsidiary (s. 186(א)), unless the demand is in bad faith (s. 186(ב)). Every shareholder may get a copy of the articles and, in a non-reporting company, of the financial statements (s. 187(א)).

### Oppression

> התנהל ענין מענייניה של חברה בדרך שיש בה משום קיפוח של בעלי המניות שלה, כולם או חלקם, או שיש חשש מהותי שיתנהל בדרך זו, רשאי בית המשפט, לפי בקשת בעל מניה, לתת הוראות הנראות לו לשם הסרתו של הקיפוח או מניעתו, ובהן הוראות שלפיהן יתנהלו עניני החברה בעתיד, או הוראות לבעלי המניות בחברה, לפיהן ירכשו הם או החברה [...] מניות ממניותיה.

*Gist:* If the company's affairs are conducted in a way that oppresses some or all shareholders, or there is a substantial fear they will be, the court may, at a shareholder's request, give any directions to remove or prevent it, including how the company is to be run in future and orders that shareholders or the company buy shares. [Companies Law s. 191(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_191) The court's changes are deemed adopted by the company and reported to the Registrar (s. 191(ב)).

Every shareholder must act in good faith and in a customary way towards the company and other shareholders, avoid abusing his power (especially in votes on amending the articles, increasing capital, mergers and interested-party transactions) and refrain from oppressing other shareholders (s. 192(א)–(ב)). A controlling shareholder, a shareholder who knows his vote decides, and one with power under the articles to appoint or block an officer owe the company a duty of fairness (s. 193). Breaches carry contract remedies, and oppression by a shareholder also opens s. 191 (s. 192(ג)).

### Derivative claims

> כל בעל מניה וכל דירקטור בחברה (בפרק זה – תובע) רשאים להגיש תביעה נגזרת אם התקיימו הוראות סימן זה.

*Gist:* Every shareholder and every director may bring a derivative claim, if the conditions are met. [Companies Law s. 194(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות#סעיף_194) A derivative claim is a claim brought by a claimant in the company's name for the company's cause of action (s. 1).

The procedure:

1. **Demand.** A written demand to the chairman of the board that the company exercise its rights by suing, with the facts and reasons (s. 194(ב)–(ג)). No demand is needed where the organ that must decide has a personal interest (or half or more of its members do), or where there is a reasonable concern that a demand would harm the chance of getting the remedy (s. 194(ד)).
2. **The company's answer.** It may cure the cause of action, reject the demand with reasons, or decide to sue (s. 195), and must tell the claimant within 45 days, naming who decided and any personal interest (s. 196).
3. **Right to file.** The claimant may file, with the court's approval, if the cure did not remove the cause of action, the demand was rejected, the company decided to sue but did not within 75 days, the company did not answer, or a demand was excused (s. 197). The court may approve early if waiting would let limitation run out (s. 198(ב)).
4. **Approval test.** The court approves if satisfied that, prima facie, the claim and its conduct are in the company's interest and the claimant is not acting in bad faith (s. 198(א)). Before or after filing the approval motion, the claimant may ask for disclosure of documents relevant to approval, on showing an initial evidential basis (s. 198א).
5. **Costs and reward.** The claimant pays only part of the court fee; on approval the company refunds it and pays the rest; the court may order the company to fund the claimant's expenses or post security (s. 199). Costs awarded to a defendant are generally paid by the company (s. 200). If the claim succeeds, the claimant may get an award (s. 201). Any withdrawal or settlement needs court approval after publication, and shareholders, directors and (for s. 204 claims) creditors may object (s. 202).

A shareholder or director may also be allowed to defend a claim against the company in its name (derivative defence, s. 203). A creditor may bring a derivative claim for a prohibited distribution (s. 204). No derivative claim may be filed once a trustee has been appointed in insolvency (s. 205).

### Which court

Civil matters under the Companies Law are in the district court's jurisdiction (Courts Law s. 40(5)), except veil-lifting (ss. 6–7) which goes to the court competent for the claim. Derivative claims and matters of shareholders' rights under the articles are "economic matters" (Courts Law s. 42ב(א)(2)–(3)) heard by the Economic Department of the Tel Aviv District Court where it has jurisdiction (⚠️ check territorial scope).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 3051/98 דרין נ׳ חברת השקעות דיסקונט בע״מ, פ״ד נט(1) 673 (2004)

Cited by Fiegenbaum and Licht for the main test that distinguishes a direct claim from a derivative claim: the identity of the injured party. If the harm is to the company (loss of its money or opportunity), and the shareholder suffers only as a reflection of the company's loss, the claim is derivative; if the shareholder's own rights are harmed (his vote, his pre-emption right, a misrepresentation made to him), it is direct. Why it matters: filing the wrong kind of claim is the most common procedural mistake in corporate litigation. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 82](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### ע״א 667/76 גליקמן נ׳ א.מ. ברקאי, פ״ד לב(2) 281 (1978)

Listed by Fiegenbaum and Licht among the founding cases of Israeli company law, on oppression of the minority under the Companies Ordinance. Why it matters: it marks the starting point of a doctrine that the Companies Law broadened, following English law, from "oppression" to the more flexible unfair-prejudice approach. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 2](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### פרט תעשיות מתכת בע״מ נ׳ דדון (28.2.2016)

Cited by Fiegenbaum and Licht for the two parallel tests the courts use for oppression: an unfair distribution of the company's assets, or harm to shareholders' reasonable expectations. They note that courts have ordered dividends, written clauses into the articles to ensure future cooperation, and, in irreconcilable disputes, mandated buy-outs. Why it matters: in a two- or three-founder company, the "reasonable expectations" set at the start (each founder works and draws a salary, decisions are by consensus) are what the court protects. [Fiegenbaum & Licht, n. 67](https://www.ecgi.global/sites/default/files/working_papers/documents/finalfiegenbaumlicht.pdf) (⚠️ the source gives the case number incompletely; full text not opened; Hebrew title unconfirmed).

### The debate: how strong is the controlling shareholder's duty?

Kossoy held that a controlling shareholder who sells control to a buyer he knows will loot the company breaches a duty of loyalty ([Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/kossoy-and-filco-v-bank-feuchtwanger-ltd)). The Companies Law replaced that with a "duty of fairness" (s. 193) without defining it. One view reads it as a weakened loyalty that lets controllers pursue their own interests within limits; another reads it as almost the same as directors' loyalty. Fiegenbaum and Licht report that Vrednikov endorsed the weaker reading in a detailed dictum, but there is no binding interpretation yet.

## How it works in practice

### Choose the tool

| Complaint | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't know what is happening; no statements, no minutes" | Information request (ss. 184–187), then court if refused | Inspection is a direct right |
| "They doubled their salaries and pay no dividends; I was removed as director" | Oppression (s. 191) | Harm to reasonable expectations and unfair distribution of value |
| "A director diverted a company deal to his own firm" | Derivative claim (ss. 194–198) | The company is the injured party |
| "The majority voted to issue shares only to itself" | Direct claim or oppression; s. 290 pre-emption | Harm to the shareholder's own rights |
| "A listed company misled the market and the share price fell" | Class action (Class Actions Law) | Many investors, each with a small direct claim |

### Building the file

1. Collect what you have: articles, minutes, statements, emails.
2. Send an information request (template below); diary the answer.
3. Ask for a special meeting if you hold 10% (lesson 16.3).
4. For a derivative claim, send the s. 194 demand to the chairman; diary 45 days and, if the company decides to sue, 75 more.
5. Write a chronology and, for oppression, the founders' original understandings and how they were broken.

## Worked example

Three friends from a moshav in the Jezreel Valley, Ofer Golan, Rami Khoury and Nir Shalev, founded "Emek Solar Ltd" in 2021, with a third each. They agreed orally that each would work in the company, draw the same salary, and that profits would be split yearly. In 2025 Ofer and Rami removed Nir as director, cut his salary, raised their own and stopped distributing profits. In 2026 Rami's brother's firm got a large maintenance contract without a tender.

1. **Information first.** Nir asks for the general-meeting minutes and the register as of right (s. 184), copies of the articles and financial statements (s. 187), and, stating his purpose, documents on the maintenance contract, which is an extraordinary transaction with a director's relative needing general-meeting approval (ss. 185(א)(1), 270, 272). He holds 33%, so he can also demand the auditor's statement of payments to directors (s. 186).
2. **Oppression.** The founders' understanding (equal work, equal pay, yearly profit-sharing) created reasonable expectations. Removing Nir, cutting his pay and stopping distributions while raising their own salaries plausibly meets both tests (unfair distribution and harm to expectations). Remedy sought: an order that the company or Ofer and Rami buy Nir's shares at fair value, or restore him and set rules for future distributions (s. 191(א)).
3. **The contract.** Overpayment to Rami's brother's firm harms the company: a derivative cause. After Nir's removal the board has two directors, Ofer and Rami, and Rami has a personal interest: half of the deciding organ, so a demand is excused (s. 194(ד)(1)). Nir still sends a s. 194 demand to the chairman, because it costs little and builds the record; if the company rejects it or does not answer within 45 days, he files for approval (s. 197).
4. **Forum.** Both claims go to the district court (the Economic Department where it has jurisdiction).
5. **Practical outcome.** Many such disputes settle with a buy-out once the information is out.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A company in which you hold shares, or the Emek Solar case.
- The [shareholder information request (draft)](../../templates/shareholder-information-request.md).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Classify the complaint (10 min).** For each grievance, decide: information, oppression, derivative, direct, class action. Write the section.
2. **Draft the information request (15 min).** Part א׳ (as of right, s. 184/187), part ב׳ (with purpose, s. 185), and if you hold 10%, part ד׳ (s. 186).
3. **Draft a s. 194 demand outline (10 min).** To the chairman: the facts creating the company's cause, the defendants, the loss, the demand to sue, and a request for an answer within 45 days.
4. **Write the oppression theory (10 min).** Three sentences: the reasonable expectations, how they were broken, the remedy you want.

[Shareholder information request and s. 194 demand (draft)](../../templates/shareholder-information-request.md).

### How you know it worked

- Each grievance is matched to one tool with its section.
- Your s. 185 request states a purpose linked to one of its two grounds.
- Your s. 194 demand describes the company's loss, not yours.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. Information requests, meeting demands and the s. 194 demand are letters a shareholder writes in his own name. An individual may file an oppression application or a derivative-claim approval motion himself (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170), but these are complex district-court proceedings, the other side will be represented, and a derivative claim is conducted for the company: get a lawyer for the court stage. A company that is itself a shareholder generally needs a lawyer in court.

### Where and how

- Letters: to the company's registered office and digital address, by registered post and email (ss. 123, 123א).
- Court: district court; Economic Department where it has jurisdiction (Courts Law s. 42ב). Derivative claims pay a reduced fee (s. 199; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: the Israel Securities Authority may fund some derivative and class actions in public companies (⚠️ manual check of current criteria); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The company refuses information and you consider court.
- You plan an oppression application or derivative claim.
- You are a director as well as a shareholder and may be a defendant yourself.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing personally for a loss the company suffered | Claim struck out as derivative | Re-file as a derivative claim with a demand | Ask "who was injured?" first |
| Filing a derivative claim without a demand | Approval refused unless the demand was excused | Send the demand; wait 45 days | Send the demand early; diary the dates |
| Asking for "all documents" without a purpose | Refusal under s. 185 | Narrow the request and state the purpose | Tie each document to a transaction or agenda item |
| Oppression claim with no evidence of expectations | Dismissed | Gather emails, minutes, the founders' agreement | Write down founders' understandings at the start |
| Settling a derivative claim privately | Invalid without court approval (s. 202) | Apply for approval with full terms | Bring every settlement to the court |

## Review

- Inspection of minutes, register, articles and statements is of right; other documents need a stated purpose and may be refused for bad faith, secrets or harm (ss. 184–187).
- Oppression covers unfair distribution of value and harm to reasonable expectations; the court may reorganise the company or order a buy-out (s. 191).
- Shareholders owe good faith; controlling and pivotal shareholders owe fairness (ss. 192–193).
- A derivative claim needs a written demand (unless excused), the company's 45-day answer, and court approval on a prima facie company-interest and good-faith test (ss. 194–198).
- Direct or derivative depends on who was injured (Drin).
