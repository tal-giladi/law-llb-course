# 30.4 · Family and succession across borders

By the end of this lesson you will be able to work out which law governs an estate with property in two countries, whether a will made abroad is valid in form, which country's property regime applies to a couple who married abroad or immigrated, and which court in Israel can end a marriage made abroad. Lesson [18.2](../module-18/lesson-02.md) covered registration of marriages made abroad and online; lesson [19.8](../module-19/lesson-08.md) covered child abduction under the Hague Convention. This lesson adds the choice-of-law rules that sit underneath.

## Why it matters

Family and inheritance are where Israel legislated most of its private international law, because immigration made foreign elements the norm. A large share of Israeli families include someone who was born, married, worked or owns property abroad. The rules are short and mostly fixed in statute, which is good news for the self-represented: you can read them and apply them.

Two features shape the field. First, the connecting factor is mainly *domicile* (מושב), defined as the centre of a person's life, not nationality. Second, personal status for members of recognised religious communities is still decided by religious courts and religious law (lesson [10.4](../module-10/lesson-04.md)), so for divorce the international question is also a question about the rabbinical, sharia, Druze or Christian courts.

## The law

### Succession: jurisdiction and choice of law (Succession Law ch. 7)

> בית משפט בישראל מוסמך לדון בירושתו של כל אדם שמושבו ביום מותו היה בישראל או שהניח נכסים בישראל.

*Gist:* An Israeli court has jurisdiction over the estate of anyone domiciled in Israel at death, or who left assets in Israel. ([s. 136](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_136))

> על הירושה יחול דין מושבו של המוריש בשעת מותו, חוץ מן האמור בסעיפים 138 עד 140.

*Gist:* The succession is governed by the law of the deceased's domicile at death, except as ss. 138–140 provide. ([s. 137](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_137))

"Domicile" is the place where the centre of the person's life was; a minor or a person under guardianship is presumed domiciled with his representative until shown otherwise ([s. 135](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_135)).

The exceptions:

- **Assets governed only by their location** (s. 138): property that passes on death only under the law of the place where it is (typically land under a State's rule that only its own law governs succession to its land) is governed by that law.
- **Capacity to make a will** (s. 139): the testator's domicile when the will was made.
- **Form of the will** (s. 140(א)): valid if valid under any of: Israeli law; the law of the place it was made; the testator's domicile or habitual residence, or nationality, at the time of making or of death; and for land, also the law of the place of the land. The capacity required of the testator or the witnesses counts as form (s. 140(ב)).

The safeguards: characterization by Israeli concepts (s. 141), renvoi only back to Israel (s. 142), no discriminatory or anti-public-policy foreign law (s. 143), and no foreign statutory rights for persons not related by blood, marriage or adoption unless that foreign law gives Israelis the same (s. 144).

### Spouses' property: the law of domicile at the time of marriage

> על יחסי הממון בין בני זוג יחול חוק מושבם בשעת עריכת הנישואין, אולם רשאים הם בהסכם לקבוע ולשנות יחסים אלה בהתאם לחוק מושבם בשעת עשיית ההסכם.

*Gist:* The couple's property relations are governed by the law of their domicile at the time of the marriage, but they may set or change them by agreement in accordance with the law of their domicile when they make the agreement. ([Spouses Property Relations Law s. 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יחסי_ממון_בין_בני_זוג#סעיף_15))

Three consequences:

1. **The connecting factor is fixed in time.** A couple who married in Paris while living in France keep French matrimonial property law after moving to Israel, unless they make an agreement.
2. **Marrying abroad does not matter in itself.** An Israeli couple who live in Israel and marry in Cyprus have their Israeli domicile at the time of marriage; Israeli law (the balance of resources, lesson [18.4](../module-18/lesson-04.md)) applies.
3. **A property agreement can switch regimes,** in accordance with the law of their domicile when they sign it. Couples living in Israel sign under Israeli law, which requires writing and, for validity, approval or certification (lesson 18.4).

The section does not say what happens when the spouses had *different* domiciles at the time of marriage; the courts and the literature fill the gap (⚠️ open question, no ruling read for this module).

### Divorce: which Israeli forum, which law

**Jews married by Jewish law.** The rabbinical courts have exclusive jurisdiction over marriage and divorce of Jews in Israel who are citizens or residents ([Rabbinical Courts Jurisdiction Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בתי_דין_רבניים_(נישואין_וגירושין)#סעיף_1)). Since 2005 they also have international jurisdiction over a divorce suit between Jews married by Jewish law if one of six connections exists:

> לבית דין רבני יהיה שיפוט ייחודי בתביעה לגירושין בין בני זוג יהודים שנישאו על פי דין תורה, בהתקיים אחת מהזיקות האלה:

*Gist:* The rabbinical court has exclusive jurisdiction over a divorce suit between Jews married by Jewish law where one of the listed connections exists: the defendant domiciled in Israel; both spouses Israeli citizens; the plaintiff domiciled in Israel for a year before filing, or domiciled here where Israel was the last common domicile; the plaintiff an Israeli citizen domiciled here, or who lived here a year in the last two. ([s. 4א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בתי_דין_רבניים_(נישואין_וגירושין)#סעיף_4א))

The section also protects a foreign civil divorce: a suit here does not stop a competent foreign court from granting a civil divorce, and if a civil divorce suit was filed abroad before a get was given, the rabbinical court may not decide the civil divorce (s. 4א(ב)). Where a couple is already divorced under foreign law but no get was given, the rabbinical court may hear a suit for a get or to remove the bar to remarriage even if only the plaintiff is an Israeli citizen (s. 4א(ג)). The section gives no jurisdiction over matters joined to the divorce, such as property and maintenance (s. 4א(ה)).

**Couples without a common religious court** (mixed couples, couples of no recognised religion, couples married in a civil ceremony where the religious court has no jurisdiction): the Family Court decides under the 1969 Law. Its international jurisdiction rests on similar connections ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בעניני_התרת_נישואין_(מקרים_מיוחדים_וסמכות_בין-לאומית)#סעיף_2)), and it applies, in order, the internal law of the couple's common domicile, last common domicile, common nationality, or the place of the marriage, skipping a law that applies different rules to the two spouses or does not allow divorce; consent of both spouses is always a ground ([s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בעניני_התרת_נישואין_(מקרים_מיוחדים_וסמכות_בין-לאומית)#סעיף_5)). Where one spouse belongs to a recognised religious community, the court first asks the head of that religious court whether a religious divorce is needed (s. 3).

### Foreign divorces and foreign family judgments

A foreign divorce decree is not enforced under the 1958 Law in the way a money judgment is (lesson 30.5); it is usually *recognised*, either because a treaty requires it (s. 11(א) of the 1958 Law) or incidentally, where a court or tribunal sees fit, in a case before it (s. 11(ב)). The Population Registry registers a change of status on the basis of a public foreign document, as it registers a foreign marriage (Funk-Schlesinger). For Jews married by Jewish law, a foreign civil divorce does not end the marriage religiously: without a get, remarriage in Israel by Jewish law is impossible, and the children of a later relationship of the wife may face the status problem of mamzerut. That is why s. 4א(ג) exists.

Foreign maintenance orders are enforced under the 1958 Law; s. 8 allows even interim maintenance orders and orders still open to appeal to be enforced (lesson 30.5).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 2232/03 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי תל אביב-יפו (21.11.2006): recap

Lesson [18.2](../module-18/lesson-02.md) gives the facts: two Jews who could have married religiously married civilly in Cyprus. The HCJ held that the rabbinical court may dissolve such a marriage, which has "external" validity, on the ground of irretrievable breakdown. Why it matters here: a marriage made abroad under foreign law is recognised and dissolved in Israel through the domestic religious forum; private international law (validity by the law of the place of celebration) and Israeli personal-status law work together. [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/11)

### בג״ץ 143/62 פונק־שלזינגר נ׳ שר הפנים (1963): recap

Lesson 18.2: the registration official registers a marriage on a foreign public document and does not decide its validity. Why it matters here: registration is not recognition; the validity of a foreign marriage (or divorce) for inheritance, property or a later marriage is decided by the court in which the question arises, under the rules of this lesson. [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/160)

### Key text: the Succession Law, chapter 7

There is no single leading Supreme Court case on the succession rules that was verified for this module; the statute is short and complete. Read ss. 135–144 in full before any estate with a foreign element. [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה)

## How it works in practice

### An estate with foreign assets

1. **Where was the deceased domiciled at death?** Collect evidence of the centre of life: home, family, work, tax residence, medical care, where he spent his time.
2. **Which law governs?** That domicile's law (s. 137), except land or other assets that their location's law claims exclusively (s. 138).
3. **Is the will valid in form?** Test it against every law in s. 140(א); one is enough.
4. **Israeli order:** apply to the Registrar of Inheritance for a succession or probate order (lesson [26.6](../module-26/lesson-06.md)); foreign documents (death certificate, will, foreign orders) with an apostille and a certified translation. If foreign law governs, the file goes to the Family Court and foreign law must be proved, usually by an expert opinion (⚠️ check the Registrar's current guidance).
5. **Abroad:** ask a local lawyer whether the Israeli order is recognised there or a local procedure is needed.

### A couple with a foreign element

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who divides the property? | Family Court or rabbinical court, under the jurisdiction rules of lesson 18.4 |
| Which property law? | Domicile at the time of marriage (s. 15), or the law under which a later agreement was made |
| Who grants the divorce (Jews married religiously)? | Rabbinical court (s. 1; s. 4א for international cases) |
| Who grants the divorce (no common religious court)? | Family Court under the 1969 Law (ss. 2, 5) |
| A child taken abroad? | Hague Abduction Convention Law, lesson 19.8 |

## Worked example

Natalia and Sergei Petrov married in Kyiv in 2010, while both lived there, and moved to Holon in 2014. In 2025 Sergei died domiciled in Israel. He left a flat in Holon (registered in his name), a flat in Kyiv, and a typed will signed in front of one witness in Kyiv in 2012, leaving everything to Natalia. His son from a first marriage, living in Haifa, contests the will.

1. **Jurisdiction:** domicile in Israel at death, and assets in Israel (s. 136).
2. **Succession law:** Israeli law, the domicile at death (s. 137); for the Kyiv flat, s. 138 if Ukrainian law claims exclusive control of succession to its land (to be checked with a Ukrainian lawyer).
3. **Capacity:** Ukrainian law of 2012 (s. 139); no issue raised.
4. **Form:** under Israeli law a witnessed will needs two witnesses (lesson [26.2](../module-26/lesson-02.md)); the will fails that test. But it is valid in form if Ukrainian law (place of making and domicile in 2012) accepts it (s. 140(א)). The son's challenge depends on proving Ukrainian law; Natalia should obtain an expert opinion.
5. **Natalia's own share:** before the estate, what is hers as a spouse? Their domicile at marriage was Ukraine, so Ukrainian matrimonial property law governs (s. 15) unless they made an agreement in Israel. Under that law the Holon flat may be partly hers already (⚠️ for the expert).
6. **Forum:** the contested will goes from the Registrar to the Family Court (lesson 26.6).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your family's facts (or the Petrov case): where each person was domiciled when they married, made wills, and died; where the assets are.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Draw a timeline (10 min):** marriage, moves, wills, death, with the place of domicile at each point.
2. **Answer five questions (20 min)**, each with its section: jurisdiction (s. 136), succession law (s. 137–138), capacity (s. 139), form (s. 140), matrimonial property (s. 15).
3. **Mark what needs foreign-law proof (5 min).**
4. **Plan the documents (5 min):** list each foreign document, where to get it, and whether it needs an apostille and translation.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may apply to the Registrar of Inheritance for your own succession or probate order, and act for yourself in the Family Court and the rabbinical court ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)); before the rabbinical court a licensed rabbinical pleader may also represent you. An estate governed by foreign law, or with a contested foreign will, needs proof of foreign law and usually a lawyer.

### Where and how

- **Registrar of Inheritance:** online application or by hand (lesson 26.6); foreign documents with apostille and translation.
- **Rabbinical courts:** divorce suits, including s. 4א international cases.
- **Family Court:** contested estates, property division, the 1969 Law.
- **Free help:** Legal Aid (means and merit tests) for family matters; the welfare ministry's assistance units at the family courts.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Foreign law governs the estate or the matrimonial property, or the will is valid only under foreign law.
- There is land abroad, or proceedings have started in another country.
- A spouse refuses a get, or a foreign civil divorce was granted without a get.
- A child may be taken abroad, or was brought here (Hague Abduction Convention: deadlines are short).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testing a foreign will only against Israeli form rules | A valid will is treated as invalid | Test against every law in s. 140(א) | Use the s. 140 list as a checklist |
| Assuming Israeli matrimonial property law applies to immigrants | Wrong division | Apply s. 15: domicile at marriage | Ask where both lived when they married |
| Relying on a foreign civil divorce for a Jewish-law marriage | Remarriage barred; status problems | Sue for a get (s. 4א(ג)) | Arrange a get together with the civil divorce |
| Ignoring land abroad | Israeli order not accepted there | Start the local procedure | Ask a local lawyer early (s. 138) |
| No apostille or translation on foreign documents | Application returned | Obtain them | Check requirements before filing |

## Review

- Succession: Israeli jurisdiction if domiciled in Israel at death or assets here; the law of domicile at death governs, with exceptions for exclusively local assets, capacity and form.
- A will is valid in form under any of several laws (s. 140): one is enough.
- Matrimonial property: the law of domicile at the time of marriage, unless changed by agreement (s. 15).
- Divorce of Jews married by Jewish law: the rabbinical court, including international jurisdiction under s. 4א; others: the Family Court under the 1969 Law, with a fixed order of laws.
- A foreign civil divorce does not replace a get; registration of status is not a ruling on validity.
