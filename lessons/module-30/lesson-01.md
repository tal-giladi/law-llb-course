# 30.1 · What private international law is: jurisdiction, choice of law and recognition

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take any dispute that crosses a border, a parcel from a Chinese marketplace, a divorce of a couple who married in Cyprus, a Russian bank's judgment against an Israeli resident, and break it into the three questions every court asks: may an Israeli court hear it, which country's law decides it, and what an Israeli court does with a judgment given abroad. Module 19 dealt with relations between States; this module deals with private people and companies whose lives and contracts cross borders.

## Why it matters

Private international law (משפט בין־לאומי פרטי, also called conflict of laws) is not international law in the sense of module 19. It is Israeli law: the part of Israeli law that tells an Israeli court what to do when a case has a foreign element. Every State has its own set of these rules, so the same case can come out differently in Tel Aviv, Paris and New York. That is why the first step in any cross-border dispute is to decide *where* to bring it, because the forum's rules then choose the law.

The subject has grown from a niche for shipping lawyers into everyday law. Israelis buy abroad online, work for foreign employers from home, marry in Cyprus or by video link, inherit flats in Ukraine or bank accounts in France, and are sued by foreign banks. A person who acts for himself needs to know three things at least: whether an Israeli court can hear his claim against a foreign seller, whether a foreign judgment against him can be enforced in Israel and how to resist it, and which country's law decides his family and estate questions.

Israeli private international law is mostly judge-made. The Knesset has legislated only in islands: inheritance, spouses' property, divorce jurisdiction for couples without a common religious court, foreign judgments, international sale of goods, child abduction, arbitration. Between the islands the courts developed common-law rules, at first following English law and, since the 2000s, increasingly choosing their own path (lesson 30.3 shows the clearest example).

## The law

### The three questions and where Israeli law answers them

| Question | Main Israeli sources | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Jurisdiction: may an Israeli court hear the case? | Presence or consent of the defendant; service abroad under CPR regs 166–169; forum non conveniens; special statutes (Succession Law s. 136; Dissolution of Marriage Law 1969 s. 2) | 30.2 |
| 2. Choice of law: which law decides it? | Judge-made rules for contracts and torts; CISG law for international sales of goods; Succession Law ss. 137–140; Spouses Property Relations Law s. 15; Dissolution of Marriage Law s. 5 | 30.3, 30.4 |
| 3. Recognition and enforcement: what is a foreign judgment worth here? | Foreign Judgments Enforcement Law 1958 | 30.5 |

### One statute that names the field

The Foreign Judgments Enforcement Law refuses enforcement where the foreign court lacked jurisdiction by Israel's own rules of private international law:

> הפסק ניתן על ידי בית משפט שלא היה מוסמך לתתו על פי כללי המשפט הבין־לאומי הפרטי החלים בישראל;

*Gist:* A foreign judgment will not be declared enforceable if the court that gave it had no jurisdiction under the private international law rules that apply in Israel. ([s. 6(א)(3)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_6))

The statute does not list those rules; the courts supply them. The provision shows that the three questions are linked: the jurisdiction rules of question 1 come back at question 3.

### Characterization: Israeli concepts decide what kind of question it is

> לענין קביעת הסמכות והדין לפי הסעיפים 136 עד 140 תהיה לכל מונח המשמעות שיש לו בדין הישראלי.

*Gist:* For jurisdiction and choice of law in inheritance, every term has the meaning it has in Israeli law. ([Succession Law s. 141](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_141))

Before choosing a law, the court must decide whether a question is about "succession", "matrimonial property", "contract", "tort" or "procedure". Section 141 states the general approach: the forum classifies by its own concepts. The classic trap is procedure versus substance. Procedure (time limits for filing, evidence, how damages are computed in court) normally follows the forum's law; substance follows the chosen law. In Yinon (lesson 30.3) the Supreme Court held that whether a head of damage exists, and a cap on compensation, are substantive questions governed by the law that governs the tort.

### Renvoi: when the foreign law points elsewhere

> כשחל הדין של מדינה פלונית ואותו דין מפנה אל דין־חוץ, אין נזקקים להפנייה, אלא יחול הדין הפנימי של אותה מדינה; אולם כשהדין של אותה מדינה מפנה אל הדין הישראלי, נזקקים להפנייה ויחול הדין הפנימי הישראלי.

*Gist:* If the applicable foreign law refers to a third law, Israel ignores the referral and applies that State's internal law; if it refers back to Israeli law, Israel accepts the referral and applies Israeli internal law. ([Succession Law s. 142](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_142))

This is a statutory rule for inheritance only. Outside inheritance the question is open; in contracts it rarely matters, because a contractual choice of "the laws of California" is usually read as California's internal law (Facebook's clause even said so: "without regard to conflict of law provisions", lesson 30.2).

### Public policy and discrimination

> כשחל דין־חוץ, אין נזקקים לו במידה שהוא מפלה מטעמי גזע, דת, מין או לאום או סותר תקנת הציבור בישראל.

*Gist:* A foreign law that applies is not applied to the extent that it discriminates on grounds of race, religion, sex or nationality or contradicts Israeli public policy. ([Succession Law s. 143](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_143))

Public policy (תקנת הציבור) is the safety valve of the whole field. It appears again in the Foreign Judgments Enforcement Law, s. 3(3): the content of a foreign judgment must not contradict public policy. It is applied narrowly: a foreign rule is not excluded merely because Israeli law is different, only where applying it would offend fundamental values.

### Proving foreign law

Foreign law is not known to an Israeli judge. In practice the party relying on it proves it, usually by an expert opinion (in Yinon both sides relied on an expert opinion on Jordanian law). If no one proves it, Israeli courts generally decide by Israeli law, on the working assumption that the foreign law is the same. This is the practical reason that many cross-border consumer cases in Israel are decided by Israeli law even when a foreign law clause exists: nobody proved the foreign law. (⚠️ The "presumption of identity of laws" is stated here as general practice; no Supreme Court text was read for it in this module.)

## Leading cases

### ע״א 1432/03 ינון יצור ושיווק מוצרי מזון בע״מ נ׳ קרעאן (1.9.2004)

A resident of Qalqilya was injured at work in an Israeli company's factory in Alfei Menashe, an Israeli settlement in the West Bank. Jordanian law, which applies there under the military regime, gave her fixed compensation without proof of fault; Israeli law gave about seven times more but required negligence. The Supreme Court (Rivlin J., Naor and Hayut JJ. concurring) abandoned the English "double actionability" rule, adopted the law of the place of the tort as the general rule, and recognised a narrow exception where justice demands; it applied the exception and Israeli law. Why it matters for this lesson: it shows all the method problems at once: characterization (damages caps are substantive, para. 35), the interaction with public international law (which law applies in the territories), proof of foreign law by an expert, and the Supreme Court building Israeli private international law by choice rather than by copying England. The full holding is in lesson 30.3. [Full text (Haifa Faculty of Law)](https://law.haifa.ac.il/images/lawatchF/2007a_international_law_1432_03.pdf)

### Key text: Karayanni, "The challenges of the international jurisdiction of Israeli courts in the twenty-first century" (2024)

No single case sets out the structure of the field, so read the leading Israeli scholar instead. Professor Michael Karayanni explains that international jurisdiction is a matter of Israeli law like any other, that the traditional order is "jurisdiction first, choice of law later", and argues that the applicable law should play a central role in deciding whether an Israeli court should hear the case ("the appropriate forum of the appropriate law"). He also notes that the grounds for service abroad are now collected in reg. 166 of the 2018 Regulations. Why it matters: it gives you the theory behind the practical rules of lessons 30.2–30.3, and the vocabulary that Israeli judges use. [Free PDF (Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities)](https://www.academy.ac.il/SystemFiles2015/Iggeret45_IASH_Karayanni.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### The order of questions

1. **Is there a foreign element at all?** A foreign party, a contract performed abroad, a website abroad, assets or a death abroad, a judgment from abroad.
2. **Where could the case be heard?** Israel, the other country, an agreed forum, arbitration. For each forum: does it have jurisdiction, will the defendant be served there, can a judgment be enforced where the defendant's money is?
3. **In the chosen forum, which law applies?** Look for a statute first (inheritance, spouses' property, sale of goods), then an agreed choice of law, then the judge-made rule.
4. **Can you prove that law?** If not, expect Israeli law to be applied by default, which may be good or bad for you.
5. **Where will the judgment be enforced?** A judgment against a seller with no assets in Israel may need enforcement abroad; a foreign judgment against you in Israel goes through the 1958 Law (lesson 30.5).

### Where the self-represented person meets the field

| Situation | Which question bites first |
|---|---|
| Claim against a foreign website | Jurisdiction and service abroad (30.2); then whether you can collect (30.6) |
| Foreign judgment or a demand to enforce one | Recognition and enforcement (30.5) |
| Estate with assets in two countries | Jurisdiction and applicable law (30.4) |
| Divorce or property division after a marriage abroad | Jurisdiction of the religious court or Family Court; matrimonial property law (30.4) |
| Child taken abroad or brought to Israel | The Hague Abduction Convention Law, lesson [19.8](../module-19/lesson-08.md) |

## Worked example

Ruth Abramov, 42, lives in Haifa. Her late father, a resident of Riga until he moved to Israel in 2019, left a flat in Riga, a bank account in Israel and a handwritten will made in Latvia in 2018. Ruth's brother in Germany says the will is invalid because Latvian law requires witnesses.

1. **Foreign element:** a foreign will, foreign land, an heir abroad.
2. **Jurisdiction:** her father's domicile at death was Israel (he lived here from 2019), and he left assets here, so an Israeli court and the Registrar of Inheritance can deal with the estate (Succession Law s. 136; lesson 30.4).
3. **Characterization:** "Is the will valid in form?" is a question of the form of the will, classified by Israeli concepts (s. 141). The capacity of witnesses counts as form (s. 140(ב)).
4. **Choice of law:** the will is valid in form if it complies with any of the laws listed in s. 140(א): Israeli law, the law of the place it was made, the testator's domicile or residence, or his nationality, at the time of making or at death. A handwritten will complying with Israeli law is valid in form even if Latvian law demands witnesses.
5. **Renvoi and the Riga flat:** for the flat itself, Latvia may apply its own rules to land situated there; an Israeli succession order may need recognition in Latvia. That is a question of Latvian law, for a Latvian lawyer.
6. **Result:** Ruth applies to the Registrar in Israel for a probate order (lesson [26.6](../module-26/lesson-06.md)) and asks a lawyer in Riga about using it for the flat.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One real or imagined cross-border situation from your own life or family (a purchase, a job, a will, a marriage).
- 30 minutes and a notebook.

### Steps

1. **Write the facts (5 min)** in five lines: who, where each person lives, where things happened, where the assets are.
2. **List the foreign elements (5 min).**
3. **Sort into the three questions (10 min):** for each, write the source of law you expect (statute, regulation or judge-made rule) using the table in "The law".
4. **Mark the method traps (5 min):** a characterization question, a renvoi question, a public-policy question, a proof-of-foreign-law question. Not every case has all four.
5. **Decide the next lesson to read (5 min)** for your case: 30.2, 30.3, 30.4, 30.5 or 30.6.

### Template

None. Use the three-question table from "The law" as your worksheet.

### How you know it worked

- Each of the three questions has an answer or a named source.
- You can say which question must be answered first in your case, and why.
- You found at least one method trap or explained why none arises.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain why private international law is part of Israeli law, not of international law.
- [ ] I can name four Israeli statutes that contain private international law rules.
- [ ] I can define characterization and renvoi and give the Succession Law rule for each.
- [ ] I know who must prove foreign law and what usually happens if no one does.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. The analysis is the first step in lessons 30.6 and 30.7, where you may act in your own name in an Israeli court ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Acting in a foreign court is governed by that country's rules; many allow self-representation in small claims, but check.

### Where and how

- Statute texts: Wikisource consolidations (free, unofficial) linked in this lesson.
- Court rulings: the University of Haifa and other free hosts used in this module; English translations of some rulings at the Cardozo Israeli Supreme Court Project.
- Hague Conference conventions and status tables: hcch.net (free).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The case turns on foreign law that you cannot prove without an expert opinion.
- There are assets or proceedings in more than one country at the same time.
- The other side has already started proceedings abroad (a race to judgment is a strategic matter).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking "which law applies" before "which court" | You prepare an argument for a forum that will not hear you | Restart from question 1 | Always answer jurisdiction first |
| Assuming "international law" governs a private dispute | Wrong sources, wrong arguments | Look for Israeli statutes and case law on the foreign element | Remember module 19 is about States |
| Relying on a foreign law without proving it | The court applies Israeli law instead | Ask for time to submit an expert opinion, if allowed | Decide early whether the foreign law helps you enough to prove it |
| Treating a damages cap as procedure | Wrong law applied to compensation | Cite Yinon para. 35: caps are substantive | Characterize each issue separately |
| Invoking public policy whenever foreign law is different | Argument rejected | Show a fundamental value, not a difference | Use public policy as a last resort |

## Review

- Private international law is the part of Israeli law that handles foreign elements: jurisdiction, choice of law, recognition and enforcement.
- Statutes cover islands (inheritance, spouses' property, divorce jurisdiction, foreign judgments, sale of goods, abduction); the rest is judge-made.
- Characterization is by Israeli concepts (Succession Law s. 141); renvoi is accepted only back to Israel in inheritance (s. 142).
- Public policy and anti-discrimination limits apply to foreign law (s. 143) and to foreign judgments (FJEL s. 3(3)).
- Foreign law must be proved, usually by an expert; otherwise Israeli law tends to be applied.
