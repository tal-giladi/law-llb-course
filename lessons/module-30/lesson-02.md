# 30.2 · International jurisdiction of Israeli courts and service abroad

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say whether you can sue a foreign company or person in Israel, how to get the claim to them abroad, what a foreign defendant can do to stop the case, and what to do if you are the one served from abroad. Lesson [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md) set out the service rules in outline; this lesson covers international jurisdiction as a whole, forum non conveniens, foreign jurisdiction clauses, the Hague Service Convention and apostilles.

## Why it matters

In Israeli law, the jurisdiction of a court over a foreign defendant is tied to service. A court has jurisdiction over anyone who can be validly served with the claim: in Israel, by ordinary service; abroad, only on a ground listed in the Regulations and with the court's approval of the method. That means the plaintiff's first practical question, "how do I serve them?", is also the legal question "can an Israeli court hear this at all?".

The rules matter in both directions. For a consumer suing a foreign website, they decide whether the case can be heard in Haifa rather than Dublin or Shenzhen. For an Israeli defendant sued abroad, the same ideas (where the defendant lives, where the contract was made, an agreed forum) decide whether a foreign judgment against him will later be enforced in Israel (lesson 30.5). And the defendant served from abroad in Israel has a short, strict window to object.

## The law

### Three ways to have jurisdiction

1. **Presence and service in Israel.** A defendant served in Israel, even on a short visit, is within the court's jurisdiction (the common-law "tag" rule; Karayanni cites רע״א 9810/05 Hecke נ׳ Pimcapco, 30.8.2009, ⚠️ not read). A foreign resident with a permanent representative in Israel for his Israeli affairs may be served through that representative in a claim about those affairs (reg. 163(ג)).
2. **Consent.** A defendant who agreed to Israeli jurisdiction, or who appears and argues the merits without objecting, accepts it (reg. 166(1) covers an agreed Israeli jurisdiction).
3. **Service abroad on a listed ground**, with the court's approval of the method:

> בעל דין רשאי להמציא כתב טענות מחוץ לתחום המדינה בהתקיים אחד מאלה ובכפוף לתקנות 167 ו־168:

*Gist:* A pleading may be served abroad only on one of the listed grounds, and subject to regs 167–168. ([reg. 166](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_166))

The grounds most used by individuals:

| Ground (reg. 166) | Typical case |
|---|---|
| (2) defendant domiciled in Israel | An Israeli now abroad |
| (3) land in Israel | Dispute over a flat in Israel |
| (4)(א) contract made or breached in Israel, or performance in Israel prevented | Goods never delivered to an Israeli address |
| (4)(ב) contract governed by Israeli law | Israeli choice-of-law clause |
| (4א) act or omission in Israel | A tort committed here |
| (5) damage in Israel from a product, service or conduct, where foreseeable and the defendant (or a related person) trades internationally on a significant scale | A defective product bought from a global platform |
| (7) enforcement or recognition of a foreign judgment or award | Lesson 30.5 |
| (9)–(10) a necessary or proper party; one claim with a listed connection | Suing the Israeli importer and the foreign maker together |

Ground (5) was written for online commerce: it does not ask where the contract was made, only whether the damage was suffered in Israel, was foreseeable, and the defendant group does significant international business.

### The request (reg. 167)

> בעל דין המעוניין להמציא כתב טענות מחוץ לתחום המדינה, יגיש בקשה בכתב לשם קביעת דרך ביצוע ההמצאה; בתצהיר התומך בבקשה יפורטו העובדות המבססות את עילת התביעה, העובדות המבססות את עילת ההמצאה מחוץ לתחום המדינה ופירוט המקום שבו נמצא הנמען או ייתכן שנמצא.

*Gist:* The plaintiff files a written request for the method of service abroad, with an affidavit showing the cause of action, the ground for service abroad and where the defendant is or may be. ([reg. 167(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_167))

The court sets the method (reg. 167(ב)): by the Hague Convention, by registered mail, by e-mail to an address the defendant uses, or another method; it may also refuse service abroad "in the circumstances". The same rules apply in small claims, whose regulations adopt chapter 19 of the Civil Procedure Regulations for service (Small Claims Regulations, [reg. 7(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_שיפוט_בתביעות_קטנות_(סדרי_דין)#סעיף_7)).

### The defendant's answer (regs 168–169)

> הומצא לבעל דין כתב טענות מחוץ לתחום המדינה, רשאי הוא לכפור בסמכות בית המשפט לדון בתובענה או לטעון כי הפורום הישראלי אינו הפורום הנאות לדון בתובענה; הנתבע לכפור או לטעון כאמור, יגיש בקשה בכתב לא יאוחר מהמועד הקבוע להגשת כתב ההגנה; עשה כן, יימנה המועד להגשת כתב הגנה מיום ההחלטה בבקשה.

*Gist:* A defendant served abroad may challenge jurisdiction or plead that Israel is not the appropriate forum, by a written application filed no later than the defence deadline; the defence period then runs from the decision. ([reg. 168](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_168))

Regulation 169 protects the defendant who comes to Israel or hires an Israeli lawyer only to make that challenge: he cannot be served in Israel in the meantime, and nothing he or his lawyer does counts as waiving the challenge, until 14 days after the decision (or as the court orders). A defendant served in Israel raises the same arguments at the first opportunity (lesson [15.1](../module-15/lesson-01.md), reg. 29).

### Forum non conveniens

Having jurisdiction does not oblige the court to use it. Under forum non conveniens the court asks which forum has the closest connection to the dispute (the "natural forum"), weighing where the parties and witnesses are, where the events took place, which law applies, the language of the documents, and the parties' reasonable expectations; it declines only where another forum is clearly more appropriate. Karayanni describes how choice-of-law considerations have become central to this inquiry. The plea is raised under reg. 168 (served abroad) or at the first opportunity (served in Israel). (The weighing factors are stated here from the doctrine as described by Karayanni and lower-court practice; ⚠️ no leading Supreme Court text on the test was read for this module.)

### Foreign jurisdiction clauses in consumer contracts

A clause sending all disputes to a foreign court is valid in principle between businesses. In a standard contract with a consumer it meets the Standard Contracts Law: a term that sets the forum is presumed depriving (s. 4(9)), and a term that bars the customer from going to court is void (s. 5). Lesson [20.7](../module-20/lesson-07.md) explained how to test such terms.

### Security for costs

> בית המשפט רשאי, אם נראה לו הדבר, לצוות על תובע לתת ערובה לתשלום הוצאותיו של נתבע.

*Gist:* The court may order a plaintiff to give security for the defendant's costs; if it is not given in time, the claim is struck out. ([reg. 157](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_157))

Foreign plaintiffs with no assets in Israel are the classic case; expect the request if you sue in Israel while living abroad.

### The Hague Service Convention

Israel ratified the 1965 Convention on the Service Abroad of Judicial and Extrajudicial Documents on 14.8.1972; it entered into force for Israel on 13.10.1972 ([HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=17)). The Israeli central authority is the Courts Director:

> הרשות המרכזית בישראל לצרכי האמנה היא מנהל בתי המשפט.

*Gist:* The Courts Director is Israel's central authority under the Convention. ([1975 Regulations, reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_המצאת_מסמכים_לפי_אמנת_האג_1965#סעיף_3))

To serve in another Convention State, the party files the request on the Convention's model form, in three copies, completed in the official language of the receiving State or in English or French, with two copies of the document, at the court where the case is pending, and deposits the expected costs; the court passes it to the Courts Director, who sends it to the foreign central authority (regs 5–7). Before giving a default judgment against a defendant served under the Convention, the court applies arts 15–16 of the Convention and Israel's declaration under art. 15 (reg. 8): in essence, it must be shown that the defendant was served in time to defend, or that the Convention's fallback conditions (a long waiting period and real efforts to obtain a certificate) are met.

### Apostilles: documents that cross the border

Israel acceded to the 1961 Apostille Convention; it has been in force for Israel since 14.8.1978 ([HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41)). A public document from another Convention State (a court judgment, a certificate, a notarial deed) needs only an apostille from that State, not legalisation by a consulate ([1977 Regulations, reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_לביצוע_אמנת_האג_(ביטול_אימות_מסמכי_חוץ_ציבוריים)#סעיף_3)). In Israel, apostilles are issued by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, magistrates' court registrars and other authorised officials (reg. 5). An affidavit made abroad before a foreign notary is accepted in an Israeli civil court if it carries a notarial certification of the signer's ID and an apostille confirming the notary (CPR reg. 1, definition of "תצהיר", para. (4)).

## Leading cases

### רע״א 5860/16 Facebook Inc. נ׳ בן חמו (31.5.2018)

An Israeli user filed a class-action request in the Central District Court against Facebook Inc. (US) and Facebook Ireland. The terms of use sent all disputes to the courts of California, under California law. The Supreme Court (Hayut P., Melcer D.P. and Baron J.) held that the terms did not govern the user's relationship with Facebook US at all; as to Facebook Ireland, the foreign jurisdiction clause in the standard contract was a depriving term: the presumption was not rebutted, because the inability to sue in Israel exposed Israeli users to a real risk of no remedy, and the free service did not offset that risk. The choice-of-law clause, however, stood: the user had not shown that deciding the case under California law would bar him from raising particular claims, so the case proceeds in Israel under California substantive law. Why it matters: it is the leading authority for suing a global platform in Israel despite a foreign-court clause, and a reminder that winning on the forum does not win on the applicable law (lesson 30.3). [Full text (law.co.il)](https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/ben_hemo2.pdf)

### Hotels.com נ׳ Zuz Tourism Ltd. (רע״א 4716/04, 7.9.2005): recap

Lesson [15.1](../module-15/lesson-01.md) explains it: a foreign *arbitration* clause covered by the New York Convention is enforced by a stay of proceedings even where the plaintiff has joined a second defendant who is not bound by it. Why it matters here: unlike a foreign court clause in a consumer contract, an international arbitration clause leaves the court little discretion; read the clause carefully before suing.

## How it works in practice

### For the plaintiff: suing a foreign defendant in Israel

1. **Find the ground** in reg. 166 and the facts that prove it (order confirmation to an Israeli address, the site in Hebrew or prices in shekels, the damage here).
2. **File the claim** with a request for service abroad and a supporting affidavit (reg. 167). In small claims, ask in the claim form itself or in a separate request; the court sets the method.
3. **Propose a practical method:** an e-mail address the company actually uses, a registered address in the EU, or the Hague route for a State that requires it. Service through the Convention can take months; plan the hearing date accordingly.
4. **Keep proof of service** (delivery confirmation, read receipt, the foreign certificate).
5. **Think about enforcement now:** a judgment against a company with no assets or presence in Israel may need enforcement abroad. Lesson 30.6 discusses alternatives (payment-card dispute, platform protection).

### For the defendant: served from abroad, or in Israel by a foreign plaintiff

1. **Diarise the defence deadline at once.** The challenge must be filed by that date (reg. 168).
2. **Decide what to argue:** no ground under reg. 166; the ground is not proved by the affidavit; Israel is not the appropriate forum; an agreed foreign forum or arbitration.
3. **Do not argue the merits first.** File the jurisdiction application as your first step; reg. 169 protects you while you do.
4. **If you are sued in Israel by a foreign plaintiff,** consider a request for security for costs (reg. 157).

### Fees and time

Court fees for a claim and for requests follow the general fee rules (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Hague service costs are deposited in court as the registrar sets (1975 Regulations, reg. 5(ד)). Allow several months for Convention service.

## Worked example

Yusuf Haddad, 35, a teacher from Nazareth, bought a ₪3,400 laptop from a website run by a company registered in Cyprus. The site was in Hebrew, prices were in shekels, and delivery was to his home. The laptop arrived dead; the company stopped answering. Its terms say "all disputes shall be decided exclusively by the courts of Limassol under Cypriot law".

1. **Ground for service:** reg. 166(4)(א), the contract was to be performed (delivery) in Israel and was breached here; also (5), damage in Israel from a product of a company trading internationally (if he can show significant international trade).
2. **The clause:** a foreign jurisdiction clause in a consumer standard contract; following Ben Hamo it is presumed depriving and likely void as against him. The Cypriot-law clause may stand; Cyprus is in the EU, but neither side is likely to prove Cypriot law in a small claim, so the court will in practice apply Israeli law (lesson 30.1).
3. **Forum:** small claims court in Nazareth (his residence; see lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md) on venue).
4. **Service:** in the claim, he asks the court to approve service by e-mail to the company's support address and by registered mail to its Limassol address; Cyprus is a Hague Convention State, so the court may prefer the Convention route.
5. **Parallel route:** he also asks his card issuer to stop the charge (lesson 30.6).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real or invented dispute with a foreign defendant (your own purchase, a foreign landlord, a foreign employer).
- The defendant's terms of service, if any; the order and delivery documents.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Pick the ground (10 min).** Choose the reg. 166 paragraph and write the facts that prove it, each with its exhibit.
2. **Test any jurisdiction clause (10 min).** Is it in a consumer standard contract? Is it a court clause or an arbitration clause? Apply Ben Hamo and the Standard Contracts Law ss. 4(9), 5.
3. **Draft the request for service abroad (20 min)** in Hebrew, using the structure below, with a short affidavit.
4. **Write the defendant's best answer (5 min):** which forum non conveniens factors favour the foreign court, and your reply.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own claim or defence: a party may take any step in person (CPR reg. 170), and small claims are designed for it ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). A company you own normally needs a lawyer in the civil courts. You may not file for a relative or friend (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- **Filing:** Net HaMishpat for unrepresented parties, or at the court registry; the request for service abroad goes with the claim.
- **Hague service:** through the court where the case is pending, which forwards to the Courts Director.
- **Apostille in Israel:** a magistrates' court registrar or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (check the gov.il service page for the current procedure and fee, ⚠️ manual check).
- **Free help:** court information centres; Kol-Zchut pages on small claims.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The defendant has hired Israeli lawyers and files a jurisdiction or forum non conveniens application with foreign-law evidence.
- The clause is an international arbitration clause.
- You were served from a foreign court and the amount is significant: whether to appear there affects enforcement in Israel later (lesson 30.5).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing against a foreign company with no request for service abroad | The case stalls; no valid service, no default judgment | File the reg. 167 request with an affidavit | Prepare the request with the claim |
| Affidavit that does not state the ground's facts | Request refused | Supplement the affidavit | Use the three headings of reg. 167(א) |
| Defendant files a defence first, then contests jurisdiction | Treated as consent to jurisdiction | Argue that the objection was made at the first opportunity, if true | Contest jurisdiction first, by the defence deadline |
| Assuming a foreign forum clause is always void | It is enforced in a business contract, or an arbitration clause is enforced | Argue consumer status and the standard-contract test | Check who you are and what kind of clause it is |
| Sending documents abroad without apostille or translation | Rejected or delayed | Obtain apostille and certified translation | Ask the receiving authority what it requires |

## Review

- An Israeli court has jurisdiction over a defendant served in Israel, a defendant who consents, or one served abroad on a reg. 166 ground with the court's approval (reg. 167).
- Reg. 166(5) lets consumers sue foreign sellers for damage in Israel where the seller trades internationally on a significant scale.
- The defendant served abroad contests jurisdiction or forum by the defence deadline; reg. 169 shields him meanwhile.
- A foreign court clause in a consumer standard contract is presumed depriving (Ben Hamo); a choice-of-law clause may still stand.
- Israel is party to the Hague Service (1972) and Apostille (1978) Conventions; the Courts Director is the central authority.
