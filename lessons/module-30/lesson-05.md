# 30.5 · Enforcing foreign judgments

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read a foreign judgment and say whether an Israeli court is likely to make it enforceable, what the creditor must prove, which defences the debtor has, and what happens next in the Execution Office. Whether you hold a judgment from abroad or a foreign creditor is coming after your assets in Israel, the same short statute governs.

## Why it matters

A judgment is only as good as the assets it can reach. An Israeli who wins in Cyprus against a Cypriot seller with a flat in Tel Aviv needs that judgment enforced here; a foreign bank that won against an Israeli guarantor in Moscow needs the same. Israel has no general treaty network for judgments: almost everything goes through the Foreign Judgments Enforcement Law 1958, which gives the court a discretion ("רשאי") but sets firm conditions and defences.

For a debtor, the law is a shield as well as a threat. The defences are real but narrow: the Israeli court does not reopen the merits, and an argument that the foreign court got the facts or the law wrong fails. The defences are about process (notice and a fair chance to defend), jurisdiction, fraud, conflicting judgments and public policy, and they must be proved by the debtor.

## The law

### What counts and the exclusive route

> ”פסק־חוץ“ – פסק דין שניתן על ידי בית משפט במדינה זרה בענין אזרחי, לרבות פסק דין לתשלום פיצויים או נזקים לצד שנפגע, אף כשלא ניתן בענין אזרחי.

*Gist:* A foreign judgment is a judgment of a court in a foreign State in a civil matter, including an award of compensation to an injured party even in a non-civil (for example criminal) case. ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_1))

A foreign judgment is enforced in Israel only under this Law ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_2)). You cannot take it straight to the Execution Office.

### The four conditions

> בית משפט בישראל רשאי להכריז פסק־חוץ כפסק אכיף אם מצא שנתקיימו בו תנאים אלה:
> (1) הפסק ניתן במדינה שלפי דיניה בתי המשפט שלה היו מוסמכים לתתו;
> (2) הפסק אינו ניתן עוד לערעור;
> (3) החיוב שבפסק ניתן לאכיפה על פי הדינים של אכיפת פסקי דין בישראל, ותכנו של הפסק אינו סותר את תקנת הציבור;
> (4) הפסק הוא בר־ביצוע במדינה בה הוא ניתן.

*Gist:* The court may declare a foreign judgment enforceable if the foreign court had jurisdiction under its own law, the judgment is no longer appealable, the obligation is of a kind enforceable under Israeli enforcement law and its content does not contradict public policy, and it is enforceable in the State where it was given. ([s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_3))

The creditor proves these, usually with a certified, apostilled copy of the judgment, a certified translation, and an opinion or certificate on foreign law showing finality and enforceability. Interim maintenance orders are an exception to finality (s. 8).

### Reciprocity

> (א) פסק־חוץ לא יוכרז אכיף אם ניתן במדינה שלפי דיניה אין אוכפים פסקים של בתי המשפט בישראל.
> (ב) על פי בקשת היועץ המשפטי לממשלה רשאי בית המשפט לאכוף פסק־חוץ, אף כשלא נתקיימה הדדיות כאמור בסעיף קטן (א).

*Gist:* A foreign judgment is not enforced if the State that gave it does not enforce Israeli judgments, unless the Attorney General asks the court to enforce it anyway. ([s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_4))

The courts read this liberally: what is needed is a reasonable potential that an Israeli judgment would be enforced in that State, not proof of actual enforcement (the Gazprom test, below). Once the Supreme Court has found reciprocity with a State, a party who says it has ended must bring evidence (VTB, below).

### Time limit

> לא ייזקק בית המשפט לבקשת אכיפה של פסק־חוץ שהוגשה כעבור יותר מחמש שנים מיום מתן הפסק, אלא אם הוסכם בין ישראל לבין המדינה בה ניתן הפסק על תקופה אחרת, או אם מצא בית המשפט סיבות מיוחדות המצדיקות את האיחור.

*Gist:* An application filed more than five years after the judgment was given will not be heard, unless a treaty sets another period or the court finds special reasons for the delay. ([s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_5))

The period runs from the date the judgment was *given*, not from when it became final (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### The debtor's defences

> פסק־חוץ לא יוכרז אכיף אם הוכח לבית המשפט אחד מאלה:
> (1) הפסק הושג במרמה;
> (2) האפשרות שניתנה לנתבע לטעון טענותיו ולהביא ראיותיו לפני מתן הפסק לא היתה, לדעת בית המשפט, סבירה;
> (3) הפסק ניתן על ידי בית משפט שלא היה מוסמך לתתו על פי כללי המשפט הבין־לאומי הפרטי החלים בישראל;
> (4) הפסק נוגד פסק דין אחר שניתן באותו ענין בין אותם בעלי דין ושעודנו בר־תוקף;
> (5) בעת הגשת התביעה בבית המשפט במדינה הזרה היה משפט תלוי ועומד, באותו ענין ובין אותם בעלי דין, בפני בית משפט או בית דין בישראל.

*Gist:* Enforcement is refused if the debtor proves fraud; no reasonable opportunity to present his case and evidence; lack of jurisdiction under Israel's private international law rules; a conflicting valid judgment between the same parties; or Israeli proceedings on the same matter pending when the foreign suit was filed. ([s. 6(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_6))

Appearing abroad only to contest jurisdiction, to ask for a stay in favour of arbitration or another court, or to protect seized assets, is not consent to the foreign court's jurisdiction, even if the debtor also argued the merits before the foreign court's final decision on jurisdiction (s. 6(ב)–(ג)). Under Israeli rules a foreign court has jurisdiction where the debtor was resident there or consented, expressly or impliedly, for example by a jurisdiction clause (VTB, para. 22).

Two further bars: public policy (s. 3(3)) and harm to Israel's sovereignty or security (s. 7).

### After the declaration

> פסק־חוץ שהוכרז אכיף, דינו לענין הוצאה לפועל כדין פסק שניתן כדין בישראל.

*Gist:* Once declared enforceable, a foreign judgment is executed like an Israeli judgment; a debt in foreign currency is paid in shekels at the rate on the day of payment, or in the foreign currency itself. ([s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אכיפת_פסקי-חוץ#סעיף_10))

The creditor then opens an Execution Office file (lesson [28.1](../module-28/lesson-01.md)), and the debtor has the ordinary execution rights.

### Recognition without enforcement

Section 11 deals with *recognition* (using a foreign judgment's outcome, for example as res judicata or a status decision): mandatory where a treaty requires it (s. 11(א)), and incidental, in a case within the court's jurisdiction, where it sees fit "מן הדין והצדק" (s. 11(ב)). The protections of s. 6(ב)–(ג) on appearance apply.

### Treaties and foreign arbitral awards

Israel has a few bilateral arrangements (for example with Germany, implemented by regulations) that change procedure and periods; check whether one applies to your State. Israel is listed in the HCCH status table of the 2019 Judgments Convention with the date 3.3.2021, but the Convention is not in force for Israel ([HCCH](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=137); ⚠️ the table shows this as a signature, not a ratification). Foreign **arbitral awards** are a different track: the Arbitration Law and the New York Convention, not the 1958 Law.

### Procedure

The creditor files an application in an Israeli civil court, with the judgment and supporting affidavit, and serves the debtor; if the debtor is abroad, reg. 166(7) allows service out of the jurisdiction for an application to enforce or recognise a foreign judgment. (⚠️ The 2018 Regulations do not set a dedicated procedure for these applications; practice is to file in the court with subject-matter jurisdiction over a claim of that amount. Confirm with the registry.) The debtor answers with an affidavit setting out his defences.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 1948/15 JSC VTB Bank נ׳ מרגוליס (6.3.2017)

A Russian bank lent money to companies owned by Dr Margolis, a dual Israeli–Russian citizen, who guaranteed the loans personally while living in Russia; the guarantees chose Russian courts and Russian law. The bank won three judgments in Moscow and asked the Haifa District Court to enforce them; it refused, holding that Margolis had no reasonable opportunity to defend (s. 6(א)(2)) because of how he was served. The Supreme Court (Zilbertal J., Hayut and Fogelman JJ. concurring) allowed the appeal and declared the judgments enforceable. An Israeli court is not an appeal court over the foreign procedure; the only question is whether the debtor had a reasonable opportunity to know of the proceedings and present his case. Service at the official address that Margolis himself gave the bank in the guarantees was reasonable. On jurisdiction, a foreign court has international jurisdiction where the defendant was resident there or consented, and consent may be express or implied; the jurisdiction clause decided it (para. 22). Reciprocity with Russia, found in 2014, stood absent evidence that it had ended. Why it matters: it is the current leading statement of the s. 6(א)(2) defence and of the "residence or consent" test, and it shows the courts' liberal, pro-enforcement approach. [Full text (law.co.il)](https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/jsc_vtb.pdf)

### ע״א 3081/12 דאבל קיי מוצרי דלק (1996) בע״מ נ׳ גזפרום טרנסגז אוכטה בע״מ (9.9.2014)

As described in VTB (para. 21): the Supreme Court held that a reasonable potential that an Israeli judgment would be enforced in Russia satisfies the reciprocity requirement of s. 4(א), while noting that the position could be revisited if the law in Russia changed or Israeli judgments were in practice not enforced there. Why it matters: reciprocity is assessed as a realistic legal possibility, not a track record. (⚠️ Holding taken from its summary in VTB; the full Gazprom text was not read.)

## How it works in practice

### Creditor's file

1. Certified copy of the judgment with apostille (lesson 30.2) and certified Hebrew translation.
2. Evidence that it is final and enforceable where given (foreign-law opinion or certificate).
3. Evidence of reciprocity (a prior Israeli ruling about that State, or a foreign-law opinion).
4. Proof that the debtor was served abroad and had an opportunity to defend.
5. Date check: within five years of the date given (s. 5), or reasons for delay.

### Debtor's file

1. **Deadline:** the time to respond set by the court; if served abroad, consider a jurisdiction challenge as well (reg. 168).
2. **Process defence (s. 6(א)(2)):** how and where were you served? Did you know of the case in time? Could you have defended? Evidence: addresses, travel dates, mail logs.
3. **Jurisdiction defence (s. 6(א)(3)):** were you resident there? Did you consent (a clause, appearance on the merits)?
4. **Other defences:** fraud, conflicting judgment, earlier Israeli proceedings, public policy, the five years.
5. **What does not work:** "the foreign judge was wrong on the facts or the law".

### Fees and time

Court fees for the application follow the general fee rules (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Translations and foreign-law opinions are the major costs.

## Worked example

Avi Mizrahi from Rishon LeZion leased a holiday flat in Larnaca to a Cypriot company that stopped paying rent. In 2022 he sued in the Larnaca District Court, won a judgment for €18,000 in 2023, and the time to appeal has passed. The company has no assets in Cyprus but holds a bank account in Israel through its Israeli parent.

1. **Is it a foreign judgment?** Yes, a civil judgment of a foreign court (s. 1).
2. **Conditions:** the Cypriot court had jurisdiction under Cypriot law (company and land in Cyprus); no longer appealable; a money debt is enforceable in Israel and not against public policy; enforceable in Cyprus (s. 3). Avi obtains an apostilled certified copy and a short opinion from his Cypriot lawyer on finality and enforceability.
3. **Reciprocity:** he needs evidence that Cypriot courts would enforce an Israeli judgment (s. 4(א)); his Cypriot lawyer's opinion covers it.
4. **Time:** judgment given in 2023; he must file by 2028 (s. 5).
5. **Defences the company may raise:** none obvious; it was served at its registered office and defended. Jurisdiction under Israeli rules: residence (registered office) is enough.
6. **Then:** with the declaration, Avi opens an Execution Office file and asks for a lien on the account (lesson 28.2); the debt is paid in shekels at the rate on the day of payment (s. 10(ב)). He must check whether the account really belongs to the Cypriot company and not to its Israeli parent, which is a separate legal person.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A foreign judgment (yours, or the Larnaca example), or a demand letter from a foreign creditor.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Classify (5 min):** court judgment or arbitral award? Civil? Money or status?
2. **Run the conditions (15 min):** ss. 3, 4, 5, each with the document that proves it.
3. **Run the defences (15 min):** s. 6(א)(1)–(5), s. 7, public policy; for each, the facts and evidence on each side.
4. **Decide (10 min):** likely outcome, the weakest link, and the next step (file, settle, defend).

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes in principle, as creditor or debtor in your own case: an individual may act in person in the civil courts (CPR reg. 170; [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). In practice these applications depend on foreign-law evidence, and most parties are represented. A company needs a lawyer.

### Where and how

- **Filing:** the civil court (see the procedure note above), with the documents in "Creditor's file".
- **After the declaration:** the Execution Office, as for any Israeli judgment (lesson 28.1).
- **Help:** court information centres; Legal Aid applies its subject, means and merit tests (check eligibility before relying on it).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are the debtor and the amount is significant: the defences are narrow and turn on evidence.
- The judgment comes from a State where reciprocity has not been decided.
- The judgment is in a family matter (custody, status) or is an arbitral award.
- More than five years have passed and you rely on "special reasons".

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taking a foreign judgment straight to the Execution Office | Refused: only the 1958 Law route exists (s. 2) | Apply to the court for a declaration | Start with the application |
| Debtor argues the foreign court erred on the merits | Defence rejected; no appeal on the merits | Reframe as a process or jurisdiction defence, if the facts allow | Focus on s. 6 grounds |
| Debtor ignores a foreign suit, then claims no opportunity | Fails if he was served at an address he gave (VTB) | Show he did not actually receive notice in time | Keep addresses in contracts up to date; respond to foreign suits |
| Creditor files after five years | Not heard (s. 5) | Show special reasons | Diarise the date the judgment was given |
| No proof of finality or reciprocity | Application fails | Add a foreign-law opinion | Prepare the creditor's file before filing |

## Review

- A foreign civil judgment is enforced in Israel only under the 1958 Law (s. 2), by a court declaration.
- Conditions: jurisdiction under foreign law, final, enforceable here and not against public policy, enforceable at home (s. 3); reciprocity (s. 4, read as a reasonable potential); within five years of the judgment (s. 5).
- Defences: fraud, no reasonable opportunity, no jurisdiction under Israeli rules (residence or consent, also implied), conflicting judgment, prior Israeli proceedings (s. 6); sovereignty and security (s. 7).
- Once declared, it is executed like an Israeli judgment (s. 10); recognition is separate (s. 11).
