# 30.7 · Practice lab: a foreign judgment and an international purchase dispute

This lab puts the module to work on two files that ordinary people actually face: a judgment from a foreign court that someone wants to enforce in Israel (you may be the creditor or the debtor), and an online purchase from a foreign seller that went wrong. You finish with a written plan for the first and a ready-to-send set of letters for the second.

## Why it matters

Both files are won or lost on documents and dates. A foreign creditor who cannot prove finality and reciprocity loses; a debtor who argues that the foreign judge was wrong loses too. A consumer who writes to the card issuer before cancelling with the seller, or after the 30-day window, gives up a statutory right. The lab trains the habit of building the file before writing the first letter.

## The law

The law for this lab is in lessons 30.2 (service abroad, regs 166–169; apostilles), 30.5 (Foreign Judgments Enforcement Law ss. 1–11) and 30.6 (Payment Services Law ss. 17, 27, 29; Consumer Protection Law ss. 14ג, 14ה, 31א). Keep those lessons open; quote the sections in your documents exactly.

## Leading cases

Use VTB v. Margolis (ע״א 1948/15) for the "reasonable opportunity" defence and the "residence or consent" test, and Facebook v. Ben Hamo (רע״א 5860/16) for foreign-court clauses in consumer terms. Both are summarised in lessons 30.5 and 30.2.

## How it works in practice

Choose your track for each part:

| Part | Track A | Track B |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Foreign judgment | You hold the judgment (creditor) | A judgment was given against you, or a foreign creditor wrote to you (debtor) |
| 2. Online purchase | A real failed purchase of your own: real, own name | The supplied case below: dry run |

Default level is a dry run. Send real letters only in your own matter, and only the seller and issuer letters; filing the small claim is optional and only after the earlier steps fail.

## Worked example

Supplied case for part 1: Daniel Cohen of Ra'anana guaranteed his former business partner's lease in London. In March 2022 the County Court in London gave judgment against both for £14,000. Daniel says he never received the claim: it was sent to his old London flat, which he left in 2020, though the guarantee gave that address and said "notices to the address above". In September 2026 the landlord's Israeli lawyer sends Daniel a letter threatening enforcement in Israel.

Supplied case for part 2: Maya Suleiman of Shfar'am ordered noise-cancelling headphones for ₪900 from a site in Hebrew, operated by a company in Poland, paid by credit card in one payment, with delivery promised within 14 days. On day 30 they have not arrived and the seller's chat says "processing".

Short model answers are in the self-check below; the module project, [a cross-border file](../../projects/m30-cross-border-file.md), extends both parts.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- For part 1: the judgment (or Daniel's case), the contract that led to it, any correspondence, a calendar.
- For part 2: the order confirmation, listing, tracking, messages, card statement (or Maya's case).
- The draft templates [Foreign judgment enforcement and defence worksheet](../../templates/foreign-judgment-worksheet.md) and [International purchase dispute kit](../../templates/international-purchase-dispute-kit.md).
- 150 minutes: about 70 for part 1 and 80 for part 2.

### Steps

#### Part 1: the foreign-judgment plan (70 min)

1. **Identify the judgment (5 min):** court, State, date given, parties, amount, currency, whether appealable.
2. **Compute the deadline (5 min):** five years from the date the judgment was given (s. 5); note any treaty.
3. **Conditions, s. 3 (15 min):** for each of the four, write "proved by" (creditor) or "attack" (debtor), and the document.
4. **Reciprocity, s. 4 (5 min):** is there an Israeli ruling about this State? What evidence would be needed?
5. **Defences, ss. 6–7 and public policy (20 min):** for each, the facts on each side. For a debtor, the strongest is usually s. 6(א)(2) or (3); apply VTB honestly to your facts.
6. **Evidence list (10 min):** apostilled certified copy, translation, foreign-law opinion, proof of service, proof of address history.
7. **Next step (10 min):** creditor: file the application (and reg. 166(7) service if the debtor is abroad). Debtor: answer the lawyer's letter (no admissions), prepare the affidavit, consider settlement; if served abroad, the reg. 168 deadline.

#### Part 2: the international purchase dispute (80 min)

1. **Facts and timeline (10 min):** order date, promised delivery, today, payment method and instalments.
2. **Classify (5 min):** not supplied (s. 17), not your charge (s. 29), defective or different (contract and chargeback), subscription after cancellation.
3. **Letter 1, to the seller (15 min):** cancellation and demand, with a 14-day refund deadline; English with the Hebrew legal basis; send through the site and by e-mail.
4. **Letter 2, to the issuer (15 min):** the s. 17 notice (or s. 29), using the lesson 30.6 template, with the cancellation attached.
5. **Platform route (5 min):** if you paid through a platform, note its dispute deadline and do not run it in parallel with a chargeback unless its terms allow it.
6. **Regulator (10 min):** draft a short complaint to the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority if the site targets Israel.
7. **Small claim, only if steps 3–6 fail (20 min):** draft the statement of claim (template [small claim: contract or consumer](../../templates/small-claim-contract-consumer.md)) with a request for service abroad (lesson 30.2 template), and a paragraph on the foreign-court clause (Ben Hamo).

### How you know it worked

- Part 1: every condition and defence has a document or a reason; the five-year date is written down; the next step has a date.
- Part 2: the seller was notified before the issuer; the issuer letter cites s. 17 or s. 29 with exhibits; you know what you will do on day 15 and day 30.
- You never cited the repealed Debit Cards Law.

### Self-check

- [ ] Daniel: judgment given March 2022, so the five years run to March 2027; England is the forum of the guarantee; his best defence, no reasonable opportunity (s. 6(א)(2)), is weak under VTB because service was at the address he gave and the guarantee said notices go there, unless he can show he told the landlord of a new address. Jurisdiction (s. 6(א)(3)): he lived in London when he signed and the guarantee chose England, so residence or consent exists. His realistic options: negotiate, or check whether the English judgment can still be set aside in England.
- [ ] Maya: delivery date passed, so s. 17(א)(1) is met once she cancels with the seller (s. 17(א)(2)); with a single payment already debited, s. 17 stops future charging only, so she also asks for a "goods not received" chargeback; the Hebrew site and shekel prices support reg. 166(4)(א) and Israeli consumer law if she later sues.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Part 2: yes, fully, for your own purchase. Part 1: you may act in person as creditor or debtor, but a contested enforcement application usually needs foreign-law evidence; see lesson 30.5 ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)).

### Where and how

- Issuer: its written disputes channel. Regulator: gov.il online complaint (⚠️ manual check).
- Court filings: Net HaMishpat for unrepresented parties, or the registry; fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are the debtor in part 1 and the sum matters to you, or the creditor has filed in court.
- The seller or creditor has a lawyer and raises foreign law.
- You are tempted to send letters for a friend or relative: do not (Bar Association Law s. 20).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debtor ignores the foreign lawyer's letter | Application filed and granted by default | Respond in court within the time set | Answer in writing, without admissions |
| Creditor files without finality and reciprocity evidence | Application fails | Add a foreign-law opinion | Use the worksheet's evidence list |
| Consumer writes to the issuer first | s. 17 conditions not met | Cancel with the seller, then renew the notice | Follow the order of steps |
| Small claim filed with no service request | Case stalls | File the reg. 167 request | Attach it to the claim |

## Review

- Build the file first: dates, documents, then letters.
- For a foreign judgment, the five-year date, finality, reciprocity and the s. 6 defences decide the case; VTB sets a high bar for "no reasonable opportunity".
- For a failed purchase: seller, then platform or issuer (s. 17 / s. 29), then regulator, then small claim with service abroad.
