# 30.3 · Choice of law in contracts and torts

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say which country's law governs a contract with a foreign party or an accident abroad, when the Vienna Convention on the International Sale of Goods replaces Israeli sale law, and how a choice-of-law clause in a website's terms affects your claim. Lesson 30.2 decided where the case is heard; this lesson decides what law the court applies once it is there.

## Why it matters

The choice of law can decide the case. In Yinon, below, the injured worker would have received about ₪187,000 under Jordanian law and about ₪1.4 million under Israeli law. In a sale between an Israeli importer and a Chinese factory, the CISG may apply automatically, with its own rule that a buyer who does not give notice of a defect within a reasonable time loses the claim. In a consumer's claim against a foreign platform, the platform's clause choosing Californian or Irish law may survive even though its clause choosing a foreign court does not.

There is no general Israeli statute on choice of law in contracts or torts. The rules are judge-made, with two statutory islands: the CISG law for international sales of goods, and the Standard Contracts Law, which can strike out a choice-of-law clause that deprives the customer of claims. That makes the leading cases, and the reasoning behind them, central.

## The law

### Contracts: the parties choose, within limits

**Party autonomy.** Israeli courts give effect to the parties' choice of law, express (a clause) or implied (clear from the contract and the circumstances, for example a contract drafted on an Israeli standard form with references to Israeli statutes). The choice is usually read as a choice of the chosen State's substantive law, not its choice-of-law rules.

**Limits.**
1. *Standard contracts.* A choice-of-law clause in a standard contract is a "term" like any other. It can be presumed depriving if it limits the customer's right to raise certain claims in court:

> תנאי השולל או המגביל את זכות הלקוח להשמיע טענות מסויימות בערכאות משפטיות, או הקובע כי כל סכסוך בין הספק והלקוח יידון בבוררות;

*Gist:* A term that removes or limits the customer's right to raise particular claims in court, or sends every dispute to arbitration, is presumed depriving. ([Standard Contracts Law s. 4(8)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_האחידים#סעיף_4))

But the burden is on the customer to show that the foreign law actually bars a claim he wants to make (Ben Hamo, below).

2. *Mandatory rules and public policy.* A choice of foreign law does not displace Israeli rules meant to apply regardless of the contract's law (the public-policy reservation of lesson 30.1), for example certain protective rules for employees working in Israel or for consumers. Which consumer and employment rules are "mandatory" in this sense is debated in Israeli law; there is no statutory list. (⚠️ No Supreme Court holding on the mandatory force of the Consumer Protection Law against a foreign-law clause was found.)

**No choice: the closest connection.** Where the parties did not choose, Israeli courts apply the law with which the contract has the closest connection, looking at where it was negotiated and signed, where it is to be performed, the parties' places of business and residence, the language and currency, and the subject matter (land is governed by the law of its location). This is the common-law approach Israel inherited and kept. (⚠️ The test is stated here from the general doctrine; no leading Supreme Court judgment on it was read for this module.)

### International sales of goods: the CISG is Israeli law

> להוראות האמנה בנוסח שבתוספת יהיה תוקף של חוק.

*Gist:* The UN Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods (Vienna, 1980), in the text in the Schedule, has the force of law. ([Sale (International Sale of Goods) Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר_(מכר_טובין_בין-לאומי)#סעיף_2))

Israel acceded to the Convention on 22.1.2002; it entered into force for Israel on 1.2.2003 ([UNCITRAL status](https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/salegoods/conventions/sale_of_goods/cisg/status)). The Convention applies to sales between parties whose places of business are in different States, where both States are parties or the conflict rules point to a party State (art. 1(א)). Israel goes further:

> נוסף על האמור בסעיף 1 לאמנה, יחולו הוראות האמנה גם על חוזה שצד לו מי שמקום עסקו במדינה שאינה צד לאמנה.

*Gist:* In Israel, the Convention also applies to a contract with a party whose place of business is in a non-party State. ([s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר_(מכר_טובין_בין-לאומי)#סעיף_3))

**Consumers are excluded:**

> טובין שנקנו לשימוש אישי, משפחתי או ביתי, אלא אם כן המוכר לא ידע ולא היה עליו לדעת, בכל עת לפני כריתת החוזה או בעת כריתתו, שהטובין נקנו לשימוש כאמור;

*Gist:* The Convention does not apply to goods bought for personal, family or household use, unless the seller neither knew nor should have known of that purpose. ([Schedule art. 2(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר_(מכר_טובין_בין-לאומי)#תוספת_חלק_א_פרט_2))

**Opt-out:** the parties may exclude the Convention or vary its provisions (art. 6). A clause choosing "Israeli law" is, on the usual reading, a choice of Israeli law *including* the CISG; to exclude it the contract should say so.

**What it does not cover:** the validity of the contract and its terms, and the effect on ownership (art. 4), and liability for death or personal injury (art. 5). Those go to the law chosen by the ordinary rules.

**The trap: notice of defects.**

> הקונה מאבד את זכותו להסתמך על חוסר התאמה של הטובין, אם לא נתן למוכר הודעה המפרטת את טיבו של חוסר ההתאמה, בתוך זמן סביר לאחר שגילה אותו, או שהיה עליו לגלותו.

*Gist:* The buyer loses the right to rely on a lack of conformity unless he gives the seller notice specifying its nature within a reasonable time after he discovered it or ought to have discovered it; in any case, within two years of actual delivery unless a contractual guarantee says otherwise. ([Schedule art. 39](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר_(מכר_טובין_בין-לאומי)#תוספת_חלק_ג_סימן_2_פרט_39))

### Torts: the place of the tort, with a narrow exception

There is no statute. Since Yinon (2004) the rule is the law of the place where the tort was committed, with a narrow exception where applying it would be unjust because the tort's real connection is elsewhere. The English "double actionability" rule (a claim had to be actionable both in the forum and in the place of the tort), which Israeli courts had followed, was abandoned. Questions such as the existence of a head of damage and a cap on compensation are substantive and go to the law that governs the tort.

The hard cases are about *where* a tort was committed: a defamatory post written in one State and read in another, a defective product made abroad that injures in Israel, a financial loss suffered in an Israeli bank account through acts abroad. Yinon does not answer them; the general approach is to look for the place of the decisive act or the place where the damage occurred, and the exception allows the court to move to a more connected law.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 1432/03 ינון יצור ושיווק מוצרי מזון בע״מ נ׳ קרעאן (1.9.2004)

A Palestinian worker from Qalqilya was injured in an Israeli company's factory in the settlement of Alfei Menashe. Jordanian law, which applies in the West Bank under the military regime, gave strict-liability compensation of about ₪187,200; the district court applied Israeli law and awarded about ₪1.4 million for negligence. Rivlin J. (Naor and Hayut JJ. concurring) surveyed English, Canadian, Australian, continental and American law, abandoned the double actionability rule (para. 26), and adopted the law of the place of the tort as the general rule, justified by territorial sovereignty, certainty and the parties' expectations (para. 28), with discretion to depart from it "in rare cases where considerations of justice require it" (para. 27). He held that Jordanian law's strict liability and its cap could not be split (para. 35) and then applied the exception: it is not right to apply Jordanian law to a work accident caused by an Israeli employer's negligence in an Israeli-owned factory in an Israeli settlement; in the unique legal reality of the settlements, the reasons behind the place-of-the-tort rule do not hold (paras 36 ff.). The appeal was dismissed and the worker's cross-appeal was partly allowed.

Why it matters: it is the leading Israeli choice-of-law case in tort. Read it for the rule, for the method (a comparative survey and a deliberate choice), and for the debate it reflects: the exception was applied in the special legal setting of the settlements, which some commentators see as a pragmatic recognition of reality and others as letting the legal status of the territories shape private law. The rule itself (law of the place of the tort) is accepted on both sides of that debate. [Full text (Haifa Faculty of Law)](https://law.haifa.ac.il/images/lawatchF/2007a_international_law_1432_03.pdf)

### רע״א 5860/16 Facebook Inc. נ׳ בן חמו (31.5.2018): the choice-of-law clause

Recap from lesson 30.2, now on the second clause. Facebook's terms said California law governs "without regard to conflict of law provisions". The Court read the clause, against the drafter, as choosing California's substantive law only, not its procedure (para. 41). The district court had struck it as depriving under s. 4(8); the Supreme Court reversed: the burden of showing a presumption of deprivation is on the customer, and no evidence showed that deciding under California law would prevent the user from raising particular claims (para. 42). Why it matters: a consumer who wants to escape a foreign-law clause must show concretely what claim the foreign law takes away; a general objection to foreign law fails. [Full text (law.co.il)](https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/ben_hemo2.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### Contract checklist

1. **Is there a clause?** Read the terms (and any later version: shortly before judgment, Facebook told the Court that from 14.7.2018 its terms would let consumers sue at home under their own law, Ben Hamo para. 7; check the version that applied when you contracted).
2. **Is it a standard contract with you as a customer?** If so, can you show a concrete claim the chosen law removes (s. 4(8))? Collect evidence of that law (an official text, an expert, a regulator's guidance).
3. **Is it a sale of goods between businesses in different States?** Then the CISG applies unless excluded; check art. 39 notice now.
4. **No clause?** List the connections and argue the closest one.
5. **Can anyone prove the foreign law?** If not, the court will likely decide by Israeli law (lesson 30.1).

### Tort checklist

1. Where was the act? Where did the damage occur?
2. Which law is that place's law? (For the West Bank: Yinon.)
3. Is there a strong reason for the exception: both parties from the same State, a pre-existing relationship (employment, contract) governed by another law?
4. Is the issue substantive (heads of damage, caps) or procedural (limitation in Israeli courts may be treated as procedural: check)?

### Costs

An expert opinion on foreign law is the main cost of relying on it; in small claims it is rarely worth it. Fees: see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Worked example

Lior Ben-David runs a one-woman ceramics studio in Mitzpe Ramon. She ordered a ₪28,000 kiln for the business from a Turkish manufacturer by e-mail; the order form said nothing about the applicable law. The kiln arrived in March; she used it in April and found in July that it does not reach the stated temperature. She complained in October.

1. **Consumer or business?** The kiln is for her business, so the consumer exclusion (art. 2(1)) does not apply.
2. **CISG?** Places of business in Israel and Turkey (a party State; and in Israel, s. 3 extends the Convention anyway). The CISG applies, as Israeli law, unless excluded; it was not.
3. **Notice:** was October "a reasonable time" after she discovered, or ought to have discovered, the defect? If she ought to have noticed in April when she first fired it, three to six months may be too late under art. 39(א). She should collect evidence of when the defect could first be detected.
4. **Validity questions** (for example, a claim that the contract was induced by misrepresentation) are outside the CISG (art. 4) and go to the law with the closest connection, arguably Turkish law (seller's place, place of manufacture) or Israeli law (place of delivery and use).
5. **Forum:** reg. 166(4)(א) (performance in Israel) supports service in Turkey (lesson 30.2).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One international contract or purchase from your own life (online order, freelance contract with a foreign client, a car rental abroad), or Lior's case.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Find the law clause (5 min)** and copy its exact words.
2. **Classify the contract (5 min):** consumer, business-to-business sale of goods, services, employment.
3. **Run the checklist (15 min)** above and decide the governing law, with a reason for each step.
4. **If the CISG applies, compute the notice risk (5 min)** under art. 39.
5. **Write a one-paragraph Hebrew argument (10 min)** for the governing law you prefer, citing the source.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- You named the governing law and the source of the rule (clause, CISG, closest connection).
- If you attacked a clause, you identified a concrete claim the foreign law removes.
- If the CISG applies, you know whether you gave notice in time.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain when a choice-of-law clause in a standard contract is depriving, and who must prove it.
- [ ] I can state when the CISG applies in Israel, including s. 3 and the consumer exclusion.
- [ ] I can state the Yinon rule, its exception and why the exception was applied.
- [ ] I can tell a substantive issue from a procedural one in a tort claim.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may make choice-of-law arguments in your own case in any Israeli court where you act for yourself ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Proving foreign law needs an expert; in a small claim you will usually argue Israeli law and the invalidity or irrelevance of the foreign clause.

### Where and how

- The CISG text in Hebrew: Wikisource; in English and other languages: UNCITRAL. Case law on the CISG worldwide: the free CLOUT database on uncitral.un.org.
- Israeli rulings: free hosts used in this module.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A business contract of significant value turns on the CISG notice rule or on foreign law.
- A tort abroad caused serious injury; the choice of law can change the compensation many times over, and limitation periods differ.
- The other side relies on an expert opinion on foreign law.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attacking a foreign-law clause in general terms | Clause upheld (Ben Hamo) | Show the specific claim the foreign law bars | Research the foreign law's remedies first |
| Assuming "Israeli law" excludes the CISG | CISG applies, including art. 39 | Argue an implied exclusion if the contract shows it | Write "excluding the CISG" if you want that |
| Late notice of a defect in a business purchase | Right to rely on the defect lost (art. 39) | Argue the defect could not be discovered earlier | Inspect and notify in writing promptly |
| Applying the CISG to a consumer purchase | Wrong rules | Use Israeli consumer and sale law | Check art. 2(1) first |
| Treating a damages cap as procedure | Wrong amount awarded | Cite Yinon para. 35 | Characterize each issue |

## Review

- In contracts, the parties' choice governs, subject to the Standard Contracts Law, mandatory rules and public policy; without a choice, the law of the closest connection.
- A foreign-law clause in a consumer standard contract survives unless the customer shows a concrete claim it removes (Ben Hamo).
- The CISG is Israeli law for international business sales of goods, even with a non-party State (s. 3); consumers are excluded; art. 39 notice is strict.
- In torts, Yinon applies the law of the place of the tort, with a narrow justice-based exception; double actionability is gone.
