# 10.5 · Practice lab: find Jewish-law reasoning in a Supreme Court ruling

In this lab you take one Supreme Court ruling in which justices disagreed about Jewish law, find every Jewish-law reference in it, trace the main principle back to the Talmud and the codes, check whether the judges used the sources accurately, and write a two-page analysis of how much Jewish law actually mattered to the result.

## Why it matters

Reading a ruling that cites the Talmud is a different skill from reading a ruling that cites a statute. The judge may be filling a gap, interpreting a statute, invoking values or simply comparing; the source may be a ruling of the codes or one view in a Talmudic debate; and another justice may answer with a different source from the same tradition. This lab trains you to separate those layers, the skill lessons 10.1 and 10.2 prepared.

## The law

### The Israeli rule in the case: Land Law ss. 2, 14

> בעלות וזכויות אחרות במקרקעין, אין בהן כשלעצמן כדי להצדיק עשיית דבר הגורם נזק או אי נוחות לאחר.

*Gist:* Ownership of land does not in itself justify doing something that causes damage or inconvenience to another. [Land Law s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_14)

Section 2 defines ownership as the right to possess, use and deal with land "subject to the limitations under law or agreement". The question in the case was whether these limits, and the general good-faith duty (Contracts Law ss. 39 and 61(ב)), give a court discretion to refuse a demolition order for a building put on common property without consent.

### The Jewish-law sources the justices used

- "כגון זה – כופין על מדת סדום" ([Bava Batra 12b](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.12b)), codified in [Mishneh Torah, Neighbors 12:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Neighbors.12.1) and [Choshen Mishpat 174:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Choshen_Mishpat.174.1) (lesson 10.1).
- "לֹא תַסִּיג גְּבוּל רֵעֲךָ אֲשֶׁר גָּבְלוּ רִאשֹׁנִים" ([Deuteronomy 19:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.19.14)).

## Leading cases

### רע״א 6339/97 רוקר נ׳ סלומון (23.12.1999)

Owners in a four-flat condominium (the case began in the Haifa Magistrates' Court) extended their flat into the common property with a building permit but without the other owners' consent. In the two months when no injunction was in force they finished the extension. The lower courts ordered demolition. An expanded panel of seven heard the appeal and denied it (Justice Turkel, with President Barak and Justices Cheshin, Mazza and S. Levin; Justice Strasberg-Cohen partly dissenting; Justice Englard dissenting).

- **Justice Englard (dissent)** turned to Jewish law on abuse of rights: "כופין על מידת סדום" means one who denies another a benefit that costs him nothing is compelled to desist. He proposed that the Jewish tradition's limits on ownership serve as "a source of inspiration" in interpreting s. 14 of the Land Law, in a Jewish and democratic state; on the facts, demolition would be disproportionate to the owners' "somewhat abstract" interest, and damages would be the right remedy, consistent with "מידת סדום" and perhaps "בל תשחית".
- **Justice Turkel (majority)** agreed that the principle is an important rule of equity in Jewish law, but answered that it cannot uproot a positive commandment of the Torah, "לא תסיג גבול רעך" (Deut. 19:14), and that the sources do not show it being used to infringe property rights in land. It could not help those who built "unjustly and unlawfully".

Why it matters: both sides argued from Jewish law, about the same principle, and the disagreement was about its scope inside the tradition. The result rested on Israeli property law; Jewish law was inspiration and a test of fit with values. [Cardozo translation (Englard dissent and part of Turkel's response)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/316)

### For comparison: ע״א 6370/00 קל בנין (2002)

In lesson 10.2 you met Justice Englard using the Foundations of Law Act route on an assumption of a gap. In Roker he uses the values-and-interpretation route instead. Compare the two. [Cardozo](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/219)

## How it works in practice

Where to find rulings with Jewish-law reasoning, free:

- The [Cardozo opinions index](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/) tags rulings with keywords such as "Jewish Law", "Jewish Law -- Family Law", "Jewish Law -- Torts". Each page has an abstract and the full English translation.
- The Hebrew original: search the case number in the Judiciary's decision search (module 2). The Supreme Court's site may block automated tools; it works in a normal browser.
- Look in the Hebrew text for markers: "המשפט העברי", "חוק יסודות המשפט", references in the form "בבא בתרא", "רמב״ם", "שו״ע חו״מ", "שו״ת".

## Worked example

Yonatan, a student in Be'er Sheva, picks Shavit (lesson 10.2) as a quick warm-up before Roker.

1. **Find the references.** Searching the English text for "Jewish law" and "halakh" gives the passages on the prohibition of foreign dates and letters on tombstones.
2. **Classify the route.** Values and balance: Jewish law enters as the content of the religious sensibility the court weighs against dignity. Not gap-filling (there is case law), not binding.
3. **Layer.** The passages refer to halakhic rulings on tombstone inscriptions, not to a single verse.
4. **Accuracy check.** The court says the prohibition is "not sweeping and comprehensive"; Yonatan notes that he would need the Hebrew original and the sources cited there to test that, and records it as an open question rather than guessing.
5. **Weight.** The result follows from dignity and public-law duties; Jewish law reduced the weight of the opposing interest. He writes this as one paragraph.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is a research exercise. Nothing is filed. Write as an analyst, not an advocate: you are describing what the judges did, not arguing which tradition is right.

### You need

- The Cardozo page for Roker v. Solomon (and optionally the Hebrew original from the Judiciary's search).
- Sefaria for the sources.
- The [Jewish-law reasoning worksheet](../../templates/jewish-law-reasoning-worksheet.md) (Hebrew headings, English guidance).
- About two hours, in two sittings.

### Steps

**Part A — map the ruling (40 min)**

1. **Facts and question (10 min).** In five sentences: the parties, the building, the permit, the injunction gap, and the legal question about the remedy.
2. **Israeli law first (10 min).** List the statutes and doctrines the justices relied on (Land Law ss. 2 and 14, the Condominium rules on common property, good faith, abuse of right). Note which carried the majority.
3. **Every Jewish-law reference (20 min).** Go through the translated opinions and list each reference in the worksheet: who cites it, the source as cited, the English phrase, and the route (gap-filling, interpretation, values, comparison).

**Part B — trace the sources (40 min)**

4. **מידת סדום.** Open [Bava Batra 12b](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.12b). Find the cases where Rabbah applies the rule and where Rav Yosef objects, and the ruling ("והלכתא"). Then [Neighbors 12:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Neighbors.12.1) and [Choshen Mishpat 174:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Shulchan_Arukh%2C_Choshen_Mishpat.174.1) with the Rema.
5. **השגת גבול.** Open [Deuteronomy 19:14](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.19.14). Note that the verse is about moving a boundary marker; write one sentence on whether a building on common property is the same thing or an analogy.
6. **Accuracy check.** For each justice, answer: does the source say what the justice says it says? Is it a ruling or one view? Is there a dissent in the codes that the justice did not mention? Mark anything you could not check as "open".

**Part C — the analysis (40 min)**

7. **Write two pages** under four headings: (1) The legal question and the Israeli rules; (2) How Jewish law entered (route and layer) for each justice; (3) Accuracy and completeness of the use of sources; (4) Weight: would the result have been the same without the Jewish-law passages? Give a reason.
8. **Neutral close.** End with one paragraph setting out the strongest version of Englard's approach and the strongest version of Turkel's response, without choosing.
9. **Optional extension (20 min).** Repeat Part A for Kal Binyan and compare Englard's route there (s. 1, on an assumption of a gap) with his route in Roker (interpretation and values).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. The skill is used when you read the rulings you cite in your own letters and claims: you check that a ruling's statement of a source is accurate and that you are not relying on a dissent or an obiter remark.

### Where and how

- Cardozo for English translations; the Judiciary's decision search for the Hebrew; Sefaria for the sources.
- In your own case, cite the Israeli holding (Roker's majority on remedies in common property), not a dissent, unless you say clearly that it is a dissent.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You have a real condominium dispute about building on common property: the remedies (demolition, damages, the superintendent of condominiums) are a year-2 property-law topic and the stakes are usually high.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citing Englard's dissent as the law | Your argument relies on a minority view | Cite the majority; mention the dissent as such | Check who joined which opinion |
| Copying the judge's description of a source without checking | You repeat an inaccuracy | Trace the source on Sefaria | Do Part B before Part C |
| Treating the Cardozo partial translation as the whole ruling | You miss the majority's full reasoning | Read the Hebrew original | Note the translator's scope line |
| Taking sides in the neutral close | The exercise loses its point | Rewrite each view in its strongest form | Write each view as its author would |

## Review

- Map the Israeli legal basis first; then list every Jewish-law reference with its route and layer.
- In Roker both the dissent and the majority used Jewish law; they disagreed about the scope of "מידת סדום" against "לא תסיג גבול".
- Tracing a source to the Talmud and codes lets you test a judge's use of it.
- The weight question asks whether the result would change without the Jewish-law passages.
