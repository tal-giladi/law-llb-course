# 10.3 · Personal status: religious courts and their jurisdiction

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a Jewish couple's separation, sort each issue (the get, property, spousal and child maintenance, custody) into the forum that may hear it, explain the "race of jurisdiction" and the mandatory dispute-resolution stage that now precedes it, and describe fairly the current dispute about child maintenance in the rabbinical courts.

## Why it matters

Israel has no civil marriage or divorce for Jews inside the country. A Jewish couple who are citizens or residents marry and divorce through the state rabbinical courts, under Jewish law, whatever their beliefs. Around that core, the law gives the rabbinical court and the Family Court overlapping powers over property, maintenance and children. Which court hears those issues can change the applicable rules of procedure, the court's culture, and sometimes the outcome; that is why spouses have historically rushed to file first.

The religious monopoly over Jewish marriage is itself contested. Supporters argue that one shared track keeps all Jews able to marry one another under Jewish law (avoiding doubts of personal status such as mamzerut) and reflects the status-quo arrangement on which the 1953 Law rests. Critics argue that it imposes religious law on citizens who do not hold it, leaves some people (those without a recognised religion, interfaith and same-sex couples) no way to marry in Israel, and disadvantages women in divorce. Proposals for civil marriage or civil unions have not been enacted as of October 2026; this lesson works with the law as it stands. ([Overview of the debate, Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/נישואים_אזרחיים_בישראל))

Lesson 01.4 introduced the map and two cases (Amir: consent cannot create jurisdiction; Lev: the HCJ supervises religious courts). This lesson goes deeper: the statutory text, joinder (כריכה), the race and its regulation since 2016, the civil statutes the rabbinical court must apply, and the 2025 controversy over child maintenance.

> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## The law

### Marriage and divorce: ss. 1–2

> עניני נישואין וגירושין של יהודים בישראל אזרחי המדינה או תושביה יהיו בשיפוטם היחודי של בתי דין רבניים.

> נישואין וגירושין של יהודים ייערכו בישראל על פי דין תורה.

*Gist:* Marriage and divorce of Jews who are Israeli citizens or residents are exclusively for the rabbinical courts, and are performed in Israel according to Torah law. [Full text, ss. 1–2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בתי_דין_רבניים_(נישואין_וגירושין)#סעיף_1)

Sections 4א–4ב1 add international jurisdiction for divorce where only one spouse is in Israel, including a special route for a Jewish woman whose husband abroad refuses a get.

### Joinder: s. 3, and the 2025 temporary order

> הוגשה לבית דין רבני תביעת גירושין בין יהודים, אם על ידי האשה ואם על ידי האיש, יהא לבית דין רבני שיפוט יחודי בכל ענין הכרוך בתביעת הגירושין, לרבות מזונות לאשה ולילדי הזוג.

*Gist:* Once a divorce claim is filed in the rabbinical court, by either spouse, that court has exclusive jurisdiction over every matter joined to it, including maintenance for the wife and the children. [Full text, s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בתי_דין_רבניים_(נישואין_וגירושין)#סעיף_3)

For two years from 18 November 2025, a temporary order (Amendment 6) reads s. 3 as covering "מזונות לאישה, מזונות לילדי הזוג והשבה של הוצאות מזונות לילדי הזוג שהוציא אחד ההורים, להורה השני", and defines children's maintenance as all their economic needs, including housing, education and health. It also applies to pending joined claims, unless a court had already decided jurisdiction in favour of the Family Court first. [Reshumot, ס״ח 3459, 18.11.2025](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lsr_9835045.pdf)

### Wife's maintenance without divorce: s. 4; consent: s. 9

> הגישה אשה יהודיה לבית דין רבני תביעת מזונות, שלא אגב גירושין, נגד אישה היהודי או נגד עזבונו, לא תישמע טענת הנתבע שאין לבית דין רבני שיפוט בענין.

*Gist:* A Jewish wife may sue her husband for maintenance in the rabbinical court even without a divorce claim, and he cannot object to its jurisdiction. [s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיפוט_בתי_דין_רבניים_(נישואין_וגירושין)#סעיף_4)

Section 9 gives the rabbinical court jurisdiction in other personal-status matters (and inheritance) only if all parties consent. Consent can open the door to a *personal-status* matter; it cannot make the rabbinical court an arbitrator in civil disputes outside personal status (Amir, lesson 01.4).

### The Family Court and parallel jurisdiction: Family Court Law ss. 3, 25

> (ב) בענין הנתון לסמכותו המקבילה של בית דין דתי, יהיה בית המשפט לעניני משפחה מוסמך לדון, כל עוד אין בית הדין הדתי דן בו.

*Gist:* Where a religious court has parallel jurisdiction, the Family Court may hear the matter as long as the religious court is not already hearing it. [s. 25(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_25)

Section 3(א) sends "family matters" (defined in s. 1: personal-status claims and civil claims between family members arising from a family dispute) to the Family Court. The rabbinical court's own procedure regulations have the mirror rule: where a claim is filed in two competent courts, "יתברר המשפט באותו בית־הדין אשר לפניו הוגשה התביעה בראשונה" (reg. כג). [Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_הדיון_בבתי-הדין_הרבניים_בישראל) Together these create the race: whoever files first, properly, in a competent forum, takes the joined issues there.

### Slowing the race: Family Dispute Resolution Law 2014, s. 3

> (א) המבקש להגיש לערכאה שיפוטית תובענה בעניין של סכסוך משפחתי, יגיש תחילה לערכאה השיפוטית בקשה ליישוב סכסוך; הבקשה לא תכלול טענות או עובדות בקשר לסכסוך או בקשר לסמכות השיפוט של הערכאה השיפוטית.

*Gist:* Before filing any claim in a family dispute, a spouse must first file a short request to settle the dispute, with no allegations and no arguments about jurisdiction. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה#סעיף_3)

The parties are then invited to up to four meetings (מהו״ת) at the assistance unit; neither side may file a claim in any forum, nor may any forum decide jurisdiction, during the stay period and the days after it (s. 3(ה); periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Urgent and interim relief stays available (s. 3(ז)), and an interim order does not decide jurisdiction (s. 3(ח)). After the periods, the spouse who filed the request has a short window to file a claim in *any* competent forum; then the other spouse may file the issues left out (s. 4). The law applies to all religious courts and the Family Court (s. 2), excludes proceedings under the Prevention of Domestic Violence Law (s. 4א), and "אינן באות להוסיף על סמכויות השיפוט [...] או לגרוע מהן" (s. 6).

### Civil statutes the rabbinical court must apply

> (ב) כל בתי המשפט ינהגו לפי חוק זה; כן ינהג לפי חוק זה כל בית דין המוסמך לדון בעניני המעמד האישי, אלא אם כל הצדדים הם בני שמונה־עשרה שנה ומעלה והסכימו לפני בית הדין, מרצונם הטוב, להתדיין לפי דיני עדתם.

*Gist:* Religious courts must apply the Women's Equal Rights Law unless all adult parties freely agree before the court to litigate under their community's law. [Women's Equal Rights Law, s. 7(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיווי_זכויות_האשה#סעיף_7)

> (ב) בענין שחוק זה דן בו ינהג גם בית דין דתי לפי הוראות חוק זה, זולת אם הסכימו בעלי הדין לפני בית הדין להתדיין לפי הדין הדתי.

*Gist:* Religious courts apply the Spouses (Property Relations) Law to the matters it covers unless the parties agree before the court to religious law. [Property Relations Law, s. 13(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יחסי_ממון_בין_בני_זוג#סעיף_13)

The Supreme Court's case law treats these civil property rules as binding on a rabbinical court that decides property joined to a divorce; HCJ 4602/13 below starts from that premise and asks only whether the rabbinical court departed from it.

## Leading cases

### בר״ע 120/69 שרגאי נ׳ שרגאי, פ״ד כג(2) 171 (1969)

Justice Sussman held that a child's claim for maintenance is the child's own, independent claim. What a parent joins to a divorce in the rabbinical court is a claim to *reimburse* the parent's expenses for the child, which does not bar the child from suing for maintenance in the civil court unless the child was itself a party in the rabbinical court. The aim was to protect children from a maintenance figure traded away in get negotiations.

Why it matters: the Sharagai rule is the starting point of the current dispute. (Citation and holding as described in the explanatory notes to the 2025 government bill, [ה״ח 1851](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_ls1_5852775.pdf).)

### בג״ץ 5988/21 פלוני נ׳ פלונית (19.2.2025) and Amendment 6 (2025)

After an obiter remark in בע״מ 7628/17 (2019) by Justice Mazuz, and a 2021 ruling of the Great Rabbinical Court that it would continue to hear child maintenance joined to divorce, petitions reached the HCJ. On 19 February 2025 the majority held that the rabbinical court may hear, as a joined matter, only reimbursement of known expenses a parent has paid or is about to pay, not the substantive question of the children's maintenance, and may not award interim child maintenance. The government responded with a bill, and the Knesset passed Amendment 6 as a two-year temporary order on 17 November 2025.

**The legal argument for the amendment** (government bill): s. 3 says in terms "לרבות מזונות [...] לילדי הזוג"; the 1953 committee record and decades of case law (including expanded panels in 2013–2014) read it as full jurisdiction; thousands of joined child-maintenance claims were heard without objection; and the 2025 reading leaves families with two proceedings in two courts.

**The legal argument for the court's reading** (the Sharagai rationale on which the majority built): the child's maintenance right is the child's own, independent of the parents' divorce (Sharagai); joinder in the parents' divorce should not decide it; and the Family Court is the forum designed around the child's best interests, with its own social-work services.

The current state of the law: during the temporary order, s. 3 as amended applies, including to pending joined claims subject to the transition rule. [HCJ ruling as described in ה״ח 1851](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_ls1_5852775.pdf); [the amendment](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lsr_9835045.pdf). Check whether the temporary order has been extended or challenged before relying on it.

### בג״ץ 2232/03 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי תל אביב-יפו (21.11.2006)

Two Jewish Israelis who could have married religiously chose a civil wedding in Cyprus. When the marriage broke down, the rabbinical court dissolved it. The HCJ held that the rabbinical courts have jurisdiction to dissolve civil marriages of Jews contracted abroad; such a marriage has "external" validity even if not valid under Jewish law, and the ground for dissolving it is that the marriage has irretrievably broken down, without fault and without the consent of both parties.

Why it matters: civil marriage abroad is the main route for couples who cannot or will not marry religiously in Israel, and this ruling sets how such a marriage ends. Registration of such marriages is a separate question (Funk-Schlesinger, lesson 10.4). [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/11)

### בג״ץ 4602/13 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי חיפה (18.11.2018)

After a 20-year marriage the wife claimed joint ownership of a house brought in and registered by the husband. The Great Rabbinical Court, by majority, denied it; the wife argued it had given weight to her alleged infidelity, i.e. applied religious law to a property question governed by civil law. The HCJ (2–1) denied the petition: it found the decision rested on a factual finding that a specific partnership was not proved, and reiterated that it does not sit as an appeal court over religious courts; it intervenes for lack of jurisdiction, breach of natural justice, or departure from a statute that binds the religious court. The dissent would have intervened.

Why it matters: it shows both the rule (civil property law binds the rabbinical court) and the limit of review (facts and weight are for the religious court). [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/3) (Check for any later further hearing before relying on it.)

## How it works in practice

### Sorting the issues

| Issue | Rabbinical court | Family Court |
|---|---|---|
| Divorce (the get) | Exclusive (s. 1) | No |
| Wife's maintenance | Yes, alone (s. 4) or joined (s. 3) | Yes, if first and not joined |
| Child maintenance | Joined to divorce: yes under the 2025 temporary order (s. 3 as amended) | Yes; the child's own claim |
| Property division | If properly joined to divorce, applying civil property law | Yes, if first |
| Custody and contact | If properly joined to divorce | Yes, if first |
| Inheritance, other personal status | Only by consent of all parties (s. 9) | Yes |
| Domestic-violence protection order | Yes (separate procedure) | Yes; also any magistrates' court |

**Joinder (כריכה).** A matter is joined if the divorce claim expressly includes it. Case law requires a genuine divorce claim and a proper, sincere joinder, examined on the facts; a joinder made only to win the race can be struck out. The HCJ case law the government bill quotes speaks of a matter "נכרך כדין" (properly joined).

### The sequence today

1. **Request to settle (בקשה ליישוב סכסוך).** On the court's form, to the rabbinical court or the Family Court. No facts, no arguments. A small fee applies (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
2. **Mahut meetings** at the assistance unit of the forum where the request was filed. Both spouses must attend in person. The unit gives information, explores mediation and may help agree interim arrangements for the children.
3. **Stay period.** No claims in any forum; urgent and interim relief remain available.
4. **Filing window.** The spouse who filed the request may file claims in any competent forum within the short window; the other spouse then files any issues left out.
5. **Where the claims are filed** decides the race. Filing the request in the rabbinical court does not oblige you to file your claims there, and vice versa.

### What it costs and how long it takes

Rabbinical-court fees are set by the Dayanim (Fees) Regulations and updated every January; a maintenance claimant is exempt (reg. 14א), people represented by Legal Aid are exempt (reg. 22א), and a fee committee may defer fees for those who cannot pay (reg. 15). Amounts and the dispute-resolution periods are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Worked example

Michal and Avi Ben-David from Petah Tikva, both Jewish Israeli citizens, married by the Rabbinate 12 years ago and have two children. Michal wants a divorce, the house (registered in Avi's name, bought during the marriage), child maintenance and primary custody. Avi wants shared custody and is willing to give a get.

1. **The get.** Rabbinical court only (s. 1).
2. **First step.** Whoever moves first must file a request to settle the dispute (s. 3(א)). Michal files it in the Family Court in Petah Tikva on 1 March. Avi cannot now file claims anywhere until the periods pass.
3. **Mahut.** Both attend the Family Court's assistance unit. They agree an interim parenting schedule; property talks fail.
4. **Filing window.** Michal, as first filer, now chooses: she files a divorce claim in the rabbinical court joining nothing, and files property, custody and child maintenance in the Family Court. Because she filed those issues first, in a competent forum, the Family Court keeps them (Family Court Law s. 25(ב)); the rabbinical court hears only the divorce.
5. **Alternative.** Had Avi filed the request first and then filed a divorce claim in the rabbinical court, properly joining property, custody and child maintenance, the rabbinical court would hear all of them. It would have to apply the Property Relations Law to the house (s. 13) and, during the temporary order, could decide child maintenance in full (s. 3 as amended).
6. **Lesson.** The race has not disappeared; it has moved to the request and the filing window. Both forums apply the civil property law; they differ in procedure, tempo and approach, which each spouse may value differently.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may file a request to settle the dispute, attend the mahut meetings and appear in the rabbinical court or the Family Court in person. In the rabbinical court, the Bar Association Law's monopoly does not apply (s. 21); you may be represented by a lawyer or a licensed rabbinical pleader (טוען רבני), and the court may permit another agent for a specific step (lesson 10.6). See [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The request to settle is filed with the court's secretariat or through its online services (lesson 10.6 for the rabbinical court; module 8 for Net HaMishpat).
- Free help: the assistance units at the family and religious courts (information and mediation, not representation); Legal Aid for eligible applicants, including in rabbinical-court proceedings; see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
- Fees and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side has already filed, or you suspect they are about to, and joint property or the children are at stake: the choice of forum and the content of the joinder are strategic and hard to undo.
- There is violence or a threat of it (protection orders run on a separate, urgent track).
- A spouse refuses a get, or one of you is abroad (international jurisdiction, restriction orders).
- A pension, a business or real estate is involved.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing a full claim before the request to settle | The claim is not heard during the stay; you lose the advantage | File the request and wait for the window | Always start with s. 3(א) |
| Writing facts or jurisdiction arguments in the request | The request is defective and may irritate the court | Refile in the short form | Use the official form; keep it bare |
| A vague divorce claim "joining everything" | Joinder challenged as insincere or improper | Particularise each joined issue | Join only issues you genuinely want decided there, in detail |
| Agreeing in the rabbinical court to litigate "according to Torah law" without understanding it | You waive the Women's Equal Rights Law or Property Relations Law protection | Ask the court to record that you do not consent | Never consent to religious law in property or equality matters without advice |
| Missing the filing window after mahut | The other spouse chooses the forum for those issues | File promptly; diarise the date | Diarise every period on the day you file the request |
| Assuming the 2025 temporary order is permanent | Wrong forum for child maintenance after it lapses | Check the current text before filing | Check Wikisource and the Knesset site for extensions |

## Review

- Marriage and divorce of Jewish citizens and residents are exclusively for the rabbinical courts (ss. 1–2); other personal-status matters are by consent (s. 9).
- A divorce claim filed in the rabbinical court gives it exclusive jurisdiction over properly joined matters (s. 3); otherwise the Family Court may hear them while the rabbinical court is not (s. 25(ב)). That is the race.
- Since 2016 a bare request to settle the dispute comes first, then mahut meetings and a stay; the race now runs through the filing window that follows.
- Rabbinical courts must apply the Women's Equal Rights Law and the Property Relations Law unless the parties consent otherwise before the court; the HCJ reviews jurisdiction, natural justice and compliance with such statutes, not the merits of religious law.
- Child maintenance: the HCJ narrowed rabbinical jurisdiction in February 2025; the Knesset restored it by a two-year temporary order in November 2025. Both readings have serious legal arguments.
