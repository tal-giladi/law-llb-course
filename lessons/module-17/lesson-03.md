# 17.3 · Wages and payslips: Wage Protection Law, Notice to Employee Law and late-wage compensation

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read a payslip line by line, spot what is missing or wrong, calculate the statutory compensation when wages are paid late, and know which documents your employer had to give you in writing. The payslip is the single most useful document in an employment dispute; this lesson teaches you to make it speak.

## Why it matters

The Wage Protection Law 1958 is the procedural backbone of the wage relationship: when wages are due, what may be deducted, what the payslip must show, and what happens if pay is late. The Notice to Employee Law 2002 adds the duty to give written terms at the start of employment and whenever they change. Together they give an employee powerful presumptions: if the employer kept no records, issued no payslip or never gave written terms, the burden of proof moves to the employer. Most self-represented claims that succeed in the labour court are built on these two laws.

## The law

### When wages are due, and when they are "late"

> שכר עבודה המשתלם על בסיס של חודש ישולם עם תום החודש בעדו הוא משתלם.

> ”היום הקובע“ – היום התשיעי שלאחר המועד לתשלום שכר העבודה [...]

*Gist:* A monthly wage is due at the end of the month; it becomes "late" (מולן) if unpaid by the ninth day after that, which in practice means the 9th of the following month. ([Wage Protection Law ss. 9, 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_השכר)) A departing employee's last wage is due on the same date as if they had stayed (s. 12).

### Late-wage compensation

> לשכר מולן יווסף הסכום הגבוה מבין אלה (להלן – פיצוי הלנת שכר): (1) בעד השבוע הראשון שלאחר המועד לתשלום שכר העבודה – החלק העשרים מהשכר המולן, ובעד כל שבוע או חלק משבוע שלאחריו – החלק העשירי מהשכר המולן; (2) הפרשי הצמדה [...] בתוספת 20% [...] בעד כל חודש [...]

*Gist:* Late wages carry the higher of (1) 5% for the first week and 10% for every further week or part of a week, or (2) CPI linkage plus 20% a month. (s. 17) The compensation is large on purpose, which is why courts often reduce it (below).

> הזכות לפיצויי הלנת שכר, להבדיל משכר עבודה, תתיישן אם לא הוגשה תובענה [...] תוך שנה מהיום שבו רואים את השכר כמולן, או תוך 60 ימים מהיום שקיבל העובד את השכר שבו קשור הפיצוי, הכל לפי המוקדם [...]

*Gist:* The right to late-wage compensation (not the wage itself) expires unless you sue within one year of the wage becoming late, or within 60 days of receiving the late wage, whichever is earlier (the court may extend 60 to 90 days); three late payments within 12 months extend the period to three years. (s. 17א) The court may reduce or cancel the compensation for an honest mistake, circumstances beyond the employer's control, or a genuine dispute, provided the undisputed part was paid on time (s. 18). Late severance pay has its own regime: late after 15 days, linkage only to day 30, then linkage plus 20% a month (s. 20).

### The payslip

> מעסיק חייב לנהל פנקס שכר ולמסור לכל עובד, בכתב, תלוש שכר [...]
> (ב) פרטי השכר בפנקס השכר ובתלוש השכר יכללו את הפרטים המנויים בתוספת.
> (ג) הרישום בפנקס השכר ומסירת תלוש השכר לעובד ייעשו לא יאוחר מהיום הקובע.

*Gist:* Every employer (except an individual employing someone outside a business, for example in the home) must keep a payroll and give each employee a written payslip with the Schedule's details by the determining day. (s. 24) The Schedule lists nine groups: (1) employee and employer identity; (2) start date and seniority; (3) job percentage or pay basis and grade; (4) the pay period, working days and hours in the workplace and actually worked, leave taken and balance, sick days used and balance; (5) the hourly value, regular wage and every other payment (overtime, recuperation, leave pay, sick pay) with its quantity and rate, and the totals subject to income tax, National Insurance and pension; (6) every deduction by type; (7) the employer's own payments for social rights, including pension contributions; (8) gross, net and the bank account paid into; (9) the current monthly and hourly minimum wage.

> מצא בית הדין לעבודה כי המעסיק לא מסר לעובדו, ביודעין, תלוש שכר [...] רשאי הוא לפסוק לעובד פיצויים שאינם תלויים בנזק (להלן – פיצויים לדוגמה), בשל כל תלוש שכר שלגביו פעל המעסיק כאמור;

*Gist:* For each payslip knowingly not given, or given without the required details, the labour court may award exemplary damages without proof of loss, up to a statutory ceiling linked to the CPI (s. 26א(ב); amount in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); two breaches within 24 months create a presumption that the breach was knowing.

### Deductions and presumptions

> לא ינוכו משכר עבודה אלא סכומים אלה: (1) סכום שחובה לנכותו, או שמותר לנכותו על פי חיקוק; [...] (6) חוב על פי התחייבות בכתב מהעובד למעסיק, בתנאי שלא ינוכה על חשבון חוב כאמור יותר מרבע שכר העבודה;

*Gist:* Only listed deductions are allowed: those required or permitted by law (tax, National Insurance, health tax), consented donations, union dues, disciplinary fines under a collective agreement, pension contributions, a written debt (no more than a quarter of the wage) and advances; on leaving, the employer may set off a remaining debt from the last wage. (s. 25) Deducting "damage" the employee allegedly caused, without a written undertaking, is unlawful.

> [...] תהא חובת ההוכחה על המעסיק כי העובד לא עמד לרשות העבודה במשך שעות העבודה השנויות במחלוקת, אם המעסיק לא הציג רישומי נוכחות [...]

*Gist:* In a claim for overtime or rest-day pay, if the employer does not produce attendance records it was obliged to keep, it must prove the employee was not working the disputed hours, up to 15 overtime hours a week or 60 a month. If no payslip (or one missing these items) was given, a claim for overtime, rest-day pay, leave, recuperation or travel is presumed to involve an unlawful "global" wage. (s. 26ב)

### Written terms of employment

> מעסיק ימסור לעובד, לא יאוחר משלושים ימים מהיום שהעובד התחיל לעבוד אצלו [...] הודעה בכתב שבה יפרט את תנאי העבודה של העובד לפי הוראות חוק זה

*Gist:* Within 30 days of starting (seven for a youth), the employer must give written notice of the terms: identity, start date and fixed term if any, job, direct manager, all wage payments and pay dates, working day or week, weekly rest day, social-benefit payments and the bodies they are paid to, and any union or collective agreement. ([Notice to Employee Law ss. 1, 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הודעה_לעובד_ולמועמד_לעבודה_(תנאי_עבודה_והליכי_מיון_וקבלה_לעבודה))) Changes require notice within 30 days, unless they appear on the payslip or follow from the law (s. 3). For a knowing failure the labour court may award exemplary damages up to a CPI-linked ceiling (s. 5(ב); [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), and where no notice was given the burden of proving the disputed terms may shift to the employer (s. 5א, ⚠️ section text not quoted).

## Leading cases

### ע״ע 44405-10-15 ו־ע״ע 28480-02-16 (בית הדין הארצי לעבודה, 2018), ״פסק דין הטיפים״

Two appeals by restaurant waiters were heard together by an expanded bench of the National Labour Court presided over by President Yigal Plitman. Regional courts had split on whether tips are part of wages. The Court held that in restaurants, tips are income of the restaurant intended to pay the wages of the service staff; the tips component therefore counts as wages for social rights and National Insurance purposes, however it was paid, and an employer may use tips towards the minimum wage only if they are properly recorded as wages on the payslip. The ruling took effect on 1 January 2019.

Why it matters: it shows the labour courts deciding what "wage" means by substance, not by the way money changes hands, and it changed payslips across the restaurant sector. [Full text (CapiTax PDF)](https://www.capitax.co.il/Attachments/44405-10-15.pdf) (⚠️ unofficial host; parties' names not confirmed).

For the general approach to classifying pay components (a component paid for normal work is wage, a true refund of expenses is not), the courts follow a substantive test going back to the National Labour Court's 1979 ruling in Israel Ports Authority v. Mika (דב״ע לט/3-56); no free full text was found, so read it through later cases.

## How it works in practice

### Reading a payslip in five passes

1. **Identity and seniority (items 1–3).** Is the start date right? Seniority drives leave, recuperation and notice.
2. **Time (item 4).** Do the days and hours worked match your record? Are leave and sick balances moving correctly?
3. **Pay (item 5).** Is overtime shown with hours and rate, or hidden in a "global" line? Is recuperation paid in the month it is due?
4. **Deductions and employer payments (items 6–7).** Are pension contributions shown, both employee and employer, and to which fund? Any deduction without a legal basis?
5. **Net and minimum (items 8–9).** Was the net paid into your account by the 9th? Is the minimum wage stated?

### Cross-checks outside the payslip

- **Pension fund statement.** Compare the employer's reported contributions with the fund's annual or quarterly report; missing deposits are wage debts (lesson 17.4).
- **National Insurance.** Your personal area on the NII site shows the employer's reported wages per month.
- **Form 106.** The annual tax summary should match the 12 payslips.

### Fees

Claims under the Wage Protection Law (including late-wage compensation and payslip damages) and the Minimum Wage Law are exempt from court fees (Labour Court Fees Regulations, reg. 4(15), 4(18)). Claims under other laws pay a fee, often a percentage of the claim with a minimum; check [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Worked example

Rivka Levi, 52, a bookkeeper in Bnei Brak, was paid her ₪9,000 June 2026 wage on 20 July. Her employer has never given her written terms, and her payslips show "salary ₪9,000" with no hours, no leave balance and no pension line, although her fund statement shows deposits.

1. **Late wage.** Due 30 June; determining day 9 July; paid 20 July. Option (1): first week (to 7 July) 5%, then the second and third weeks or parts (to 20 July) 10% each: 25% of ₪9,000 = ₪2,250. Option (2): linkage plus 20% a month for about 20 days ≈ 13% ≈ ₪1,200. The higher, ₪2,250, is the statutory compensation. (This counts from the due date, as the words of s. 17 say; ⚠️ calculations that start only from the determining day give a lower figure, about 15%, so claim the statutory method and expect the employer to argue for the other.)
2. **Deadline.** She received the wage on 20 July 2026, so she must sue for the compensation within 60 days (by 18 September 2026), or the court may extend to 90 days. After that only the wage itself, now paid, would remain.
3. **Payslip.** Items 4, 5 and 7 are missing (hours, leave and sick balances, employer pension payments). If she claims, she may ask for exemplary damages per payslip; two months in 24 create a presumption of knowledge.
4. **Written terms.** None in three years: a separate head under the Notice to Employee Law s. 5.
5. **Strategy.** A court is likely to reduce the late-wage compensation if this was a one-off delay with the undisputed amount paid soon after (s. 18). Rivka writes first, asks for corrected payslips and written terms, and keeps the 60-day deadline in her diary.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Three consecutive payslips of your own (or Rivka's facts), your bank statement for the same months, and your pension fund statement.
- The payslip checklist in the [payslip and final-settlement worksheet](../../templates/payslip-and-final-settlement-worksheet.md).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Nine items (15 min).** Tick each of the nine Schedule groups on each payslip; list what is missing.
2. **Dates (5 min).** For each month note the day the net reached your account; mark any month paid after the 9th.
3. **Late-wage arithmetic (10 min).** For a late month, compute both options of s. 17 and the deadline under s. 17א.
4. **Deductions (5 min).** For each deduction write its legal basis from s. 25; mark any without one.
5. **Cross-check (10 min).** Compare pension contributions with the fund statement.

### Template

[Payslip and final-settlement worksheet](../../templates/payslip-and-final-settlement-worksheet.md), part A (payslip check).

### How you know it worked

- You can point to the Schedule item behind every defect you list.
- Your late-wage figure uses the higher of the two methods and states the last day to sue.
- Every deduction has a section of s. 25 next to it, or is flagged.

- [ ] I can compute both methods of late-wage compensation.
- [ ] I know the 60-day and one-year limits in s. 17א.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. A wage claim is the classic self-represented labour-court case: the procedure regulations provide standard forms for wage, severance and benefit claims (reg. 13), and the court is not bound by the rules of evidence (Labour Court Law s. 32). Small claims up to the expedited-hearing ceiling go to a single fast hearing (lesson 17.9).

### Where and how

- Written demand to the employer first: corrected payslips, written terms, the late-wage compensation.
- Then the regional labour court; no fee for Wage Protection and Minimum Wage claims (reg. 4); other heads: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: the Ministry of Labour's wage-enforcement unit takes complaints about payslips and late wages (administrative fines); Kol-Zchut's payslip guide (⚠️ manual check); workers' rights NGOs.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The employer is insolvent or in liquidation (wage claims then go through the liquidator and the NII's insolvency benefit, a specialised route).
- The employer answers with a counterclaim for damage you allegedly caused.
- Large sums depend on whether a component (car, phone, bonus) is "wage" for pension and severance.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accepting a late wage and waiting months to sue | The compensation is time-barred after 60 days | Sue at once for the compensation; ask the court to extend to 90 days | Diary 60 days from every late payment |
| Claiming only the late-wage percentage the employer "offered" | Under-claiming | Compute both s. 17 methods | Use the worksheet |
| Ignoring missing pension lines | Missing deposits discovered years later | Ask the fund and the employer in writing | Check the fund report each quarter |
| Letting the employer deduct "damage" | Unlawful deduction stands | Demand repayment citing s. 25 | Sign no open-ended undertakings |
| Throwing away payslips | Weak evidence | Ask for copies from the payroll register | Keep every payslip and the 106 form |

## Review

- A monthly wage is due at the month's end and late from the 9th of the next month (ss. 9, 1).
- Late-wage compensation: the higher of 5% then 10% a week, or linkage plus 20% a month; sue within 60 days of receiving the wage (s. 17א).
- The payslip must contain the nine Schedule groups by the determining day; exemplary damages per defective payslip (s. 26א).
- Only listed deductions are lawful (s. 25); missing records shift the burden to the employer (s. 26ב).
- Written terms within 30 days of starting and of any change (Notice to Employee Law ss. 1–3).
