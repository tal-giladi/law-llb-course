# 17.6 · Collective labour law: unions, collective agreements and strikes

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find the collective agreements and extension orders that govern your own job, explain how a union becomes the representative body that may sign them, and judge whether a strike is protected. Even if you never join a union, extension orders built from collective agreements set some of your rights, such as pension, recuperation and travel pay.

## Why it matters

Israeli labour law has two layers. The individual layer (lessons 17.2–17.5) gives every employee a statutory floor. The collective layer lets a representative workers' organisation bargain above that floor, and lets the Minister extend general agreements to the whole economy. About a quarter of employees are covered directly by collective agreements (⚠️ estimate, no source opened), but almost all are covered by extension orders. The right to organise and to strike is protected, but strikes are also among the most contested questions in Israeli public law, because they can paralyse public services and because some are aimed at government policy rather than the employer.

## The law

### What a collective agreement is and whom it binds

> הסכם קיבוצי הוא הסכם בין מעסיק או ארגון מעבידים לבין ארגון עובדים שנעשה והוגש לרישום לפי חוק זה, בעניני קבלת אדם לעבודה או סיום עבודתו, תנאי עבודה, יחסי עבודה, זכויות וחובות של הארגונים בעלי ההסכם, או בחלק מענינים אלה.

*Gist:* A collective agreement is made between an employer (or employers' organisation) and a workers' organisation and submitted for registration; it may cover hiring, dismissal, terms and the parties' mutual duties. ([Collective Agreements Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסכמים_קיבוציים)) A special agreement binds all employees of the covered types at the employer, union members or not (s. 15).

> הוראות שבהסכם קיבוצי בדבר תנאי עבודה, סיום עבודה, וחובות אישיות [...] יראו אותן כחוזה עבודה בין כל מעסיק וכל עובד שעליהם חל ההסכם, ותקפן אף לאחר פקיעת תקפו של ההסכם הקיבוצי [...]; השתתפות בשביתה לא יראו כהפרת חובה אישית.

*Gist:* Personal provisions take effect as part of each employee's contract and survive the agreement's expiry until lawfully changed; joining a strike is not a breach of personal duty. (s. 19) Rights under a collective agreement cannot be waived by the individual (s. 20).

### The representative union

> ארגון יציג של עובדים לענין הסכם קיבוצי מיוחד הוא ארגון העובדים שעם חבריו נמנה המספר הגדול ביותר של עובדים מאורגנים שעליהם יחול ההסכם [...] ובלבד שמספר זה אינו פחות משליש כלל העובדים שעליהם יחול ההסכם.

*Gist:* The representative union for a special agreement is the one with the most organised members among the covered employees, and at least one third of all of them. (s. 3)

### Extension orders

> שר העבודה רשאי, ביזמתו הוא או לפי בקשת בעל הסכם קיבוצי כללי, להרחיב, בצו, היקף תחולתה של כל הוראה שבהסכם קיבוצי כללי (להלן – צו הרחבה) [...]

*Gist:* The Minister may extend provisions of a general collective agreement by order to employers and employees outside it. (s. 25) Mandatory pension, recuperation pay, travel refunds and the 42-hour week all come from extension orders.

### Organising is protected

> לכל עובד הזכות לפעול למען התארגנות עובדים בועד עובדים ובארגון עובדים, להיות חבר בועד עובדים ובארגון עובדים ולפעול במסגרתם.

*Gist:* Every employee may organise and be active in a workers' committee or union (s. 33ח); an employer may not dismiss, worsen terms or refuse to hire because of union membership or activity, or non-membership (s. 33י), and may not bar a union representative from the workplace, subject to work needs and privacy (s. 33ט).

### Strikes: notice

> [...] חייב צד לסכסוך למסור הודעה כאמור באותו סעיף לצד שני ולממונה הראשי על כל שביתה או על כל השבתה, לפי הענין, חמישה עשר יום לפחות לפני תחילתן.

*Gist:* A strike or lockout needs at least 15 days' notice to the other side and to the Chief Labour Relations Officer. ([Settlement of Labour Disputes Law s. 5א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יישוב_סכסוכי_עבודה)) In the public services further limits apply (ss. 37א–37ב on unprotected strikes). Collective disputes go to the labour courts (Labour Court Law s. 24 and s. 25), which often decide strike injunctions within hours.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 1074/93 היועץ המשפטי לממשלה נ׳ בית הדין הארצי לעבודה (10.4.1995)

The Histadrut called a general strike at Bezeq against a government plan to open the telephone market to competition, fearing for employees' jobs. A regional labour court enjoined the strike; the National Labour Court lifted the injunction. The Supreme Court granted the Attorney General's petition and divided strikes into economic (against the employer about terms: protected), political (against government policy with no direct link to terms: not protected) and quasi-political (against government policy that directly affects terms: protected only for a short protest). Harm to Bezeq employees was not shown to be clear and immediate, so the extended strike was at most quasi-political, and the injunction was right.

Why it matters: the classification still frames every strike over privatisation, budget cuts or reform. Supporters see it as protecting democratic decision-making from sectoral pressure; critics say it narrows the right to strike when the State is the real decision-maker. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/67)

### בג״ץ 1181/03 אוניברסיטת בר־אילן נ׳ בית הדין הארצי לעבודה (28.4.2011)

The question was whether a union may strike over the rights of its retired members, with whom the employer has no current employment relationship. The Supreme Court (Procaccia J., six justices concurring) denied the petition: the right to strike has a high normative status, a union that may represent pensioners may in principle use a strike as a sanction, and such a strike can be a protected economic strike, subject to legal limits; the union cannot take away pension rights already fixed at retirement. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/bar-ilan-university-v-national-labor-court)

### עס״ק (ארצי) 25476-09-12 הסתדרות העובדים הכללית החדשה נ׳ פלאפון תקשורת בע״מ (2.1.2013)

During an initial organising campaign at Pelephone, management spoke against the union. The National Labour Court (President Arad) held that during initial organising an employer may not express itself, directly or indirectly, against the organising; the Supreme Court refused to intervene (בג״ץ 4179/13, 2014, ⚠️ date not confirmed). Why it matters: it fuelled a decade of organising in previously non-union sectors (mobile phones, insurance, credit cards). Critics argue it limits the employer's freedom of expression more than other systems do. (⚠️ no free full text confirmed; see the Hebrew Wikipedia article "פסק דין הסתדרות העובדים הכללית החדשה נגד פלאפון תקשורת בע"מ".)

### בג״ץ 1893/11 איגוד חברות השמירה נ׳ בית הדין הארצי לעבודה (30.8.2015)

The Court (Barak-Erez J.) held that the class-action bar for unionised workplaces does not apply where the collective agreement has no enforcement mechanism and the union is not actually enforcing workers' rights. Why it matters: it connects the collective and individual layers, letting workers in a nominally unionised workplace bring a class action for unpaid rights. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/211)

## How it works in practice

### Which agreements apply to you?

1. **Ask.** Your written terms must name the union and any collective agreement that covers you (Notice to Employee Law s. 2(א)(9), lesson 17.3).
2. **Search.** The Ministry of Labour's collective agreements database lists registered agreements and extension orders (⚠️ manual check of the current address on gov.il; earlier links used workagreements.labor.gov.il).
3. **Sector orders.** Some sectors (construction, security, cleaning, cafés and restaurants, metal and electricity) have sector extension orders with higher wages or extra rights.
4. **Read the personal provisions.** Wages, increments, leave beyond the statute, dismissal procedures (often a joint committee), and severance above the statute.

### If you want to organise

Contact a union (the Histadrut, Koach LaOvdim, or a sector union). Membership forms are signed individually; the union counts towards one third. Record any employer pressure, which may breach s. 33י and the Pelephone rule.

### If your workplace is on strike

You need not work during a lawful strike, and joining it is not a breach of your contract (s. 19), but you are not paid for strike days. If you are not a union member and do not strike, the employer must let you work where possible.

## Worked example

Workers at a chain of 30 pharmacies in the north, about 400 employees, most of them Arab and Druze pharmacists and assistants, sign up with a union. After 150 sign, a regional manager sends an email telling staff that "a union will cost you your bonuses".

1. **Representative status.** 150 of 400 is more than a third (134), so if no other union has more members the union is representative (s. 3) and the employer must negotiate (s. 33ח1, ⚠️ text not quoted).
2. **The email.** During initial organising, an employer statement against the union is prohibited under Pelephone; the union can ask the labour court for an injunction and damages.
3. **Individual protection.** A pharmacist moved to a worse shift after joining may claim under s. 33י, which covers worsening of terms.
4. **Strike.** If negotiations fail, the union must give 15 days' notice (s. 5א) before a strike, which would be an economic, protected strike.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your written terms of employment or payslip (or the pharmacy case).
- Internet access to the Ministry's agreements database (⚠️ manual check) and to Wikisource.
- 30 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Union and agreement (10 min).** From your written terms or HR, find whether a special or general agreement covers you.
2. **Extension orders (10 min).** List the extension orders that give you rights (pension, recuperation, travel, sector order).
3. **One right above the statute (10 min).** Find one personal provision that gives you more than the statute, and quote it.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- You can name the agreement or order and its date.
- You found at least one right that comes from the collective layer.

## Without a lawyer

Not a filing lesson for most learners: collective disputes are conducted by unions and employers' organisations, which may represent their members in the labour court (Labour Court Law s. 34) and in labour arbitration (Bar Association Law s. 21). An individual can act alone to enforce a personal provision of a collective agreement as part of a wage claim (lesson 17.10), or to claim under s. 33י for being harmed because of union activity.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly: individual claims yes; collective disputes through the union.

### Where and how

- Individual claims: the regional labour court (lesson 17.9 for procedure).
- Organising: a union of your choice; the Ministry's Chief Labour Relations Officer for dispute notices.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are dismissed or threatened during an organising campaign: injunctions are urgent and union lawyers usually handle them.
- You are a committee member facing disciplinary action.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assuming no union means no collective rights | Extension-order rights missed | Check the extension orders for your sector | Search the agreements database |
| Waiving a collective right in a personal contract | The waiver is void, but you may not know your rights | Claim the collective right | Read s. 20 |
| Treating every strike against the government as protected | Injunction and possible liability | Ask whether the strike is economic | Learn the 1074/93 classes |
| Not recording employer pressure during organising | Hard to prove breach | Save emails and messages | Keep a dated log |

## Review

- A collective agreement's personal provisions become part of each covered employee's contract and survive expiry (s. 19); they cannot be waived (s. 20).
- A representative union has the most members and at least a third of covered employees (s. 3).
- Extension orders spread general agreements to the whole economy (s. 25): pension, recuperation, travel.
- Organising is protected (ss. 33ח–33י); under Pelephone an employer may not campaign against initial organising.
- Strikes need 15 days' notice (s. 5א); political strikes are unprotected, quasi-political strikes protected only briefly (1074/93), and strikes for pensioners can be protected (1181/03).
