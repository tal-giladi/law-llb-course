# 21.3 · Structuring a legal paper and its argument

By the end of this lesson you will be able to build the skeleton of a 15-page legal paper: an introduction that tells the reader your question, your answer and how you will get there; a body in which each section is one step of the argument; a method you can defend; and a conclusion that says what follows. You will leave with a full outline of your own paper.

## Why it matters

A legal paper is an argument, not a report. The reader, usually a busy supervisor with forty papers, should know on page one what you claim and why it matters, and should be able to follow the steps without rereading. Structure is also how you think: when you cannot write the heading of the next section, you have found a gap in your argument. The research memo of lesson [02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md) was built for a decision ("what should I do?"); the paper is built for a claim ("the law is, or should be, X, because…"). The skeleton below is the one Israeli law journals use, and it is what proseminar supervisors expect.

## The law

A paper's structure is not regulated, but a doctrinal argument follows the hierarchy of sources that Israeli courts themselves follow. Two provisions set that order.

### The order of sources (Foundations of Law Act s. 1)

> ראה בית המשפט שאלה משפטית הטעונה הכרעה, ולא מצא לה תשובה בדבר חקיקה, בהלכה פסוקה או בדרך של היקש, יכריע בה לאור עקרונות החירות, הצדק, היושר והשלום של המשפט העברי ומורשת ישראל.

*Gist:* A court looks first to legislation, then to case law, then to analogy; only where none answers the question does it decide in light of the principles of freedom, justice, equity and peace of Jewish law and the Jewish heritage. ([Foundations of Law Act 1980 s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יסודות_המשפט))

### Binding precedent (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20)

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* A ruling guides lower courts, and a Supreme Court ruling binds every court except the Supreme Court itself. ([Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה))

So a doctrinal section starts with the statute, then the binding Supreme Court rulings, then lower-court rulings and analogies, and only then the literature and principle. A paper that leads with a district-court ruling, or with an article, when a statute answers the question, has its structure upside down. How courts read the statute itself is lesson [02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md).

## Leading cases

Three published papers show the three main structures. Read their tables of contents: that alone teaches a great deal.

### ניבין עמנואל ואורן גזל-אייל "מאסר על תנאי – הקשר בין אורכו לסיכוי שהוא יופעל" דין ודברים יח 107 (התשפ"ה)

The classic empirical structure, visible in its table of contents: א. מבוא; ב. רקע (the law of suspended sentences, minimum sentences, then "research questions" and "research hypotheses"); ג. שיטת המחקר (design, sample, variables); ד. ממצאים (descriptive then inferential statistics); ה. דיון; ו. מסקנות. Why it matters: the questions and hypotheses are stated before any data, so the findings test them rather than being fitted to them afterwards. [Free PDF](https://law.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/din18-02-gazal_emanuel.pdf)

### Elkin-Koren & Netanel, "Transplanting Fair Use Across the Globe", 72 Hastings L.J. 1121 (2021)

A comparative and empirical structure: Part I compares open fair use with closed lists of exceptions; Part II sets out the opposing position (United States and industry opposition to exporting fair use); Part III tells the Israeli story (how courts and the 2007 reform moved from fair dealing to fair use); Part IV is the empirical study (method, then results); then a conclusion. Why it matters: the opposing view gets its own Part before the authors answer it, which is the strongest way to handle a counter-argument. [Free PDF](https://www.uclawjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/72.4-NetanelElkin-Koren.pdf)

### דור פסקא ורונן קריטנשטיין "ההצדקה הנורמטיבית לשלילת הדוקטרינה החוזית: ההתחייבות לתת מתנה כמשל" דין ודברים יח 165 (התשפ"ה)

A normative structure in two announced stages: first, theories that identify the mismatch between contract doctrine and the gift; second, an explanation of the mechanism by which applying the doctrine undermines the institution. Why it matters: the abstract tells the reader exactly what each stage will do, which is the roadmap technique in its purest form. [Free PDF](https://law.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/din18-03-paska-kritenshtein.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### The skeleton of a 15-page proseminar paper

| Part | Pages | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| א. מבוא | 1–1.5 | Hook (a case, a fact, a puzzle); the question; the thesis in one or two sentences; why it matters and what is new; the roadmap |
| ב. המסגרת הנורמטיבית | 2–3 | The law as it is: statute, binding rulings, the main lines in the literature. Only what the argument needs |
| ג.–ד. The argument | 6–8 | Two or three sections, each one step of the argument, each with its own heading that states a claim |
| ה. Counter-arguments | 1.5–2 | The strongest objections, stated fairly, and your answers |
| ו. Implications or proposal | 1–1.5 | What should change: a reading, a rule, a reform, with its costs |
| ז. סיכום | 0.5–1 | The answer to the question; limits; further research |

Footnotes come on top of these pages. Check your course's page, font and spacing rules.

### The introduction in five moves

1. **Hook:** "In 2023 the Tel Aviv University disciplinary officer convicted a first-year student who had used ChatGPT in two assignments…"
2. **Question:** "This paper asks whether…"
3. **Thesis:** "I argue that…" Use the first person if your faculty allows it; Israeli journals increasingly do ("במאמר זה אטען כי").
4. **Contribution:** what the literature has not yet said, in one or two sentences with footnotes to the closest existing work.
5. **Roadmap:** "חלק ב מציג… חלק ג טוען… חלק ד בוחן טענות נגד… חלק ה מציע…"

If you can only write the introduction after the body, that is fine: most authors do. But write a draft first, because it forces you to state the thesis.

### Headings that argue

Weak: "ג. הפסיקה". Strong: "ג. הפסיקה מאז 2013 מתעלמת ממבחן המטרה". A reader who reads only your headings should get your argument.

### Choosing a method, and saying so

- **Doctrinal.** Read the statute and rulings closely, synthesise them into a rule, and expose inconsistencies. Limits: you describe what courts say, not what they do in unreported cases.
- **Comparative.** Choose systems for a reason (a common origin such as English common law, a legal transplant, or a contrasting solution). Compare how each solves the same functional problem, not just their labels. Limits: rules work inside institutions; a transplant may behave differently (this is exactly Elkin-Koren and Netanel's question).
- **Empirical.** State hypotheses, the data set and how cases were coded before the results (lesson [11.4](../module-11/lesson-04.md)). Limits: selection effects, missing data, correlation not causation.
- **Theoretical or normative.** Use one or two theories (module 9: positivism, Dworkin, law and economics, critical theories) as lenses, and apply them to the Israeli material. Limits: say which values you assume.

Put a short "method and limits" paragraph at the end of the introduction or the start of Part ב.

### Building each section

Each section is a claim with support: claim, legal source, application, the best objection, reply, mini-conclusion that leads to the next section. Each paragraph begins with a topic sentence that states its point. Signpost transitions: "מכאן עולה…", "עם זאת…", "טענה זו מעוררת שני קשיים".

### Counter-arguments and neutrality

State the strongest version of the opposing view, ideally in the words of its best proponent, with a footnote. If your paper touches a contested public question (judicial review, religion and state, the 2023–2026 dispute over the judiciary), set out each side's best legal argument and the current state of the law before you argue; supervisors grade the argument, not the side.

## Worked example

Hila Mizrahi, a student from Sderot, has the question: "Should university disciplinary rules treat undisclosed use of generative AI as 'cheating' (הונאה) or as 'breach of instructions'?" Her provisional thesis: "As breach of instructions, unless the student presented AI text as her own work in a course that banned it."

Her outline:

- **א. מבוא.** Hook: TAU disciplinary ruling ד30-2023, where a cheating count was dropped in a plea agreement because rules on AI had not yet been settled. Question, thesis, contribution (her search found no Israeli article comparing the universities' AI rules; she says so cautiously), roadmap. Method: doctrinal and comparative across three Israeli universities.
- **ב. המסגרת הנורמטיבית.** TAU disciplinary rules ss. 29.3 and 29.4; Bar-Ilan rule 4(ה); the universities' AI guides; Copyright Law s. 19 (only as background).
- **ג. Two models of AI use in the rules: "another person's help" (Bar-Ilan) versus "course-by-course permission" (TAU).**
- **ד. Why the line between cheating and breach should turn on misrepresentation, not on the tool.**
- **ה. Counter-arguments.** Deterrence requires treating all undisclosed use as cheating; students cannot be trusted to self-report; detection is unreliable either way. Replies.
- **ו. Proposal.** A disclosure form in every course (as Bar-Ilan already uses), and a rule that undisclosed use where the course allowed it is breach of instructions.
- **ז. סיכום.**

Each heading of ג and ד states a claim. The counter-arguments section gives the deterrence view its strongest form.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your question, thesis and literature map from lessons 21.1 and 21.2.
- The [proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md), part C.
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Read the tables of contents of the three model papers above (10 minutes).
2. Write your thesis in one sentence, then list the two or three steps needed to prove it (10 minutes).
3. Turn each step into a section heading that states a claim (10 minutes).
4. Under each heading, list the sources from your map that support it and the strongest objection (15 minutes).
5. Write the method-and-limits paragraph (10 minutes).
6. Draft the introduction in five moves, under one page (20 minutes).

### Template

[Proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md), part C (skeleton and introduction).

### How you know it worked

- Reading only your headings, a friend can state your thesis.
- Every source on your literature map is attached to a section, or you have decided to drop it.
- The counter-arguments section names a real author or ruling for each objection.
- Your introduction fits on one page and ends with a roadmap.

### Self-check

- [ ] The legal framework starts with the statute and binding rulings.
- [ ] My method is named and its limits are stated.
- [ ] Every argument heading is a claim, not a topic.
- [ ] The strongest counter-argument is in the outline, with a reply.
- [ ] Page allocation adds up to about 15 pages.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Structure is a writing skill, and it is the skill that most improves arguments you write to a court in your own case. Courts' procedure rules require pleadings to be concise and orderly (lesson [15.2](../module-15/lesson-02.md)), and judges read summations and appeals the way supervisors read papers: looking for the claim, then the support.

### Where and how

Use the same skeleton for written summations or an appeal: a short opening that states what you ask and why; the legal framework, statute first; numbered arguments, each under a heading that states the point; the other side's best argument and your answer; the relief requested. The [appeal outline](../../templates/appeal-outline.md) and [written motion](../../templates/written-motion.md) templates follow this order.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Your outline shows that the case turns on a contested point of law with no clear statute or binding ruling, and the stakes are high, or the other side is represented and the court has set strict page limits you cannot meet without dropping arguments.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis hidden on page 9 | Reader does not know what you are arguing | Move it to the first page of the introduction | Write the five-move introduction first |
| Background takes half the paper | No room for the argument | Cut to what the argument uses | Limit Part ב to 3 pages |
| Topic headings ("הפסיקה") | The argument is invisible | Rewrite each heading as a claim | Draft headings from the thesis steps |
| Straw-man counter-arguments | Supervisor sees a one-sided paper | State the best objection, cited | Ask who would disagree and quote them |
| Method never stated | Claims look unsupported; limits ignored | Add a method-and-limits paragraph | Include it in the outline |

## Review

- A paper is an argument: question, thesis, contribution and roadmap on page one.
- The doctrinal framework follows the hierarchy of sources: statute, binding rulings, analogy, then principle and literature.
- Choose the method to fit the question, name it and state its limits.
- Headings state claims; each section is one step; the strongest objection is answered.
- The same skeleton makes your own court arguments clearer.
