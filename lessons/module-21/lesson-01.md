# 21.1 · Choosing a research question

By the end of this lesson you will be able to turn a topic that interests you ("tenants' rights", "AI in courts", "the Law of Return") into one precise research question that a 15-page proseminar paper can actually answer, name the method that question needs, and put both on a one-page proposal you could hand to a supervisor tomorrow.

## Why it matters

Every Israeli law faculty makes second-year students write a first research paper before they are allowed into a seminar. At Tel Aviv University it is the proseminar (פרוסמינר, 3 credits, a condition for the year-3 seminar); Bar-Ilan teaches research methods (999118) in year 2; the Hebrew University has a first-year writing and research course (62121) and then two seminars. The point is the same everywhere: a lawyer, judge or academic must be able to ask a legal question, find what has already been said about it, take a position and defend it in writing.

Most failed papers fail at the first step. The student picks a topic, not a question, reads everything, and writes a survey: twenty pages of "on the one hand, on the other hand" with no claim. A question does the opposite. It tells you what to read, what to leave out, how to structure the paper (lesson [21.3](lesson-03.md)) and when you are finished. In module 2 you wrote a research memo that answered a question about your own affairs ([02.6](../module-02/lesson-06.md)). An academic paper asks a question about the law itself: what it is, whether it is coherent, what it should be, or how it works in practice.

## The law

A research question is not governed by statute, but two rules shape what you may choose and how.

### You may write about an idea someone else had (Copyright Law s. 5)

> זכות יוצרים ביצירה כאמור בסעיף 4 לא תחול על כל אחד מאלה, ואולם על דרך ביטוים תחול זכות היוצרים: (1) רעיון; (2) תהליך ושיטת ביצוע; (3) מושג מתמטי; (4) עובדה או נתון; (5) חדשות היום.

*Gist:* Copyright protects the expression, not the idea, method, fact or datum behind it. ([Copyright Law 2007 s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים))

So nobody owns a research question. You may take a question another scholar asked and answer it differently. What you may not do is present their idea as yours: that is an academic-integrity rule, not a copyright rule (lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md)), and it is why you must cite the article that first asked your question.

### You may not quietly recycle your own earlier work (TAU disciplinary rules s. 29.3.7)

> בידוי נתונים או שימוש בנתונים ממחקר אחר, לרבות מחקר של התלמיד עצמו, מבלי לגלות עובדה זו.

*Gist:* Tel Aviv University treats inventing data, or using data from another study, including the student's own earlier study, without disclosing it, as cheating in a paper. ([TAU Student Disciplinary Rules, instruction 12-002, s. 29.3.7](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/default/files/media_server/General/yoets/12-002.pdf))

If you want to build on a paper you wrote for another course, say so in the proposal and in a footnote, and ask the supervisor. Bar-Ilan's student guide says the same: an earlier paper of your own may be a cited source, never a resubmission (lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md)).

## Leading cases

There are no court rulings on how to choose a research question. The best teachers are good questions in published papers. Both papers below are free on the Haifa faculty's site and appeared in the same 2025 volume.

### ניבין עמנואל ואורן גזל-אייל "מאסר על תנאי – הקשר בין אורכו לסיכוי שהוא יופעל" דין ודברים יח 107 (התשפ"ה)

An empirical question, stated so narrowly that data can answer it: does the length of a suspended sentence affect the chance that a court activates it after a new offence? The authors use a dataset of 5,764 adult defendants in the magistrates' courts and find that the longer the suspended sentence, the less likely the court is to activate it. Why it matters as a model: the abstract states the question in one sentence, the paper lists its hypotheses before the data, and the conclusion is a surprising, testable claim (a heavier suspended sentence often helps the defendant). It also places itself as the third paper in a series, which is how a narrow question earns its significance. [Free PDF](https://law.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/din18-02-gazal_emanuel.pdf)

### דור פסקא ורונן קריטנשטיין "ההצדקה הנורמטיבית לשלילת הדוקטרינה החוזית: ההתחייבות לתת מתנה כמשל" דין ודברים יח 165 (התשפ"ה)

A normative question: should contract doctrine govern institutions built on close personal relationships, with the promise to give a gift as the test case? The authors argue that applying contract doctrine to such institutions undermines their purpose, and draw on several theories to show why. Why it matters as a model: a big question ("is contract the wrong frame?") is made manageable by one example (the gift), which is exactly the narrowing move you need in a 15-page paper. [Free PDF](https://law.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/din18-03-paska-kritenshtein.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### From topic to question: the funnel

1. **Topic** (a field): "small claims".
2. **Issue** (a problem within it): "companies send trained claims officers to small-claims hearings, while consumers come alone".
3. **Question** (one sentence, ending in a question mark): "Does the rule that lawyers appear in small claims only with leave (Courts Law s. 63) still achieve equality of arms when the defendant is a repeat-player company?"
4. **Provisional thesis** (your expected answer, which you may change): "Not fully: the rule removes lawyers but not expertise; courts should use their discretion under s. 63 and the procedure rules to rebalance."

Stop at step 3 until the question passes the tests below.

### Six types of question, six methods

| Type | Asks | Typical method | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive / doctrinal | What is the law on X? | Close reading of statutes and rulings, synthesis | How do courts apply the open list of purposes in Copyright Law s. 19? |
| Critical / analytical | Is the law on X coherent? | Doctrinal analysis that exposes tension between rulings or principles | Are the Supreme Court's tests for good faith in negotiation consistent? |
| Normative | What should the law on X be? | Theory (module 9), policy, values; a proposal | Should a promise to give a gift be enforceable? |
| Empirical | How does the law on X work in practice? | Data: rulings coded, court statistics (lesson [11.4](../module-11/lesson-04.md)), surveys | Are longer suspended sentences activated less often? |
| Comparative | How do other systems solve X, and what can Israel learn? | Two or three systems compared on the same function | How do England and Israel treat quotation in academic works? |
| Historical | How did the law on X develop, and why? | Archives, Knesset protocols, old reports | Why did the 2007 Copyright Law replace the 1911 "fair dealing" exception with an open fair-use clause? |

A proseminar paper is usually doctrinal or critical with a normative conclusion. Empirical and comparative papers are welcome but need more time: check with the supervisor before you commit to coding 200 rulings.

### Six tests for a good proseminar question

- **One sentence.** If it needs "and", it is two questions.
- **Answerable in 15 pages.** "Is Israeli constitutional law legitimate?" is not; "Did the Supreme Court in case X apply its own test from case Y?" is.
- **Genuinely open.** Someone informed could disagree with your answer. If a single statute section answers it, it is a memo question.
- **Sources exist and are free to you.** You can find the statute, the leading rulings and three or four articles (lesson [21.2](lesson-02.md)).
- **It matters to someone.** A court, a legislator, a group of people affected, or the literature.
- **You can state a provisional thesis.** If you cannot guess an answer, you do not yet understand the question.

### Where good questions come from

- A recent Supreme Court ruling with a dissent: the dissent hands you the counter-argument.
- A new statute or amendment: how will courts read it, and does it fit existing doctrine?
- A gap between the law on the books and practice you have seen yourself (your rental, your employer, your local authority).
- The "further research" paragraph at the end of an article.
- A question your own legal affairs raised in module 2, now asked at the level of the law, not your case.

### The proposal

Most supervisors ask for a one-page proposal (הצעת מחקר) and meet you once. It has: a working title, the question, the provisional thesis, why it matters, the method, a rough structure and five to ten starting sources. Expect the supervisor to narrow it further. That is normal; it is the most valuable ten minutes of the course.

## Worked example

Rana Khoury, a second-year student from Haifa, wants to write about "Arabic in the courts". That is a topic.

1. **Issue.** In her own small claim against a mobile operator she noticed that the court forms were in Hebrew and the hearing was in Hebrew, with an interpreter on request. She wonders whether a party who reads Arabic better than Hebrew gets a fair hearing.
2. **First question (too broad):** "Does Israeli law protect the use of Arabic in courts?" Fails the 15-page test: Basic Law: Israel as the Nation State of the Jewish People, the Courts Law, procedure rules and many rulings are all in play.
3. **Narrower:** "Does the right to an interpreter in civil proceedings protect self-represented parties whose first language is Arabic?" Better, but still descriptive.
4. **Final question:** "Do the civil procedure rules on interpreters give a self-represented party a sufficient opportunity to understand written pleadings, as opposed to oral hearings?" One sentence, open, answerable from the procedure rules and a handful of rulings and articles.
5. **Provisional thesis:** "The rules address oral hearings but not documents; courts should read the right to a fair hearing to include a summary translation of key documents for self-represented parties."
6. **Method:** doctrinal (rules and rulings), with a short comparative note on one other system if the supervisor agrees.
7. **Neutrality check.** The question is about procedure for any self-represented party whose first language is not Hebrew (Arabic, Russian, Amharic). The paper will set out the cost and administrative arguments against her proposal at full strength.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A topic from your own interests or from earlier modules.
- The two model papers above (read only their first two pages).
- The proposal part of the [proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write your topic in three words (5 minutes).
2. Write three issues inside it, each in one sentence (10 minutes).
3. For the most interesting issue, write three candidate questions of different types from the table (10 minutes).
4. Run each candidate through the six tests and keep one (10 minutes).
5. Write a provisional thesis of no more than two sentences (5 minutes).
6. Fill in the one-page proposal: title, question, thesis, why it matters, method, five sources you expect to use (you will find them properly in lesson 21.2) (20 minutes).

### Template

[Proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md), part A (the proposal).

### How you know it worked

- Your question is one sentence that ends with a question mark and contains no "and".
- A friend who reads only the question can guess what the paper's sections will be.
- Your thesis is a claim someone could reasonably reject.
- You can name the method in one word from the table.

### Self-check

- [ ] My question is narrower than my topic by at least two steps of the funnel.
- [ ] I can answer it in 15 pages with sources I can reach for free.
- [ ] I wrote a provisional thesis, not a promise to "examine" the issue.
- [ ] If I am reusing a topic from an earlier paper, my proposal says so.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Choosing a research question is an academic skill. It shows up in practice every time you write for a court or authority in your own case: a statement of claim, a defence, an appeal or summations all turn on the question in dispute (השאלה שבמחלוקת). Judges reward parties who state the question narrowly and early, because it tells the court what it has to decide.

### Where and how

Before you draft any court argument of your own, write one sentence: "The question is whether ___." Put it near the top of the argument (lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md) on summations, [15.11](../module-15/lesson-11.md) on appeals). If you need more than one sentence, you have several questions: number them and deal with each in turn.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

A narrow legal question in your own case turns out to be genuinely open in the case law (two lines of rulings, or a recent dissent) and a lot depends on it. Writing a good paper about an open question is an achievement; litigating one alone is a risk. Free help is listed in [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing a topic, not a question | A survey with no claim; low grade | Run the funnel again and write one sentence with a question mark | Do not start reading widely until the question passes the six tests |
| Question too big for 15 pages | Shallow treatment of everything | Narrow by one example, one court, one period or one statute | Ask "could a whole book answer this?"; if yes, narrow |
| Question already settled | Nothing to argue; a memo, not a paper | Move to a critical or normative angle: is the settled rule right? | Check the literature first (lesson 21.2) |
| Empirical question without access to data | Weeks lost; method collapses | Switch to a doctrinal question on the same issue | Confirm data access before the proposal |
| Recycling an earlier paper without saying so | Disciplinary complaint (TAU s. 29.3.7) | Disclose and ask the supervisor | Mention earlier work in the proposal |

## Review

- A topic is a field; a question is one sentence with a question mark that a 15-page paper can answer.
- Match the method to the question: doctrinal, critical, normative, empirical, comparative or historical.
- A good question is narrow, open, sourced, significant, and comes with a provisional thesis.
- Ideas are free (Copyright Law s. 5), but you must credit the scholar who raised your question first, and disclose reuse of your own earlier work.
- The same skill frames every argument you write in your own case: the question in dispute comes first.
