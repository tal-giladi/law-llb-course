# 21.4 · Footnotes and the uniform citation rules in depth

By the end of this lesson you will be able to write every footnote an academic legal paper needs: books, journal articles, chapters in edited volumes, theses, web pages and English-language sources, each in the format of the uniform citation rules, with the right introductory signal and in the right place. Lesson [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md) covered statutes, rulings and repeat citations; this lesson adds the literature and the craft of footnoting.

## Why it matters

In a legal paper the footnotes are half the work. They show the reader where every claim comes from, what you read, how sources relate to your argument, and that you did not take anyone's words or ideas without credit (lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md)). Supervisors check footnotes at random: one wrong page or a source that does not say what you claim costs more marks than a clumsy sentence.

The standard is still the **third edition (2021)** of the uniform citation rules. On 2026-10-17 the rules page at Tel Aviv University still offered the third edition and the short "main rules and examples" file, and announced no newer edition. The rules were written by the editorial boards of four journals (משפטים, משפט וממשל, מחקרי משפט, עיוני משפט), and most Israeli journals and faculties use them. If your course's instructions differ, they win.

## The law

The citation rules are not law, but in academic writing they are the binding standard. Their formulas, quoted from the [third edition](https://law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/izkur/izkur2021pdf.pdf):

### Books (rule 23.1)

> [שם המחבר] [שם הספר] [מספר הכרך] [הפניה ספציפית] ([המהדורה] [שם העורך] [שם המתרגם] [שנת פרסום הספר])

*Gist:* Author, title, volume, pinpoint, then in brackets the edition, editor, translator and year. The rules' example: "אוריאל פרוקצ'יה דיני חברות חדשים בישראל: דין נוהג, דין רצוי והדרך לחקיקה (1989)." Note: no commas between author and title, and the title is not in quotation marks.

### Journal articles (rule 24.1)

> [שם המחבר] "[שם המאמר]" [שם כתב העת] [מספר הכרך][(מספר החוברת)] [עמוד תחילת המאמר][, הפניה ספציפית] ([שנת פרסום המאמר])

*Gist:* Author, article title in quotation marks, journal, volume (Hebrew letters for Hebrew journals), first page, comma and pinpoint, year. Examples from the rules: "רונית לוין-שנור "הפרטה, הפרדה והפליה" עיוני משפט לד 183 (2011)." and, with a pinpoint, "זמר בלונדהיים ונדיב מרדכי "לקראת דוקטרינת אפקט מצבר: אגרגציה בביקורת שיפוטית חוקתית" משפטים מד 569, 584–586 (2014)." Do not write "בעמ'" before the pinpoint (rule 24.8). If the issue gives only a Hebrew year, give it with ה"א; if it gives both, give only the Gregorian year (rule 24.9).

### Online journals (rule 24.10)

Articles in online journals and online platforms of journals (משפטים על אתר, פורום עיוני משפט, הארת דין) are cited exactly like print articles. Example from the rules: "בלהה כהנא "פיצוי בגין קיצור תוחלת החיים ו'השנים האבודות' בתביעות בעילה של הולדה בעוולה" משפטים על אתר ד 1 (2009)."

### Articles in edited books (rule 24.11)

> [שם מחבר המאמר] "[שם המאמר]" [שם מחבר הספר] [שם הספר] [מספר הכרך] [עמוד תחילת המאמר][, הפניה ספציפית] ([המהדורה] [שם העורך] [שם המתרגם] [שנת פרסום הספר])

*Gist:* The chapter author and title, then the book as in rule 23, with the editors in the brackets. Example: "דפנה לוינסון-זמיר "פגיעות עקיפות במקרקעין על-ידי תוכניות" ספר זיכרון לגד טדסקי: מסות במשפט אזרחי 361 (יצחק אנגלרד ואח' עורכים 1995)."

### Theses and course papers (rule 26)

> [שם המחבר] [שם העבודה] [הפניה ספציפית] ([סוג העבודה], [שם המוסד האקדמי שאליו הוגשה העבודה] [שנת ההגשה])

*Gist:* For a paper submitted in a course, add "בקורס" and the course name after the type of work. Example: "ערן נריה החוויה הפרופסיונאלית… (עבודה סמינריונית בקורס אנתרופולוגיה רפואית, האוניברסיטה העברית 2003)." Forthcoming and unpublished works (rule 27) are cited by their type, with "צפוי להתפרסם", "בדפוס" or "לא פורסם" in place of the year.

### Web sources (rule 34.2.1)

> [שם המחבר] "[שם העמוד או התוכן]" [סוג התוכן] [שם האתר] [הפניה ספציפית] ([תאריך]) [כתובת]

*Gist:* Use this only for sources that exist only online (sites, pages, social media, newspaper websites), not for online journals. Example: "טובה צימוקי "פתרון לסחבת במערכת המשפט" ynet (21.12.2017) https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5060004,00.html."

### Foreign sources (rule 35.1)

Sources in English or another language written in non-Hebrew, non-Arabic script are cited by the latest edition of the Bluebook. The rules (2021) named the 21st edition (2020); a newer edition may have appeared since (⚠️ check legalbluebook.com). They are placed in the Hebrew footnote as if they were Hebrew citations, with the footnote's final punctuation at the end (rule 35.3). Example: "Niva Elkin-Koren & Neil Weinstock Netanel, *Transplanting Fair Use Across the Globe: A Case Study Testing the Credibility of U.S. Opposition*, 72 HASTINGS L.J. 1121 (2021)."

### Signals (rule 38.4)

| Signal | Meaning (rule 38.4) |
|---|---|
| (none) | The source says what the text says, or the text quotes it |
| ראו | The text follows clearly from the source; one direct step of reasoning |
| ראו גם | Additional support, after sources cited without a signal |
| השוו ל | The source supports an argument from which you can reason by analogy |
| ראו והשוו | Some of the source supports directly or by inference, some by analogy |
| מנגד | The source contradicts the text |
| אבל ראו | A contradiction follows clearly from the source |
| אבל השוו ל | The source supports an analogy pointing the other way |

Add "למשל" when the sources are only some of many, and "לאחרונה" for a recent source (rule 38.4.4). No colon after the signal. Where the relation is not direct support, explain it in brackets after the citation (rule 38.1.2).

### Placing the mark (rule 38.5)

Put the footnote mark at the end of what it refers to (a quotation, a sentence, a clause, a word). Next to punctuation you may put it before or after, but keep the same choice throughout the paper.

## Leading cases

### ע"א 7996/11 סייפקום בע"מ נ' רביב (18.11.2013)

A copyright dispute over technical drawings copied into a United States patent application. Justice Danziger, for the Court, held that s. 19(a) of the Copyright Law sets out an open list of permitted purposes and applied the four s. 19(b) factors; the use was commercial, not transformative, copied in full and harmful to the market, so it was not fair use (para. 37). Why it matters here: in the same paragraph the Court relied on empirical scholarship on American fair-use cases to say which factors weigh most. Supreme Court judgments cite academic work, with full citations and pinpoints, which is one reason your footnotes must let a reader find the exact page. (⚠️ The Hebrew rendering of the party name is unconfirmed against the Hebrew original.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/safecom-ltd-v-raviv)

### A model of footnoting: Emmanuel and Gazal-Ayal, דין ודברים יח 107 (התשפ"ה)

The article's first footnote (marked **) states that all websites cited were last viewed on 04.12.2024; the second (*) gives the authors' positions and thanks. Its source footnotes cite a Hebrew court-system report with a short name ("להלן: …") and an English article by the Bluebook inside a Hebrew footnote. Why it matters: these are exactly the conventions your paper should follow. [Free PDF](https://law.haifa.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/din18-02-gazal_emanuel.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### What goes in the text and what in the footnote

- **Text:** your argument, the key holdings, and short quotations you analyse.
- **Footnote:** the citation; supporting or contrary sources with signals; a brief side point that would interrupt the argument; a long quotation you do not analyse.
- A footnote that runs for half a page usually belongs in the text or nowhere.

### First mention, then short form

The first time a source appears, cite it in full; if you will cite it again, add a short name: "(להלן: לוין-שנור)". Afterwards: "לוין-שנור, לעיל ה"ש 4, בעמ' 190." "שם" only for the source cited immediately before (rule 37, recap in [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)).

### The access-date footnote

Instead of dating every web citation separately, many papers add a starred first footnote: "כל אתרי האינטרנט המאוזכרים להלן נצפו לאחרונה ב-…". Check that every link still works on that date.

### Tools

Word and Google Docs insert footnotes automatically (Insert, Footnote). Reference managers such as Zotero (free) store your sources but do not produce the Israeli format reliably; check every footnote by hand. If you use an AI tool to format citations, check each one against the source (lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md)): such tools invent page numbers and even whole sources.

## Worked example

Noam Peretz, a student from Kiryat Shmona, drafted five footnotes:

1. ``לוין-שנור, רונית, "הפרטה, הפרדה והפליה", עיוני משפט, כרך 34, עמ' 183, 2011``
2. ``עמנואל ני' וגזל-אייל א', מאסר על תנאי, דין ודברים 18, 2025, בעמ' 110``
3. ``Elkin-Koren and Netanel, Transplanting Fair Use, Hastings Law Journal 2021``
4. ``ראו: ynet, פתרון לסחבת במערכת המשפט``
5. ``שם`` (referring back to footnote 1, after footnote 4)

Corrected:

1. "רונית לוין-שנור "הפרטה, הפרדה והפליה" עיוני משפט לד 183 (2011)." First name first, no commas between author, title and journal, volume in Hebrew letters, year in brackets at the end.
2. "ניבין עמנואל ואורן גזל-אייל "מאסר על תנאי – הקשר בין אורכו לסיכוי שהוא יופעל" דין ודברים יח 107, 110 (התשפ"ה)." Full names in the order printed, full title in quotation marks, first page then pinpoint, no "בעמ'", the year as printed on the issue.
3. "Niva Elkin-Koren & Neil Weinstock Netanel, *Transplanting Fair Use Across the Globe: A Case Study Testing the Credibility of U.S. Opposition*, 72 HASTINGS L.J. 1121 (2021)." Bluebook form (rule 35.1).
4. "ראו טובה צימוקי "פתרון לסחבת במערכת המשפט" ynet (21.12.2017) https://www.ynet.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-5060004,00.html." Author, date and address; no colon after "ראו".
5. "לוין-שנור, לעיל ה"ש 1, בעמ' 190." "שם" cannot jump over footnotes 2 to 4.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Footnotes are an academic convention. Court pleadings usually cite sources in the text or in brackets rather than in footnotes, and courts do not require the uniform rules. But the core habit, a citation from which the reader can find the exact page, is the same in your own court arguments.

### Where and how

When you cite an article or book in a pleading or summations of your own, give the full citation once in the rules' format, quote the short passage you rely on, and give the page. Then use a short name. The citation kit of lesson [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md) covers statutes and rulings.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Citation alone never requires a lawyer. If the other side relies on a source you cannot find, ask for a copy; you are entitled to see what is cited against you.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commas between author, title and journal; "כרך 34, עמ' 183" | Looks careless; not the rules' format | Rewrite to the formula: author "title" journal לד 183 (2011) | Copy the formula into your document's first page |
| Page range instead of first page and pinpoint | Reader cannot tell where the article starts or which page you mean | First page, comma, pinpoint | Record both in the literature map |
| English sources in Hebrew order | Mixed, unfindable citations | Use the Bluebook (rule 35.1) | Keep one Bluebook example to copy |
| Wrong signal ("ראו" for a contrary source) | Misrepresents the source | Use מנגד or אבל ראו | Check the signal table for each footnote |
| AI-formatted footnotes not checked | Invented pages or sources; integrity problem | Verify every footnote against the source | Format by hand or check each one |

## Review

- Books: author, title, volume, pinpoint (edition, editor, year); no quotation marks on the title.
- Articles: author "title" journal volume first page, pinpoint (year); online journals the same.
- Theses, course papers and web sources have their own formulas (rules 26 and 34.2.1); foreign sources follow the Bluebook (rule 35.1).
- Signals (rule 38.4) tell the reader how a source relates to your text: none, ראו, ראו גם, השוו ל, מנגד, אבל ראו.
- Every footnote must lead a reader to the exact page.
