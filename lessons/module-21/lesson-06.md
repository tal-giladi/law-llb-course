# 21.6 · Practice lab: write and self-review the proseminar paper

In this lab you write the whole paper: about 15 pages plus footnotes, on your own research question or on one of two supplied questions, using everything from lessons 21.1 to 21.5. You finish by scoring your draft with the self-review rubric and fixing its weakest part. Peer review and the final revision are in the module project.

## Why it matters

A paper is learned by writing one. The earlier lessons gave you the parts; this lab puts them in sequence, in the order experienced writers actually use: framework and argument first, introduction last, footnotes and integrity checks before anyone else reads it. Plan about twelve hours over at least a week; papers written in one night read like it.

## The law

Two rules from earlier lessons apply to every hour of this lab.

> שימוש הוגן ביצירה מותר למטרות כגון אלה: לימוד עצמי, מחקר, ביקורת, סקירה, דיווח עיתונאי, הבאת מובאות, …

*Gist:* Quoting for research and criticism is a fair-use purpose; keep quotations short, analysed and attributed. ([Copyright Law 2007 s. 19(a)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים); lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md))

> העתקה, תרגום או שכתוב של קטעים מעבודה אחרת או מכל מקור אחר מבלי לציין את מקורם.

*Gist:* Copying, translating or rewriting passages without citing their source is cheating. ([TAU disciplinary rules s. 29.3.6](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/default/files/media_server/General/yoets/12-002.pdf))

## Leading cases

Keep the three model papers from lesson [21.3](lesson-03.md) open as you write: Emmanuel and Gazal-Ayal (empirical structure), Elkin-Koren and Netanel (comparative structure, counter-argument in its own Part) and Paska and Kritenshtein (normative structure, roadmap in the abstract). For Supplied question A, the TAU disciplinary decision ד30-2023 (lesson [21.5](lesson-05.md)) is your leading authority.

## How it works in practice

- **Length:** about 15 pages of text (roughly 5,000–6,000 words), plus footnotes. Check your course's font, spacing and whether footnotes count.
- **Order of writing:** framework, argument, counter-arguments, proposal, conclusion, then the introduction, then footnotes, then the checks.
- **Supervisor:** if you have one, send the outline (lesson 21.3) before you write the argument, not the finished paper.
- **AI:** follow your course's policy (lesson 21.5). For self-study, follow the strictest of the rules you know: no AI-generated text, verify every source, declare any use.

## Worked example

Hila Mizrahi (lesson [21.3](lesson-03.md)) writes on Supplied question A. Day 1: framework (TAU ss. 29.3, 29.4, 32; Bar-Ilan rule 4(ה); both AI guides), 2.5 pages. Day 2: section ג compares the two models of AI regulation; section ד argues that the line should follow misrepresentation. Day 3: counter-arguments (deterrence; detection; fairness to students who complied), proposal, conclusion. Day 4: introduction, footnotes (34 in all, 9 sources on her map plus 3 found while writing). Day 5: integrity check; she finds one footnote copied from an article and converts it to "מובא אצל"; rubric score 15 of 20, weakest line "counter-argument fairly stated" (1 of 2). She rewrites section ה to give the deterrence view its strongest form, citing the disciplinary decision itself, and rescores 17.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your proposal, literature map, outline and footnote list (lessons 21.1 to 21.4), or one of the supplied questions below.
- The [proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md) and the [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md).
- A word processor with automatic footnotes.
- About 12 hours, in at least four sessions.

**Supplied question A.** "Should Israeli universities' disciplinary rules treat undisclosed use of generative AI in a paper as cheating (הונאה) or as breach of instructions (הפרת הוראות)?" Starting sources: TAU disciplinary rules ss. 29.3, 29.4, 32; TAU decision ד30-2023; Bar-Ilan disciplinary rules rule 4(ה) and AI instructions; TAU GenAI student guide (all linked in lesson 21.5).

**Supplied question B.** "Is the purpose test in Copyright Law s. 19(a) an independent precondition for fair use, and should it be?" Starting sources: s. 19; Safecom (CA 7996/11, Cardozo Versa); Elkin-Koren and Netanel, 72 Hastings L.J. 1121 (2021) and the rulings it discusses (Premier League 2012, Nestlé 2019); two Hebrew articles you find yourself (lesson 21.2).

### Steps

1. **Final check of the proposal (30 minutes).** Re-run the six tests of lesson 21.1. Update the thesis if your reading changed it.
2. **Legal framework, Part ב (90 minutes).** Statute first, then binding rulings, then the literature's main lines. Maximum three pages. Quote only what you will use.
3. **Argument, Parts ג–ד (240 minutes).** One claim per section, heading as a claim, each with sources, application and a mini-conclusion. Write fast; edit later.
4. **Counter-arguments, Part ה (60 minutes).** The two strongest objections, cited, in their best form; your replies.
5. **Proposal and conclusion, Parts ו–ז (60 minutes).** What should change, its costs, and the answer to your question in one paragraph. Name one limit and one question for further research.
6. **Introduction, Part א (45 minutes).** Five moves; one page; end with the roadmap. Check that the roadmap matches the headings.
7. **Footnote pass (60 minutes).** Full citation at first mention, short names afterwards, signals, pinpoints, Bluebook for English sources, and a starred access-date footnote.
8. **Integrity check (45 minutes).** [Paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 1. Write the AI-use declaration.
9. **Self-review (30 minutes).** Score the rubric (part 2) honestly. Write down the weakest line.
10. **Fix the weakest part, cut to length and proofread (60 minutes).** Read the paper aloud once; cut every sentence that does not advance the argument.

### How you know it worked

- A reader of page 1 alone can state your question and thesis.
- Reading only the headings gives the argument.
- Every footnote leads to the exact page of a source you read.
- The rubric score is at least 16 of 20 with no line at 0.
- The paper is within the length limit and your declaration is attached.

**The core of Part ד in three sentences:** The cheating offence protects the truth of the representation "this is my work"; using a tool is not in itself a false representation where the course allows it. Undisclosed use where the course banned it, or presenting AI text as one's own argument, is a false representation and should be cheating. Undisclosed use where the course allowed it with disclosure is a breach of instructions, which fits the outcome in decision ד30-2023.

**The best counter-argument (Part ה):** treating all undisclosed use as cheating deters better and is easier to enforce, because misrepresentation is hard to prove. The reply: the existing rules already distinguish between cheating and breach of instructions (ss. 29.3, 29.4); a clear disclosure form makes non-disclosure itself provable; and over-broad charges invite plea agreements that drop the cheating count anyway, as in ד30-2023.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Writing an academic paper is a degree requirement, not a legal act. The skills transfer directly to writing your own court arguments: a summations brief, an appeal or a response to a motion is a short paper with a question, a thesis, a framework, numbered arguments, an answer to the other side and a requested remedy.

### Where and how

When you next write a court document in your own case (module 15, module 22), use this lab's order: framework and arguments first, opening paragraph last; then check every citation as in step 7 and every source as in step 8. Courts set page and format limits; respect them as you respect the 15-page limit here.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Your court document turns on a contested legal question that would need a paper of this length to argue, the stakes are significant and the other side is represented. Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing the introduction first and never revising it | Roadmap does not match the paper | Rewrite the introduction last | Follow the order in the steps |
| Leaving footnotes for the last night | Missing pinpoints, wrong formats, integrity risks | Footnote pass with the literature map open | Insert at least a short reference while drafting |
| Going over the page limit | Penalty or cut by the supervisor | Cut background first, then repetition | Allocate pages in the outline |
| Skipping the rubric | Weakest part goes unnoticed | Score honestly; fix the lowest line | Book 30 minutes for it in your plan |
| One long writing session | Tired argument, more errors | Split into four or more sessions | Start a week before the deadline |

## Review

- Write in this order: framework, argument, counter-arguments, proposal and conclusion, introduction, footnotes, checks.
- Each section is one step of the argument under a heading that states a claim.
- Footnote and integrity passes are separate steps, not afterthoughts.
- Score the rubric honestly and fix the weakest part before anyone else reads the paper.
- The same method produces clear court arguments in your own case.
