# 21.5 · Academic integrity, plagiarism and AI tools

By the end of this lesson you will know what Israeli universities treat as cheating in a paper, how the current rules on generative AI at Tel Aviv University and Bar-Ilan work, how much of someone else's text you may quote and how, and a routine for checking every source an AI tool gives you, whether the document is a proseminar paper or your own court filing.

## Why it matters

A disciplinary conviction for cheating in a paper means, at Tel Aviv University, that the paper is automatically disqualified, and it may bring further penalties, which the disciplinary officer may order recorded on the transcript (ss. 31.3, 32). Most cases are not deliberate theft. They come from careless notes, paraphrase that stays too close to the source, a footnote copied from someone else's footnote, or AI-generated text and citations nobody checked.

Generative AI has made the rules more important, not less. Universities now allow many uses, but under conditions that change from course to course. And courts have started to see filings with invented case law: in February 2025 a Supreme Court panel criticised a lawyer's petition in which, according to press reports, several cited rulings did not exist and many quotations were fabricated (see How it works in practice). The same routine that keeps your paper honest keeps your court arguments credible.

## The law

### What counts as cheating: Tel Aviv University

> 29.3 הונאה בבחינה, עבודת בית, עבודה סמינריונית, עבודת גמר או עבודה אחרת המוטלת על התלמיד בקשר עם לימודיו.

> 29.3.5 הגשת עבודה שנכתבה, כולה או מקצתה, בידי אחר: כתיבת עבודה או חלק ממנה לשם הגשתה בידי אחר או העברתה לידיו לתכלית זו.
> 29.3.6 העתקה, תרגום או שכתוב של קטעים מעבודה אחרת או מכל מקור אחר מבלי לציין את מקורם.
> 29.3.7 בידוי נתונים או שימוש בנתונים ממחקר אחר, לרבות מחקר של התלמיד עצמו, מבלי לגלות עובדה זו.

*Gist:* Cheating in any paper includes submitting work written wholly or partly by someone else (and writing it for someone else); copying, translating or rewriting passages from another work or any other source without citing it; and fabricating data or using data from another study, including your own, without disclosure. The list is not closed. ([TAU Student Disciplinary Rules, instruction 12-002, s. 29.3](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/default/files/media_server/General/yoets/12-002.pdf))

> 29.4 הפרה של הוראות המתייחסות לבחינה, עבודת בית, עבודה סמינריונית, עבודת גמר או עבודה אחרת המוטלת על התלמיד בקשר עם לימודיו …

*Gist:* Breaching instructions relating to a paper is a separate, lesser offence; it is the offence most relevant when a course's instructions on AI are broken. (same, s. 29.4) Under s. 32 a conviction for cheating requires the disciplinary officer or tribunal to disqualify the paper, whatever other penalty is imposed.

Note "rewriting" (שכתוב) in s. 29.3.6: changing the words of a passage does not make it yours. A paraphrase still needs a footnote.

### What counts as cheating: Bar-Ilan

> הונאה בבחינה, בעבודות בית, בעבודה סמינריונית, בעבודת גמר, בעבודת דוקטור או בעבודה אחרת המוטלת על התלמיד בקשר עם לימודיו, בין אם התבצעו בצורה פרונטלית ובין אם בצורה מקוונת.

*Gist:* Bar-Ilan's rules (as of 2.1.2024) make cheating in any paper, in person or online, a disciplinary offence, and rule 4(ה)(2) adds breach of instructions relating to such assignments. ([BIU Student Disciplinary Rules, rule 4(ה)](https://acad-sec.biu.ac.il/sites/acad-sec/files/shared/Takanonim/Takanonim%20Senat/TakMishmaatStudentimH.2.1.2024.pdf)) Its student guide defines plagiarism as using another's words or ideas and presenting them as your own, whether deliberately or in good faith, and says an earlier paper of your own may be cited as a source but never resubmitted. ([BIU plagiarism guide](https://acad-sec.biu.ac.il/sites/acad-sec/files/shared/Mishmaat/Plagiat.2021.pdf))

### Generative AI: the current university rules (checked 2026-10-17)

**Tel Aviv University.** The university's student guide says TAU encourages responsible use of AI tools, but that use in a specific course is at the lecturer's sole discretion:

> השימוש בכלי AI במסגרת קורס ספציפי נתון לשיקול דעתם הבלעדי של המרצים והמרצות.

*Gist:* Read the syllabus and assignment instructions to see whether AI is allowed, which uses, and whether you must declare it; you remain responsible for verifying everything, including checking that AI-suggested sources exist. ([TAU GenAI student guide](https://innovative-learning.tau.ac.il/sites/innovative-learning.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/innovative-learning/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9A%20AI%20%D7%9C%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%95%D7%93%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%99%D7%9D/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9A%20%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94%20%D7%9E%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA%20%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A6%D7%A8%D7%AA%20%D7%9C%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%95'.pdf))

**Bar-Ilan.** The university's instructions set a default:

> במידה והמרצה לא הורה/תה מפורשות לסטודנט/ית שהשימוש בכלי בינה מלאכותית יוצרת אסור בקורס האמור, תהא ברירת המחדל שהשימוש מותר, אך תוך חובת גילוי נאות של הסטודנט/ית ביחס לאופן השימוש.

*Gist:* Unless the lecturer explicitly forbade it, use is allowed, but the student must disclose how it was used; students must declare use or non-use on a form listing the purposes (ideas, research, drafts, language, citation formatting, data, translation, code), the duty to find out the course's policy is entirely the student's, and the student is responsible for every error in the work, including AI errors. Where the rules forbid help from another person, the same help from AI is forbidden too. ([BIU instructions on generative AI](https://stuad.biu.ac.il/sites/stuad/files/shared/genaihe.pdf))

The Hebrew University's own student rules on generative AI could not be found in a free official source when this lesson was checked; HUJI students should check the course syllabus and the faculty's announcements.

### Quoting: fair use and the moral right (Copyright Law ss. 19 and 46)

> (ב) לצורך בחינה של הוגנות השימוש ביצירה לעניין סעיף זה, יישקלו, בין השאר, כל אלה: (1) מטרת השימוש ואופיו; (2) אופי היצירה שבה נעשה השימוש; (3) היקף השימוש, מבחינה איכותית וכמותית, ביחס ליצירה בשלמותה; (4) השפעת השימוש על ערכה של היצירה ועל השוק הפוטנציאלי שלה.

*Gist:* Section 19(a) permits fair use for purposes such as research, criticism, review and quotation; s. 19(b) lists four factors for deciding whether a use is fair: purpose and character, the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the work's market. ([Copyright Law 2007 s. 19](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים))

> זכות מוסרית ביחס ליצירה היא זכות היוצר – (1) כי שמו ייקרא על יצירתו בהיקף ובמידה הראויים בנסיבות העניין; …

*Gist:* The author has a moral right to be named on the work to the extent appropriate in the circumstances, and to its integrity. (same, s. 46)

For a paper: a short quotation, analysed in your text, attributed and cited, for research and criticism, is the core case of fair use. Copying pages of an article into your paper, even with a footnote, is not made fair by the footnote; and quoting without naming the author also infringes the moral right. Plagiarism of an idea (s. 5: ideas are not protected) is not a copyright problem, but it is still cheating under s. 29.3.6.

## Leading cases

### החלטת הממונה על המשמעת, אוניברסיטת תל אביב, ד30-2023 (29.11.2023)

A first-year film student used ChatGPT-type tools in two interim assignments in a guided reading and writing course. The complaint charged cheating (s. 29.3) and breach of instructions (s. 29.4). In a plea agreement the cheating count was dropped, taking into account that the technology was new and the rules on its use had not yet been settled when the act was done; she was convicted of breach of instructions, the course was disqualified, and she received a two-semester suspension, suspended for two years. Why it matters: it is the leading published Israeli example of AI misuse in coursework; it shows that the distinction between cheating and breach of instructions is real, and that the "rules were not yet settled" mitigation is fading now that every university has published guidance. [Decision (PDF)](https://acad-sec.tau.ac.il/sites/acadesc.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/acadsec/pd-2023/pd-30-2023.pdf)

### ע"א 7996/11 סייפקום בע"מ נ' רביב (18.11.2013)

The Supreme Court held that s. 19(a) is an open list of purposes and applied the four factors. Copying technical drawings, almost exactly, into a patent application for a competing product was commercial, not transformative, complete in extent and harmful to the market, so it was not fair use (para. 37). Why it matters: the factors that sank the defendant (copying almost everything, adding nothing new) are the same ones that make wholesale copying in a paper unfair; a short, critical, transformative quotation sits at the other end. (⚠️ The Hebrew rendering of the party name is unconfirmed.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/safecom-ltd-v-raviv)

### ע"א 9183/09 The Football Association Premier League Ltd נ' פלוני (13.5.2012) and ע"א 3425/17 Société des Produits Nestlé S.A. נ' אספרסו קלאב בע"מ (2019)

As reported by Elkin-Koren and Netanel: in Premier League the Supreme Court said that streaming an original broadcast "as is" infringed copyright and was not fair use, and cited the American transformative-use approach; in Nestlé it held that the s. 19(a) purpose test remains an independent preliminary test, and found the defendant's parodic use transformative and fair. Why they matter: together with Safecom they are the core of Israeli fair-use doctrine: the purpose must resemble a listed one, and transformation weighs heavily. (⚠️ Hebrew captions, proceeding types and the Nestlé date are unconfirmed; free Hebrew texts were not found.) [Elkin-Koren & Netanel, 72 Hastings L.J. 1121 (2021)](https://www.uclawjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/72.4-NetanelElkin-Koren.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### The seven integrity failures in legal papers

| Failure | What it looks like | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Verbatim copying | A sentence from an article with no quotation marks, even if footnoted | TAU 29.3.6 |
| Patchwriting | The source's sentence with words swapped and order changed | TAU 29.3.6 ("שכתוב") |
| Idea theft | Someone's original argument in your own words, uncited | TAU 29.3.6; BIU guide |
| Citation laundering | Copying another author's footnote to a source you never read | Misrepresentation; cite "as quoted in" (מובא אצל) |
| Self-plagiarism | Reusing your earlier paper without disclosure | TAU 29.3.7; BIU guide |
| Ghost-writing | A friend, a paid service or an AI tool writes part of the paper, against the course rules | TAU 29.3.5; BIU AI rules |
| Fabrication | Invented data, quotations or sources (including AI hallucinations you did not check) | TAU 29.3.7 |

### Quote, paraphrase or summarise

- **Quote** when the exact words matter: a statutory phrase, a holding, a formulation you will analyse. Quotation marks, a pinpoint, short. Introduce and analyse it; never leave a quotation to speak for itself.
- **Paraphrase** when the idea matters but not the words. Close the source, write the point in your own structure, then check against the source that you neither copied phrases nor changed the meaning. Footnote it.
- **Summarise** when you need the gist of a long argument. Footnote it.

### Using AI tools within the rules

1. **Find the course policy first** (syllabus, assignment instructions; ask the lecturer if unclear). At Bar-Ilan the default without a ban is "allowed with disclosure"; at TAU it is whatever the lecturer decides.
2. **Uses that are usually acceptable when allowed:** brainstorming questions, explaining a concept you then check, finding leads to sources, language polishing of text you wrote, formatting checks. **Usually not acceptable:** generating the paper's text or argument, or presenting AI output as your own reflection.
3. **Verify everything.** Every ruling, article, page number and quotation an AI tool gives you must be opened and read in the original. TAU's guide warns that tools sometimes invent sources that do not exist.
4. **Do not paste personal or confidential data** into AI tools (both universities' guides say so).
5. **Declare** as the course requires: which tool, for what, and that you checked the output. Bar-Ilan provides a form; you can adapt it where no form exists.

### When courts meet invented citations

In February 2025 a Supreme Court panel (Kanfi-Steinitz J., with Mintz and Elron JJ.) dealt with a petition, filed by a lawyer, that cited 36 purported rulings; according to the press report, five did not exist, fourteen had basic errors and twenty-four quotations were fabricated. The Court called it the first case of its kind before it, imposed no sanction on that occasion, and warned that lawyers' use of AI does not relieve them of responsibility, and that future cases could lead to dismissal and personal costs. ([Ice report, 24.2.2025](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/1052606); ⚠️ the case number and the decision text are unconfirmed.) Press reports describe later Supreme Court decisions in the same line ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/byrl7ey211e)). The same responsibility applies to a self-represented party.

## Worked example

Yael Ben-Ami, a student from Modi'in, is writing her proseminar paper. The syllabus says: "AI tools may be used for language editing and for locating sources; any use must be declared in an appendix."

1. **Language.** She writes each section herself, then asks an AI tool to point out unclear sentences. She accepts some suggestions and rewrites others. Permitted; she will declare it.
2. **Sources.** The tool suggests four articles. Two exist and are relevant; she reads them and adds them to her literature map. One exists but says the opposite of what the tool claimed. One does not exist in any journal index. She cites only the two she read, and uses the third with the signal "מנגד" after reading it. The fourth is dropped.
3. **A paraphrase check.** Her draft says: "בתי המשפט נוטים לבחון תחילה את מטרת השימוש ורק לאחר מכן את הוגנותו". Comparing it with her source, she finds the structure and three key phrases are the author's. She rewrites it in her own structure, adds her own point, and footnotes the author.
4. **A copied footnote.** She had copied a footnote citing a 1990s article she never saw. She now cites it as "מובא אצל" the article where she found it, and orders the original through the library.
5. **Declaration.** Appendix: "נעשה שימוש בכלי בינה מלאכותית יוצרת (שם הכלי) לצורך הערות לשוניות על טיוטה שכתבתי ולאיתור ראשוני של מקורות. כל המקורות נבדקו על ידי במקור. לא נעשה שימוש בכלי לכתיבת טקסט או טיעון."

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your draft introduction and footnotes from lessons 21.3 and 21.4.
- Your course's syllabus or assignment instructions (or, for self-study, the TAU and Bar-Ilan rules above).
- The [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 1 (integrity).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write down your course's AI policy in one sentence; if there is none, write the Bar-Ilan default and the TAU rule and decide which you will follow (5 minutes).
2. For every footnote, confirm you read the source yourself; mark any copied from another footnote and convert to "מובא אצל" or read the original (10 minutes).
3. Take your three longest paraphrases and compare them line by line with the sources; rewrite any that keep the source's structure or phrases (15 minutes).
4. Check every quotation for marks, accuracy and a pinpoint (5 minutes).
5. If you used AI, open every source and quotation it produced; delete anything you cannot find (5 minutes).
6. Write your AI-use declaration, or a one-line statement that none was used (5 minutes).

### Template

[Paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 1 (integrity), and the AI-use declaration at its end.

### How you know it worked

- Every footnote points to a source you have opened, and every quotation matches it word for word.
- No paraphrase shares more than a few words in sequence with its source.
- Your declaration is accurate and matches the course policy.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know my course's AI policy and followed it.
- [ ] I read every source I cite, or cited it "מובא אצל".
- [ ] Paraphrases are in my own structure and footnoted.
- [ ] Quotations are short, exact, in quotation marks, with pinpoints.
- [ ] No AI-generated source or quotation is in the paper unless I verified it in the original.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Integrity is an academic rule, but its core, never put before a reader a source you have not checked, is also the rule for your own court arguments. A court will not forgive invented case law because you are self-represented; you sign the document and you are responsible for it, exactly as a lawyer is.

### Where and how

Before you file anything you wrote with the help of an AI tool: open every ruling you cite on a free host (lesson [02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)), confirm the case number, the parties, the date and the paragraph you quote, and delete what you cannot confirm. Quote statutes from the current text (lesson [02.1](../module-02/lesson-01.md)). Keep your own drafts, so that you can show how the document was written.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

You receive a university disciplinary complaint. At Tel Aviv University a student may be helped by another student or a relative as defender, may be represented by a lawyer, and is entitled to a defender provided by the student union (TAU rules s. 25); the outcome can affect your grades, your record and your degree. Take advice early, before you respond in writing.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paraphrase that keeps the source's structure | "שכתוב" without credit: cheating under s. 29.3.6 | Rewrite in your own structure and footnote | Paraphrase with the source closed, then compare |
| Citing an AI-suggested ruling without reading it | Invented or misdescribed source; disciplinary or court sanction | Delete or replace with a verified source | Open every source in the original |
| Using AI where the course banned it, or not declaring it | Breach of instructions (s. 29.4) or cheating | Disclose to the lecturer at once | Check the syllabus before you start |
| Copying a footnote from another article | Citation laundering; errors copied too | Cite "מובא אצל" or read the original | Note in your map where you found each source |
| Long quotations instead of analysis | Weak paper; may not be fair use | Cut to the key words and analyse them | Quote only words you will discuss |

## Review

- TAU s. 29.3 lists submitting others' work, copying, translating or rewriting without credit, and undisclosed fabrication or reuse of data as cheating; breach of a course's instructions is a separate offence (s. 29.4).
- AI rules are set per course: TAU leaves it to the lecturer; Bar-Ilan allows use unless banned, with mandatory disclosure. You are responsible for every error.
- Quote short, exact, attributed and analysed: that is fair use (s. 19) and respects the moral right (s. 46).
- Safecom, Premier League and Nestlé: the purpose must fit s. 19(a), and transformation and amount weigh heavily.
- Verify every AI-assisted source before it reaches a supervisor or a court.
