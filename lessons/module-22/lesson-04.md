# 22.4 · Family court: the request to settle a dispute and mediation

Lesson 18.9 taught the request to settle a family dispute, its forms and its timetable as a dry run. In this clinic lesson you decide whether and when to start that process in your own family, prepare for the assistance-unit meetings and a mediation as the person in the room, and finish either with an agreement that a court approves or with a ready plan for the claims in the 15-day window.

## Why it matters

Since 2016 almost every family dispute between spouses, or between parents about their children, starts with a bare request to settle and up to four meetings at the court's assistance unit (מהו״ת). Most self-represented parents meet the system here, and many leave it with an agreement. The process rewards preparation and patience: the parties attend in person, the first meeting is with the parties alone, and nothing may be signed until each has had a chance to consult. It punishes three things: telling the story of the marriage in the request, missing a meeting, and signing under pressure.

A clinic case in the family court is also different from every other clinic matter: the other party is your children's other parent, what is said in the unit is privileged, and the proceedings are closed to the public. Building the file therefore includes building a record you can show your children one day without regret.

## The law

### The request and the stay (summary)

The request must not include allegations or facts about the dispute or about jurisdiction (Dispute Resolution Law s. 3(א)); it is filed on Form 1 at the secretariat (reg. 2), served by the court (reg. 3), and followed by up to four meetings within 45 days (extendable by 15), attended in person (s. 3(ב)). During the stay, claims are frozen except urgent, interim, status-quo and exit-ban requests on Forms 5–7 (s. 3(ז); regs 10–16). After the stay the first filer has 15 days to file claims in any competent forum (s. 4). Full detail and forms: lesson [18.9](../module-18/lesson-09.md).

### The outcome you are working towards

> הגיעו הצדדים להסדר בהליך ליישוב סכסוך בהסכמה, רשאית ערכאה שיפוטית לתת תוקף של פסק דין להסדר, בעניין שהיא מוסמכת לדון בו.

*Gist:* If the parties reach an arrangement in the process, a competent court may give it the force of a judgment. ([Dispute Resolution Law s. 3(ט)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה)) An approved agreement on child maintenance can be enforced through the Execution Office like any judgment; an unapproved one is only a contract.

### Nothing signed without consultation

> לא יחתמו הצדדים בעת פגישות המהו״ת ביחידת הסיוע על הסכם בעניין הסכסוך המשפחתי, ולא יקבלו על עצמם התחייבות משפטית, לרבות לעניין הסדרים זמניים [...] אלא לאחר שניתנה להם הזדמנות להיוועץ בבאי כוחם או בכל אדם אחר, לרבות בנוגע לניסוח ההסכם [...]

*Gist:* No agreement is signed and no legal commitment, including an interim arrangement, is undertaken at the meetings until the parties have had an opportunity to consult a lawyer or anyone else, including on the wording; the unit must tell them so and inform them about Legal Aid. ([reg. 5(ה)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה)) This is your right to say "not today". The unit may also stop a meeting for improper behaviour and report it to the court, which may award costs (reg. 5(ט)).

### Privilege and privacy

What is said to the assistance unit is privileged, may not be published and is inadmissible (Family Court Law s. 5א). Family matters are heard behind closed doors unless the court orders otherwise:

> עניני משפחה, כמשמעותם בחוק בית המשפט לעניני משפחה [...] יידונו בדלתיים סגורות, אלא אם כן הורה בית המשפט לדון בענין, כולו או מקצתו, בפומבי;

*Gist:* Family matters are heard in camera ([Courts Law s. 68(ה)(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט)), and no one may publish anything about a closed hearing without the court's leave (s. 70(א)). A social-media post about your case can breach the law, not just good sense.

### The court's flexibility

The Family Court acts on evidence and procedure "in the way that seems best to it for doing justice" where no other rule applies, and may hear a child without the parents present (Family Court Law s. 8(א)–(ב)).

## Leading cases

### בע״מ 919/15 פלוני נ׳ פלונית (19.7.2017)

Taught in lesson [18.5](../module-18/lesson-05.md): the Supreme Court's leading child-maintenance ruling for Jewish parents in the Family Court, under which, for children aged 6 to 15, both parents share the children's needs according to their relative incomes, taking parenting time into account. Why it matters for the meetings: your maintenance proposal should be built on both incomes and the parenting schedule, not on a fixed "father pays" sum. Source as cited in lesson 18.5 ([Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/בע%22מ_919/15_פלוני_נ%27_פלונית), ⚠️ secondary).

### בע״מ 1858/14 פלוני נ׳ פלונית (3.4.2014)

Taught in lesson [18.6](../module-18/lesson-06.md): while the statute is unchanged, the tender-years presumption applies to children under six, and departing from it needs weighty evidence. Why it matters for the meetings: a parenting plan for a child under six is negotiated against that default, which both sides should know before they argue about "equal time". Source as cited in lesson 18.6 ([Hebrew Wikipedia: משמורת ילדים](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/משמורת_ילדים), ⚠️ secondary).

## How it works in practice

### Decide before you file

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Is there violence or a threat? | Protection order first (lesson [18.7](../module-18/lesson-07.md)); the request process does not apply to it, and the stay can be shortened | Continue |
| Does the other party live abroad, or might a child be taken abroad? | The law does not apply where a party lives abroad (s. 4א); for a feared removal, file an exit-ban request with the request (Form 5) | Continue |
| Do you already agree on everything? | No request is needed for a divorce by agreement; you can ask a court to approve the agreement directly | File the request |
| Is a filing race likely (a religious court)? | Get advice before filing (lessons 10.3, 18.1) | You may file yourself |

### Filing

Form 1 is filed at the secretariat of the Family Court (or the competent religious court) under the ordinary venue rules, by hand; the Judiciary's service page refers unrepresented filers to Net HaMishpat (⚠️ manual check whether an unrepresented party can file Form 1 online, or only book a secretariat appointment online). A fee is paid, or an exemption requested (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). The court serves the other party with the invitation to the first meeting (reg. 3).

### The meetings and the mediation

- **First meeting:** parties alone, acquaintance and assessment; interim arrangements only if both wish and after a chance to consult.
- **Later meetings:** information about routes (mediation, counselling, collaborative divorce), the children's needs and wishes, interim arrangements; lawyers may join.
- **Mediation:** often with a family mediator from the official list; you may bring a draft and you sign only a document you understand. Within five days of the last unit meeting each party sends Form 3 saying whether they will continue an alternative process (reg. 8).
- **Agreement:** a written agreement, signed after consultation, filed with an application to give it the force of a judgment (s. 3(ט)). The court may ask questions, require a social worker's view, or see the parties.

### What the case log records

Each meeting's date, who attended, the topics, what was proposed (not what the other parent said in confidence), every interim arrangement and when it starts, every document exchanged, and the date of each Form 3 and of the end of the stay. Keep the log factual and private: it is privileged material in a closed proceeding.

## Worked example

**Supplied case C.** Almaz Tesfaye, 34, a pharmacy technician in Rehovot, separated in August 2026 from Yosef, a bus driver. Their children are 5 and 9. They rent; the lease is in both names. Yosef sees the children irregularly and pays "when he can". Neither has a lawyer. There has been no violence.

1. **Decision.** Almaz has nothing to agree "already", no one lives abroad, and Yosef has said he does not want "a rabbi to decide". She files the request herself on Form 1 at the secretariat of the Family Court for her area on 2 November 2026, ticking: child maintenance and housing; parental responsibility and parenting time. No facts. She pays the fee.
2. **Preparation for meeting 1.** One page: the children's routine; a proposed interim schedule (Yosef: two afternoons a week and alternate weekends); the children's monthly needs (₪4,900, with receipts for after-school care and clothing); her net income (₪8,200) and her estimate of his (₪9,500); her three red lines (no change of school, no overnight weekdays before the younger child starts first grade, nothing signed today).
3. **Meeting 1 (both alone).** They agree in principle on the interim schedule. The social worker reminds them of reg. 5(ה). Almaz writes in her log: date, attendance, "interim schedule proposed by me, agreed in principle, to be signed after consultation".
4. **Consultation.** She consults a Legal Aid lawyer once (she qualifies on means; see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)). The lawyer suggests adding holidays and a maintenance figure linked to the CPI.
5. **Meetings 2–3.** They sign the interim schedule at meeting 2. The unit recommends a mediator from the list. Both send Form 3 agreeing to 60 days of mediation.
6. **Mediation.** Using the 919/15 approach (both incomes, the parenting time, the children's ages), they agree that Yosef pays ₪2,300 a month plus half of the after-school care, CPI-linked, and a parenting plan for the next two years with a review clause when the younger child turns six.
7. **Approval.** They file a joint application to give the agreement the force of a judgment (s. 3(ט)). The court approves it after a short hearing at which both confirm they understand it. Almaz enters the judgment date in her log and diarises the review clause.

**The other ending.** Had mediation failed, Almaz, as the first filer, would have had 15 days after the stay ended to file a child-maintenance claim and a parenting claim in the Family Court, each with Form 4 (s. 4; reg. 9), and her maintenance claim would be treated as filed on 2 November (reg. 15(ב)). ## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- Your own family situation, or supplied case C. In shared or submitted work, do not identify children or the other parent; use initials.
- The [request-to-settle template](../../templates/request-to-settle-family-dispute.md) (parts A, E and F) and your [clinic case file](../../templates/clinic-case-file.md).
- Lessons 18.5 and 18.6 open for the maintenance and parenting principles; a calendar with Hebrew dates.
- About two hours.

### Steps

1. **Decide (15 min).** Answer the four questions in "Decide before you file" in writing. If violence, a party abroad or a filing race applies, stop and follow the referral.
2. **Form 1 (15 min).** Fill in part A: parties, children, subjects, nothing else. Re-read it and delete every fact.
3. **Calendar (15 min).** From a filing date, mark the 45 days (60 if extended), the five-day Form 3 notice, any mediation period agreed, and the 15-day window.
4. **Budget (20 min).** The children's monthly needs with receipts; both incomes as far as you know them; housing costs.
5. **Parenting proposal (20 min).** A two-month interim schedule and a longer plan (school days, weekends, holidays, handovers, communication), with a review date.
6. **Red lines and the sentence (10 min).** Three things you will not agree without advice, and the sentence you will say if pressed: "אני רוצה להתייעץ לפני שאני חותמת/חותם, כפי שמאפשרת תקנה 5(ה)."
7. **Log template (10 min).** Create the meeting log: date, attendance, topics, proposals, interim arrangements, documents, next step.
8. **Endings (15 min).** Draft the short application for the force of a judgment (model above), and a one-paragraph plan for the claims you would file in the 15-day window if there is no agreement.

### Template

[Request to settle a family dispute and requests during the stay](../../templates/request-to-settle-family-dispute.md) (module 18): part A (Form 1), part E (Form 3), part F (checklist before the first meeting). Model application for approval: in the worked example above.

### How you know it worked

- Your Form 1 contains no facts or allegations.
- Your budget and parenting proposal could be handed to a mediator as they are.
- Your log has a place for every meeting and every deadline, and contains facts, not accusations.
- You know which outcome you are working towards and what you file at each ending.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know that I attend in person and sign nothing until I have had a chance to consult (reg. 5(ה)).
- [ ] I know that an agreement needs a court's approval to become a judgment (s. 3(ט)).
- [ ] I know that the proceedings are closed and that I may not publish them (Courts Law ss. 68(ה), 70(א)).
- [ ] I know what to file, and by when, if the process ends without agreement.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. The request, the meetings and mediation are designed for parties in person, and the parties attend the meetings themselves even when they have lawyers. The assistance unit does not represent either parent. In the rabbinical and Sharia courts licensed pleaders may also act (lesson 18.9). See [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The secretariat of the Family Court for your area (or the competent religious court); online filing for unrepresented parties: ⚠️ manual check on the Judiciary's site on the day.
- The assistance unit beside the court; family mediators from the official list; Legal Aid for family matters if you meet the means test.
- Fees, exemptions and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- There is violence, a threat or a child at risk (lesson 18.7).
- The other parent is represented, or files, or threatens to file, in a religious court in the window.
- Property, pensions, a business, a home you own or a get are on the table.
- You are asked to sign anything you do not fully understand.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posting about the case on social media | Breach of the publication ban; harm to the children; costs | Delete at once; apologise to the court if raised | Remember ss. 68(ה) and 70(א) |
| Signing the interim schedule at the first meeting under pressure | A commitment you did not test | Ask the unit to record it as a proposal; consult | Use reg. 5(ה) and the prepared sentence |
| Treating the agreement as final without approval | It is a contract only; enforcement is harder | File a joint application under s. 3(ט) | Plan the approval step from the start |
| A maintenance proposal with no budget | The figure looks arbitrary | Add the children's needs and both incomes | Build the budget before meeting 1 |
| Letting the 15-day window pass while waiting for "one more talk" | The other parent chooses the forum for open issues | File what remains | Prepare the claims before the stay ends |

## Review

- The family clinic starts with a bare Form 1 request and up to four meetings at the assistance unit; urgent and status-quo relief stays available (lesson 18.9).
- Attend in person, prepare a budget, a parenting proposal and red lines, and sign nothing without a chance to consult (reg. 5(ה)).
- An agreement reached in the process becomes a judgment only when a court gives it that force (s. 3(ט)).
- Family proceedings are closed and privileged; do not publish them, and keep your log factual.
- Without agreement, the first filer has 15 days after the stay to file claims with Form 4.
