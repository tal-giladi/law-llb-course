# 32.5 · Trademarks: registration, distinctiveness, infringement and passing off

By the end of this lesson you will be able to judge whether a name or logo can be registered as a trade mark, whether someone else's sign infringes yours (or yours infringes theirs), and what you can still do if your mark is not registered, using the Trade Marks Ordinance and the passing-off tort in the Commercial Torts Law.

## Why it matters

A brand is often a small business's most valuable asset and the one most often lost by accident. The usual story: someone opens a shop under a descriptive name that cannot be registered, or uses a name for years without registering it, and a competitor registers a similar mark first; or a business adopts a name that turns out to be someone else's registered mark and must rebrand after printing everything. Israeli trade-mark law rewards those who choose a distinctive sign and register it, and gives limited protection, through passing off, to those who built a reputation without registering.

## The law

### What a trade mark is and what can be registered

> ”סימן“ – אותיות, ספרות, מלים, דמויות או אותות אחרים או צירופם של אלה, בשני ממדים או בשלושה;

*Gist:* A "sign" can be letters, numbers, words, figures or other signs, or combinations, in two or three dimensions; a trade mark is a sign used or intended for use in relation to goods a person makes or trades in, and the Ordinance applies equally to service marks. ([Trade Marks Ordinance ss. 1–2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סימני_מסחר))

> אין סימן כשר לרישום כסימן מסחר אלא אם יש בו כדי להבחין בין הטובין של בעל הסימן לבין הטובין של אחרים (להלן – בעל אופי מבחין).

*Gist:* Only a distinctive sign can be registered; the Registrar or court may consider how far actual use has made it distinctive (acquired distinctiveness). (same, s. 8)

### Grounds of refusal (selected)

> (6) סימן שיש בו כדי להטעות את הציבור, סימן המכיל ציון מקור כוזב וסימן המעודד התחרות בלתי הוגנת במסחר;

> (9) סימן הזהה עם סימן שהוא שייך לבעל אחר וכבר הוא רשום בפנקס לגבי אותם טובין או טובין מאותו הגדר, והוא הדין בסימן הדומה לסימן כאמור עד כדי שיש בו להטעות;

> (10) סימן המורכב מספרות, אותיות או מלים, הנוהגות במסחר לציונם או לתיאורם של טובין או סוגי טובין או הנוגעות במישרין למהותם או לאיכותם, זולת אם הוא בעל אופי מבחין כמשמעותו בסעיפים 8(ב) או 9;

> (11) סימן שמשמעותו הרגילה גיאוגרפית או שם משפחה, אם אינו מוצג בדרך מיוחדת, זולת אם הוא בעל אופי מבחין כמשמעותו בסעיפים 8(ב) או 9;

*Gist:* Among the s. 11 grounds: misleading signs and false indications of origin (6); a sign identical or confusingly similar to a mark already registered by another for the same goods or goods of the same description (9); words that describe the goods or their quality, unless they have acquired distinctiveness (10); ordinary geographical names and surnames not presented in a special way, unless distinctive through use (11); and signs identical or confusingly similar to a well-known mark (13–14). (same, s. 11)

### Registration, opposition, term and non-use

> תוך שלושה חדשים מיום הפרסום, רשאי כל אדם להגיש לרשם הודעה על התנגדותו לרישום הסימן.

*Gist:* After examination and acceptance the application is published; anyone may oppose within three months (s. 24(א)), on any ground for refusal or by claiming ownership of the mark (s. 24(א1)). Registration lasts ten years from the application and is renewable for ten-year periods (ss. 31–32). A registered mark may be cancelled for lack of bona fide use in the three years before the cancellation application, unless the non-use was due to special trade circumstances (s. 41).

### The exclusive right and infringement

> רישומו התקף של אדם כבעל סימן מסחר יזכה אותו לשימוש ייחודי בסימן המסחר על הטובין שלגביהם נרשם הסימן ובכל הנוגע אליהם, בכפוף לכל תנאי וסייג הרשומים בפנקס.

*Gist:* Valid registration gives the exclusive right to use the mark on the goods for which it is registered (s. 46(א)). "Infringement" is use by an unauthorised person of the registered mark or a similar mark for the same goods or goods of the same description, or in advertising such goods; for well-known marks, protection extends to similar goods even if unregistered, and to dissimilar goods if the mark is registered and the use suggests a connection that may harm the owner (s. 1, "הפרה"; s. 46א).

> רישום לפי פקודה זו לא ימנע אדם מהשתמש שימוש אמת בשמו או בשם עסקו או בשמו הגאוגרפי של מקום עסקו, שלו או של קודמיו בעסק, או מהשתמש בהגדר אמיתי של מהותם או איכותם של טובין שלו.

*Gist:* Registration does not stop anyone from making bona fide use of his own name, his business's name or place, or a true description of his goods. (same, s. 47) Only the owner of a registered or well-known mark may sue for infringement (s. 57(א)); remedies are an injunction, damages and orders about the infringing goods (ss. 59, 59א), and for an unregistered well-known mark, an injunction only (s. 59(ב)). Marking or importing goods with a registered mark for trade without permission is an offence (s. 60).

### Passing off

> לא יגרום עוסק לכך שנכס שהוא מוכר או שירות שהוא נותן, ייחשבו בטעות כנכס או כשירות של עוסק אחר או כנכס או כשירות שיש להם קשר לעוסק אחר.

*Gist:* A trader must not cause his goods or services to be mistaken for another trader's or for goods connected with him; good-faith use of one's own name is not in itself passing off. ([Commercial Torts Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עוולות_מסחריות)) The duty runs between traders (s. 4). Passing off is a tort (s. 11); the court may award statutory damages up to a ceiling for each tort (s. 13; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) and, on proof of a real risk, appoint a receiver to seize goods and evidence, even ex parte (ss. 16–17). The same law prohibits false descriptions of one's own or a competitor's business (s. 2).

## Leading cases

### ע"א 563/11 עדידס סלומון א.ג. נ' יאסין (27.8.2012)

adidas, owner of the registered three-stripes mark for footwear, sued an importer whose shoes had four diagonal stripes and the name "SYDNEY", detained at Ashdod port. The Supreme Court (Hayut J.) held that the three stripes had strong acquired distinctiveness but weak inherent distinctiveness, so protection extends to the mark itself and very close variations, not to every striped design; and that confusing similarity is judged by the triple test: sight and sound, the type of goods and customers, and all other circumstances, comparing the marks as a whole and, for a decorative visual mark, together with the product. The four-stripe shoes with another name did not infringe and were not passing off. Why it matters: it is the leading modern statement of the infringement test, and of the principle that a strong brand does not get a monopoly over a common design element. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/salomon-v-yaasin)

### ע"א 11487/03 Storck KG נ' אלפא (23.3.2008)

The maker of Toffifee sued over a candy of similar shape. The Supreme Court (Grunis J.) held that the three-dimensional shape of a product cannot be registered on the basis of inherent distinctiveness, only on proof of acquired distinctiveness; and that a shape that serves a functional or aesthetic role beyond a negligible one cannot be a trade mark even if it has acquired distinctiveness. The case was remanded. Why it matters: trade-mark law cannot be used to get an endless monopoly on a product's appearance; that is the domain of design law, which is limited in time (lesson 32.6). (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/storck-kg-v-alpha)

### ע"א 4410/06 NRG Energy International Inc. נ' Texaco Inc. (31.8.2010)

The owner of a registered "NRG" mark for a motor-oil additive sought to strike the word "Energy" from Texaco's "Havoline Energy" mark. The Supreme Court (Naor J.) applied the triple test and refused: "energy" is a descriptive term in the field that no one can monopolise; the applicant had told the Patent Office that its letters "have no meaning"; and the marks, containers and products differed. Why it matters: descriptive words stay free for all, and what you say to the Registrar when you register binds you later. [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/nrg-energy-international-inc-v-texaco-inc)

### ע"א 6316/03 אילן זגגות רכב בע"מ נ' ברוך (1.8.2007)

A company bought a car-glass business and its name, but the sellers kept using "Car Glass" (in Hebrew). The Supreme Court (Rivlin D.P., Naor J. dissenting in part) held that passing off requires proof of goodwill, that the public identifies the name with the claimant's business, and a likelihood that customers will think they are dealing with the claimant; even a fairly descriptive name can acquire such goodwill. The sellers were barred from the Hebrew name, but allowed to use a foreign company's English "Carglass" logo they had licensed. Why it matters: an unregistered, even descriptive, name can be protected by passing off, but only to the extent of proven goodwill and confusion, and the protection is narrow. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ilan-car-glazing-ltd-v-baruch)

## How it works in practice

### The distinctiveness spectrum

| Type | Example (for a bakery) | Registrable? |
|---|---|---|
| Invented word | "Zelvo" | Yes, the strongest |
| Arbitrary (real word, unrelated meaning) | "Lighthouse" for bread | Yes, strong |
| Suggestive | "Golden Crust" | Usually yes |
| Descriptive | "Fresh Bread", "Best Bakery" | No, unless acquired distinctiveness (s. 11(10)) |
| Generic | "Bakery", "Challah" | Never |
| Geographic or surname | "Holon Bakery", "Cohen" | Only in a special presentation or with acquired distinctiveness (s. 11(11)) |

### The triple test in practice

1. **Sight and sound:** compare the marks as a whole, as a consumer with imperfect memory would; for word marks, sound often matters most (orders by phone, over the counter).
2. **Goods and customers:** the same goods or "goods of the same description" (טובין מאותו הגדר), the same trade channels, the same buyers; cheap everyday goods mean less careful buyers.
3. **Other circumstances:** how the marks are actually used, packaging, prices, evidence of actual confusion, the defendant's intent.

### Registered mark or passing off?

| | Registered mark | Passing off |
|---|---|---|
| What you prove | Registration and infringing use (triple test) | Goodwill, misrepresentation, likely confusion |
| Territory of protection | All of Israel, for registered goods | Where your goodwill exists |
| Cost and speed | Registration fee in advance; easier claim | No fee; harder, evidence-heavy claim |
| Statutory damages | Not under the Ordinance; available under CTL s. 13 when passing off is also proved | CTL s. 13 |

In practice, claims often plead both.

### Company names and trade marks

The Companies Registrar refuses a company name that is the same as or misleadingly similar to a registered trade mark in a similar field (Companies Law s. 27; lesson [16.2](../module-16/lesson-02.md)). Registering a company name gives no trade-mark right, and a registered trade mark beats a later company name. Search both registries.

## Worked example

Yonatan Levi, a coffee roaster in Jerusalem, sells beans as "Har Coffee" (הר קפה) with a mountain logo. A competitor in Haifa starts selling "Harim Coffee" (הרים קפה) with a different mountain.

1. **Is Yonatan's mark registered?** If yes, he checks the registered goods (class 30, coffee) and sues for infringement; if not, only passing off is available.
2. **Triple test.** Sight and sound: "Har" and "Harim" share the dominant element and sound close; but a mountain is a common image, and "הר" alone is weak for a Jerusalem product. Goods and customers: identical goods, sold in the same shops and online, cheap and bought quickly; this favours confusion. Other circumstances: different logos and packaging colours; any customer messages showing confusion would be important evidence.
3. **Defences.** The competitor will say "הר" is descriptive or geographic and cannot be monopolised (NRG, adidas: weak marks get narrow protection), and that the full get-up differs.
4. **Passing off** (if unregistered): Yonatan must prove goodwill in "Har Coffee" in Haifa as well as in Jerusalem, and likely confusion; harder.
5. **Plan.** He sends a reasoned letter asking the competitor to change the name and colours; files a trade-mark application now if he has none (lesson 32.9); and keeps evidence of any confusion.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A name or logo you use or plan to use (or "Har Coffee" above).
- The Patent Office trade-mark database (lesson 32.9 shows how to search it).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Place your mark on the distinctiveness spectrum and write why (10 minutes).
2. Check it against s. 11(6), (9), (10), (11), (13) and (14); write any ground that might apply (10 minutes).
3. Find one existing mark that is close to yours (in the database or the market) and run the triple test (15 minutes).
4. Decide: register as is, change the mark, add a distinctive logo, or rely on passing off for now, and write why (10 minutes).

### Template

The [trade-mark search and application worksheet](../../templates/trademark-search-and-application-worksheet.md), parts A and B (used in full in lesson 32.9).

### How you know it worked

- Your mark has a reasoned place on the spectrum and a list of possible refusal grounds.
- Your triple-test analysis addresses all three limbs, with facts for each.
- Your decision follows from the analysis, not from attachment to the name.

### Self-check

- [ ] I did not treat a descriptive or generic word as strong.
- [ ] I compared marks as a whole, as an ordinary consumer would.
- [ ] I considered goods "of the same description", not only identical goods.
- [ ] I know that passing off requires proof of goodwill and confusion.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. An individual may apply to register a trade mark in his own name, file or answer an opposition, and sue for infringement or passing off in his own name (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). Representing others before the Registrar for pay is reserved to patent attorneys and lawyers (Patents Law s. 154), and a company needs a lawyer in court outside small claims.

### Where and how

- Search and apply: the Patent Office's trade-mark database and online filing (lesson 32.9); fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Opposition: within three months of publication, to the Registrar (s. 24).
- Infringement and passing off: usually the District Court, which hears IP claims (Courts Law s. 40(4)); a money claim within the small-claims ceiling may be filed there by an individual.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You receive a cease-and-desist letter about your business name and a rebrand would be costly.
- You plan to oppose a registration or defend an opposition with evidence and hearings.
- You need an urgent injunction or a seizure order (CTL ss. 16–17), which require security and a careful affidavit.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing a descriptive name ("Best Pizza") | Refused (s. 11(10)); no exclusivity | Add a distinctive element or rebrand early | Prefer invented or arbitrary words |
| Using a name for years without registering | A competitor may register first; only passing off remains | Apply now; oppose the competitor's application within 3 months | Register before launch |
| Assuming a registered company name protects the brand | No trade-mark right | Apply to register the mark | Search and register both |
| Claiming a monopoly on a common element (stripes, a mountain) | Claim fails (adidas, NRG) | Rely on the whole mark and get-up | Keep the mark's distinctive core |
| Registering and never using the mark | Cancellation for non-use after three years (s. 41) | Start genuine use; keep evidence | Use it and document use |

## Review

- A trade mark must be distinctive; descriptive, generic, geographic and surname marks are refused unless distinctive through use (ss. 8, 11).
- Registration gives the exclusive right for the registered goods for ten renewable years; anyone may oppose within three months of publication; non-use for three years risks cancellation.
- Infringement of a similar mark is judged by the triple test (adidas, NRG); weak marks get narrow protection.
- Product shapes are rarely trade marks: functional or aesthetic shapes are excluded (Storck).
- Passing off protects unregistered goodwill against confusion between traders (CTL s. 1; Ilan Car Glazing), with statutory damages and seizure orders available.
