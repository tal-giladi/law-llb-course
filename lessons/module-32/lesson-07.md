# 32.7 · Trade secrets and employee inventions

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide whether information your business (or your employer) holds is a legally protected trade secret, what a departing employee may and may not take, whether a non-compete clause is enforceable, and who owns what an employee invents, designs or writes, including the employee's right to claim compensation for a service invention.

## Why it matters

Most IP disputes between people who know each other are about secrets and employment: an employee leaves to a competitor or opens a business, and the former employer claims he took clients, price lists, code or know-how. Israeli law protects the employee's freedom of occupation strongly and protects the employer's trade secrets, and the line between "general professional skill" and "the employer's secret" decides the case. On the other side, employees who invent valuable products often do not know that the Patents Law gives them a right to claim compensation unless they waived it.

## The law

### What a trade secret is

> ”סוד מסחרי“, ”סוד“ – מידע עסקי, מכל סוג, שאינו נחלת הרבים ושאינו ניתן לגילוי כדין בנקל על ידי אחרים, אשר סודיותו מקנה לבעליו יתרון עסקי על פני מתחריו, ובלבד שבעליו נוקט אמצעים סבירים לשמור על סודיותו;

*Gist:* A trade secret is business information of any kind that is not public, cannot easily be discovered lawfully by others, gives its owner a business advantage over competitors, and is kept secret by reasonable measures. ([Commercial Torts Law s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עוולות_מסחריות))

### Misappropriation

> (1) נטילת סוד מסחרי ללא הסכמת בעליו באמצעים פסולים, או שימוש בסוד על ידי הנוטל; ... (2) שימוש בסוד מסחרי ללא הסכמת בעליו כאשר השימוש הוא בניגוד לחיוב חוזי או לחובת אמון, המוטלים על המשתמש כלפי בעל הסוד; (3) קבלת סוד מסחרי או שימוש בו ללא הסכמת בעליו, כאשר המקבל או המשתמש יודע או שהדבר גלוי על פניו ... כי הסוד הועבר אליו באופן האסור ...

*Gist:* Misappropriation is taking a secret by improper means and using it; using it in breach of a contractual or fiduciary duty (the usual employee case); or receiving or using it knowing that it was passed on improperly. Reverse engineering is not in itself an improper means. (same, s. 6) Use of a modified secret counts if there is substantial similarity (s. 9), and use is presumed where the defendant had access and uses substantially similar information (s. 10).

### The employee's skills and the public interest

> (1) הידע הגלום בסוד המסחרי הגיע אליו במהלך עבודתו אצל בעליו של הסוד המסחרי וידע זה הפך לחלק מכישוריו המקצועיים הכלליים; (2) השימוש בסוד המסחרי מוצדק בשל תקנת הציבור.

*Gist:* No liability where the knowledge reached the person during his work for the secret's owner and became part of his general professional skills, or where the use is justified by public policy (for example, exposing wrongdoing). (same, s. 7(א))

### Remedies and forum

The court may award statutory damages up to a ceiling per tort (s. 13; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), order accounts (s. 15), and appoint a receiver to search and seize, even ex parte and before a claim is filed, against security and a deposit (ss. 16–19). Claims between employee and employer arising from the employment relationship, including before it begins and after it ends, are in the exclusive jurisdiction of the regional labour court (s. 22). Courts may protect secrets disclosed in proceedings (s. 23).

### Employee inventions

> אמצאה של עובד, שהגיע אליה עקב שירותו ובתקופת שירותו (להלן – אמצאת שירות), תקום לקנין מעבידו, אם אין ביניהם הסכם אחר לענין זה, זולת אם ויתר המעביד על האמצאה תוך ששה חדשים מיום שנמסרה לו ההודעה לפי סעיף 131.

*Gist:* An employee must notify the employer in writing of any invention made as a result of or during his service (s. 131). A service invention, made as a result of the service and during it, belongs to the employer unless they agreed otherwise or the employer waived it within six months of the notice; an employee's notice may say that silence for six months means the invention is his (s. 132). ([Patents Law ss. 131–132](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפטנטים)) Disputes over whether an invention is a service invention go to the Registrar (s. 133).

> באין הסכם הקובע אם זכאי העובד לתמורה בעד אמצאת שירות, ובאיזו מידה ובאילו תנאים, ייקבע הדבר על ידי הועדה לעניני פיצויים ותמלוגים שהוקמה לפי פרק ו׳.

*Gist:* Where there is no agreement on whether and how much compensation the employee gets for a service invention, the Compensation and Royalties Committee decides (s. 134), considering the employee's role, the link between the invention and his work, his initiative, the invention's exploitation, and his expenses in protecting it (s. 135). The Committee is a Supreme Court judge, the Registrar and an academic (s. 109); it has exclusive jurisdiction and its decisions are final (s. 111), so review is by petition to the High Court of Justice. Employee and employer must keep the invention confidential until a patent application is filed (s. 141).

### Employees' works and designs

- **Copyright:** the employer owns works made for the job and in its course, unless agreed otherwise (Copyright Law s. 34; lesson 32.2).
- **Designs:** the employer owns a design made for the job **or with substantial use of the employer's resources**, unless agreed otherwise; the Registrar decides disputes (Designs Law s. 12). The resources test is broader than copyright's.

## Leading cases

### ע"א 6601/96 AES System Inc. נ' סער (28.8.2000)

A computer technician signed non-compete and confidentiality undertakings, was dismissed after 28 months, opened his own service business, advertised for clients of his former employer's systems and approached its customers using its customer list. The Supreme Court (Barak P., with Or and Rivlin JJ.) held that a clause restricting an employee's freedom of occupation is valid only if it protects a legitimate interest of the employer (trade secrets, customer lists, reputation and the like) and is reasonable in time, place and type of activity; a restraint whose only purpose is to prevent competition or to keep the employee's general knowledge and skills from the market is contrary to public policy. It drew on the National Labour Court's Frumer judgment (ע"ע 164/99). Why it matters: it sets the test every non-compete is measured by today, and it shows that the employer's protectable interest is the secret and the customer list, not the employee's experience. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/aes-systems-v-saar)

### בג"ץ 4353/14 ברזני נ' ישקר בע"מ (8.7.2015)

As reported by Szatmary (2020): an engineer sought compensation from the Committee for six inventions made while employed; the Committee rejected the claim because he had signed a clear waiver on leaving. The High Court of Justice upheld the Committee, holding that s. 134 is not mandatory (cogent) and that the right to compensation is not a social right requiring special protection, so it can be waived by agreement. Why it matters: whether you can claim depends on what you signed; read the IP clauses in your employment contract and exit documents, and do not sign a general waiver without understanding what you give up. (⚠️ The judgment text was not verified on a free host; Committee decisions are generally unpublished.) [Szatmary, Runi Law Review blog](https://runilawreview.org/2020/12/30/szatmary-m/)

## How it works in practice

### Trade-secret hygiene for a small business

1. **Identify** the few things that really give you an edge: a formula, a supplier and price structure, a customer list with purchase history, source code, a process.
2. **Mark and restrict:** "סודי", passwords, access only for those who need it, logs.
3. **Contract:** confidentiality clauses for employees and contractors, NDAs for suppliers and investors, a return-of-materials clause on leaving.
4. **Exit routine:** remind the departing employee in writing what is confidential, collect devices, revoke access.

Without reasonable measures there is no trade secret (s. 5), however valuable the information.

### For the employee who leaves

| You may | You may not |
|---|---|
| Use your general skill, experience and knowledge of the trade (s. 7(א)(1)) | Copy files, customer lists, price lists or code to take with you |
| Work for a competitor, unless a valid restraint applies | Use a confidential customer list to solicit clients (AES) |
| Tell people publicly where you now work | Use secrets in breach of your confidentiality undertaking (s. 6(ב)(2)) |
| Challenge an unreasonable non-compete | Ignore a valid, limited restraint protecting a legitimate interest |

### Service inventions: the timeline

1. The employee invents something connected to his job: he notifies the employer in writing promptly (s. 131).
2. The employer has six months to waive; otherwise the invention is the employer's (s. 132), unless the contract says otherwise.
3. Compensation: if the contract settles it (including by a valid waiver), the contract governs; if not, the Committee decides (ss. 134–135).
4. Limitation: claims to the Committee are subject to limitation, and the High Court has addressed when the period starts (as reported by Szatmary; ⚠️ check before relying on a date).

## Worked example

Samir Khoury worked six years as a process engineer at a Nazareth food-flavours company. He developed an improved drying process that the company now uses in all its plants. He resigns to open a consultancy; his contract has a confidentiality clause and a 12-month non-compete covering "any work in the food industry in Israel".

1. **The drying process.** Made as a result of his service and during it: a service invention, the company's (s. 132), unless his contract says otherwise. Compensation: he checks his contract and exit documents. If there is no agreement on compensation and no waiver, he may apply to the Committee (s. 134), relying on his initiative and the process's wide use (s. 135). If he signed a clear waiver, Barzani suggests it will be enforced.
2. **The non-compete.** "Any work in the food industry in Israel" for a year is broad. Under AES, it is enforceable only to the extent it protects a legitimate interest, such as the company's secret formulas and customer list, not his general expertise. A court would likely limit it to soliciting the company's clients or using its secrets.
3. **What he may take.** His knowledge of drying technology generally: yes (s. 7(א)(1)). The company's formulas, client list and process documents: no (ss. 5–6).
4. **Forum.** A dispute with his former employer about secrets or the non-compete goes to the regional labour court (CTL s. 22); compensation for the invention goes to the Committee.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your own employment or contractor agreement (or a sample from a friend, with permission), or Samir's facts.
- The [IP asset inventory](../../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md), part A.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find every clause in the contract about confidentiality, IP ownership, inventions, non-competition and waiver of compensation; copy them into a table (10 minutes).
2. For each restraint, apply the AES test: what legitimate interest, how long, where, which activities (10 minutes).
3. List three items of information from your work: decide for each whether it is a trade secret (s. 5) or general skill (s. 7(א)(1)) (10 minutes).
4. If you have invented anything at work, draft a short written notice to the employer under s. 131: what the invention is, when you made it, and whether you claim it is not a service invention (10 minutes).
5. Write one question for a labour lawyer about the weakest clause (5 minutes).

None. A minimal s. 131 notice:

```text
לכבוד [שם המעביד]
הנדון: הודעה על אמצאה לפי סעיף 131 לחוק הפטנטים, התשכ"ז–1967

2. [לעמדתי האמצאה אינה אמצאת שירות, משום ש___ / האמצאה הושגה עקב שירותי.]
3. [אופציונלי, לפי סעיף 132(ב): בהיעדר תשובה נוגדת בתוך שישה חודשים ממועד הודעה זו, תקום האמצאה לקנייני.]
4. אין באמור כדי לגרוע מזכותי לתמורה לפי סעיף 134 לחוק.
[תאריך, שם, חתימה]
```

### How you know it worked

- Every restraint in your contract has an AES analysis with a conclusion: likely valid, likely limited, or likely void.
- You can say which information you may use after leaving and which you may not.
- Your notice is in writing, dated and reserves the compensation right.

- [ ] I checked whether my contract settles or waives invention compensation.
- [ ] I know that employer-employee secret disputes go to the labour court.
- [ ] I did not include more confidential detail in my notice than necessary.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for reviewing your own contract, sending a s. 131 notice, and representing yourself in the labour court (as an employee, in your own name) or before the Registrar. Before the Compensation and Royalties Committee, self-representation by the employee is not regulated in the sources checked (⚠️ manual check). An employer company usually needs a lawyer in court. Drafting for or advising other employees as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Labour court: claims about secrets, non-competes and employment (lessons [17.1](../module-17/lesson-01.md), [22.2](../module-22/lesson-02.md)); fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Service-invention status: the Registrar (s. 133); compensation: the Committee (s. 134), through the Patent Office (⚠️ manual check of the filing procedure).
- Free help: union legal departments for organised workers; Kol-Zchut's employment pages (⚠️ manual check).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your former employer threatens an ex parte seizure order or injunction (CTL ss. 16–17).
- You are about to sign an exit agreement with a waiver of rights in inventions.
- A service invention is commercially significant: Committee proceedings are technical and the stakes can be high.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copying the client list "for reference" before leaving | Misappropriation; injunction, damages (AES) | Return and delete; document it | Take only your own contacts and skills |
| Relying on secrecy without measures | No trade secret (s. 5) | Introduce measures now | Mark, restrict, contract |
| Treating a sweeping non-compete as void and ignoring it | Liability for the valid part | Comply with the protectable part | Get an AES analysis before you start |
| Signing an exit waiver without reading it | Compensation claim lost (Barzani) | Usually none | Read and negotiate before signing |
| Suing a former employee in the District Court over secrets | Wrong forum; transfer and delay | Move to the labour court | CTL s. 22 |

## Review

- A trade secret is non-public business information giving an advantage and kept secret by reasonable measures (CTL s. 5); misappropriation includes use in breach of a contractual or fiduciary duty (s. 6).
- General professional skills are free; public-policy use is excused (s. 7).
- Non-competes are valid only to protect legitimate interests and only to a reasonable extent (AES).
- Service inventions belong to the employer unless agreed otherwise or waived within six months of notice; compensation is fixed by agreement or by the Committee, and the right can be waived (ss. 131–135; Barzani).
- Employer-employee trade-secret disputes belong to the labour court (CTL s. 22); designs made with substantial use of the employer's resources belong to the employer (Designs Law s. 12).
