# 32.6 · Patents and designs: the basics

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell whether something you built might be patentable, what you must not do before filing, whether its look can be protected as a design (registered or not), who owns it if you made it at work or on commission, and what filing costs and how long protection lasts, so you can decide whether and when to see a patent attorney.

## Why it matters

Patents and designs are the two IP rights where timing is everything. A patent can be lost by one social-media post before filing; a design can be protected for three years without any filing, but only against copying, and only if you register within a year if you want more. Many inventors also spend money on patents for inventions that are not new, or that nobody will license, when a design or simple secrecy would have served them better. Patent drafting is a professional skill, and this lesson does not teach you to draft claims; it teaches you to make the decisions that come before and around a patent attorney's work.

## The law

### Patentability

> אמצאה, בין שהיא מוצר ובין שהיא תהליך, בכל תחום טכנולוגי, שהיא חדשה, מועילה, ניתנת לשימוש תעשייתי ויש בה התקדמות המצאתית – היא אמצאה כשירת פטנט.

*Gist:* A product or process in any field of technology is patentable if it is new, useful, industrially applicable and involves an inventive step. ([Patents Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפטנטים))

> אמצאה נחשבת לחדשה, אם לא נתפרסמה בפומבי, בין בישראל ובין מחוצה לה, לפני תאריך הבקשה – (1) על ידי תיאור, בכתב או במראה או בקול או בדרך אחרת, באופן שבעל מקצוע יכול לבצע אותה לפי פרטי התיאור; (2) על ידי ניצול או הצגה, באופן שבעל מקצוע יכול לבצע אותה לפי הפרטים שנודעו בדרך זו.

*Gist:* Novelty is absolute: any public disclosure anywhere before the application date, in writing, picture, sound, use or display, that would let a skilled person carry out the invention destroys it, including the inventor's own disclosure. (same, s. 4)

> התקדמות המצאתית היא התקדמות שאינה נראית כענין המובן מאליו לבעל מקצוע ממוצע על סמך הידיעות שכבר נתפרסמו, לפני תאריך הבקשה, בדרכים האמורות בסעיף 4.

*Gist:* Inventive step means the advance is not obvious to an average skilled person in light of everything published before the application date. (same, s. 5)

The only exceptions to novelty are narrow: disclosure obtained from the inventor and published without consent, with prompt filing; display at a recognised exhibition, or a lecture to a scientific society notified to the Registrar in advance, with filing within six months (s. 6). There is no general grace period for the inventor's own posts, articles or sales. Methods of medical treatment of the human body, and new plant and animal varieties, are excluded (s. 7). Israel is first-to-file (s. 9), and a foreign application in a Paris Convention or WTO country gives twelve months' priority for an Israeli filing (s. 10).

### Process and rights

Applications are published for public inspection 18 months from filing or priority (s. 16א); after examination and acceptance, acceptance is published and anyone may oppose within three months (s. 30). The patent owner may prevent others from exploiting the invention, as claimed or in a similar way that takes "the essence of the invention" (s. 49(א)). The term is twenty years from the application date (s. 52), subject to renewal fees (s. 56). A person who exploited the invention in good faith in Israel, or made real preparations, before the application date may continue (s. 53). In an infringement claim the court may award damages, considering direct loss, the scope of infringement, the infringer's profits and a reasonable royalty, and up to double where the infringement continued after a warning (s. 183).

### Filing fees

The filing fee for a patent application (first 50 claims) is set in the Patent Regulations, updated for 2026; an applicant who is not a company or partnership (or a small company, or an academic institution) pays 60% for a first application for a given invention. (Patent Regulations, Second Schedule, item 1; amounts in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

### Designs: what is protected

> ”עיצוב“ – מראהו של מוצר או של חלק ממוצר, המורכב ממאפיין חזותי אחד או יותר של המוצר או של חלק מהמוצר, לפי העניין, ובכלל זה מיתאר, צבע, צורה, עיטור, מרקם או החומר שממנו הם עשויים;

*Gist:* A design is the visual appearance of a product or part of a product. ([Designs Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העיצובים)) It may be registered if it is new and of individual character (s. 3).

> עיצוב ייחשב בעל אופי ייחודי, אם הרושם הכללי שהוא יוצר אצל משתמש מיודע שונה מהרושם הכללי שיוצר עיצוב אחר, שפורסם בציבור לפני המועד הקובע, אצל משתמש מיודע;

*Gist:* Individual character means the overall impression on an informed user differs from that of any earlier published design, taking into account the designer's freedom in that field. (same, s. 7) Features dictated solely by function, and "must-fit" interfaces, are excluded (s. 10).

> פרסום בציבור של עיצוב לא יפגע בכשירות להגנה על העיצוב כעיצוב רשום ... אם נעשה במהלך 12 החודשים שקדמו למועד הקובע ... בידי בעל העיצוב או כתוצאה ממידע שמקורו בבעל העיצוב ...

*Gist:* Unlike patents, a design has a twelve-month grace period: the owner's own publication in the year before filing does not destroy registrability. (same, s. 9)

### Designs: ownership, term and unregistered designs

- **Owner (ss. 11–13):** the designer; but the employer for a design made by an employee for the job or with substantial use of the employer's resources (s. 12), and the **commissioning party** for a commissioned design unless agreed otherwise (s. 13). Note the contrast with copyright, where the commissioned author keeps the right (Copyright Law s. 35(א)).
- **Registered design:** up to 25 years from the application, subject to renewal fees (ss. 39–40).
- **Unregistered design (ss. 4, 61–65):** a new design of individual character, offered for sale or distributed commercially in Israel within six months of its first publication, is protected for three years, against copying only (s. 61); the owner may still apply to register within twelve months (s. 64).
- **Remedies:** injunction, damages and statutory damages up to a ceiling per infringement (ss. 74–75; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- **Copyright overlap:** a design used or intended for industrial production has no copyright (Copyright Law s. 7).

## Leading cases

### ע"א 345/87 Hughes Aircraft Company נ' מדינת ישראל (2.7.1990)

Hughes owned an Israeli patent for head-up display systems using holographic lenses; the State ordered similar systems for its aircraft from another supplier. The District Court found the patent valid but not infringed, reading the claims narrowly. The Supreme Court (Shamgar P.) reversed: a patent is read as a whole, and although the monopoly is defined by the claims, the claims are interpreted in light of the description and drawings; a defendant who does not copy literally but takes the substance of the invention infringes; the patentee bears the burden of proving infringement, while once a patent is registered, the party attacking validity bears the burden; and earlier documents may not be combined to show lack of novelty unless the combination was obvious to a skilled person. Why it matters: it remains the Israeli starting point for claim interpretation and for the "essence of the invention" now written into s. 49(א), and it shows why claim drafting decides what you own. [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/hughes-aircraft-company-v-state-israel)

### Storck: shapes belong to design law

Lesson 32.5 showed that the Supreme Court in Storck refused to let trade-mark law protect a functional or aesthetic product shape indefinitely. The consequence for this lesson: if the value of your product lies in how it looks, the right tool is a design, registered early, not a trade mark later. [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/storck-kg-v-alpha)

## How it works in practice

### Choosing the right protection for a product

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| Is the value in how it works, and is it new and not obvious? | Consider a patent; file before any disclosure |
| Is the value in how it looks? | Register a design (grace period of 12 months for your own publication); you have 3 years' unregistered protection against copying if you start selling in Israel |
| Can the feature be kept secret even after sale (a process, a recipe, a setting)? | Consider a trade secret instead of a patent (lesson 32.7) |
| Is it a drawing, manual or software not used for industrial production? | Copyright protects it automatically |
| Is the value in the brand? | Trade mark (lessons 32.5, 32.9) |

### Before you talk to anyone

1. **Do not publish.** No posts, videos, crowdfunding pages, sales or conference talks before filing a patent application.
2. **Use a non-disclosure agreement** for manufacturers, investors and testers; a disclosure under a duty of confidence is not "public".
3. **Keep a dated inventor's notebook:** what you built, when, with whom.
4. **Search prior art yourself first:** Google Patents and the Patent Office's databases. If you find your idea already published, you have saved the attorney's fee.

### What filing involves

- **Patent:** a specification with a description, drawings and claims (ss. 11–13). The claims define your monopoly (Hughes). Individuals may file their own application, but a professionally drafted one is usually worth the cost; patent attorneys have the exclusive right to do this for pay for others (s. 154). A first application gives a year to decide whether to file abroad (s. 10, Paris priority; or the PCT route).
- **Design:** drawings or photos of the product from several views, the product type and the classification; fees per design, reduced for individuals and small businesses ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Both through the Patent Office's online services (⚠️ manual check of the current e-filing system and identification method).

## Worked example

Dalia Ben-David, an industrial-design student in Holon, developed a folding baby-bottle drying rack. The hinge mechanism lets it fold flat in one move; the rack's curved petal shape is striking.

1. **The hinge.** Possibly patentable: it is a product, useful and industrial. Novelty: she showed it in a class presentation to 20 students and her lecturer, with no confidentiality. Is that "public"? A class presentation without a duty of confidence may well be a disclosure; she should tell a patent attorney the exact facts before filing. She must not post the video she planned.
2. **The petal shape.** A design candidate: new and of individual character to an informed user of kitchen accessories. If she posted photos on Instagram three months ago, the twelve-month grace period (Designs Law s. 9) still lets her register. If she starts selling in Israel, she also gets three years of unregistered protection against copying.
3. **Ownership.** A student project: does the college's regulation claim rights? She checks. If a company commissioned the rack from her, the company would own the design (s. 13) unless agreed otherwise, but she would keep copyright in her drawings unless assigned, and the copyright would end once the design is used industrially (Copyright Law s. 7).
4. **Plan.** Prior-art search on Google Patents for folding drying racks (one evening); a meeting with a patent attorney about the hinge and the class presentation; file a design application for the petal shape herself, within the grace period.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A product you made or want to make (or Dalia's rack).
- Access to [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/).
- The [IP asset inventory](../../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md), part C.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write a one-paragraph description of the product: what it does and how it looks (5 minutes).
2. Separate the functional features from the appearance features (5 minutes).
3. Search Google Patents with three different keyword combinations for the functional feature; note the three closest documents and how your feature differs (20 minutes).
4. List every disclosure you have made: who, when, where, under confidence or not (5 minutes).
5. Decide for each feature: patent, design, secret or nothing; note the deadlines (12 months for priority abroad, 12 months design grace, 6 months for unregistered-design sales) (10 minutes).
6. Write the three questions you would bring to a patent attorney (5 minutes).

[IP asset inventory](../../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md), parts B–D.

### How you know it worked

- Your search found documents and you can state the difference in one sentence each.
- Every disclosure is listed with a date and whether it was confidential.
- Each feature has a protection route and a deadline.

### Self-check

- [ ] I have not published the functional invention.
- [ ] I separated function (patent) from appearance (design).
- [ ] I checked who owns the design if it was commissioned or made at work.
- [ ] I know the filing fees and that individuals pay a reduced fee for a first application.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may file your own patent or design application and conduct your own prior-art search; the law reserves this work for others, for pay, to patent attorneys and lawyers (Patents Law s. 154). Filing your own design application is realistic. Drafting patent claims yourself is legally allowed but risky: a narrow or unclear claim protects little, and missing elements cannot be added later without losing the filing date.

### Where and how

- Search: [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/) and the Patent Office's patent and design databases (⚠️ manual check of current addresses on gov.il).
- File: the Patent Office's online services; fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free reading: WIPO's patent and design guides (Learn more).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The invention may be commercially valuable: get a patent attorney before any disclosure.
- You have already disclosed it and want to know whether an exception applies (s. 6).
- You receive a warning letter claiming infringement of a patent: continuing after a warning can double the damages (s. 183(ג)).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A crowdfunding video before filing | Novelty destroyed; patent invalid | Get advice at once on s. 6 exceptions; consider design or secrecy | File first, then publish |
| Assuming the design grace period applies to patents | Patent lost | None for the patent | Patents have no general grace period |
| Relying only on unregistered design protection | Protection only against copying, for 3 years | Register within 12 months (s. 64) | Decide early on registration |
| Ignoring that a commissioned design belongs to the client | Designer cannot sell it elsewhere (s. 13) | Agree on ownership in writing | Address design rights in the commission contract |
| Drafting narrow claims to save money | Competitors design around them | Amend before acceptance if possible | Use a patent attorney for claims |

## Review

- A patent requires novelty (absolute, worldwide), utility, industrial application and inventive step; the inventor's own disclosure destroys novelty, with narrow exceptions (ss. 3–6).
- The patent lasts 20 years from filing, is published after 18 months, can be opposed within three months of acceptance, and is defined by its claims read with the description (Hughes).
- A design protects appearance: registered for up to 25 years if new and of individual character, with a 12-month grace period; unregistered for 3 years against copying if sold in Israel.
- A commissioned design belongs to the commissioning party (Designs Law s. 13), unlike a commissioned copyright work.
- Industrial designs lose copyright (Copyright Law s. 7); product shapes are rarely trade marks (Storck).
