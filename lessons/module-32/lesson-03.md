# 32.3 · Infringement, fair use and moral rights

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a real use of someone's work (a photo on a website, a song in a video, a quotation in a post, a modified drawing) and decide whether it infringes copyright or the author's moral right, which permitted use may cover it, and what a court could award, so that you can decide whether to claim, settle or defend.

## Why it matters

Copyright cases in Israel are usually won or lost on three points: whether what was taken was a substantial part of the work, whether a permitted use applies, and how much to award. Statutory damages (פיצויים ללא הוכחת נזק) make even a single photo used without permission a real financial risk for a business, and a real opportunity for a photographer, which is why small claims and magistrates' courts see many such cases. The moral right adds a uniquely personal layer: even a lawful use can be wrongful if the author is not named or the work is distorted.

Fair use itself was covered in lesson [21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md) (the s. 19 purposes and four factors, Safecom, Premier League and Nestlé). This lesson places it among the other permitted uses and the remedies.

## The law

### The exclusive rights and direct infringement

> זכות יוצרים ביצירה היא הזכות הבלעדית לעשות ביצירה, או בחלק מהותי ממנה, פעולה, אחת או יותר, כמפורט להלן, בהתאם לסוג היצירה:

*Gist:* Copyright is the exclusive right to do, in the work or in a substantial part of it, the acts listed in s. 11: copying (including electronic storage and temporary copies, s. 12), first publication, public performance, broadcasting, making available to the public (online, at a time and place chosen by the user, s. 15), making a derivative work (s. 16) and, for some works, rental. ([Copyright Law s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים))

> העושה ביצירה פעולה מהפעולות המפורטות בסעיף 11, או מרשה לאחר לעשות פעולה כאמור, בלא רשותו של בעל זכות היוצרים, מפר את זכות היוצרים, אלא אם כן עשיית הפעולה מותרת לפי הוראות פרק ד׳.

*Gist:* Doing one of those acts, or authorising someone else to do it, without the owner's permission infringes, unless a permitted use in chapter 4 applies. (same, s. 47(א))

### Indirect infringement

> העושה אחת מהפעולות האלה בעותק מפר של יצירה, מפר את זכות היוצרים, אם בעת ביצוע הפעולה, ידע או היה עליו לדעת כי העותק הוא עותק מפר:

*Gist:* Selling, renting, holding for business, distributing commercially, displaying commercially or importing (other than for personal use) an infringing copy infringes if the person knew or should have known it was infringing. (same, s. 48(א)) Section 48א, added in 2019, extends indirect infringement to a person who, as a business and for profit, facilitates or expands public access to a work made available illegally online, knowing or having to know it (for example, a site that profits from organised links to pirated streams).

### Permitted uses

Chapter 4 (ss. 18–32) lists uses allowed without permission or payment. Besides fair use (s. 19, lesson 21.5), the ones you are most likely to meet:

> שימוש ביצירה בהליכים משפטיים או בהליכים מינהליים לפי דין, לרבות דיווח על הליכים אלה, מותר בהיקף המוצדק בשים לב למטרת השימוש כאמור.

*Gist:* Using a work in legal or administrative proceedings, including reporting on them, is allowed to the extent justified by the purpose. (same, s. 20) You may attach the infringing page, the photo or the contract to your own court filing.

- **Incidental use (s. 22):** a work caught incidentally in a photo, film or recording; deliberately including music in another work is not incidental.
- **Works in a public place (s. 23):** architecture, sculpture and applied art permanently in a public place may be photographed, drawn and broadcast.
- **Software (s. 24):** backup copies and copies needed for use, maintenance or compatibility, within limits.
- **Temporary copies (s. 26):** technical copies made in transmission or lawful use, without independent economic value.
- **Orphan works (s. 27א, added 2019):** a work whose owner cannot be found after reasonable diligence may be used with a clear notice and contact details, stopping or paying a reasonable royalty once the owner appears; commercial use also requires a prior public notice.

### The moral right

> זכות מוסרית ביחס ליצירה היא זכות היוצר – (1) כי שמו ייקרא על יצירתו בהיקף ובמידה הראויים בנסיבות העניין; (2) כי לא יוטל פגם ביצירתו ולא ייעשה בה סילוף או שינוי צורה אחר, וכן כי לא תיעשה פעולה פוגענית ביחס לאותה יצירה, והכל אם יש באילו מהם כדי לפגוע בכבודו או בשמו של היוצר.

*Gist:* The author has the right to be named on the work as appropriate in the circumstances, and the right that the work not be distorted, mutilated or subjected to a derogatory act that harms the author's honour or reputation. (same, s. 46) It belongs to the author of literary (other than software), artistic, dramatic and musical works, is personal and non-transferable, and survives an assignment of the copyright (s. 45).

> על אף הוראות סעיף קטן (א), עשיית פעולה ביצירה הפוגעת בזכות המוסרית האמורה בסעיף 46(2) אינה מהווה הפרה של הזכות האמורה, אם הפעולה היתה סבירה בנסיבות העניין;

*Gist:* A change to the work is not an infringement of the integrity right if it was reasonable in the circumstances; the court may consider the nature of the work and of the act, whether the work was made by an employee or on commission, industry practice, and the need for the act against the harm to the author. (same, s. 50(ב)–(ג)) After the author's death, close relatives may sue (s. 55).

### Remedies

> הופרה זכות יוצרים או זכות מוסרית, רשאי בית המשפט, על פי בקשת התובע, לפסוק לתובע, בשל כל הפרה, פיצויים בלא הוכחת נזק בסכום שלא יעלה על 100,000 שקלים חדשים.

*Gist:* For each infringement of copyright or the moral right, the court may award damages without proof of damage up to a statutory ceiling (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), considering the scope, duration and severity of the infringement, actual damage, the defendant's profit, the nature of its activity, the relationship between the parties and its good faith. Infringements in one series of acts count as one. (same, s. 56)

- **Injunction (s. 53):** the claimant is entitled to an injunction unless the court finds reasons not to grant one.
- **The online exception (s. 56א):** no statutory damages against someone who posted online an artistic work (such as a photo) that was already publicly available, if they stopped within a reasonable time after the owner's notice, the work was not copied from a sale or licensing catalogue, and the use was non-commercial or by a small non-profit (turnover ceiling in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- **Innocent infringer (s. 58):** no damages if the infringer did not know and should not have known that copyright existed in the work; it is a narrow defence, because almost everything is protected.
- **Accounts (s. 57), infringing copies (s. 60), no injunction against a building already started (s. 59).**
- **Offences (ss. 61–62):** commercial copying, importing, dealing and large-scale illegal streaming are crimes; a private criminal complaint is possible (lesson [05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md)).

## Leading cases

### ע"א 3616/92 דקל שירותי מחשב להנדסה בע"מ נ' חשב – יחידה בין־קיבוצית לשירותי ניהול (10.12.1997)

The defendant copied price lists the claimant had published in eleven booklets. Under the old Copyright Ordinance, the District Court counted one infringement and refused statutory damages because actual damage was not proved. The Supreme Court (Strasberg-Cohen J.) held that "infringement" means each infringement of a separate copyright, so copying eleven booklets, each with independent economic value, was eleven infringements; and that statutory damages do not depend on proving damage, though evidence offered for actual damage may be used to fix them. Why it matters: it is the foundation of the Israeli approach that statutory damages are counted per work and need no proof of loss. Under the 2007 Law, s. 56(ג) treats infringements "in one series of acts" as one, and how that rule combines with the per-work count is argued case by case. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/dekel-computer-engineering-services-ltd-v-heshev-inter-kibbutz)

### ע"א 1697/11 גוטסמן נ' ורדי (23.1.2013)

An architect published computer simulations of a unique house he had designed on his firm's website, against the owner's objection. The Supreme Court (Vogelman J.) held that publishing simulations of the interior infringed the owner's privacy, which outweighed the architect's economic interest, but that the exterior, which is visible to passers-by, could be shown. Why it matters: owning the copyright in your own work does not license every use of it; other rights (privacy, contract, confidentiality) can limit how you show your portfolio, and the outcome depends on a balance the court strikes. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/gottesman-v-vardi)

### ע"א 7996/11 סייפקום בע"מ נ' רביב (18.11.2013)

Already covered twice (lessons 21.5 and 32.2). For infringement, its lesson is the comparison: the court looks at whether a substantial part was copied, qualitatively, and with functional works protection is thinner, so the copying must be closer. Almost exact copying of drawings into a competitor's patent application infringed and was not fair. [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/safecom-ltd-v-raviv)

### Creative Commons licences enforced

As reported by Stanford's Center for Internet and Society, an Israeli court in 2010–2011 enforced for the first time a Creative Commons licence (attribution, non-commercial, no derivatives) against a publisher that printed 15 amateur photos from Flickr in a book: the breach of the licence was treated as copyright infringement, fair use was rejected because attribution was missing, and statutory damages were awarded per photo. (⚠️ The court, case number and amounts are not given in the report; the ruling text was not verified.) [Stanford CIS report](https://cyberlaw.stanford.edu/blog/2011/01/israeli-court-enforces-first-time-creative-commons-license/)

## How it works in practice

### The infringement checklist

1. **Is there a work and do you own it (or hold an exclusive licence)?** Only the owner or exclusive licensee may sue for copyright (s. 54); only the author (or close relatives after death) for the moral right (s. 55).
2. **What act was done?** Copying, making available online, public performance, a derivative work (s. 11). The statute does not say whether a mere link is itself "making available"; it does make a business that profits from organised access to illegal copies an indirect infringer (s. 48א).
3. **Was a substantial part taken?** Quality matters more than quantity: the heart of a song or the key image of a series can be substantial even if short.
4. **Was there permission?** A licence, including an open licence such as Creative Commons, must be followed exactly; using outside its conditions is infringement.
5. **Does a permitted use apply?** Fair use (s. 19), legal proceedings (s. 20), incidental use (s. 22), public-place works (s. 23), orphan works (s. 27א).
6. **Moral right:** was the author named appropriately; was the work changed in a way that harms honour or reputation; was the change reasonable (s. 50)?
7. **Remedies and their limits:** injunction, actual or statutory damages, the s. 56א online exception, the innocent-infringer defence (s. 58).

### Statutory damages in practice

Courts award statutory damages well below the ceiling in ordinary cases (a single photo on a small business's site) and higher amounts for commercial, deliberate or repeated infringement, using the s. 56(ב) factors. There is no tariff: read recent judgments in your court for comparable facts before you set your demand, and remember that a demand far above what courts award weakens your position in settlement and can affect costs.

### Where claims are filed

- An individual's money claim up to the small-claims ceiling: small claims court ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); a claim for an injunction or large damages: usually the District Court, which also hears IP claims joined to them (Courts Law s. 40(4)).
- Limitation: seven years, as for most civil claims (lesson [15.9](../module-15/lesson-09.md)).

## Worked example

Avraham Cohen, a nature photographer from Safed, finds three of his photos of the Hula valley on two sites.

1. **A guesthouse's website** uses one photo as its header, with no credit. Avraham owns the copyright (he took it; s. 33(1)). Making it available online is an act under s. 11(5); no permission; no permitted use (commercial advertising, whole work, market harm under s. 19(ב)). The missing credit also infringes the attribution right (s. 46(1)). The guesthouse is a business, so s. 56א does not apply. Remedies: removal, statutory damages (within s. 56), possibly a separate amount for the moral right.
2. **A hiking blog run by a retiree** posts two photos with Avraham's name, copied from Avraham's own public Instagram. The use is non-commercial and the photos were already public. If the blogger removes them within a reasonable time after Avraham's notice, s. 56א bars statutory damages; Avraham can still claim actual damage, which is probably small. A polite notice is the right first step.
3. **A local magazine** prints one photo, cropped and recoloured, in an article criticising tourism development, with a credit. Criticism is a fair-use purpose, but the use of the whole image as decoration rather than the subject of the critique weighs against fairness (lesson 21.5). The recolouring may affect integrity, but a change can be reasonable in the circumstances (s. 50(ב)–(ג)).

Avraham's plan: notice to the blogger; a demand letter to the guesthouse with a settlement offer; a letter to the magazine asking for a licence fee, keeping a small claim as the fallback.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One real case: a work of yours used without permission, or a use of someone else's work you are considering (a photo for your business page, music in a video).
- The checklist above and lesson 21.5's four factors.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the facts in five lines: the work, the owner, the act, the user, the context (5 minutes).
2. Run the seven-step checklist and write one sentence of conclusion per step (20 minutes).
3. If you are the owner: list the s. 56(ב) factors that support a higher or a lower award, and decide on a realistic settlement figure (10 minutes).
4. If you are the user: decide whether to get a licence, change the use (credit, shorter quotation, your own photo), or stop (5 minutes).
5. Write the first message you would send, in three sentences, without threats you cannot carry out (10 minutes).

### Template

The demand letter in the [copyright notice and response kit](../../templates/copyright-notice-and-response-kit.md) (part C); lesson 32.8 uses all of it.

### How you know it worked

- Your conclusion names the act, the permitted use you considered and why it does or does not apply.
- Your figure is justified by the s. 56(ב) factors, not by the statutory ceiling.
- Your message is accurate, polite and keeps your options open.

- [ ] I checked that the claimant owns the copyright or an exclusive licence.
- [ ] I considered all the relevant permitted uses, not only fair use.
- [ ] I checked attribution and integrity separately from copyright.
- [ ] I checked s. 56א for non-commercial online uses of photos.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. An individual may sue in their own name in any civil court (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170), and copyright claims within the small-claims ceiling are often handled without lawyers. A company usually needs a lawyer outside small claims. Bringing claims for other photographers or authors as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- First a notice or demand letter (lesson 32.8), then, if needed, a small claim ([08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)) with the evidence of authorship, screenshots with date and URL, and your calculation.
- Court fees and the small-claims ceiling: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Attaching the infringing material to your claim is a permitted use (s. 20).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side is a large company with lawyers and denies your ownership.
- You need an urgent injunction (lesson [15.5](../module-15/lesson-05.md)), for example against a product launch.
- You are accused of large-scale or commercial infringement, which may also be a criminal offence (ss. 61–62).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demanding the statutory ceiling for one photo on a small site | No settlement; court awards far less; possible costs | Revise the figure using s. 56(ב) | Research comparable judgments first |
| Using a Creative Commons photo without the required credit | Infringement; fair use rejected | Add the credit as the licence requires, or remove | Copy the licence terms when you save the image |
| Assuming a credit makes any use lawful | Copyright still infringed | Get a licence or rely on a real permitted use | Credit is the moral right, not permission |
| Suing for the moral right as a buyer of the copyright | No standing: only the author (s. 55) | The author must sue | Check who holds which right |
| Ignoring s. 56א against a private blogger | Statutory damages refused | Claim actual damage or settle | Send a notice first and wait a reasonable time |

## Review

- Copyright is infringed by doing, or authorising, a s. 11 act in a substantial part of the work without permission (s. 47); dealing in infringing copies or profiting from access to illegal online copies is indirect infringement (ss. 48, 48א).
- Permitted uses include fair use (s. 19), legal proceedings (s. 20), incidental use (s. 22), public-place works (s. 23) and orphan works (s. 27א).
- The moral right protects attribution and integrity, stays with the author, and is subject to a reasonableness defence for changes (ss. 45–50).
- Statutory damages need no proof of damage and are counted per infringement, a series of acts being one (s. 56; Dekel); s. 56א and s. 58 limit them.
- Owning copyright does not override other people's rights, such as privacy (Gottesman).
