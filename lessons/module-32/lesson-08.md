# 32.8 · Practice lab: protect your own work and send or answer a takedown notice

In this lab you build a protection file for one of your own works, find out whether it is used elsewhere, prepare the right first step against an infringer (a platform request or a demand letter), and then switch sides and answer a demand letter of the kind photo agencies and authors send to businesses, so that you know both sides of an online copyright dispute.

## Why it matters

Israel has no copyright registry, so when a dispute comes, the person who can prove authorship and the date of creation quickly is in the stronger position. Most disputes then end at the first letter: a platform removes the content, a business pays a licence fee, or the claimant drops a weak demand. The quality of that first step decides whether you recover money, reach a fair settlement, or end up in court on poor terms.

## The law

> מופיע על היצירה בדרך המקובלת שמו של אדם כיוצר היצירה, חזקה היא שאותו אדם הוא יוצר היצירה;

*Gist:* A name shown on a work in the usual way creates a presumption of authorship in any civil or criminal copyright proceeding, unless rebutted. ([Copyright Law s. 64(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים))

> הופרה זכות יוצרים או זכות מוסרית, ואולם המפר לא ידע ולא היה עליו לדעת, במועד ההפרה, כי קיימת זכות יוצרים ביצירה, לא יחויב בתשלום פיצויים עקב ההפרה.

*Gist:* An infringer who did not and should not have known that copyright existed in the work pays no damages (s. 58); a visible credit and licence notice make this defence much harder to use. Only the owner or exclusive licensee may sue for copyright, and only the author for the moral right (ss. 54–55); statutory damages and their online exception are in ss. 56–56א (lesson 32.3).

## Leading cases

The cases for this lab were covered in lessons 32.3 and 32.4: Dekel (statutory damages counted per work, no proof of damage needed), the Creative Commons enforcement reported by Stanford CIS (licence conditions matter), Rotter (notice and takedown for hosts) and Mor v. Barak (no disclosure of anonymous users outside the copyright chapter). Reread the relevant one before you send anything.

## How it works in practice

- **No registration, only evidence.** Keep originals (camera RAW files, layered design files, drafts with version history), first publication with date, and any contract or assignment. Metadata is useful but easy to alter; several independent sources are stronger than one.
- **Notices on the work.** "© [name] [year]" is not required for protection, but it uses the s. 64 presumption and undercuts s. 58. A short licence line ("שימוש מסחרי בתיאום מראש" or a Creative Commons licence) tells honest users how to get permission.
- **Find copies.** Reverse image search (Google Lens, TinEye, Bing) for photos and graphics; quoted-phrase searches for text.
- **First step.** Platform form for content on a platform; demand letter for a business site; a polite notice for a private non-commercial user (s. 56א). Court only if these fail (lessons [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md), [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md)).
- **When you receive a demand.** Do not ignore it and do not pay at once: check ownership, the facts and the defences; remove the work while you check; answer without admissions.

## Worked example

Noa Shapira, a freelance illustrator in Kiryat Gat, discovers that a Tel Aviv event-planning company uses her illustration of the Jaffa clock tower on its home page, cropped to remove her signature.

1. **Evidence.** She exports the original layered file with its creation date, her Instagram post from two years ago, and a PDF and screenshot of the company's page with the URL and date.
2. **Rights.** She is the author and owner (s. 33); the cropped signature also raises the attribution right (s. 46(1)) and the integrity right, if the crop harms her reputation (s. 46(2)).
3. **Route.** A business site: a demand letter (kit part C) asking for removal within seven days and a licence fee of ₪2,500 for past use, reasoned by the commercial use, the removal of her signature (bad faith factor, s. 56(ב)(8)) and the period of use.
4. **Result.** The company removes the image, says a former employee took it from "Google Images", and offers ₪1,200. Noa answers that searching on Google does not create permission, and they settle at ₪1,800 with a written licence for the past use only.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One work of your own: a photo, an illustration, an article, a video or a piece of code.
- The [copyright notice and response kit](../../templates/copyright-notice-and-response-kit.md) (all four parts) and the [IP asset inventory](../../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md).
- A computer with a browser; a folder for the evidence file.
- 150 minutes, in four sessions if you like.

### Steps

**Part 1: the protection file (35 minutes)**

1. Create a folder named for the work. Put in it the original file(s), any drafts, and the first publication (link, screenshot, date).
2. Write a one-page "work card": title, type, date of creation, date and place of first publication, author, owner (and why, under ss. 33–35), any licence you gave.
3. Add a credit line and licence statement to your published copy, if appropriate. If you choose a Creative Commons licence, read the licence chooser and record the exact licence ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/)).

**Part 2: find uses (25 minutes)**

4. Run a reverse image search (or a quoted-phrase search for text) on two search engines.
5. For each use found, record the URL, the user (private, business, platform), the date and whether there is a credit. Save a PDF and a screenshot of each.

**Part 3: the first step against one use (40 minutes)**

6. Choose one use (or, if you found none, use Noa's facts) and decide the route: platform request (kit part A), demand letter (part C) or polite notice.
7. Check before sending: are you the owner (s. 54); is there a permitted use (ss. 19–23); does s. 56א apply; what figure do the s. 56(ב) factors support?
8. Draft the request or letter in full. Do not send it unless it concerns your own work and you have decided to act.

**Part 4: switch sides (40 minutes)**

9. Assume your small business received this letter: "You used our client's photograph on your website for 14 months without a licence. Pay ₪15,000 within 7 days or we will sue for statutory damages."
10. Fill in the kit's part D: ownership proof requested, the facts, any licence, permitted uses, s. 56א, s. 58.
11. Remove the photo, and draft a reply without admissions that asks for proof of ownership and opens a discussion.
12. Decide your settlement range, and when you would get a lawyer.

**Part 5: the log (10 minutes)**

13. Start a log: date, step, reply, next deadline. A clear log helps in any later small claim.

### Template

[Copyright notice and response kit](../../templates/copyright-notice-and-response-kit.md), parts A–D.

### How you know it worked

- Your protection file would let a stranger confirm, in five minutes, who made the work and when.
- Your first step names the work, your ownership, the use, the legal ground, a clear request and a deadline, and contains no threat you are not ready to carry out.
- Your reply to the demand asks for proof, makes no admission, and stops the use.

### Self-check

- [ ] My evidence includes originals and the first publication, not only screenshots.
- [ ] I checked fair use, s. 56א and s. 58 before sending anything.
- [ ] My figure is justified by the s. 56(ב) factors.
- [ ] In part 4 I removed the work at once and answered without admissions.
- [ ] Nothing was sent about anyone else's work.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Protecting, licensing and enforcing your own works, sending notices and letters, filing platform requests and suing in your own name are all open to you (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). Doing this for other creators as an occupation or for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Where and how

- Platform forms: lesson 32.4 (⚠️ check the live form).
- Letters: e-mail and registered mail to the business's registered address (Companies Registrar extract, lesson [16.8](../module-16/lesson-08.md)).
- Small claim, if needed: lessons [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md) and [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md); the ceiling and fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side denies your ownership with documents of its own.
- You received a demand for a large sum that names several works or a long period, or a court claim.
- The infringer is abroad and the amounts justify service abroad (lesson [30.2](../module-30/lesson-02.md)).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keeping only screenshots of your own work | Weak proof of authorship | Find originals, drafts, first posts | Archive originals at creation |
| Threatening "₪100,000 per photo" | No settlement; credibility lost | Send a corrected, reasoned figure | Use the s. 56(ב) factors |
| Paying a demand without checking ownership | Paying the wrong person, or too much | Ask for proof before paying more | Always request proof first |
| Ignoring a demand | Default judgment risk if sued | Answer now, even briefly | Diary every deadline |
| Admitting liability in your first reply | Admission used against you | Keep later letters factual | Use "without admission" wording |

## Review

- Israel has no copyright registry: authorship is proved by originals, first publication and the s. 64 presumption.
- A credit and licence notice strengthen your position and weaken the innocent-infringer defence (s. 58).
- Choose the first step by the user: platform form, demand letter to a business, polite notice to a private user (s. 56א).
- When you receive a demand: stop the use, ask for proof, check permitted uses and s. 56א, answer without admissions.
- Keep a log; it becomes your evidence if the matter reaches a small-claims court.
