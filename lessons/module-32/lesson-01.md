# 32.1 · What intellectual property protects: an overview

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take any creation or business asset of your own (a photo, a logo, a gadget you built, a recipe, a customer list) and say which Israeli law can protect it, whether you must register anything, who owns it first, how long the protection lasts, and what it will not protect, before you spend money on registration or a lawyer.

## Why it matters

Intellectual property (קניין רוחני) is a set of separate statutory monopolies, each with its own subject, conditions, owner, duration and remedies. People lose rights not because the law is unkind but because they reach for the wrong one: they "copyright" a business name (copyright does not protect names), publish an invention on social media before filing a patent (and destroy its novelty), or assume that a freelancer's logo belongs to whoever paid for it (under the Copyright Law the default is the opposite). Others threaten competitors with rights they do not have and end up paying costs.

Israeli law is also unusual in one respect worth learning early: there is no general "anti-copying" right. If none of the statutes protects something, copying it is usually lawful, and the Supreme Court has refused to use unjust enrichment to create a monopoly the Knesset did not grant (see Leading cases). Knowing the edges of each regime is therefore as important as knowing its centre.

## The law

### Copyright: exclusive and automatic

> לא תהא זכות יוצרים ביצירה אלא לפי הוראות חוק זה.

*Gist:* Copyright exists only under the Copyright Law 2007; there is no common-law copyright beside it. ([Copyright Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכות_יוצרים))

> זכות יוצרים תהא ביצירות אלה: (1) יצירה מקורית שהיא יצירה ספרותית, יצירה אמנותית, יצירה דרמטית או יצירה מוסיקלית, המקובעת בצורה כלשהי; (2) תקליט;

*Gist:* Copyright subsists in original literary, artistic, dramatic and musical works fixed in any form, and in sound recordings, provided there is a connection to Israel or a treaty (ss. 8–9). No registration exists or is needed. (same, s. 4(א))

> זכות יוצרים ביצירה כאמור בסעיף 4 לא תחול על כל אחד מאלה, ואולם על דרך ביטוים תחול זכות היוצרים: (1) רעיון; (2) תהליך ושיטת ביצוע; (3) מושג מתמטי; (4) עובדה או נתון; (5) חדשות היום.

*Gist:* Ideas, processes, mathematical concepts, facts and the news of the day are free for all; only their expression is protected. (same, s. 5) The term is the author's life plus seventy years (s. 38).

### Trade marks: registration gives the exclusive right

> אדם המבקש לו זכות ייחודית להשתמש בסימן פלוני כסימן מסחר רשאי לבקש רישום הסימן על פי הוראות פקודה זו.

*Gist:* The exclusive right to a trade mark comes from registering it with the Registrar of Trade Marks. ([Trade Marks Ordinance s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סימני_מסחר)) A mark must be distinctive (s. 8), and registration lasts ten years from the application, renewable (ss. 31–32). Only a registered or a well-known mark supports an infringement action (s. 57(א)); an unregistered mark is protected, if at all, by passing off.

### Passing off and trade secrets: the Commercial Torts Law

> לא יגרום עוסק לכך שנכס שהוא מוכר או שירות שהוא נותן, ייחשבו בטעות כנכס או כשירות של עוסק אחר או כנכס או כשירות שיש להם קשר לעוסק אחר.

*Gist:* A trader must not cause his goods or services to be mistaken for another trader's, or for goods connected with him. ([Commercial Torts Law s. 1(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עוולות_מסחריות)) The same law protects trade secrets (ss. 5–6) and allows statutory damages of up to a ceiling for each tort (s. 13; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### Patents and designs: examination and registration

> אמצאה, בין שהיא מוצר ובין שהיא תהליך, בכל תחום טכנולוגי, שהיא חדשה, מועילה, ניתנת לשימוש תעשייתי ויש בה התקדמות המצאתית – היא אמצאה כשירת פטנט.

*Gist:* A patentable invention is a product or process in any field of technology that is new, useful, industrially applicable and involves an inventive step. ([Patents Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפטנטים)) A patent lasts twenty years from the application date (s. 52).

> ”עיצוב“ – מראהו של מוצר או של חלק ממוצר, המורכב ממאפיין חזותי אחד או יותר של המוצר או של חלק מהמוצר, לפי העניין, ובכלל זה מיתאר, צבע, צורה, עיטור, מרקם או החומר שממנו הם עשויים;

*Gist:* A design is the appearance of a product or part of one: outline, colour, shape, ornament, texture or material. ([Designs Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העיצובים)) A registered design lasts up to 25 years from the application (s. 39); an unregistered design, three years from the relevant date (s. 65).

### Where IP cases are heard

> תביעה בעניני קנין רוחני, הכרוכה בתביעה בעניני קנין רוחני שהיא בסמכות בית המשפט המחוזי לפי פסקה (1), אף אם סכום התביעה או שווי נושא התביעה אינו עולה על הסכום כאמור בסעיף 51(א)(2);

*Gist:* The District Court may hear an IP claim (under the listed laws: copyright, patents, trade marks, commercial torts, designs and others) joined to an IP claim within its jurisdiction, even below the magistrates' ceiling. ([Courts Law s. 40(4)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט)) A money claim within the magistrates' or small-claims ceiling may be filed there (ss. 51, 60).

## Leading cases

### ע"א 8485/08 The FA Premier League Ltd נ' המועצה להסדר ההימורים בספורט (25.1.2010)

The English and Scottish leagues claimed copyright in their published fixture lists, which Israel's sports-betting monopoly used on its betting forms. The Supreme Court (Joubran J., with Naor and Hendel JJ.) held that originality requires both investment and a minimal degree of creativity; listing all the matches in the most logical order showed no creativity, so there was no copyright, and in any case only the information needed was taken. The alternative claim in unjust enrichment failed too: such a claim beside IP law is allowed only where the defendant engaged in improper business practices, which was not the case. Why it matters: it is the clearest statement that Israeli law has no general right against copying; where the IP statutes do not protect, copying is usually free, and "it cost me a lot to make" is not enough. (⚠️ Hebrew caption rendered from the English translation.) [English translation, Cardozo Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/fa-premier-league-v-israel-sports-betting-council)

### Telran and the limits of unjust enrichment

Module 20 covers the Supreme Court's Telran judgment (decoder cards for foreign channels): no copyright infringement, but the case was remanded on unjust enrichment. Read it there rather than here: [20.8 · Unjust enrichment](../module-20/lesson-08.md). Together, Telran and Premier League set the rule you apply in this module: first ask which statute protects; only if none does, ask whether the defendant's conduct was improper enough for unjust enrichment, and expect that it usually is not.

## How it works in practice

### The map

| Regime | What it protects | Registration? | First owner | Duration | Typical remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copyright | Original expression: text, photos, art, music, software, compilations | None; arises on fixation | Author; employer for works made by an employee for work (s. 34) | Life + 70 years (s. 38); State works 50 years (s. 42) | Injunction, damages or statutory damages up to a ceiling per infringement (s. 56) |
| Moral right | Attribution and integrity of the author's work | None | Author only; not transferable (s. 45) | As copyright | Damages, statutory damages |
| Trade mark | Signs that distinguish goods or services | Yes, at the Patent Office | Applicant who uses or intends to use it | 10 years, renewable (ss. 31–32) | Injunction, damages, destruction (ss. 59, 59א) |
| Passing off | Goodwill in a name, get-up or look | None | The trader whose goodwill it is | As long as the goodwill lasts | Injunction, damages, statutory damages (CTL s. 13) |
| Patent | New, inventive, industrially applicable inventions | Yes, examined | Inventor or successor; employer for service inventions (s. 132) | 20 years from filing (s. 52) | Injunction, damages (s. 183) |
| Design | Appearance of a product | Registered or unregistered | Designer; employer; commissioning party (ss. 11–13) | 25 years registered; 3 years unregistered | Injunction, damages, statutory damages (s. 75) |
| Trade secret | Business information kept secret with reasonable measures | None | Whoever lawfully controls it | As long as it stays secret | Injunction, damages, seizure order (CTL ss. 13–17) |

### Overlaps and gaps

- **A logo** can be an artistic work (copyright, owned by its designer unless assigned) and a trade mark (registered by the business). The business needs both: an assignment from the designer and a registration.
- **A product's shape** may be a design; copyright does not protect a design used or intended for industrial production (Copyright Law s. 7). The shape is a trade mark only in narrow cases (lesson 32.5).
- **An invention** is either patented (published, protected for 20 years) or kept as a trade secret (unpublished, protected only against improper taking). You cannot do both for the same feature once the patent application is published.
- **A name, a slogan or a short phrase** is usually too short for copyright; protection, if any, is through a trade mark or passing off.
- **A business method, a format or an idea** is protected by none of the regimes; at most by a contract (a non-disclosure agreement) or by being first to market.

### Where to deal with it

- **Israel Patent Office (רשות הפטנטים)**: patents, designs, trade marks, the trade-mark database and the registers. Fees (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- **Courts**: the District Court for injunctions and large claims; the magistrates' court or small claims for money within their ceilings (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)).
- **Customs**: may detain suspected infringing imports (Copyright Law s. 65; Trade Marks Ordinance s. 69א).
- **Police and private complaint**: copyright and trade-mark offences are criminal (Copyright Law ss. 61–62; Trade Marks Ordinance s. 60), and the Second Schedule to the Criminal Procedure Law lets the owner bring a private criminal complaint (lesson [05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md)).

## Worked example

Rawan Haddad, a ceramicist in Haifa, sells hand-painted mugs online under the name "חרס רוואן" with a logo a freelancer drew for her. She has also developed a glaze recipe nobody else uses and a jig that lets her paint straight lines quickly.

1. **The paintings on the mugs.** Original artistic works: copyright, automatically, owned by Rawan as author. But if the painted pattern is used for industrial production (say she licenses it to a factory), s. 7 removes copyright and only design protection remains.
2. **The logo.** Copyright belongs to the freelancer unless they agreed otherwise (s. 35(א)); Rawan should get a written assignment (s. 37(ג)). For exclusivity in the name and logo for ceramics, she should search the trade-mark database and apply to register (lesson 32.9).
3. **The name "חרס רוואן".** No copyright. Registrable as a trade mark if distinctive; meanwhile, passing off protects her only once she has goodwill.
4. **The glaze recipe.** Not a patent candidate unless new and inventive; easiest to keep as a trade secret, with reasonable secrecy measures (CTL s. 5).
5. **The jig.** Possibly patentable; she must not post a video of it before filing, because publication destroys novelty (Patents Law s. 4).
6. **The mug shape.** If new and of individual character, a registered design; if she started selling it in Israel recently, possibly an unregistered design for three years, and she can still register within twelve months (Designs Law ss. 4, 64).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A list of things you or your business created or use: texts, photos, videos, a logo, a name, products, a method, a client list, software.
- The [IP asset inventory](../../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md) worksheet.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. List at least six assets of your own (10 minutes).
2. For each, write which regime could protect it, and which cannot, using the map above (10 minutes).
3. For each, record who created it, whether that person was an employee or a freelancer, and whether there is a written agreement (5 minutes).
4. For each, mark whether protection is automatic or needs registration, and the duration (5 minutes).
5. Pick the two most valuable assets and write the one next action for each: get an assignment, start a secrecy routine, search the trade-mark database, keep dated drafts, do not publish before filing (10 minutes).

### How you know it worked

- Every asset has a regime or "none", with the reason.
- You can say who owns each asset today and what document proves it.
- Your two actions are concrete and dated.

- [ ] I did not claim copyright in a name, an idea or a method.

- [ ] I know which assets need registration to be protected at all.
- [ ] I have not published anything I might want to patent.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for the inventory and planning. Registering a trade mark, a design or a patent in your own name is open to an individual: the Patents Law gives patent attorneys (and lawyers) the exclusive right to do this work **for pay** for others (s. 154), which does not prevent you from filing your own. Advising others on their IP is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20) and, before the Patent Office, to patent attorneys.

### Where and how

- Trade marks, designs and patents: the Israel Patent Office online services (lessons 32.6 and 32.9); fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Copyright: nothing to file; build your evidence of authorship (lesson 32.8).
- Free reading: WIPO's guides in Learn more below; Kol-Zchut has no detailed IP guide.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are about to invest in a product launch, a brand or an invention that depends on exclusivity.
- Someone accuses you of infringement and demands money or an injunction.
- An invention may be patentable: patent drafting is a specialised skill, and a badly drafted claim cannot be fixed later.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Copyrighting" a business name | No protection at all for the name | Search and apply for a trade mark | Use the map: names are marks |
| Assuming a paid freelancer's work belongs to you | The freelancer owns the copyright (s. 35(א)) | Get a written assignment (s. 37(ג)) | Put IP terms in every commission contract |
| Posting an invention before filing | Novelty lost; no valid patent | Consult a patent attorney at once about the narrow exceptions (s. 6) | File first, publish later |
| Suing for "copying" something no statute protects | Claim dismissed, costs (Premier League) | Reassess under each regime | Check the regime before you threaten |
| Using a product shape you saw abroad because "it has no Israeli patent" | Possible design, trade-mark or passing-off claim | Stop, check designs and marks | Search before you copy |

## Review

- Israeli IP is a set of separate regimes: copyright and moral rights, trade marks, passing off, patents, designs and trade secrets, each with its own conditions, first owner and term.
- Copyright, unregistered designs, passing off and trade secrets arise without registration; trade marks, registered designs and patents need the Patent Office.
- Ideas, facts, methods and names are not copyright subject matter (s. 5).
- Outside the statutes, copying is usually lawful; unjust enrichment helps only against improper conduct (Premier League, Telran).
- Ownership defaults matter: employees' works go to the employer (s. 34); commissioned works stay with the author (s. 35(א)), except family portraits and event photos (s. 35(ב)).
