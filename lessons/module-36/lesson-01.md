# 36.1 · From proseminar to seminar: what changes

By the end of this lesson you will know what Israeli law faculties require of a seminar paper, why "original contribution" is the one thing that separates it from the proseminar paper you wrote in module 21, and how to plan a 30-page paper that makes such a contribution. You will leave with a one-page seminar research plan and a 12-week timeline.

## Why it matters

In module 21 you learned the whole craft of a first legal paper: a research question ([21.1](../module-21/lesson-01.md)), the free journals ([21.2](../module-21/lesson-02.md)), structure ([21.3](../module-21/lesson-03.md)), footnotes ([21.4](../module-21/lesson-04.md)), integrity and AI ([21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md)) and a 15-page paper ([21.6](../module-21/lesson-06.md)). None of that is repeated here. The seminar paper uses all of it and asks for more.

Every Israeli law faculty makes its students write at least one seminar paper. At Tel Aviv University it is a 4-credit seminar in year 3 with an independent paper, and a one-time library-databases session (course 1411-7777) is a condition for submitting it. The Hebrew University, Haifa and Reichman require two seminars; Bar-Ilan requires 8 credits of seminars (see the curricula notes in Sources). For many students the seminar paper is the longest piece of legal writing they produce before the internship, and for some it becomes their first publication or the writing sample in a job application.

What changes is not only length. A proseminar paper shows that you can find, organise and argue from what is already known. A seminar paper must add something: new data, a source nobody has read closely, a comparison not yet made, a better account of a line of rulings, or a proposal that the literature has not considered. Supervisors grade the contribution first. A well-written 30-page survey still fails the test that matters.

## The law

The seminar paper is governed by faculty rules, not statute. Two kinds of rule matter: what the programme requires, and how the paper must be written and submitted.

### What the programme requires (TAU yedion 2025–26)

> בשנה ג' כל תלמיד חייב להשתתף בסמינר בהיקף 4 ש"ס ולהגיש בו עבודה סמינריונית עצמאית. יש להשתתף בהדרכה חד-פעמית בנושא שימוש במאגרי מידע בספרייה כתנאי להגשת העבודה הסמינריונית.

*Gist:* In year 3 every TAU law student must take a 4-credit seminar and submit an independent seminar paper in it, and attending a one-time session on the library's databases is a condition for submitting the paper. ([TAU yedion 2025–26, programme 1411-11-01-0000, s. 2.3](https://www.tau.ac.il/tochniot/pdf/2025/heb/141111010000.pdf)) The session appears in the same yedion as course 1411-7777, "הדרכה בספרייה ובמאגרי מידע משפטיים".

### Independent work, and no second use of a paper (TAU faculty regulations s. 82)

> כל עבודה סמינריונית המוגשת על ידי תלמיד לתואר בוגר חייבת להיות עצמאית ופרי עטו (ללא שותפים).

> אסור לתלמיד להגיש עבודת בית או עבודה סמינריונית זהה או דומה לעבודה שהוגשה על ידו בקורס אחר.

*Gist:* An undergraduate seminar paper must be the student's own independent work, with no co-authors, and a student may not submit a paper identical or similar to one already submitted in another course. ([TAU Faculty of Law study regulations 2016–17, s. 82(א), (ד)](https://yedion-law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/yedion2016-17/takanon2016-7.pdf)) This is the most recent full edition of the faculty regulations the course could open on 2026-10-18; check the current edition on the faculty's site before you rely on details.

The second rule matters more at seminar level than in the proseminar, because the obvious move is to "expand" your 15-page proseminar paper to 30 pages. That is a similar paper. You may build on it, but only with a new question or a new contribution, and only after you tell the supervisor (the disclosure rule is in [21.1](../module-21/lesson-01.md)).

## Leading cases

No ruling governs seminar papers. The best teachers are two published papers that each make a different kind of contribution. Both are free.

### קרן וינשל-מרגל ואלון קלמנט "יישום חוק תובענות ייצוגיות בישראל – פרספקטיבה אמפירית" משפטים מה 707 (2016)

The contribution is **new data**. The authors built an original database of every class-action application filed in Israel during the study period (N = 2,056) and used it to test claims that until then had been argued from anecdote: that class actions deter wrongdoing, give access to court and compensate victims. They found that the benefit was limited mainly to enforcement and stopping breaches, with little compensation or access to court. Why it matters as a model: the abstract says in one sentence what is new ("לראשונה נתונים מתוך מאגר נתונים מקורי"), and the paper first builds a framework (four goals, costs against benefits) and only then counts. Data without a framework is a table, not a contribution. [Free PDF (TAU class actions centre)](https://law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/Tovanot/Articles/1481038186i86JF.pdf)

### אסף לחובסקי "בין 'מנדט' ל'מדינה': על חלוקת ההיסטוריה של המשפט הישראלי לתקופות" משפטים כט 689 (התשנ"ט)

The contribution is **a new framing** of known material. Likhovski argues that dividing Israeli legal history into "Mandate" and "State" reflects ideological assumptions, and shows that developments usually dated to the 1950s, in labour law and constitutional-rights discourse, began during the Second World War, while changes one would expect after 1948 (a break with English law) did not happen. Why it matters as a model: no new archive is needed to make a contribution; asking a different question about familiar sources can change how a whole field is read. [Free PDF (משפטים)](https://lawjournal.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/2018-03/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%93%D7%98%20%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94.pdf) (the HUJI server sometimes refuses automated downloads; open it in a browser).

## How it works in practice

### Proseminar and seminar side by side

| | Proseminar (module 21) | Seminar (this module) |
|---|---|---|
| Length used in this course | About 15 pages | About 30 pages of text (roughly 10,000–12,000 words) plus footnotes; your seminar's own rules prevail |
| What it must show | You can find, organise and argue from existing sources | You add something the literature does not have |
| Sources | Statute, leading rulings, 8+ articles | All of that, plus primary sources read at scale (lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md)) |
| Method | Named in a paragraph | Designed and defended in its own section (lesson [36.2](lesson-02.md)) |
| Supervision | One meeting on the proposal | A seminar group, several meetings, a presentation (lesson [36.5](lesson-05.md)) |
| Writing process | One draft and a self-review | Chapters, an argument map and two or more revision cycles (lesson [36.4](lesson-04.md)) |
| Grade | The paper | The paper; often also the presentation and participation |

### Six kinds of original contribution

1. **New data.** You count, code or measure something no one has: rulings on a section, registry entries, outcomes in one court (Weinshall-Margel and Klement).
2. **A neglected primary source.** The committee protocols of a law nobody has read since enactment, an early bill that was changed, an unpublished set of decisions (lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md)).
3. **A new comparison.** A system that solved your problem differently and has not been compared with Israel on this point.
4. **A doctrinal synthesis or critique.** You show that a line of rulings, read together, rests on a test the courts have never stated, or contradicts itself.
5. **A new framing.** You ask a different question about familiar material (Likhovski).
6. **A proposal.** A rule, reading or reform that the literature has not put forward, with its costs.

A seminar paper usually combines one main contribution with a second, smaller one (for example, a doctrinal critique supported by a small data set).

### The gap sentence

State your contribution as a gap in one sentence before you start writing: "Earlier work has examined ___, but no one has ___; this paper ___." If you cannot fill in the middle blank with something you checked in the literature (lesson [21.2](../module-21/lesson-02.md)), you do not yet have a contribution. Supervisors often ask for exactly this sentence in the first meeting.

### How a seminar runs

Most seminars meet weekly or every two weeks for a semester or a year. Typical milestones: a topic and a one-page plan by week 3 or 4; a meeting with the teacher; a presentation of work in progress to the group; a full draft; submission after the semester. At TAU in 2016–17 the faculty set fixed submission dates for each semester, with up to two weeks' extension by the lecturer and two more by the faculty officer; check your own year's dates (lesson [36.5](lesson-05.md)).

### The library session

The TAU session (1411-7777) introduces the library's paid databases. As a self-study learner you do not need them: everything in this module uses free sources (Wikisource, the Knesset's databases, Reshumot, Net HaMishpat, the free journals). If you are enrolled at a university, attend the session anyway; it is a condition of submission and it shows you the English-language databases that a comparative chapter needs.

## Worked example

Avraham Tesfaye, a student from Netanya, wrote his proseminar paper on whether the Class Actions Law's list of permitted claims (the Second Schedule, lesson [33.1](../module-33/lesson-01.md)) is coherent. It was a doctrinal paper based on the statute, three Supreme Court rulings and four articles.

1. **Tempting move:** expand the proseminar paper to 30 pages. Under TAU s. 82(ד) that risks a "similar paper"; it also adds no contribution.
2. **Look for a gap.** In Weinshall-Margel and Klement he reads that their data end in the early 2010s. The Second Schedule has been amended since, and the Knesset is debating a further amendment (lesson 33.1).
3. **Choose the contribution.** Main: new data, a count of applications against mobile operators in the Class Actions Registry for two recent years, coded by the type of breach. Secondary: a neglected source, the committee protocols of the 2006 Law on why consumer claims against any "business" were included.
4. **Gap sentence.** "Earlier empirical work counted all class actions up to the early 2010s, but no one has examined what recent consumer applications against mobile operators claim; this paper codes them and asks whether they serve the purposes in s. 1 of the Law."
5. **Research question.** "Do recent class-action applications against mobile operators serve the access-to-court purpose of the Class Actions Law, or mainly its enforcement purpose?"
6. **Disclosure.** His plan says the proseminar paper exists and that the new paper uses its framework chapter only as background, with a new question and new data.
7. **Neutrality.** The plan lists the strongest case for the current law (enforcement against small, widespread harms) and the strongest case for reform (meritless claims), and commits to stating both before arguing (lesson [21.3](../module-21/lesson-03.md)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your proseminar paper or proposal from module 21, or a new topic.
- The [seminar research plan](../../templates/seminar-research-plan.md) template.
- 75 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write down your seminar's rules if you are enrolled: length, deadline, presentation, grade components. If you are studying alone, use the course defaults: 30 pages, 12 weeks, one presentation to a study partner (5 minutes for this step).
2. Choose one of the six kinds of contribution as your main one, and one as secondary (10 minutes).
3. Search the literature for the closest existing work (lesson 21.2) and note what it does not do (20 minutes).
4. Write the gap sentence (10 minutes).
5. Write the research question and a provisional thesis (10 minutes).
6. Fill in the rest of the research plan: method, primary sources, risks, and the 12-week timeline (20 minutes).

### Template

[Seminar research plan](../../templates/seminar-research-plan.md), parts A to C.

### How you know it worked

- Your gap sentence names a specific earlier work and a specific thing it does not do.
- Someone who reads only the plan could tell what is new in your paper.
- Your question cannot be answered by your proseminar paper.
- The timeline ends at least two weeks before your deadline.

### Self-check

- [ ] I chose a main and a secondary contribution.
- [ ] I checked the literature before claiming a gap.
- [ ] If I build on an earlier paper of mine, the plan says so.
- [ ] My plan fits my seminar's rules (or the course defaults).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Planning original research is an academic skill. It shows up in your own legal affairs when existing rulings do not answer your question: then the "gap" is in the case law, and you must build the argument yourself from the statute, its history and its purpose.

### Where and how

In your own case, the gap sentence becomes a paragraph in your claim, defence or appeal: "No Supreme Court ruling has decided whether ___. The statute's language, its legislative history and its purpose point to ___." Lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md) shows how to find the legislative history for such a paragraph, and lesson [02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md) how courts weigh it.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Your case depends on a point no court has decided and much is at stake. Arguing a new point is legitimate, but courts rarely accept a novel reading from a self-represented party without strong support; free help is in [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expanding the proseminar paper | "Similar paper" problem (TAU s. 82(ד)); no contribution | New question, new data or new source; disclose the earlier paper | Start from the gap sentence, not from your old file |
| Claiming a gap you did not check | The supervisor names the article that already did it | Search again; narrow the claim | Fill in the literature map before the plan |
| A survey of 30 pages | Fails the contribution test however well written | Choose one of the six contributions and cut the rest | Write the gap sentence first |
| A contribution that needs data you cannot get | Weeks lost | Switch to a source you can reach free (lesson 36.3) | Test access in week 1 |
| Ignoring the seminar's own rules | Late or rejected paper | Read the syllabus and faculty rules now | Note every rule in part A of the plan |

## Review

- At TAU the seminar is 4 credits in year 3, the paper must be independent, and the library-databases session (1411-7777) is a condition of submission; other faculties require two seminars or 8 credits.
- The seminar paper must make an original contribution: new data, a neglected source, a comparison, a synthesis, a framing or a proposal.
- State the contribution as a one-sentence gap that names the closest existing work.
- Do not resubmit or merely expand an earlier paper; build on it only with a new question and with disclosure.
- Plan the paper as a 12-week project with milestones, not as a long proseminar.
