# 36.2 · Original research: doctrinal, comparative, empirical and historical

By the end of this lesson you will be able to choose the method your seminar question needs, design it in enough detail that a supervisor can check it, and run a small pilot: for an empirical paper, coding fifteen entries from a free Israeli court database; for a comparative or historical paper, a one-page plan of sources and comparisons. Lesson [21.3](../module-21/lesson-03.md) introduced the methods in a paragraph each; here you build one.

## Why it matters

A proseminar paper can say "the method is doctrinal" in one line. A seminar paper's contribution usually lives in its method: the data you collected, the archive you read, the system you compared. If the method is weak, the contribution falls with it. Supervisors read the method section to answer three questions: could someone else repeat what you did, does it actually answer your question, and do you know its limits?

You do not need statistics software or a research grant. Israeli law offers free material that a student can work through in a few weeks: the Class Actions Registry, decisions on Net HaMishpat, the Judiciary's annual statistics (lesson [11.4](../module-11/lesson-04.md)), the Knesset's legislative records (lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md)) and the free foreign databases for comparison. What you need is a clear design.

## The law

Empirical and historical papers use court files. Two sections of the Courts Law set the limits on what you may report.

### Hearings are public by default (Courts Law s. 68(א))

> בית משפט ידון בפומבי.

*Gist:* Courts sit in public. ([Courts Law s. 68(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט)) This is why most civil and criminal decisions, and the documents in the Class Actions Registry, are open for research. Section 68(ב) lists the grounds on which a court may sit behind closed doors (state security, foreign relations, protecting minors and vulnerable persons, sexual offences, trade secrets and others), and s. 68(ה) makes family matters and some juvenile proceedings closed by default.

### What you may not publish (Courts Law s. 70)

> לא יפרסם אדם דבר על דיון שהתנהל בבית משפט בדלתיים סגורות אלא ברשות בית המשפט.

> לא יפרסם אדם שם או מספר זהות של בעל דין התובע פיצויים בשל נזקי גוף, למעט פיצויים בשל עוגמת נפש, אלא אם כן ניתנה הסכמתו של אותו בעל דין לפני הפרסום או ברשות בית המשפט.

*Gist:* No one may publish anything about a hearing held behind closed doors without the court's permission, and no one may publish the name or ID number of a party claiming compensation for bodily injury without that party's consent or the court's permission. ([Courts Law s. 70(א), (ג1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט)) A seminar paper is a publication if it is circulated or posted. Practical rule: code family, juvenile and personal-injury cases without names, report only aggregates, and cite decisions the way the court itself published them (often with initials).

## Leading cases

There are no rulings on research method. The texts below are the models; each is free.

### קרן וינשל-מרגל ואלון קלמנט "יישום חוק תובענות ייצוגיות בישראל – פרספקטיבה אמפירית" משפטים מה 707 (2016)

An empirical design to copy. The paper defines its **population** (all class-action applications filed during the study period, N = 2,056, not a sample), explains that data collection was done partly in cooperation with the Judiciary's research department, and sets out the method in its own part (part ב) between the theory and the findings. The findings are mostly descriptive: trends in filings, outcomes of applications that ended, use of the Law's special mechanisms, and costs. Why it matters: it shows that a strong empirical legal paper can rest on counts and percentages if the framework is clear. [Free PDF](https://law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/Tovanot/Articles/1481038186i86JF.pdf)

### אסתר חיות "התובענה הייצוגית ככלי לאכיפה אזרחית-ציבורית" משפט ועסקים יט 935 (2016)

How data enter the legal debate. Writing as a Supreme Court justice, Hayut opens with the "disappointing results" of the empirical study presented at a 2014 conference (many meritless claims and little involvement of public authorities as applicants), then asks which legislative and judicial mechanisms let the class action still serve public purposes. Why it matters: an empirical finding is the start of a legal argument, not its end; the doctrinal chapters that follow are what make it law. [Free PDF](https://law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/Tovanot/Articles/1479313050s50Us.pdf)

### אסף לחובסקי "בין 'מנדט' ל'מדינה'" משפטים כט 689 (התשנ"ט)

A historical design: the paper tests a periodisation (1948 as the break) against two fields, labour legislation and constitutional-rights discourse in the 1940s, and against two "revolutions that did not happen" (Jewish law and Continental law as replacements for English law). Why it matters: a legal-historical paper needs a claim about change or continuity, tested on chosen fields, not a chronicle. [Free PDF](https://lawjournal.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/2018-03/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%93%D7%98%20%D7%9C%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94.pdf)

For a comparative-and-empirical model, reread Elkin-Koren and Netanel from lesson [21.2](../module-21/lesson-02.md).

## How it works in practice

### Which method for which question

| Your question asks | Main method | What makes it original | Typical free sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is the law, really, on X? | Doctrinal | A synthesis or critique of all the rulings, not the famous three | Statute (Wikisource), rulings (lesson [02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)) |
| How do others solve X? | Comparative | A comparison not yet made, or one that explains a difference | Foreign official databases (below) |
| How does X work in practice? | Empirical | New data | Class Actions Registry, Net HaMishpat, Judiciary annual report |
| Why is the law on X as it is? | Legal-historical | A neglected source or a new framing | Knesset records, Reshumot, Wikisource history (lesson 36.3) |

### Doctrinal at seminar level

1. **Define the corpus.** "All Supreme Court rulings citing s. 19 since 2008", not "the leading cases". Use the free-text search methods in lesson 02.2.
2. **Make a case table.** For each ruling: date, bench, facts in one line, holding on your point, test used, dissent.
3. **Read across the table.** Look for a pattern the courts never stated, a conflict between two lines, or a test that changed without acknowledgment. That is your synthesis.
4. **State limits.** Unreported decisions and settled cases are invisible to doctrinal research.

### Comparative

- **Functional question.** Compare how systems solve the same problem ("how is a small-sum mass claim brought?"), not institutions with the same name.
- **Choose systems for a reason.** English law (common origin of much Israeli private law), a system Israel borrowed from (a legal transplant), or one that solved the problem differently.
- **Context.** A rule works inside courts, costs rules and culture; describe the context before you recommend a transplant.
- **Free foreign sources.** UK statutes: [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/); UK and Irish case law: [BAILII](https://www.bailii.org/); EU law: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/); US federal law: [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/). Cite them by the Bluebook inside Hebrew footnotes (lesson [21.4](../module-21/lesson-04.md)).
- **Classic error:** a "comparative" chapter that describes the foreign law for ten pages and never returns to Israel.

### Empirical with simple data

You need counts, percentages and medians, not regression. The design has six parts.

1. **Population.** What are you studying? "All class-action applications against mobile operators opened in 2024–2025 in the registry."
2. **Unit.** One row per what? Per application (not per decision, not per company).
3. **Source and collection.** The [Class Actions Registry](../module-33/lesson-01.md) on Net HaMishpat, advanced search by party name and opening date. Note that the registry's list shows a limited number of documents per search, so narrow by year and company and record each search you ran.
4. **Coding sheet.** Variables with fixed categories decided in advance (below). Add a "notes" column for anything that does not fit.
5. **Reliability check.** Code every tenth entry twice, a week apart (or ask a study partner to code them). If more than one in ten disagree, your categories are unclear; fix them and recode.
6. **Descriptive results and limits.** Counts and percentages per category; state what the data cannot show (settlements outside court, cases not in the registry, cases opened before the registry's current version).

```text
Coding sheet (one row per application)
id | case no. | court | opened (yyyy-mm) | defendant | item in Second Schedule | type of breach (billing / disclosure / accessibility / other) | sum claimed (band) | status (open / withdrawn / settled / certified / dismissed) | coder | notes
```

For figures from the Judiciary's annual report (caseloads, durations), follow lesson 11.4 and always give the year of the data.

### Legal-historical

- **Claim about change.** "The 2006 Law changed X; earlier practice was Y; the reason was Z." A chronicle is not a contribution.
- **Sources.** The Knesset's bills, explanatory notes and committee protocols, the original Reshumot text, and the version history of the Wikisource text (lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md)). Older material: the Israel State Archives and the National Library's historical newspapers (both block automated access; open them in a browser).
- **Avoid anachronism.** Read a 1950s protocol against the law and language of the 1950s, not today's doctrine.
- **Avoid "the Knesset intended".** One member's speech is evidence of one member's view (lesson [02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md)).

### The method section

One to two pages, usually at the end of the introduction or as part ב: the question, the design, the sources and how you searched them, the coding or comparison scheme, and the limits. Put the coding sheet or the list of searches in an appendix.

## Worked example

Chaya Rosenberg, a student from Bnei Brak, asks: "Do small-claims courts apply the Consumer Protection Law's cancellation rights consistently?" Her first plan is doctrinal, but the Supreme Court rarely hears small claims, so there is little to synthesise.

1. **Method.** She switches to an empirical design: published small-claims judgments on the right to cancel a remote sale.
2. **Population and limits.** Small-claims judgments are not all published, and searching Net HaMishpat needs a case number (lesson 02.2). She therefore defines her population as "small-claims judgments on remote-sale cancellation found through free web search and verified on Net HaMishpat, 2024–2025", and says in the method section that this is a convenience sample, not the population of all cases.
3. **Coding sheet.** Variables: court, date, product, days between purchase and cancellation notice, whether the consumer cancelled in writing, outcome, sum awarded, whether the judge cited the statute section.
4. **Reliability.** She codes 40 judgments, then recodes four of them a week later; one disagreement (on "in writing", for a WhatsApp message) leads her to split the category into "email or letter" and "messaging app".
5. **Privacy.** The judgments name private persons; her paper reports only counts and refers to judgments by case number.
6. **Results stated with limits.** "In this sample, judges cited the statute in most judgments that granted cancellation; the sample cannot show how often consumers do not sue at all."

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your seminar research plan from lesson 36.1.
- Internet access to Net HaMishpat (Class Actions Registry) or to the sources your method needs.
- A spreadsheet.
- 90 minutes.

### Steps

1. Using the table "Which method for which question", name your main method and write two sentences justifying it (10 minutes).
2. **Empirical:** define the population and the unit, write the coding sheet, and code 15 entries from the Class Actions Registry or another free source (50 minutes). **Comparative:** choose the system, write the functional question, and list five foreign primary sources you opened. **Historical:** write the claim about change and list the primary sources for each period, with where you found them.
3. Run the reliability check on three entries, or reread your list of sources and mark each as primary or secondary (10 minutes).
4. Draft a one-page method section with limits (20 minutes).

### Template

[Seminar research plan](../../templates/seminar-research-plan.md), part B (sources and risks); the coding sheet above.

### How you know it worked

- Someone could repeat your search or comparison from your method section alone.
- Each variable in your coding sheet has fixed categories and every entry fits one of them.
- Your limits paragraph names at least two things the method cannot show.
- No personal details from closed or personal-injury cases appear in your notes for the paper.

### Self-check

- [ ] My method answers my question, not a nearby one.
- [ ] I tested access to the data or sources this week.
- [ ] For empirical work, I recorded every search I ran and the date.
- [ ] For comparative work, I compare functions and return to Israel.
- [ ] For historical work, I make a claim about change, not a chronicle.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Research design is an academic skill, but its tools serve your own case. Before you sue, the registry tells you whether a class action already covers your claim (lesson [33.1](../module-33/lesson-01.md)); a small set of similar judgments shows how courts treat your kind of dispute; and the Judiciary's statistics give realistic time frames (lesson 11.4).

### Where and how

Use the same free sources. In a pleading, you may refer to similar judgments to show a consistent practice, but cite them precisely (lesson [02.5](../module-02/lesson-05.md)) and remember that only Supreme Court holdings bind. Do not attach your own spreadsheet as "evidence" of how courts decide; it is argument, not proof.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Your case turns on statistics or an expert's empirical report that the other side relies on and you cannot evaluate, or on foreign law (which must usually be proved as a fact by an expert opinion).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling a convenience sample "all cases" | Overclaiming; the supervisor rejects the findings | Rename it a sample and add limits | Define the population before collecting |
| Categories invented while coding | Inconsistent data | Freeze categories, recode | Pilot on 10–15 entries first |
| Names from closed or personal-injury cases in the paper | Breach of Courts Law s. 70 | Remove; report aggregates only | Code without names from the start |
| Comparative chapter with no return to Israel | Description, not comparison | Add the lessons for Israel and their limits | Write the functional question first |
| History as chronicle | No contribution | State the claim about change | Write the claim before reading the archive |

## Review

- The method carries the contribution: choose it to fit the question and defend it in its own section.
- Doctrinal research at seminar level means a defined corpus and a case table, not three famous rulings.
- Comparative research compares functions, picks systems for a reason and returns to Israel.
- Simple empirical work needs a population, a unit, a coding sheet, a reliability check and honest limits; counts and percentages are enough.
- Hearings are public by default, but nothing from closed hearings and no names of personal-injury claimants may be published (Courts Law ss. 68, 70).
