# 36.6 · Practice lab: write the 30-page seminar paper

In this lab you write the whole seminar paper over about twelve weeks: about 30 pages of text plus footnotes, on your own question or on one of two supplied questions, with at least one chapter built on primary sources you read systematically. You run two rounds of peer review, present the work in progress, score the paper on the seminar rubric and revise. The module project packages and submits it.

## Why it matters

Everything in this module and in module 21 comes together here: a question with a gap, a method, a primary-source chapter, a long structure kept under control, and an argument tested on other people. Plan about 24 hours of work spread over twelve weeks. The spread matters more than the hours: the best improvements come from time away from the draft and from readers who are not you.

## The law

The rules are those of lessons 36.1, 36.4 and 36.5 and of module 21: an independent paper with no co-authors and no resubmission, the library-databases session as a condition of submission at TAU, the format and title-page rules, the extension limits, the uniform citation rules (third edition) and your course's AI policy ([21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md)). Check your own seminar's syllabus before week 1; if it differs from anything here, the syllabus wins.

## Leading cases

Use the model papers of this module: Weinshall-Margel and Klement (data and structure, lessons [36.1](lesson-01.md), [36.2](lesson-02.md), [36.4](lesson-04.md)) and Likhovski (framing and history, lesson 36.1). If you take supplied question A, the class-action rulings in module 33 are your doctrinal starting point ([33.1](../module-33/lesson-01.md)).

## How it works in practice

- **Length:** about 30 pages of text, roughly 10,000–12,000 words, plus footnotes; check whether your rules count footnotes in the word limit.
- **Primary-source chapter:** at least one chapter rests on material you read systematically: a legislative history ([36.3](lesson-03.md)), a coded set of registry entries or judgments ([36.2](lesson-02.md)), or a set of foreign primary sources. Its method and limits are stated, and the coding sheet or search log goes in an appendix.
- **Peer review:** two rounds, with the protocol below. If you have no classmate, use a study partner who reads Hebrew legal writing; failing that, review your own draft after at least three days away, with the same forms.
- **AI:** follow your course's policy; for self-study, the strictest one you know (lesson 21.5).

### Peer-review protocol

| Round | When | What is exchanged | Reviewer uses | Reviewer time | Returned within |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Week 7 | Research plan, chapter plan and argument map (3–4 pages) | Questions 1–3 and 5–6 of the [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 3 | 45 minutes | 5 days |
| 2 | Week 11 | Full draft | All ten questions of part 3, plus seminar lines 11–12 of the rubric below | 2 hours | 7 days |

Rules: agree dates in writing; comment, do not rewrite; be specific (page and paragraph); start with what works; keep the draft confidential; never use the other paper's ideas in your own.

### The seminar rubric

Score lines 1–10 from the [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 2, then add four seminar lines. Each line is 0 (missing), 1 (partly) or 2 (fully).

| # | Seminar criterion | 0 / 1 / 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | The contribution is stated as a gap that names the closest existing work, and the paper delivers it | ____ |
| 12 | At least one chapter rests on primary sources read systematically, with method and limits stated | ____ |
| 13 | The structure holds over 30 pages: every chapter appears on the argument map and advances the thesis | ____ |
| 14 | Comments from the presentation and peer review are answered in a response memo and reflected in the text | ____ |
| | **Total of lines 1–14 (of 28)** | ____ |

A paper ready to submit scores at least 22 of 28, with no line at 0.

## Worked example

Maya Golan, a student from Kiryat Shmona, takes supplied question A. Week 1: she reads her seminar's rules (30 pages, word count with footnotes, presentation in week 8) and tests access to the registry and the OData service. Weeks 2–4: literature map (14 sources), gap sentence, research plan approved by the supervisor, coding sheet piloted on 15 entries. Weeks 5–6: legislative-history worksheet for s. 1 and the definition of the class (21 sessions listed, 5 read in full), and 64 registry entries coded. Week 7: round 1 review; her reviewer says the argument map has no objection on deterrence, so she adds one. Week 8: presentation; nine comments, response memo. Weeks 9–10: full draft, 33 pages. Week 11: round 2 review and rubric score 19 of 28 (weakest: line 13, chapter ה does not appear on the map). Week 12: she cuts chapter ה into the conclusion, finishes footnotes and the integrity check, rescores 24 and submits.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your work from lessons 36.1 to 36.5, or one of the supplied questions below.
- The [seminar research plan](../../templates/seminar-research-plan.md), the [legislative-history worksheet](../../templates/legislative-history-worksheet.md), the [proseminar paper outline](../../templates/proseminar-paper-outline.md) (parts B and D) and the [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md).
- A word processor with automatic footnotes; a reference library (lesson 36.4).
- A reviewer for two rounds.
- About 24 hours over twelve weeks.

**Supplied question A (legislative history and simple data).** "Do recent class-action applications against mobile operators serve the access-to-court purpose of the Class Actions Law (s. 1(1)), or mainly its enforcement purpose (s. 1(2))?" Starting sources: the Law (Wikisource and Reshumot); the bill, explanatory notes and the 21 committee sessions in the Knesset OData service (lesson 36.3); Weinshall-Margel and Klement (2016) and Hayut (2016); the Class Actions Registry (lesson 33.1) for a defined period and set of companies; the rulings of module 33.

**Supplied question B (legislative history in your own affairs).** Choose one section of a law that matters in your own life (your lease, your employment, a benefit, a tax, your consumer rights). "Does the legislative history of section X support the reading that courts now give it?" Starting sources: the law on Wikisource, its Reshumot text, every amending law that touched the section, the bills, explanatory notes and protocols (lesson 36.3), and the rulings on the section (lesson [02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)).

### Steps

1. **Weeks 1–2: rules, access, literature (3 hours).** Fill in part A of the research plan with your seminar's rules; test access to every primary source (part B); build the literature map with at least 12 sources.
2. **Week 3: plan to the supervisor (1 hour).** Gap sentence, question, thesis, method; ask for one meeting.
3. **Weeks 4–6: primary sources (6 hours).** Pilot and run the coding, or complete the legislative-history worksheet, or collect the foreign sources. Keep the search log from day one.
4. **Week 7: chapter plan, argument map, round 1 review (2 hours).** Swap with your reviewer; review theirs.
5. **Week 8: presentation and response memo (2 hours).** Lesson 36.5.
6. **Weeks 9–10: full draft (6 hours).** Primary-source chapter first, then the other argument chapters, framework, counter-arguments and proposal, conclusion, and the introduction last.
7. **Week 11: round 2 review and rubric (2 hours).** Swap full drafts; score your own draft on all 14 lines; write the weakest line.
8. **Week 12: revise and finish (2 hours).** Fix the weakest line first; footnote pass; integrity check and AI declaration ([checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md) part 1); appendices; title page with word count.

### How you know it worked

- Page 1 states the question, the thesis and the gap; the conclusion answers the question.
- One chapter rests on primary sources a reader could find again from your appendix.
- Two rounds of comments are answered in a response memo.
- The rubric score is at least 22 of 28 with no line at 0, and the paper is within the length rules.

**Method paragraph (in English for the model):** Population: all applications in the Class Actions Registry naming one of two mobile operators as respondent, opened between January 2024 and December 2025, found through the registry's advanced search by party name and opening date; each search is listed in appendix A. Unit: the application. Variables: the Second Schedule item, type of breach, sum claimed (band), status. Reliability: every tenth entry recoded after a week. Limits: the registry shows only what was filed; settlements outside court and claims never filed are invisible; two companies are not the market.

**The best counter-argument (Part ה):** deterrence benefits class members too, so a class action that mainly enforces still serves them, and Tnuva accepts relief for the public where members cannot be identified. The reply: s. 1 lists access and relief separately from enforcement, so a design that serves only one does not fulfil the others; the paper does not claim that such applications are illegitimate, only that the access purpose needs its own mechanism.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. If you take supplied question B, the paper is also a research file for your own affairs: you will know the history and purpose of a section that governs your lease, job, benefit or tax.

### Where and how

Keep the legislative-history worksheet from question B with your own records. If a dispute arises on that section, the worksheet gives you the paragraph on purpose for a letter, a claim or an appeal (lesson [36.3](lesson-03.md), "Without a lawyer"). Act in your own name only.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

Your research shows that a real dispute of yours turns on a reading the courts have not accepted, or on a section whose history conflicts with a Supreme Court ruling, and much is at stake.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starting the draft before the primary sources are read | The contribution chapter is thin and late | Stop drafting; finish the coding or worksheet | Follow the week order |
| Skipping round 1 review | Structural problems found only in week 11 | Do a quick outline swap now | Book both rounds in week 1 |
| Ignoring the rubric's seminar lines | A well-written paper with no contribution | Score lines 11–14 honestly and fix the weakest | Keep the gap sentence on your desk |
| Appendix missing | Method cannot be checked | Add the coding sheet and search log | Keep the log from day one |
| Last-minute footnotes | Wrong pinpoints, integrity risks | Footnote pass with the reference library | Enter pinpoints as you read |

## Review

- The seminar paper is a 12-week project: rules and access, literature, primary sources, plan and map, presentation, draft, review, revision.
- At least one chapter rests on primary sources read systematically, with method, limits and an appendix.
- Two rounds of peer review, with a fixed protocol, catch structural and argument problems while they can still be fixed.
- Score the paper on 14 lines (the proseminar ten plus four seminar lines) and fix the weakest before submission.
- Supplied question B turns the paper into a research file on a law that governs your own affairs.
