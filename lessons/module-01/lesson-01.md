# 01.1 · What this course can and cannot make you

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any legal task in your life (writing a contract, arguing a claim, answering a tax assessor, drafting a will) and decide whether Israeli law lets you do it yourself, whether someone other than a lawyer may help you, and when only a lawyer may act. You will also know exactly what this course is: a university-level education in Israeli law with a practice track for your own affairs, not a road to the Bar.

## Why it matters

Israeli law draws a sharp line between **acting for yourself** and **acting for someone else**. On one side of the line you have almost complete freedom: you may sue, defend, appeal, petition the High Court of Justice, write your own will, negotiate your own lease and argue your own case in every court in the country. On the other side, the Bar Association Law 1961 reserves to lawyers four kinds of activity done for another person as an occupation or for pay: representing them before courts and certain offices, drafting legal documents for them, and giving legal advice or opinions.

That line shapes everything in this course. Each "Without a lawyer" section asks the same question about a different forum, and every practice lab is built so that you act only in your own name. If you ignore the line, two things can go wrong. First, you can commit an offence (s. 96 of the Bar Association Law punishes a non-lawyer who does a reserved act). Second, and more often, the court simply refuses to hear the person you brought to speak for you, and you lose the hearing you prepared for.

The line also explains what this course cannot do. A law degree in Israel is a regulated path: a recognised academic legal education, a supervised internship and the Bar exam (s. 24). Completing this course gives you knowledge and practice; it gives you no right to represent anyone, no exemption from any exam, and no professional title.

## The law

### The reserved acts: Bar Association Law, s. 20

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; ואלה הפעולות:
> (1) ייצוג אדם אחר וכל טיעון ופעולה אחרת בשמו לפני בתי משפט, בתי דין, בוררים וגופים ואנשים בעלי סמכות שיפוטית או מעין שיפוטית; [...]
> (3) עריכת מסמכים בעלי אופי משפטי בשביל אדם אחר [...];
> (4) ייעוץ וחיווי דעת משפטיים.

*Gist:* Only a lawyer may, as an occupation or for any payment, represent another person before courts, tribunals, arbitrators and listed offices, draft legal documents for another person, or give legal advice and opinions. [Full text, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_20)

Read the opening words carefully, because they are the whole test. The section does not forbid a non-lawyer from ever helping anyone; it forbids doing the listed acts **"דרך עיסוק"** (as an occupation, even free) or **"בתמורה"** (for consideration, even once). Paragraph (2) adds a list of offices where representing someone else is equally reserved: the Execution Office, the Land Registry, the condominium superintendent, the Companies, Partnerships and Cooperative Societies Registrars, the patent, design and trademark registrars, the income tax assessor, and the land-appreciation and estate tax directors.

Note also what s. 20 does **not** cover: you, acting for yourself. Nothing in the section limits a person's right to argue their own case, sign their own contract or write their own letter.

### The exceptions: s. 21

> הוראות סעיף 20 אינן פוגעות באלה: [...]
> (2) ייצוג לפני בתי דין דתיים ולפני בתי דין צבאיים;
> (3) ייצוג לפני בית משפט, בית דין, גוף או אדם אחר שהייצוג לפניהם מוסדר בחיקוק, לרבות הוראות סעיף 236 לפקודת מס הכנסה; [...]

*Gist:* Section 20 does not apply to representation before religious and military courts, or wherever another enactment regulates representation (for example, accountants and tax advisers before the income tax assessor). [Full text, s. 21](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_21)

Other items in s. 21 cover the Attorney General's representatives, patent agents, accountants acting within their own law, representatives of trade unions and employers' organisations in labour arbitration, opinions requested by a lawyer or a state authority, and foreign lawyers in some international arbitrations. There is **no** general exception for a spouse, a parent, a friend or a company employee.

### The sanction: s. 96

> מי שאינו עורך דין ועושה פעולה שנתייחדה לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין, דינו – קנס [...]

*Gist:* A non-lawyer who performs a reserved act commits an offence punishable by a fine. [Full text, s. 96](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_96)

### Your own right to act: Civil Procedure Regulations 2018, reg. 170

> כל פעולה בבית משפט הנדרשת מבעל דין או המותרת לו לפי דין, רשאי הוא לעשותה בעצמו או בידי עורך דין שמינה לכך כדין, והוא כשאין הוראה מפורשת אחרת בכל דין.

*Gist:* In the civil courts a party may take any step personally or through a lawyer, unless a specific law says otherwise. [Full text, reg. 170](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי#סעיף_170)

The regulation names two options only: "בעצמו" (yourself) or "בידי עורך דין" (a lawyer). Together with s. 20, it is why there is no "lay friend" who can argue for you in an Israeli civil court.

### A forum-specific opening: small claims, Courts Law s. 63

> (א) ייצוג בעל דין בבית משפט לתביעות קטנות על ידי עורך דין יהיה רק ברשות בית המשפט ומטעמים מיוחדים שיירשמו; [...]
> (ב) [...] יכול אדם, באישור בית המשפט לתביעות קטנות, לייצג בעל דין [...] אין לייצג אדם לפי סעיף קטן זה – (1) אם המייצג עוסק בייצוג בדרך קבע; (2) אם הייצוג נעשה במהלך הרגיל של עסקיו של המייצג; (3) אם הייצוג נעשה בתמורה.

*Gist:* In the small claims court even a lawyer needs the court's leave, and with the court's approval a non-lawyer may represent a party, but not someone who represents regularly, as part of their business, or for payment. [Full text, s. 63](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_63)

This is a good example of s. 21(3): another enactment regulates representation in that forum, so its own rules apply.

### The road to the profession: ss. 24 and 35

> אדם כשיר להיות עורך דין אם נתקיימו בו אלה: (1) הוא בעל השכלה משפטית גבוהה; (2) עבר תקופת התמחות; (3) עמד בבחינות הלשכה [...]

*Gist:* To become a lawyer you need a recognised higher legal education, an internship and the Bar exams. [s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_24)

> (א) תקופת ההתמחות תהיה 12 חודשים.

*Gist:* The internship lasts 12 months (after amendments in 2024–2025; it had been 18 months). [s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_35)

## Leading cases

### רע״א 8120/08

The Supreme Court held that, in the absence of an express provision allowing it, only a licensed lawyer may represent a party in court; Israeli civil procedure recognises no "ידיד קרוב" (close friend) who may argue for another adult party. The ruling reads reg. 170's predecessor together with s. 20: the party either appears in person or appoints a lawyer.

Why it matters: it closes the most common self-help idea, "my brother will speak for me". A relative may sit with you, help you prepare and take notes, but in a civil court they may not argue for you unless a specific law (like s. 63(ב) for small claims) allows it. [Text on the Judiciary's site](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/Home/Download?path=HebrewVerdicts%2F08%2F200%2F081%2Fv04&fileName=08081200_v04.txt&type=2)

### רע״א 5234/17

In a small-claims appeal the Supreme Court held that a judge is not obliged to tell an unrepresented party that they may ask for permission to be represented under s. 63 of the Courts Law. The small claims court is designed for parties who appear in person; asking for a representative is the party's own initiative.

Why it matters: the system assumes you know your options. If you need a representative in small claims (for example because you cannot speak at length, or your claim depends on technical facts someone else knows), you must ask for leave yourself, early and with reasons. [Text on the Judiciary's site](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/Home/Download?path=HebrewVerdicts\17\340\052\m03&fileName=17052340.M03&type=2)

## How it works in practice

Ask three questions about every task.

**1. Whose matter is it?** If it is yours (your contract, your claim, your will, your tax return), you may act. If it belongs to someone else, even a parent or a spouse, you are on the s. 20 side of the line. A married couple who are both parties to the same claim may each act for themselves; one spouse may not argue the other's separate claim. Note that a minor or a person under guardianship acts through a guardian under the Legal Capacity and Guardianship Law, not through a friend.

**2. Is it "דרך עיסוק" or "בתמורה"?** Helping a friend once, for free, to read through a lease is not an occupation and not for pay. Doing it regularly, advertising it, or accepting money or a favour in return crosses the line. "Claims services" that file small claims or write demand letters for customers for a fee are exactly what s. 20 targets.

**3. Does a forum-specific law open the door?** Some forums allow non-lawyers:

| Forum | Who besides a lawyer may represent you | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Small claims court | With leave, a person who does not represent regularly, as a business, or for pay; or an organisation the minister recognised | Courts Law s. 63 |
| Rabbinical courts | A licensed rabbinical pleader (טוען רבני) | Bar Law s. 21(2); rabbinical pleaders regulations |
| Labour courts | An authorised representative of your union or employers' organisation | Labour Court Law s. 34 |
| Income tax assessment | Accountants and licensed tax advisers | Income Tax Ordinance s. 236; Bar Law s. 21(3) |

For a full forum-by-forum table see [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

**Companies.** A company is a separate person. Israeli courts generally require a company to appear in the civil courts through a lawyer, while some forums allow a manager or authorised officer to act (the labour courts' procedure rules are one example). Check the rule per forum before you assume a sole trader's company can file the way the owner can.

**What you may still do for others.** You may share general information (this course, a Kol-Zchut page, a statute link), lend a template that the person fills in themselves, and go with them to court for moral support. You may not write their claim, argue for them, or tell them, as a service, what their legal position is.

## Worked example

Yusuf Haddad, a teacher from Nazareth, paid a gym in Afula for a two-year membership, then moved to Haifa. The gym refuses to cancel and keeps charging his card. His cousin Rami is in his third year of an LL.B. at the University of Haifa. Yusuf's neighbour, Lior, runs a small business that "files consumer claims for 15% of what you win". Yusuf asks: who can do what?

1. **Yusuf himself.** It is his own claim. He may write the cancellation letter, file a small claim in his own name and argue it at the hearing (reg. 170 and the small claims regime).
2. **Rami, the law student.** Rami is not a lawyer and not yet an intern. He may explain the law to Yusuf informally and for free, help him organise his documents and come to the hearing. He may not appear and argue as Yusuf's representative in a regular court (רע״א 8120/08). In the small claims court Yusuf could ask the judge for leave under s. 63(ב) for Rami to represent him, because Rami does not represent people regularly, not as a business and not for pay. The judge decides.
3. **Lior's business.** Lior offers drafting and filing as an occupation and for a share of the award. That is s. 20(1) and (3) territory and the court could not approve Lior under s. 63(ב) either, because the representation would be "בדרך קבע", "במהלך הרגיל של עסקיו" and "בתמורה". Yusuf should decline.
4. **Result.** Yusuf acts himself, with Rami's free help in preparing. If Yusuf freezes in court, he can ask the judge to let Rami speak under s. 63(ב), explaining why.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A notebook or a spreadsheet.
- 45 minutes and your own memory of the past two years: bills, contracts, disputes, official letters, family events.
- The table in [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Steps

1. List every legal matter you have dealt with or expect to deal with in the next year: leases, employment, purchases, fines, tax, National Insurance, family, inheritance, neighbours, authorities. Aim for at least eight items.
2. For each item write whose matter it is: yours alone, shared (you and a spouse, co-tenants), or someone else's (a parent, a child, your company).
3. For each item write the task you would need to do: read, negotiate, write a letter, file, appear.
4. Mark each task: **Y** (I may do it myself), **H** (someone other than a lawyer may help or represent, under a named exception), **L** (only a lawyer may act, because it is someone else's matter or a company's).
5. For every **H**, write the legal basis (for example "small claims, s. 63(ב)").
6. For every **L**, write who should act instead: the person themselves, a lawyer, Legal Aid, or a guardian.
7. Keep the list. You will use it in the lab in lesson 01.7 and in the module project.

### Template

None. A simple four-column table is enough: matter, whose, task, Y/H/L with basis.

### How you know it worked

- Every item has a Y, H or L and a reason in one line.
- No item marked Y belongs to someone else.
- Every H names a specific section, not "a friend can help".

### Self-check

- [ ] I can quote the two trigger words of s. 20 ("דרך עיסוק", "בתמורה") and explain them.
- [ ] I can name two forums where a non-lawyer may represent someone, and the section that allows it.
- [ ] I know why my sibling cannot argue my case in a magistrates' court.
- [ ] My inventory has at least eight matters, each marked Y, H or L.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own matters. Every person may act for themselves before every Israeli court (reg. 170 in civil courts; equivalent rules in the labour, family and religious courts). The course trains you to do exactly that and nothing more. Partly, for a company you own: whether it may act without a lawyer depends on the forum. No, for anyone else's matter, unless a forum-specific law gives a named exception.

### Where and how

- The legal basis is in the Bar Association Law ss. 20–21 and in each forum's procedure. The forum table is in [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).
- If you want a representative but cannot pay, check Legal Aid (Ministry of Justice) for civil matters, and the Public Defender for criminal ones: see [Where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
- University law clinics give free help in specific fields under a supervising lawyer. They act within the law because the lawyer is responsible.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The matter belongs to someone else and they cannot act for themselves (illness, age, disability): the solution is a lawyer, Legal Aid or a court-appointed guardian, never you acting as their "representative".
- Your company is the party and the forum requires a lawyer.
- Someone offers to handle your case for a fee and is not a lawyer: decline, and if they already filed something, get advice on whether the filing is valid.
- You face criminal exposure (an investigation, a summons to questioning): see module 5, and ask for a defender.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bringing a relative to argue your case in a magistrates' court | The court refuses to hear them; the hearing may be lost or postponed with costs | Ask for a short adjournment to prepare, then argue yourself | Prepare to speak yourself; bring the relative as support only |
| Paying a non-lawyer "claims service" | Possible invalid filings, a s. 96 offence by them, and no professional liability insurance if they err | Take over the file in your own name; check every filing they made | Use only your own name or a licensed lawyer |
| Asking the small claims judge for a representative at the last minute without reasons | Leave refused; you appear unprepared | Explain the special reasons clearly (health, language, technical facts) | Ask early, in writing, with reasons |
| Filing for your company as if it were you | The filing is struck out or must be redone by a lawyer | Check the forum's rule and appoint a lawyer if required | Separate your personal claims from your company's |
| Drafting a will "as a favour" for a parent | Risk to the will's validity and to you (s. 20(3); possible claims of influence) | The parent should write their own or see a lawyer | Help with information only; the parent writes or instructs a lawyer |

## Review

- Section 20 reserves representation, legal drafting and legal advice for others to lawyers when done as an occupation or for pay; acting for yourself is never restricted by it.
- Section 21 and forum-specific laws open narrow doors: religious courts, small claims with leave, labour organisations, tax representatives.
- There is no "lay friend" in Israeli civil courts; a relative may support you but not argue for you (רע״א 8120/08).
- This course is not an LL.B.: becoming a lawyer needs recognised legal education, a 12-month internship and the Bar exams.
- Your personal legal inventory, sorted Y/H/L, is the starting point for every lab in this course.
