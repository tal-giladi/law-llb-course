# 01.5 · Precedent and how to read a ruling

By the end of this lesson you will be able to pick up any Israeli judgment, tell within a few minutes whether it binds the court you are in, find the sentence that is actually the holding, and spot when a ruling has been overturned by a larger panel or overridden by the Knesset. That is what lets you use case law in your own letters and claims without quoting the wrong judge.

## Why it matters

Statutes are short; case law fills them. What "good faith" means in a contract, when a landlord may keep a deposit, how a court weighs a consumer's delay: much of the answer is in rulings. Israel follows a statutory version of the common-law doctrine of precedent (stare decisis), written into a Basic Law. A Supreme Court holding binds every other court in the country. A district court ruling guides the magistrates' courts below it. A magistrates' court ruling binds no one but the parties.

For a self-represented litigant this cuts both ways. A single Supreme Court sentence that fits your facts can win a small claim. A long quote from a minority opinion, or from a magistrates' court in another case, persuades very little and can make the judge doubt the rest of your argument.

## The law

### The rule of precedent: Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 20

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* A rule decided by a court guides lower courts; a rule decided by the Supreme Court binds every court except the Supreme Court itself. [Full text, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_20)

Note the two verbs. "תנחה" (shall guide) for every court's rulings toward lower courts; "מחייבת" (binds) only for the Supreme Court. In practice a magistrates' court will usually follow a clear district court ruling from its own district, but it is not legally bound to, especially where district courts disagree. And the Supreme Court may depart from its own earlier rulings.

The section speaks of "בית משפט". Religious courts and tribunals are not listed as bound in the same way, but the Supreme Court supervises them through the HCJ and expects them to respect its rulings within their field (lesson 01.4).

### Appeal and further hearing: Basic Law ss. 17–18, Courts Law ss. 26 and 30

> פסק דין של בית משפט בערכאה ראשונה ניתן לערעור בזכות, להוציא פסק דין של בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* A first-instance judgment may be appealed as of right, except a judgment of the Supreme Court. [Basic Law s. 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_17)

> בית המשפט העליון ידון בשלושה, אולם – (1) נשיא בית המשפט העליון או המשנה לנשיא רשאי להורות [...] שהדיון בו יהיה לפני מספר בלתי זוגי גדול יותר של שופטים;

*Gist:* The Supreme Court usually sits in panels of three; the President or Deputy President may order a larger odd-numbered panel. [Courts Law s. 26](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_26)

> (ב) [...] רשאים להיענות לבקשה אם ההלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון עומדת בסתירה להלכה קודמת של בית המשפט העליון, או שמפאת חשיבותה, קשיותה או חידושה של הלכה שנפסקה בענין, יש, לדעתם, מקום לדיון נוסף.

*Gist:* A further hearing before five or more justices may be granted where a Supreme Court holding contradicts an earlier one, or because of the holding's importance, difficulty or novelty. [Courts Law s. 30](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_30)

### The legislature has the last word on statutory meaning: Contracts Law s. 25

> (א) [נוסח לחוזים שנכרתו עד יום 6.1.2026] חוזה יפורש לפי אומד דעתם של הצדדים, כפי שהוא משתמע מתוך החוזה ומנסיבות העניין, ואולם אם אומד דעתם של הצדדים משתמע במפורש מלשון החוזה, יפורש החוזה בהתאם ללשונו.

*Gist:* For contracts made up to 6 January 2026, a contract is interpreted by the parties' intention as it appears from the contract and the circumstances, but where the intention is explicit in the wording, the wording governs. [Full text, s. 25](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28חלק_כללי%29#סעיף_25)

> (2) חוזה עסקי שלא נקבעו בו הוראות לעניין אופן הפרשנות יפורש בהתאם ללשונו בלבד, אלא אם כן מתקיים אחד מאלה: (א) מלשון החוזה בלבד נובעת תוצאה שאינה מתקבלת על הדעת; (ב) מלשון החוזה בלבד עולה סתירה בין הוראות שונות בו.

*Gist:* For contracts made or renewed from 7 January 2026, a business contract with no agreed interpretation method is read by its language alone, unless that produces an unreasonable result or an internal contradiction; non-business, standard-form and employment contracts are still read by the parties' intention from the text and the circumstances. [Full text, s. 25](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28חלק_כללי%29#סעיף_25)

This section is the cleanest example in Israeli law of the Knesset responding to a line of precedent (Apropim, below) twice: in 2011 and again in an amendment published in 2026. Module 3 teaches the substance; here the point is structural.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 5587/93 נחמני נ׳ נחמני, and דנ״א 2401/95 נחמני נ׳ נחמני, פ״ד נ(4) 661 (1996)

A couple froze fertilised eggs; after they separated, the husband refused consent to their use. On appeal, a five-justice panel ruled 4–1 for the husband: continuing the process required his ongoing consent. A further hearing was granted, and an eleven-justice panel ruled 7–4 the other way: having consented and allowed his wife to rely on that consent, he could not block the use of the eggs.

Why it matters: it shows the full life of a precedent at the top. A Supreme Court holding is binding on lower courts the day it is given, yet a further hearing under s. 30 can replace it. When you cite a Supreme Court ruling, check whether a further hearing (דנ״א, דנ״פ or דנג״ץ) followed. [CA 5587/93, English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/278); [CFH 2401/95, English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/277)

### ע״א 4628/93 מדינת ישראל נ׳ אפרופים שיכון ויזום (1991) בע״מ, פ״ד מט(2) 265 (1995)

In a dispute over a state housing contract, the majority (Barak, joined by D. Levin; Mazza dissenting) rejected the "two-stage" approach in which a court looks at surrounding circumstances only after finding the wording unclear. Context and purpose may inform interpretation even where the language seems plain.

Why it matters: Apropim became one of the most-cited and most-criticised holdings in Israeli private law. Business groups and many lawyers argued it made contracts unpredictable. The Knesset amended s. 25 in 2011 to give the language more weight, and again with a 2026 amendment that requires language-only interpretation for business contracts made from 7 January 2026, subject to exceptions. A precedent interpreting a statute is binding until the Knesset changes the statute. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/360)

## How it works in practice

### Is it binding here?

| Ruling from | Binds | Guides | Persuasive only |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supreme Court (any panel) | Every district, magistrates', family, labour and small claims court | — | The Supreme Court itself |
| District court | Only the parties | Magistrates' courts | Other district courts |
| National Labour Court | — | Regional labour courts, which follow it as the head of their system | General courts |
| Magistrates' court | Only the parties | — | Other magistrates' courts |

### Finding the holding

Read a ruling in this order.

1. **Header.** The procedure code tells you what kind of case it is: ע״א (civil appeal), רע״א (leave to appeal in a civil matter), ע״פ (criminal appeal), בג״ץ (High Court of Justice), דנ״א / דנ״פ (further hearing, civil / criminal), עע״מ (administrative appeal). Then the number and year (8638/03 = case 8638 of 2003), the panel and the parties.
2. **The end first.** Find the operative result ("הערעור נדחה", "העתירה מתקבלת", "הוחלט כאמור בפסק דינו של..."). Count the votes. Only reasoning that a majority shared can be a holding.
3. **The question.** Find where the leading opinion states the issue ("השאלה העומדת להכרעה היא...").
4. **The holding.** The rule of law the majority needed to decide that question. Everything else, however eloquent, is obiter.
5. **Later history.** Search the case number to see whether a further hearing was requested, whether later rulings follow or distinguish it, and whether the Knesset amended the statute.

### Citing it

In a letter or a claim, write the procedure code, number, short party names, and the date or the פ״ד reference: "ע״א 4628/93 מדינת ישראל נ׳ אפרופים, פ״ד מט(2) 265 (1995)". Module 2 teaches the uniform citation rules. Never cite a paid database's internal reference; the court can find the ruling by its number.

## Worked example

Tamar Levi-Gabay from Kiryat Shmona is suing a moving company in the small claims court in Kiryat Shmona. She found three rulings that seem to help:

- a Haifa district court ruling, given on appeal from a small claims court, holding that a mover must prove it packed fragile items with care;
- a Supreme Court ruling on the general duty of care of service providers, decided 2–1, where the minority judge wrote a sharp paragraph about movers in particular;
- a magistrates' court ruling from Tel Aviv against the same company, on almost identical facts.

Step by step:

1. **Supreme Court ruling.** It binds the Kiryat Shmona court (s. 20(ב)). But the paragraph about movers is in the minority opinion: it is not a holding. Tamar should cite the majority's general rule on duty of care.
2. **Haifa district ruling.** Kiryat Shmona is within the Northern district, not Haifa; the ruling is from a court of higher rank, so it "guides" under s. 20(א) at most and, from another district, is persuasive. Still useful because it is on point.
3. **Tel Aviv magistrates' ruling.** Persuasive only. It is useful mainly as evidence of a pattern (the same company, the same problem), not as law.
4. **Result.** Her claim cites the Supreme Court majority's rule first, the district ruling second, and mentions the Tel Aviv ruling briefly. She removes the minority quote.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One Supreme Court ruling from the list in this lesson (Nahmani CFH, Apropim) or any ruling from the Judiciary's decision search on a subject in your inventory.
- 60 minutes, a notebook.

### Steps

1. Open the ruling. Copy its header: procedure code, number, date, panel, parties.
2. Turn to the end. Write the outcome and the vote count.
3. Find the question the court decided. Write it in one sentence.
4. Find the holding in the majority opinion. Copy the key sentence (in the original language) and write it in your own words in one sentence.
5. Find one obiter remark and one point from a minority opinion. Label each.
6. Check later history: search the case number for a further hearing or later rulings; for Apropim, read the current s. 25 of the Contracts Law and note the two amendments.
7. Write two lines: which courts this holding binds today, and whether it still governs your type of situation.

### Template

None. Use a seven-line reading grid: header, outcome and votes, question, holding, obiter, minority, later history.

### How you know it worked

- Your holding sentence is drawn from the majority and answers the question you wrote.
- You can say which courts are bound and why, citing s. 20.
- You checked whether the ruling was revisited or overridden.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain the difference between "תנחה" and "מחייבת" in s. 20.
- [ ] I can find the holding in a ruling and separate it from obiter and dissent.
- [ ] I know what a דנ״א is and when it is granted.
- [ ] I can explain how the Knesset responded to Apropim.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Using precedent well is a core self-representation skill: every claim, defence and letter you write in your own name is stronger with one well-chosen binding ruling.

### Where and how

- Find rulings for free on the Judiciary's decision search (module 2, lesson on finding case law) and, for many leading cases, in English on the Cardozo Israeli Supreme Court Project.
- In a small claim, one or two rulings are enough. Quote the holding briefly and give the full citation.
- At the hearing, bring printed copies of the rulings you cite, marked at the relevant paragraph.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side relies on a ruling that you think was overturned or distinguished, and the stakes are high.
- Your case depends on asking the court to depart from a binding precedent: only the Supreme Court can do that, and only on appeal or further hearing.
- You are considering asking for a further hearing (דיון נוסף): it has strict grounds and deadlines.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quoting a minority opinion as if it were the law | The judge discounts your argument | Replace it with the majority's rule | Count votes before quoting |
| Relying on an overturned ruling | Your argument fails; credibility damaged | Find the later ruling and address it | Check later history for every ruling you cite |
| Citing many magistrates' rulings as "precedent" | Little weight; long, unfocused brief | Cut to one binding or guiding ruling | Rank sources by s. 20 before writing |
| Citing a ruling on an old version of a statute | The rule no longer applies (as with s. 25 after 2011 and 2026) | Check which version of the statute applies to your facts' date | Read the current statute and its transition provisions |
| Citing by a paid database reference | The court and the other side cannot check it easily | Give the case number, parties and date | Use the official citation form |

## Review

- Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20: Supreme Court holdings bind every court except the Supreme Court; other courts' rulings guide lower courts.
- Only the majority's reasoning needed for the decision is a holding; obiter and minority opinions persuade at most.
- A further hearing before a larger panel (Courts Law s. 30) can reverse a Supreme Court holding, as in Nahmani.
- The Knesset can override a precedent by amending the statute it interpreted, as it did twice after Apropim.
- Read rulings end-first: outcome, votes, question, holding, later history.
