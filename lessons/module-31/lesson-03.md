# 31.3 · A succession order from start to finish

In this lesson you take a real estate in which you are an heir from the death certificate to the order: you find out how many orders are needed, compute the shares, choose the route, give notice to the other heirs, file, track the publication and objection period, and plan what happens the day the order arrives. The doctrine is in lessons 26.1 and 26.6 and the full application drill in lesson 26.9; this lesson is about running the file and its decision points.

## Why it matters

A bank will not release a deceased's account, the Land Registry will not register the heirs, and the heirs cannot sell or divide anything until there is an order. Families often postpone it for years, and the cost grows: a parent who died without an order leaves a second, harder application when the surviving parent dies; heirs move abroad, documents are lost, and someone eventually needs a lawyer for what was once a simple online form.

The clinic case is deliberately the common Israeli one: one parent died years ago and "nothing was done because the other parent was alive", and now the second parent has died. Getting it right means seeing that there are two estates, in order, each with its own heirs and shares.

## The law

### Which order

> (א) הרשם לעניני ירושה רשאי להצהיר על זכויות היורשים: בירושה על פי דין – על ידי צו ירושה; בירושה על פי צוואה – על ידי צו קיום צוואה (להלן – צו קיום).

*Gist:* The Registrar declares the heirs' rights by a succession order on intestacy and a probate order under a will. [Succession Law s. 66](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_66)

Anyone holding a will must deliver it to the Registrar immediately on learning of the death (s. 75), so the first step in any estate is to check: is there a will, deposited or at home? The Registrar's register of deposited wills is searched when you apply.

### Publication and objection

> הוגשה בקשה לצו ירושה או לצו קיום, יודיע על כך הרשם לעניני ירושה ברבים ויקבע זמן מתאים שלא יפחת משבועיים להגשת התנגדויות; כל המעוניין בדבר רשאי להגיש התנגדות [...] תוך הזמן שנקבע וכל עוד לא ניתן הצו.

*Gist:* Every application is published, with at least two weeks for objections, and any interested person may object before the order is given. [s. 67](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_67)

An objection, or a question of law the Registrar sees fit to refer, moves the file to the Family Court (s. 67א). After the order, an interested person who has a fact or claim not raised before may ask to amend or cancel it (s. 72). Appeal from a Registrar's decision is in 15 days (reg. 46; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### Shares and renunciation

The shares on intestacy are in ss. 10–17; the spouse takes the household movables, including the car, and half of the rest when there are children (s. 11(א)(1); lesson [26.1](../module-26/lesson-01.md)). An heir may renounce before distribution, in writing to the Registrar, and may renounce in favour of only the deceased's spouse, child or sibling (s. 6).

### Route and fee

The application is filed with the Registrar for the deceased's last domicile (reg. 13), online or on paper. Online, the fee is 85% of the paper fee ([Fees Regulations reg. 1(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_הירושה_(אגרות_הרשם_לעניני_ירושה)), checked 18 October 2026), plus a publication charge; amounts in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Kol-Zchut reports that applications whose heirs include siblings, nephews, grandparents, uncles or cousins must be filed on paper (⚠️ manual check).

## Leading cases

### Key text: ss. 67א and 72 (where the Registrar's role ends)

There is no leading appellate ruling for an uncontested application; the statute carries the procedure. Two sections mark the boundary of the Registrar's administrative role. Section 67א sends any objection, and any question the Registrar refers, to the Family Court: once that happens, you are in litigation and the "get a lawyer" question becomes live. Section 72 lets the order be reopened after it is given, but only for a fact or claim not raised before. Why it matters: the right moment to raise a doubt (a possible will, an heir you cannot find) is before you file, not after the order. Text: [Succession Law ss. 67א, 72](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_67א).

### Key text: s. 6 (renunciation as an estate-planning tool)

Families often want one heir to keep the home. Renunciation is the route the statute offers before distribution, but only in favour of the spouse, a child or a sibling, and not conditionally (s. 6(ב), (ד)); a minor's renunciation needs court approval (s. 6(ג)). Why it matters: a renunciation in the permitted circle is a gift that avoids a later transfer between heirs, which may be treated as a sale for tax purposes (lesson [26.7](../module-26/lesson-07.md)). Text: [s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_6).

## How it works in practice

### Decision points

| Decision | Ask | Answer leads to |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Is there a will? | Search papers; the Registrar checks deposits | Will: probate (deliver it at once, s. 75). No will: succession |
| 2. How many estates? | Did a spouse or heir die earlier without an order? | One application per deceased, earliest death first |
| 3. Who are the heirs, and what share? | ss. 10–17; the spouse's household movables and half | A share table for each estate that adds up to 1 |
| 4. Online or paper? | Are you a child, parent or spouse? Are siblings or more distant relatives heirs? | Online (lower fee) or paper |
| 5. Any renunciation? | Does the family want one heir to keep an asset? Is the beneficiary in the s. 6 circle? | A renunciation affidavit, verified, filed before the order |
| 6. Land? | Is there registered land? | Ask for Land Registry registration with the online application, or file separately (lesson 31.4) |
| 7. Any dispute? | Does any heir object, is any heir a minor, abroad and unreachable, or unknown? | Stop; get advice before filing |

### The file from filing to order

| Day | Step | Case-log entry |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | File online (national ID login) or on paper; pay the fee | Application number; receipt |
| 0–7 | Send notice to every other heir on the prescribed form, with proof of delivery | Delivery proof per heir |
| A few days | Email confirming the application is complete, or asking for missing items | Missing items and the date supplied |
| Publication | The application is published; the objection period runs (at least two weeks) | Publication date; end of period |
| About 50 days from a complete application (Kol-Zchut, ⚠️) | The order is given and sent digitally | Order date and number |
| Order + 15 | Last day to appeal a decision of the Registrar (reg. 46) | Only if something went wrong |

Use the [succession order application checklist](../../templates/succession-order-application-checklist.md): parts A to H cover the deceased, the route, the heirs, the reg. 14(ב) documents, the notice, land, deadlines and the steps after the order.

### After the order

The order is sent to the banks, insurers, the Tax Authority and the Land Registry (⚠️ manual check), but each body still needs a request from the heirs: the bank releases the account to all heirs together or by their instructions; the car is transferred at the Licensing Office; land is registered under lesson 31.4. Debts come before distribution (lesson [26.5](../module-26/lesson-05.md)).

## Worked example

**Supplied case B.** Liat Sasson, 41, Petah Tikva. Her mother Esther died in 2019; nothing was done. Her father Moshe died on 3 August 2026. Neither left a will (Liat searched the papers; the Registrar's register will be checked when she files). Liat and her brother Eitan, 38, of Eilat, are the only children. The flat is registered "משה ששון ½, אסתר ששון ½". Moshe's bank account holds ₪96,000; he owned a 2014 car.

**Decision 2: two estates, earliest first.** Esther's estate must be declared first, because part of it passed to Moshe and is now in Moshe's estate.

**Decision 3: shares.**

| Estate | Heirs | Share of the deceased's ½ of the flat | Share of the whole flat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Esther (2019) | Moshe ½ (spouse with children, s. 11(א)(1)); Liat ¼; Eitan ¼. Household movables to Moshe | Moshe ½, Liat ¼, Eitan ¼ | Moshe ¼, Liat ⅛, Eitan ⅛ |
| Moshe (2026) | Liat ½, Eitan ½ (s. 12) | His own ½ plus the ¼ from Esther = ¾ | Liat ⅜, Eitan ⅜ |
| Result | | | Liat ½, Eitan ½ |

Check: ⅛ + ⅜ = ½ for each child; the fractions add up to 1.

**Decision 4: route.** Liat is a child of each deceased; the heirs are a spouse and children, so she files both applications online. The 2026 fee for each is the online fee plus the publication charge ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). In Esther's application she lists Moshe as an heir and states the date of his death, attaching his death certificate details (⚠️ check how the online form records a deceased heir; call \*2416 if unsure).

**Decision 5: renunciation?** Eitan does not want the flat; he wants his share of its value. A renunciation would give Liat his share for nothing, which is not what he wants. They decide not to renounce, to take orders in equal shares, register both heirs (lesson 31.4) and then either sell or have Liat buy Eitan out, after advice on the tax position of a buy-out.

**Decision 7: dispute?** None: two adult heirs who agree. Green.

**Notice.** Liat sends Eitan the notice for each application by registered mail and email, and Eitan replies by email that he received them; she logs both.

**Case log (excerpt).** 20.10.2026: filed Esther's application online, number [___]; paid. 20.10.2026: filed Moshe's application online; ticked the request to register the heirs in the Land Registry and attached the signed land-registration request (lesson 31.4). 21.10.2026: notices to Eitan sent. 26.10.2026: email "application complete" for both. Publication: [date]; objection period ends [date]. Order: expected December 2026.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. File only if you are an heir in a real, uncontested estate; otherwise stop before submission.

### You need

- The death certificate (or certificates) and the deceased's ID number; your ID and the other heirs' details (full name, ID, address, relationship).
- Any will found, or a written note that you searched and found none.
- A current land extract for any land (lesson 31.1).
- The [succession order application checklist](../../templates/succession-order-application-checklist.md); about two hours, plus the waiting period.

### Steps

1. **Will search (10 min).** Search papers and safes; ask the family. If you find a will, it goes to the Registrar now (s. 75) and this becomes a probate file.
2. **Estates (10 min).** List every deceased whose estate includes the assets; order them by date of death.
3. **Share table (25 min).** For each estate, heirs and shares; for each asset, the resulting fractions; check they add up to 1.
4. **Route (10 min).** Online or paper, with your reason; the Registrar for the domicile.
5. **Renunciation decision (10 min).** Does anyone want to renounce? In whose favour? Is that person in the s. 6 circle? Does the renouncing heir understand it is a gift?
6. **Documents (20 min).** Tick part D of the checklist (reg. 14(ב)).
7. **Notices (15 min).** Draft the notice for each other heir (part E); plan the proof of delivery.
8. **File or stop (10 min).** If every decision is green, file and log the number. If any red flag appears, stop and record why.
9. **Deadline diary (10 min).** Publication, end of the objection period, expected order date, appeal period.

### Template

- [Succession or probate order application checklist](../../templates/succession-order-application-checklist.md) (lesson 26.9): parts A to H.
- [Clinic case file](../../templates/clinic-case-file.md): case log and deadlines.

### How you know it worked

- Your share table adds up to 1 for every asset and matches what the order will declare.
- Every other heir received notice, and you can prove it.
- The application is filed (or filing-ready) with every reg. 14(ב) item, and the deadlines are diarised.

### Self-check

- [ ] I dealt with an earlier death before the later one.
- [ ] I know when the file would leave the Registrar for the Family Court.
- [ ] I know who may be the beneficiary of a renunciation.
- [ ] I acted only as an heir, and each other heir signed his own documents.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. The Registrar's procedure is used daily by unrepresented heirs; the online service is designed for them ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). A renunciation affidavit must be verified before a lawyer (lesson 26.1), which is verification, not representation. Filing for an estate in which you are not an heir, or for pay, is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- The Registrar of Inheritance for the deceased's domicile, online through the Administrator General's service ([gov.il](https://www.gov.il/he/service/probate_order), ⚠️ manual check) or on paper.
- Fees: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Help: the Registrar's service centre \*2416 (⚠️ manual check); Kol-Zchut's inheritance pages; Legal Aid if eligible.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Anyone objects, or the Registrar refers the file to the Family Court.
- An heir is a minor or under guardianship and the family wants a renunciation or a sale.
- An heir cannot be found, or the deceased had assets or heirs abroad.
- Only a copy of a will exists, or there are two wills.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applying only for the second parent | The flat's other half stays in the first parent's name; registration refused | Apply for the first parent's estate too | List every deceased in step 2 |
| Leaving the deceased spouse out of the first estate's heirs | Wrong order; amendment needed (s. 72) | Ask to amend the order | Apply s. 11 to the first estate |
| Renouncing "in favour of" a nephew | Void beyond the s. 6 circle | Renounce generally or gift after distribution | Check s. 6 before signing |
| No proof of notice to an heir | Application held up | Re-send with proof | Registered mail and email, logged |
| Selling the car or emptying the account before the order | Personal liability to other heirs and creditors | Account to the estate | Wait for the order; pay debts first |

## Review

- First search for a will; any will goes to the Registrar at once.
- Count the estates: an earlier death without an order must be declared first, and its heirs include the later deceased.
- Compute a share table for every asset that adds up to 1, using s. 11 for the spouse.
- File online when you can, give provable notice to every other heir, and diarise the publication, objection and appeal periods; an objection moves the file to the Family Court.
