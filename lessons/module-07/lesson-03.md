# 07.3 · Regulators and regulatory tools

By the end of this lesson you will be able to name the regulator behind most of the rules that govern your bank, phone company, insurer, landlord or employer, say which statute gives it power and what tools it may use, and choose the route that is most likely to solve your problem: a complaint, a comment on a draft rule, or a claim. You will also know the 2021 statute that now sets the ground rules for how all regulators make new rules.

## Why it matters

Between the Knesset and the citizen stands a dense layer of regulators: ministries' professional units, independent authorities, and statutory corporations. They license businesses, set standards, require disclosure, fix some prices, impose financial sanctions and prosecute. For many everyday problems (an unfair bank fee, a mobile contract that cannot be cancelled, a misleading advertisement) a regulator can act faster and more cheaply than any court, and its rules decide what a court will treat as reasonable.

Regulation is also politically contested. One side says Israel is over-regulated, with high compliance costs that raise prices; the other says weak regulation lets concentrated markets exploit consumers. The Regulatory Principles Law 2021 is the legislature's attempt to set a common method without settling that debate.

## The law

### What counts as regulation

> ”אסדרה“ – הוראה הקובעת כלל התנהגות שהוא בעל אופי כללי, ומתקיימים בה כל אלה: (1) היא נקבעה לשם הסדרת פעילות כלכלית או חברתית; (2) היא ניתנת לאכיפה פלילית או מינהלית בידי רשות ציבורית או תאגיד ציבורי; (3) היא נקבעה בחיקוק או שנקבעה על פי סמכות כדין בהנחיה, נוהל, חוזר, גילוי דעת או בהוראה מסוג דומה [...]

*Gist:* For this law, "regulation" is any general rule of conduct for economic or social activity that a public body can enforce criminally or administratively, whether it is in an enactment or in a directive, circular or procedure issued under lawful authority; a "regulator" is a public authority empowered to propose or set such rules, publish implementing guidance or grant regulatory approvals. [Regulatory Principles Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עקרונות_האסדרה#סעיף_3)

The definition is wider than "regulations" in lesson 07.2: a Bank of Israel directive or a ministry circular can be "regulation" here even though it is not a תקנה.

### The principles of good regulation

> (3) תהליך גיבוש האסדרה וקביעתה מתבססים במידה מספקת על נתונים הנוגעים לעניין, ונעשים בהתאם לעקרון השקיפות ותוך שיתוף הציבור במידה הנדרשת בנסיבות העניין;

*Gist:* Good regulation is based on sufficient data, made transparently and with public participation as the circumstances require; the other principles in s. 2 include necessity and consideration of alternatives, maximum benefit to society against compliance cost, reliance on international standards, proportionality for small businesses, accessibility and digital service, coordination between regulators, competition and the cost of living. [Regulatory Principles Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עקרונות_האסדרה#סעיף_2)

Section 19 makes these principles binding considerations for every regulator. Section 21 requires a regulator that must carry out a regulatory impact assessment (RIA) under Government decisions to consult the Regulatory Authority. Section 34 requires statutory corporations (such as independent authorities set up by law) to publish a report with the main points of new regulation, alternatives considered and expected effects, and Rules of Procedure s. 84(ו) requires a minister to bring a Government RIA report to the Knesset committee preparing the bill. Section 37 requires all existing regulation to be published in a unified database.

### One regulator's toolbox: the Consumer Protection Commissioner

> (1) לפקח על ביצוע הוראות חוק זה; (1א) לחקור חשד לביצוע עבירה לפי חוק זה ולהביא את העבריין לדין; (1ב) לנקוט הליכי אכיפה מינהלית נגד מפר לפי הוראות חוק זה; (2) לטפל בתלונות שראה בהן ממש על הפרת הוראות חוק זה או על פגיעה אחרת בצרכן;

*Gist:* The Commissioner supervises the Consumer Protection Law, investigates and prosecutes offences, takes administrative enforcement against violators and handles complaints with merit. [Consumer Protection Law s. 20(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הצרכן#סעיף_20)

Under s. 22ג the Commissioner may impose a financial sanction (עיצום כספי) for listed violations, at a fixed amount that is higher for corporations; the linked amounts are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). The same law shows nearly every tool in one place: rules of conduct, disclosure duties, criminal offences, administrative sanctions and a private right to sue.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 4374/15 התנועה למען איכות השלטון בישראל נ׳ ראש הממשלה (27.3.2016)

The Government adopted the "gas outline" (Government Decision 432) for the offshore gas fields. It included a regulatory stability clause: for ten years the Government would not change taxation, competition rules or export caps, and would oppose legislation that did. A majority (Deputy President Rubinstein, Justices Joubran, Hayut and Vogelman; Justice Sohlberg dissenting) held that the clause was unlawful: an authority given a power must keep its discretion, and the Government cannot "decide not to decide" or bind future governments and, in practice, the Knesset. The state was given one year to fix the defect, failing which the outline would be annulled.

Why it matters: regulatory certainty is a legitimate goal, but an executive promise not to regulate is limited by the rule against fettering discretion. Supporters of the outline argued that investors need stability; the Court's answer was that stability of that scope needs a statute. [English summary](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/272)

### בג״ץ 2605/05 המרכז האקדמי למשפט ולעסקים נ׳ שר האוצר (19.11.2009)

An amendment to the Prisons Ordinance let a private corporation build and run a prison. An expanded panel held the amendment unconstitutional: transferring the power to imprison, with its core use of force, to a profit-driven company infringed inmates' liberty and dignity disproportionately, regardless of how the company would behave in practice.

Why it matters: the state may regulate private providers of many services, but some powers belong to the state itself. Lesson 06.3 uses this case for proportionality; here it marks the outer edge of "regulating instead of providing". [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/20)

## How it works in practice

### Who regulates what (examples)

| Area | Regulator | Typical tools |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer transactions, misleading advertising | Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority | Complaints, financial sanctions, prosecution |
| Banks and credit cards | Bank of Israel, Supervisor of Banks | Proper conduct directives, public complaints unit |
| Insurance, pensions, savings | Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority | Circulars, licensing, public inquiries unit |
| Competition, cartels, mergers | Competition Authority | Merger control, sanctions, criminal cartel enforcement |
| Phone, internet, broadcasting | Ministry of Communications | Licences, consumer rules |
| Personal data | Privacy Protection Authority | Registration, enforcement, sanctions |
| Food, drugs, medical devices | Ministry of Health | Licences, standards, recalls |

The names and units change; check the regulator's own site (gov.il pages block bots: ⚠️ manual check for each).

### The tools, from soft to hard

1. **Information and guidance:** publishing data, guides, comparisons.
2. **Disclosure duties:** requiring firms to tell consumers prices, terms and risks.
3. **Rules of conduct:** what a firm must or must not do.
4. **Standards:** technical requirements, often based on international standards.
5. **Licensing:** no activity without prior approval; the strongest entry barrier.
6. **Price control:** setting or supervising prices for listed goods and services.
7. **Administrative enforcement:** warnings, financial sanctions, licence conditions.
8. **Criminal enforcement:** offences prosecuted in court.
9. **Private enforcement:** statutory causes of action and class actions, which make firms' compliance a question for courts as well.

The Regulatory Principles Law asks regulators to consider alternatives, including not regulating, and to prefer the least burdensome tool that achieves the protected interest.

### Complaint, comment or claim?

- **Complaint to the regulator:** best when a firm is breaking a rule that affects many people; free; it does not give you compensation and does not stop limitation periods.
- **Comment on draft regulation:** best when the rule itself is the problem (lesson 07.5).
- **Claim in court:** best when you want your money back; a regulator's finding or directive can be strong evidence.

You can do all three, in that order or in parallel.

## Worked example

Daniel Weiss, a reservist from Modi'in, signed a 36-month mobile contract. After his call-up he tried to cancel and was told he must pay an "exit fee" equal to six months of service.

1. **Identify the regulator.** Mobile services: the Ministry of Communications regulates licensees; consumer rules on ending continuing transactions are in the Consumer Protection Law, supervised by the Consumer Protection Commissioner.
2. **Find the rule.** Daniel checks the Consumer Protection Law's provisions on ending a continuing transaction and the Communications Ministry's licence conditions on exit fees. He finds that the rules limit such fees.
3. **Choose the tool.** He wants his money back (claim) and wants the practice stopped (complaint).
4. **Complaint.** He files with the regulator's public inquiries unit, attaching the contract and the fee demand.
5. **Demand letter and claim.** In parallel he sends a demand letter to the company and prepares a small claim if the fee is not refunded (module 4, lesson 04.9 for the small-claims steps).
6. **Draft regulation.** A month later the ministry publishes draft rules on exit fees for reservists for comment; Daniel comments with his facts (lesson 07.5).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One current problem with a business or service in a regulated market (bank, insurer, phone, energy, transport, health service).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Name the activity and the market.
2. Identify the regulator and the statute that empowers it. Open the statute and find the section that lists the regulator's functions.
3. Find the specific rule that applies: a statute section, a regulation, a directive or circular. Note its source and date.
4. Classify the tool the rule uses (disclosure, conduct rule, licence condition, price control, sanction).
5. Find the regulator's complaint channel and what it can and cannot do for you.
6. Decide your route: complaint, comment on a draft rule, claim, or a combination, with one reason for each.
7. Write a half-page "regulator map" for your problem with all of the above.

### Template

For the complaint or demand letter, adapt the [letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md) or the [demand letter to a company](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md).

### How you know it worked

- You named a specific regulator and a specific section that gives it power, not just "the government".
- You found the rule that applies to your situation, with its date.
- Your route choice says what you expect the regulator, or the court, to do.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Complaints to regulators and comments on draft regulation are made by individuals in their own name, with no lawyer and usually no fee. Claims for compensation in your own name can usually go to the small claims court (see [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)).

### Where and how

- Each regulator's public inquiries or complaints unit on gov.il or the regulator's own site (⚠️ manual check of the current URL; gov.il blocks automated checks).
- Draft regulations for comment: the [government legislation site](https://www.tazkirim.gov.il/s/) and the regulator's own drafts page (for example, the Bank of Israel publishes drafts for public comment on its site).
- The unified regulation database under Regulatory Principles Law s. 37, on the Regulatory Authority's site (⚠️ manual check).
- Free help: consumer organisations and [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The regulator opens proceedings against you (a licence revocation, a financial sanction, a criminal investigation).
- You want to challenge a regulator's rule or decision in court: that is administrative litigation (year 2, module 14).
- Many people share your problem and a class action is being considered: class actions are lawyer-run.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complaining to the wrong regulator | Weeks lost while it is transferred | Ask the regulator to forward it (Consumer Protection Law s. 20(ב) contemplates transfer) | Check which statute governs the activity |
| Expecting the regulator to get your money back | Disappointment; limitation keeps running | File a demand letter and a claim in parallel | Use the complaint for the practice, the claim for the money |
| Treating a circular as optional because it is "not a law" | Losing an argument the circular settles | Cite the circular as the regulator's binding rule for firms | Check whether the regulator may issue binding directives |
| Relying on an executive promise that a rule will not change | The promise may be unlawful or revocable (gas outline) | Look for statutory protection | Ask whether a statute backs the promise |
| Commenting on a draft rule with general complaints | Comment ignored | Give facts, numbers and a proposed wording (lesson 07.5) | Prepare evidence before the comment period opens |

## Review

- A regulator is a public authority empowered to set or enforce rules for economic or social activity; its rules may be in enactments or in directives and circulars.
- The tools range from information and disclosure to licensing, price control, financial sanctions, criminal enforcement and private enforcement.
- The Regulatory Principles Law 2021 makes necessity, data, transparency, public participation and proportionate compliance costs binding considerations, and adds RIA consultation, reports and a unified database.
- A government cannot fetter its own or its successors' regulatory discretion by executive promise (gas outline); some powers cannot be handed to private regulated providers at all (Academic Center).
- Complaint, comment and claim serve different goals; use them together.
