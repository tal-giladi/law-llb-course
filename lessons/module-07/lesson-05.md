# 07.5 · Practice lab: comment on a draft bill or regulation (תזכירי חוק)

In this lab you find a real government bill memorandum or draft regulation that is open for public comment, read it the way a ministry lawyer would, and send written comments in your own name before the deadline. The output is a submitted comment (or, if nothing relevant is open, a complete comment prepared on a supplied draft) and a short file note you will reuse in the module project.

## Why it matters

The public comment period is the only stage of law-making designed for ordinary people. The ministry must publish the memorandum, give a reasonable time to respond and consider the comments "בלב פתוח ונפש חפצה" (AG guideline 2.3005, para. 2). Comments that point to a concrete problem and propose wording do change drafts; vague objections do not. Learning to write the first kind is a skill that also serves you in letters to committees (lesson 07.6), objections to authorities and, later, court pleadings.

## The law

### The memorandum and its comment period

> השר הממונה יאשר להפיץ את התזכיר. לאחר מכן יפיץ היועץ המשפטי של המשרד את התזכיר לציבור לקבלת הערות. יש להפיץ את התזכיר באמצעות אתר התזכירים הממשלתי.

> בתזכיר המופץ יצוין המועד האחרון למשלוח הערות, שיהיה, ככלל, 21 ימים לפחות לאחר יום הפצת התזכיר.

*Gist:* After the minister approves, the ministry's legal adviser distributes the memorandum for public comment through the government memoranda site, stating a deadline that is, as a rule, at least 21 days after distribution; longer for long, complex or controversial drafts, shorter only for real constraints. [AG guideline 2.3005, paras. 26–27](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/legislation-documents/he/2-3005-190921.pdf) The period is in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

The guideline relies on בג״ץ 1658/10 אדם טבע ודין נ׳ ממשלת ישראל (12.3.2010), where the Court said that a substantial departure from proper practice in giving the public adequate time to comment could even justify judicial intervention (quoted in the guideline, para. 27; ⚠️ the ruling itself was not checked on a free host). Para. 36 requires the ministry to examine the comments and decide which it accepts.

### Draft regulations

There is no general statute requiring publication of draft regulations for comment. The AG guideline 2.3100 recommends prior publication and consultation where regulations may significantly affect the legal position or economic or social interests of a group, especially where they set primary arrangements; the publication should state where to send responses and by when. Some enabling statutes do require a hearing or consultation, and then it is a condition of validity (Wael, lesson 07.4). For regulators, the Regulatory Principles Law adds public participation "במידה הנדרשת בנסיבות העניין" as a binding principle (s. 2(3)) and, for statutory corporations, a published report on new regulation (s. 34).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 4926/08 Wael & Co. v. National Water and Sewage Authority (9.10.2013)

The Court held that Israeli law has no general right to be heard before regulations with legislative effect are made; a right exists where a statute grants it. Where the Water Law required an invitation to be heard, the invitation had to be published in Arabic as well as Hebrew. (Full discussion in lesson 07.4.)

Why it matters for this lab: your comment on a memorandum or draft regulation rests mostly on government practice and the AG guidelines, not on an enforceable personal right. That makes quality, not volume, decisive. Where the enabling statute does require consultation, note it in your comment: it strengthens your position later. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/393)

## How it works in practice

### The government legislation site

Since 2020 the Ministry of Justice has run the government legislation site, which replaced the earlier "קשרי ממשל" site. Ministries publish there bill memoranda, draft secondary legislation, draft support criteria and draft procedures and guidelines. Each item has its own page with a summary, the documents, the comment period and, where published, the comments received; registered users can submit comments and read others' comments, and ministries can respond on the site. The exact labels and steps change with the site's design (⚠️ manual check: the site is rendered by JavaScript and could not be read by automated tools on 2026-10-17).

Some regulators publish drafts on their own sites instead or as well (for example the Bank of Israel's "טיוטות להערות הציבור" page).

### What a ministry lawyer looks for in a comment

1. **Who you are and why it affects you**, in one or two sentences.
2. **Section-specific points.** Each comment tied to a section of the draft.
3. **Facts and numbers** the ministry may not have: how the rule works on the ground.
4. **Alternative wording** that achieves the stated purpose (from the explanatory notes) with less harm.
5. **Legal points** where relevant: authority for regulations, primary arrangements, committee approval, commencement and transition.

### Timing

Do not wait for the last day. Comments that arrive early are more likely to be read carefully, and the site may close at the deadline hour.

## Worked example

Tamar Levi-Abebe, a home-care worker in Netanya, finds draft regulations from the Ministry of Labour on the government legislation site: all home-care agencies must keep digital attendance records, and workers must "sign in through a mobile app at each visit". The comment period is 30 days.

1. **Purpose.** The explanatory notes say the aim is to prevent fictitious visits and ensure workers are paid for all hours.
2. **Legal basis.** The recital cites a section of the relevant statute authorising the minister to prescribe "records the employer must keep". Tamar notes the duty in the draft falls on workers, not employers: a possible gap in authority.
3. **Effect on her.** Many of her clients live in areas with poor reception; some workers have no smartphone.
4. **Comment 1 (reg. 3(b)).** Problem: no reception, no smartphone. Proposed wording: "or by an alternative means approved by the employer, including a landline call or a paper form signed by the patient". Reason: same purpose, less exclusion.
5. **Comment 2 (reg. 5, commencement).** Proposed 6-month transition period.
6. **Comment 3 (authority).** The enabling section speaks of the employer's records; the duty to sign in should be framed as the employer's duty to provide a system, not a worker's offence.
7. **Submission.** She submits through the site, with her name, decides to allow publication, and saves the confirmation.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Submitting a public comment is a real, low-risk act in your own name; if you do not want your name published, read the site's terms first or prepare the comment as a dry run.

### You need

- A free account on the [government legislation site](https://www.tazkirim.gov.il/s/) (or the regulator's drafts page).
- The [comment on a draft bill or regulation](../../templates/comment-on-draft-legislation.md) template.
- For draft regulations: the enabling statute on Wikisource.
- About 2.5 hours: 30 minutes to find and choose, 45 to analyse, 60 to write, 15 to check and submit.

### Steps

1. **Find.** Search the site for open items in a field that affects you. Note the title, ministry, date of distribution and deadline. If nothing relevant is open, use the supplied draft below.
2. **Save.** Download the draft and explanatory notes; screenshot the page with the deadline.
3. **Purpose.** Write the stated purpose in one sentence from the explanatory notes.
4. **Legal basis.** For a bill: what existing law does it amend? For regulations: copy the enabling section; check whether committee approval (Penal Law s. 2(ב) or the enabling law) or a hearing is required; ask whether any provision is a primary arrangement (lesson 07.2).
5. **Effect.** List concretely how the draft would affect you or people in your situation, with numbers where you have them.
6. **Choose three points at most.** The strongest, each tied to a section.
7. **Draft.** Fill in the template: who you are, summary, section-by-section comments with alternative wording, legal points, requests.
8. **Check.** Reread against the checklist at the end of the template. Cut anything without a section or a proposal.
9. **Submit** before the deadline through the site or the stated address; save the confirmation. (Dry run: save the final file instead.)
10. **File note.** One paragraph: what you sent, when, to whom, and when to look for the revised draft or a bill in the Knesset.

**Point 3: the offence (reg. 4).** The regulations create an offence with imprisonment. Under Penal Law s. 2(ב) regulations creating offences need a Knesset committee's approval; the recital mentions none. (Municipal by-laws are exempt from s. 2(ב), but these are drafted as regulations.) Imprisonment of six months is the maximum regulations may set (s. 2(א)) and is disproportionate for an unlicensed sign; propose an administrative fine instead.

**Point 4: commencement (reg. 5).** Immediate effect gives existing businesses no time to apply. Propose a transition period of six months for existing signs.

**Authority (general).** Ask which section authorises the minister; if the enabling law gives sign regulation to local authorities by by-law, a ministerial regulation may lack authority.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Public comments are open to everyone and are made in your own name. If you comment on behalf of an organisation, say so and make sure you are authorised. Writing comments for other people as a paid service is legal drafting for others (Bar Association Law s. 20), and paid advocacy before the Knesset is regulated lobbying.

### Where and how

- The [government legislation site](https://www.tazkirim.gov.il/s/): registration, then the comment form on the item's page (⚠️ manual check of the current steps).
- Regulators' own drafts pages (for example the Bank of Israel).
- No fee. Deadline: the date on the draft; for bill memoranda, as a rule at least 21 days from distribution (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: university legislative clinics and civil-society organisations in your field; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The draft could cost your business its licence or a large sum, and you need a formal legal opinion on authority or constitutionality.
- You are considering a petition because the comment period was skipped or far too short.
- You are asked to comment on behalf of others for payment.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing the deadline | Comment not considered | Send it anyway with a short apology; later, write to the Knesset committee (lesson 07.6) | Note the deadline on day one |
| A general protest with no section numbers | Filed and ignored | Rewrite as section-by-section points | Use the template's table |
| No alternative wording | The ministry cannot act on it | Add a proposed text for each point | Write the wording first, then the reasons |
| Personal details of others or attacks on officials | Comment discredited; privacy issues | Remove them | Stick to facts about the rule |
| Not saving the submission confirmation | No proof you commented | Contact the ministry's legal adviser | Screenshot and save immediately |

## Review

- Government bill memoranda are published on the government legislation site with a comment deadline, as a rule at least 21 days (AG guideline 2.3005).
- Draft regulations are often published for comment by practice and guidance (AG guideline 2.3100; Regulatory Principles Law s. 2(3)); a statutory right to be heard exists only where a law grants it (Wael).
- Effective comments are short, section-specific, factual and propose wording.
- For draft regulations, always check the enabling section, committee approval and commencement.
- Keep proof of the deadline and the submission; your comment is the start of your file on that rule.
