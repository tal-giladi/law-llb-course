# 07.6 · Practice lab: write to a Knesset committee and follow the bill

In this lab you choose a bill (or draft secondary legislation awaiting committee approval) that affects you, find everything the Knesset has published on it, write to the committee asking to be heard with a short position paper, and set up a way to follow the bill to the end. Because the 25th Knesset was dissolved in July 2026 and elections are on 27 October 2026, you will also work out what happens to your bill when the 26th Knesset convenes.

## Why it matters

After a bill passes first reading, the committee is where its wording is actually decided. Committee sessions are public by default, their protocols are published, and the chair may invite anyone with knowledge or an interest in the subject. Ministries, regulators, business associations and NGOs use this stage routinely; individuals rarely do, though they often have the most concrete knowledge of how a rule works in practice. A clear, short position paper from someone directly affected is read, sometimes quoted in the protocol, and sometimes changes a section.

Following the bill matters too. Committees change texts between sessions, reservations are decided in the plenum, and an election can end a bill or carry it over. People who only read the news learn about the final text after it is law.

## The law

### Committees and their subjects

> הכנסת תבחר מבין חבריה ועדות קבועות [...] תפקידי הועדות, סמכויותיהן וסדרי עבודתן, במידה שלא נקבעו בחוק, ייקבעו בתקנון.

*Gist:* The Knesset elects permanent committees from its members; their functions, powers and procedures are set by statute or in the Rules. [Basic Law: The Knesset s. 21(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הכנסת#סעיף_21)

Rules s. 100 lists the permanent committees and their subjects (Finance; Economy; Foreign Affairs and Defence; Internal Affairs and Environment; Constitution, Law and Justice; Education; Labour and Welfare; Health; and others); s. 101 gives them power over bills and secondary legislation in their field and oversight of the Government. Section 21(ב) of the Basic Law and Rules s. 123 let committees summon officials and demand information.

### Public sessions and published protocols

> ישיבות הוועדות הן פומביות, אלא אם כן החליטה הוועדה אחרת או אם פרוטוקול הישיבה חסוי כמפורט בפרק זה [...]

*Gist:* Committee sessions are public unless the committee decides otherwise or the protocol is classified; the chair may limit attendance in the room. [Rules s. 118(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_118)

> פרוטוקולים של ישיבות ועדות יהיו פתוחים לעיון הציבור ויפורסמו באתר האינטרנט של הכנסת, בכפוף להוראות סעיף זה.

*Gist:* Committee protocols are open to the public and published on the Knesset website, except classified ones (for example most Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee sessions). [Rules s. 120(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_120) The protocol lists the invitees who attended (s. 119(א)), and any speaker may ask to correct errors in the record of their words (s. 119(ג)).

### Who may be invited

> יושב ראש ועדה רשאי להזמין לישיבה כל מי שיש לו ידע או עניין בנושא שהוועדה דנה בו, לשמוע את דעתו ולבקש ממנו מידע.

*Gist:* The committee chair may invite anyone with knowledge of, or an interest in, the subject to hear their view and ask for information. [Rules s. 125(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנון_הכנסת#סעיף_125)

This is a power of the chair, not a right of the public: you ask, and the chair decides.

### Lobbying is regulated; speaking for yourself is not

> ”שדלן“ – מי שדרך עיסוק או בתמורה למען לקוח, נוקט פעולות לשכנוע חבר הכנסת בקשר להצעות חוק ולחקיקת משנה בכנסת או בוועדותיה [...]

*Gist:* A lobbyist is someone who, as an occupation or for payment, acts for a client to persuade MKs on bills or secondary legislation; a lobbyist needs a permit from a Knesset committee. A person speaking for themselves, or an employee for their own employer, is not a lobbyist under this definition. [Knesset Law ss. 65–66](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הכנסת#סעיף_65)

### Reservations and continuity

Only MKs and ministers propose amendments in committee and file reservations (Rules s. 86); a member of the public can ask an MK to adopt a proposal. After second and third readings (ss. 90–92) the law is published in Reshumot. When a Knesset ends, a bill that passed first reading may be carried into the next Knesset under the Continuity Law if the new Government or the competent committee asks and the Knesset approves; others lapse (lesson 07.1).

## Leading cases

### Applying בג״ץ 10042/16 קוונטינסקי נ׳ כנסת ישראל (6.8.2017)

This lab applies the case from lesson 07.1. Quintinsky's factors for a fair legislative process (debate proportionate to the bill's complexity, separate debate of complex arrangements, the factual basis provided, the time members had the text) are about MKs' participation, not the public's. They still give you a vocabulary for your position paper: ask the committee for the factual basis, for a regulatory impact assessment (Rules s. 84(ו)), and for time to examine a new version. A committee that does these things also protects the law from a process challenge. [English summary](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/305)

## How it works in practice

### Finding the bill and its committee

1. In the Knesset legislation database, search by keywords or bill number. The bill page shows its type, stage, the committee and links to documents.
2. On the committee's page you will find: the weekly agenda (usually published about a week ahead), protocols, background documents prepared by the committee's legal adviser and the Knesset Research and Information Center, the versions of the text discussed, and the committee's contact details.
3. Note the committee's legal adviser: their memos on the bill are often the clearest analysis available, and they decide which issues the committee must resolve.

The Knesset site blocks some automated tools; open it in a normal browser (⚠️ manual check of the exact menu names).

### Asking to be heard

- Write to the committee's email address as soon as a session on the bill is announced, or earlier if the bill is in the committee's work plan.
- Say who you are, why the bill affects you directly, whether you speak for yourself or for an organisation, and that you are not paid.
- Attach a position paper of one or two pages. Even if you are not invited to speak, ask that it be distributed to members and added to the background material.
- If invited, register for entry as the committee instructs, arrive early, and prepare a two-minute version of your main point. Chairs often give the public very little time.

### During an election period (October 2026)

The 25th Knesset dissolved on 17 July 2026 and the 26th will be elected on 27 October 2026 (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). During the election recess committee activity usually slows; check each committee's agenda rather than assuming. After the new Knesset convenes and forms its committees:

- bills that passed first reading may be revived by a continuity request (Continuity Law ss. 1–6);
- other bills must be tabled again; private bills then wait at least 45 days for a preliminary reading;
- draft regulations awaiting committee approval wait for the new committee.

So this month the realistic task is to prepare: build your file and position paper now, and send it when the new committee schedules a session.

### Following the bill

| What to watch | Where | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Committee agenda | The committee's page | Weekly |
| New versions of the text | Committee documents ("נוסח לדיון") | Before each session |
| Plenum agenda, second and third readings | Knesset plenum agenda | Weekly when the bill is close |
| Final law | Reshumot (ספר החוקים), then Wikisource | On passage |
| Continuity after elections | Plenum notices of the new Knesset | First months of the new term |

Keep a dated log of every document and step.

## Worked example

Ahmad Zoabi, a driving instructor in Nazareth, follows a government bill to reform driving-instructor licensing. It passed first reading in the 25th Knesset and was in the Economy Committee when the Knesset dissolved.

1. **Find.** On the bill page: government bill, first reading in March 2026, transferred to the Economy Committee; two sessions held, protocols online.
2. **Read.** In the second protocol, the committee's legal adviser questions whether the new "continuing education" requirement should be set by regulations without criteria in the law (a primary-arrangements point, lesson 07.2).
3. **Position paper.** Ahmad's paper makes two points: set the main criteria for continuing education in the law and require committee approval for the regulations; allow courses in Arabic, with numbers on Arabic-speaking instructors in the north.
4. **Request.** He writes to the committee manager asking to be heard at the next session and to have his paper distributed.
5. **Elections.** The Knesset is dissolved. Ahmad checks the Continuity Law: the bill passed first reading, so the new Government may ask to apply continuity. He sets a reminder for the first months of the 26th Knesset and keeps his paper ready.
6. **Follow.** When continuity is approved and the committee schedules a session, he re-sends the request and paper, updated to the latest version of the text.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. Writing to a committee is a real, low-risk act in your own name. Until the 26th Knesset forms its committees, prepare the letter and paper as a dry run and send them when a session is scheduled.

### You need

- A bill or draft secondary legislation that affects you (from lesson 07.1 or 07.5), or the supplied case below.
- The [letter to a Knesset committee](../../templates/letter-to-knesset-committee.md) template.
- About 2.5 hours: 45 minutes to research, 30 to build the timeline, 60 to write, 15 to set up tracking.

### Steps

1. **Bill page.** Record the bill's name, number, type, sponsor and the committee.
2. **Timeline.** List every stage with its date and document: memorandum (if any), tabling, preliminary reading, first reading, committee sessions, versions.
3. **Read the latest text** and the committee legal adviser's memo or the Research and Information Center paper, if any.
4. **Find your point.** Choose one or two sections where your experience shows a concrete problem. Draft alternative wording.
5. **Position paper.** Fill in part B of the template: summary, background, table of sections, questions the committee should ask the Government, conclusion. Keep it to two pages.
6. **Request.** Fill in part A: the request to be heard and to have the paper distributed.
7. **Election check.** Decide what happens to the bill after 27 October 2026: continuity possible (passed first reading) or lapse. Write the trigger you will watch for.
8. **Tracking.** Set up your watch list from the table above and a dated log file.
9. **Send** when a session is scheduled (or keep as a dry run), and save the email.

### The supplied case (if you have no bill)

A private bill passed first reading in the 25th Knesset: it would require every residential building with more than 8 flats to install a lift accessible to wheelchair users within three years, with the cost split among owners by floor area. It is in the Labour and Welfare Committee. You live on the fourth floor of a 10-flat building without a lift in Be'er Sheva; your neighbour uses a wheelchair; many owners are pensioners.

### Template

[Letter to a Knesset committee (פנייה לוועדה של הכנסת ונייר עמדה)](../../templates/letter-to-knesset-committee.md).

### How you know it worked

- Your timeline shows each stage with a dated document.
- Your position paper fits on two pages and proposes wording for at least one section.
- Your request states who you represent and that you are not paid.
- Your election conclusion matches the Continuity Law.
- You have a tracking plan with sources and frequency.

**Timeline:** private bill tabled; preliminary reading approved; prepared by the Labour and Welfare Committee for first reading and published in הצעות חוק – הכנסת; first reading approved; returned to the same committee (Rules s. 83(א)); Knesset dissolved 17.7.2026.

**Election check:** the bill passed first reading, so continuity is possible: the competent committee of the 26th Knesset may ask, with the sponsor's consent if the sponsor is re-elected (Continuity Law ss. 2–3), giving the Government 21 days' notice of the discussion (s. 4); a faction or the Government may object (s. 5); the Knesset votes (s. 6). **Position paper points:** (1) support the purpose (accessibility); (2) section on cost: split by floor area burdens owners who gain least (ground floor) and pensioners; propose a public grant or loan scheme and an exemption or deferral for low-income owners, set in the law, not left to regulations; (3) section on timing: three years may be unrealistic where structural permits are needed; propose three years from the grant of a building permit, with a duty to apply within one year; (4) ask the committee to request from the Government data on the number of buildings affected and the expected cost (Rules s. 84(ו)).

**Request:** to be heard as an owner in an affected building, speaking for yourself, unpaid; to distribute the paper.

**Tracking:** the committee's page weekly once the 26th Knesset forms committees; plenum notices for continuity; the text versions before each session.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Writing to a committee, attending a public session (subject to entry arrangements and the chair's control of the room) and speaking if invited are open to anyone in their own name. Paid advocacy for others is lobbying and needs a Knesset permit (Knesset Law s. 66).

### Where and how

- The Knesset legislation database and each committee's page on main.knesset.gov.il (⚠️ manual check: the Knesset site blocks automated tools).
- The committee manager's email for requests; the committee's legal adviser for substance.
- No fee. Timing: the committee's agenda, usually published about a week ahead.
- Free help: university Knesset clinics (TAU's catalogue lists a Knesset clinic), civil-society organisations that follow committees; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You represent a business or organisation with significant stakes, or someone offers to pay you to present their position.
- The bill would criminalise or license your occupation and you need a formal legal analysis.
- You want to challenge a law after it passed because of its process (lesson 07.1): that is a High Court petition.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing to the plenum or the Speaker instead of the committee | Letter goes nowhere | Re-send to the committee manager | Find the committee on the bill page |
| A five-page paper of general views | Not read | Cut to two pages with a section table | Start from the template's table |
| Commenting on an old version | Points already fixed or moved | Check the latest "נוסח לדיון" | Read the newest text before every session |
| Asking to file a reservation yourself | No such right | Ask an MK to adopt it | Know the stages and who acts at each |
| Ignoring the election | Waiting for a session that will not come | Check continuity and the new committee's plan | Apply the Continuity Law test now |
| Offensive language about MKs or officials | Request refused; public protocol | Apologise and refocus on the text | Write as if it will be published, because it may be |

## Review

- The committee writes the bill's final wording; its sessions are public by default and its protocols are published (Rules ss. 118, 120).
- The chair may invite anyone with knowledge or interest (s. 125); ask early, briefly, and with a position paper.
- Speaking for yourself is not lobbying; paid advocacy for others needs a Knesset permit (Knesset Law ss. 65–66).
- Reservations belong to MKs and ministers; the public's tool is the committee and the MKs who sit on it.
- After the 27 October 2026 elections, bills past first reading may be carried over under the Continuity Law; others must start again.
