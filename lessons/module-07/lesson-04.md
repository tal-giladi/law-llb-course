# 07.4 · Interpreting legislation and checking validity

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read a statute or regulation with the Interpretation Law at your side (counting days, reading "or" and "until", following references), and then run a structured validity check on a regulation or government decision that affects you. You will also know the two ways to attack an invalid rule, directly in the High Court or indirectly as a defence in your own case, and why a court may decline to void a rule even when it finds a defect.

## Why it matters

Lesson 02.4 taught the method of interpretation: language, purpose, and the Barak approach. This lesson adds the statutory toolkit that sits under every Israeli enactment, and the second question a practitioner always asks: is this rule valid at all? A fine, a fee or a refusal based on a regulation is only as good as the regulation. Many people pay or comply when the rule against them was made without authority, never published, or contradicts the statute it claims to implement.

Interpretation and validity are also linked. Courts prefer a reading that keeps a rule valid; you will often win not by having the rule struck down but by persuading the court that, read properly, it does not apply to you.

## The law

### Defaults, not dogma

> חוק זה יחול לגבי כל חיקוק והוראת מינהל, אף אם ניתנו לפני תחילתו, אם אין הוראה אחרת לענין הנדון ואם אין בענין הנדון או בהקשרו דבר שאינו מתיישב עם חוק זה [...]

*Gist:* The Interpretation Law applies to every enactment and administrative instruction unless the enactment provides otherwise or the context is inconsistent with it. [Interpretation Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות#סעיף_1)

### Rules you will use often

| Section | Rule | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| s. 2 | A term defined in a statute has the same meaning in regulations made under it | Read the statute's definitions before the regulation |
| s. 7 | "או", "אחר" and similar words are not limited to the listed items unless "דומה" or "כיוצא בזה" appears | "Any vehicle or other means of transport" is not limited to vehicles-like items |
| s. 8 | "עד..." for time or a quotation means "until and including" | "Until 30 June" includes 30 June |
| s. 9 | "לפי" a law includes regulations under it | "A fee under the Courts Law" includes the fee regulations |
| s. 10 | Counting periods (quoted below) | Your filing deadline |
| s. 11 | A power or duty with no time set must be exercised with due speed | An authority cannot sit on your request forever |
| s. 17 | A power includes reasonable ancillary powers and the power to set its own procedure | Limits to what can be implied |
| s. 22 | Repeal does not revive what was dead or undo past acts and rights | Old rights survive a repeal |
| s. 24 | The binding text is the language in which the law was given | Hebrew controls over any translation |
| s. 25 | A reference to an enactment is to it as amended at the time | References are dynamic |
| s. 26 | A minor deviation from a prescribed form that does not mislead does not invalidate | A form error is not always fatal |

### Counting periods

> (א) מקום שנקבעה תקופה קצובה במספר ימים או שבועות מיום פלוני, אותו יום לא יבוא במנין.
> (ב) תקופה קצובה במספר חדשים או שנים לאחר אירוע פלוני תסתיים בחדשה האחרון ביום שמספרו בחודש כמספר יום האירוע, ואם היה החודש חסר אותו יום – ביום האחרון של החודש.
> (ג) במנין ימי תקופה יבואו גם ימי מנוחה, פגרה או שבתון שעל פי חיקוק, זולת אם הם הימים האחרונים שבתקופה.

*Gist:* For periods in days or weeks, the starting day is not counted; periods in months or years end on the same date in the final month (or its last day); rest days and holidays count, unless the period ends on them. [Interpretation Law s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות#סעיף_10)

Specific procedural rules (for example the Civil Procedure Regulations) may count differently; they prevail where they apply (s. 1).

### Validity: the ladder of norms

> לא תהא תקנה סותרת הוראותיו של כל חוק;

*Gist:* A regulation may not contradict any statute. [Interpretation Ordinance s. 16(4)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הפרשנות#סעיף_16)

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* A protected right may be infringed only by a statute that meets the limitation clause, or under such a statute by virtue of express authorisation in it. [Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_8)

And one Mandate-era presumption that still bites: "אין חיקוק בא לגרוע מכל זכות של המדינה, או להטיל עליה חובה, אלא אם נאמר בו במפורש" ([Interpretation Ordinance s. 42](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הפרשנות#סעיף_42)): a statute binds the state only if it says so.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 1/49 בז׳רנו נ׳ שר המשטרה (1949)

Petition writers who acted as agents for vehicle owners at the Tel Aviv traffic office were barred by officials from appearing there. In one of its first rulings the Supreme Court held that every person has the natural right to pursue an occupation not prohibited by law, and that a public official may not prevent it by administrative action without express or implied legal authority. The order was made absolute.

Why it matters: it is the root of the rule that the administration needs authority in law for every restriction on the individual, the idea later written into Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation and s. 8 of Basic Law: Human Dignity. First question in any validity check: where is the authority? [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/86)

### בג״ץ 11163/03 ועדת המעקב העליונה לענייני הערבים בישראל נ׳ ראש ממשלת ישראל (27.2.2006)

A Government decision designated "national priority areas" whose towns received education benefits; almost no Arab towns were included. President Barak held the decision void because it was discriminatory in its result, even without discriminatory intent. Vice-President Cheshin would have voided it on another ground: designating national priority areas is a primary arrangement that only the Knesset may make, so the Government had no authority to do it by decision.

Why it matters: it shows two separate validity grounds, equality and authority (primary arrangements), applied to a Government decision rather than a regulation. A strong challenge often has both. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/378)

### ע״א 4926/08 Wael & Co. v. National Water and Sewage Authority (9.10.2013)

Water extractors refused to pay levies set in the Water (Extraction Levy) Regulations, arguing in a civil appeal (an indirect challenge) that the regulations were invalid because the invitation to be heard, required by the Water Law, was not published in Arabic. The Court held that there is no general common-law right to be heard before regulations with legislative effect are made; a right exists only where a statute grants it, as the Water Law did. Publication in Arabic was required and its absence was a defect. But under the relative-voidness doctrine (בטלות יחסית) the Court declined to void the regulations: the appellants' arguments would probably not have changed the text, they chose an indirect attack, and voiding would harm the public purse heavily. It ordered Arabic publication in future updates.

Why it matters: finding a defect is not the end. Courts weigh the defect against the consequences of voiding, especially in an indirect attack long after the fact. (The Hebrew party names were not verified; cite by docket number.) [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/393)

## How it works in practice

### The validity checklist

Run these in order. Stop at the first clear failure, but note every doubtful point.

1. **Source.** What is the norm (statute, regulation, by-law, Government decision, directive)? Who made it?
2. **Authority.** Which section empowers the maker? Does the subject of the norm fall within it (Bejerano)?
3. **Procedure.** Was a required approval given (Knesset committee, minister, Government)? Was a required consultation or hearing held (Wael)? Was it published in Reshumot where required?
4. **Primary arrangement.** Does it settle a basic, disputed policy choice the statute left open (Rubinstein, Supreme Monitoring Committee)?
5. **Rights.** Does it infringe a protected right? If so, does the statute authorise that expressly (s. 8)?
6. **Consistency.** Does it contradict a statute (s. 16(4))?
7. **Administrative norms.** Is it reasonable, proportionate, equal, made for a proper purpose with relevant considerations?
8. **Time.** Is it still in force (emergency regulations, temporary orders, repealed enabling law, s. 23)?

### Direct or indirect challenge

- **Direct:** a petition asking the court to annul the norm. For regulations and Government decisions this is usually a High Court of Justice petition (module 6, lesson 06.8); for some local and administrative decisions, the Administrative Affairs Court. Delay can bar a petition.
- **Indirect:** raising invalidity as a defence or argument in your own proceeding, for example a claim to collect a fee or a criminal charge for breaching a regulation. Courts allow it, but as Wael shows, they weigh the defect and the consequences and may apply relative voidness.

For a self-represented person, the indirect route is often the realistic one: you raise it when the rule is enforced against you.

### Interpretation first

Before arguing invalidity, try interpretation. If the regulation can reasonably be read so that it does not cover your case, or so that it stays within the enabling section, that reading usually wins (lesson 02.4, purpose; Interpretation Law s. 2 for shared definitions). It is an easier argument for a judge to accept than annulment.

## Worked example

Lina Mansour from Haifa received a letter from a statutory authority dated Sunday 1 March 2026: her licence will be cancelled unless she submits a renewal form "within 30 days from the date of this letter, and until no later than 31 March 2026". The regulations under which the authority acts also say that the licence holder must attend in person, while the enabling statute says that "the holder or a person authorised by him in writing" may apply.

1. **Counting (s. 10(א), (ג)).** The day of the letter is not counted. Day 1 is 2 March; day 30 is 31 March. Rest days count. Tuesday 31 March is not a rest day, so the period ends then.
2. **"Until" (s. 8).** "עד ליום 31 במרץ" includes 31 March. Both formulas give the same date.
3. **Definitions (s. 2).** The regulation's word "המבקש" is read according to the statute's definition, if the statute defines it.
4. **Consistency (Ordinance s. 16(4)).** The regulation requiring personal attendance contradicts the statute, which allows an authorised agent. Read narrowly, the regulation may be confined to identification on collection; if it cannot be read that way, it is invalid to the extent it contradicts the statute.
5. **Action.** Lina sends the form by 31 March through an agent with written authorisation, quoting the statute, and keeps proof of delivery. If the authority refuses, she has a strong indirect argument and, if needed, a petition (with a lawyer).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The regulation or decision you analysed in lesson 07.2, or another rule that is being enforced against you.
- Its enabling statute and the [Interpretation Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find every period, "or", "until" and cross-reference in the rule. Apply ss. 7–10 and 25 and write the result next to each.
2. Calculate one real deadline from a real date in your matter, showing the counting.
3. Run the eight-step validity checklist. For each step write: passes, doubtful (why), or fails (why), with the source.
4. Try an interpretation that resolves any doubtful point without annulment. Write it in two sentences.
5. Decide which route fits: comply, ask the authority to reconsider, raise an indirect challenge when enforced, or consider a direct petition (with advice).
6. Write a one-page validity memo using the [research memo](../../templates/research-memo.md) structure.

### How you know it worked

- Your deadline calculation shows the start day excluded and states why the end day stands.
- Each checklist step cites the source (a section, a case, a recital).
- You considered an interpretive solution before annulment.
- Your chosen route matches your position: you are the one the rule is enforced against, or you are challenging it in advance.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. Interpreting the rule, counting deadlines and raising invalidity as an argument in your own small claim, objection or defence are things you may do yourself. A direct petition to annul a regulation is legally open to an individual, but in practice it is a lawyer's task (see module 6, lesson 06.8).

### Where and how

- Texts: [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ויקיטקסט:ספר_החוקים_הפתוח), the Knesset legislation database, Reshumot for the official version.
- Raise an indirect challenge in your written defence or statement of claim, with the section numbers, and in your objection to the authority.
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md); for criminal charges, the Public Defender if eligible.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are charged with an offence under the rule you think is invalid.
- You want to petition to annul a regulation, or the deadline for a petition may be running.
- The stakes are high (a licence, a business, a large sum) and the authority has lawyers.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counting the day of the notice as day 1 | Filing one day late | Ask for an extension, explaining the error | Apply s. 10(א): the first day is not counted |
| Assuming a deadline ending on Saturday moves to Sunday automatically under every rule | Late filing where special rules differ | Check the specific procedural rule | Read s. 10(ג) and the specific rule together |
| Arguing invalidity before trying interpretation | Judge reluctant to annul | Offer the narrow reading first | Interpretation first, validity second |
| Waiting years and then attacking a rule indirectly | Relative voidness denies relief (Wael) | Show concrete harm and that the defect mattered | Raise defects early, ideally at the comment stage |
| Ignoring the enabling statute's definitions | Misreading the regulation | Reread with the statute's definitions (s. 2) | Open the statute first |

## Review

- The Interpretation Law supplies default rules (definitions, "or", "until", periods, references, forms) that apply unless the enactment or context says otherwise.
- Periods in days exclude the first day and include rest days unless the last day is one (s. 10).
- A validity check asks about source, authority, procedure, primary arrangements, rights, consistency, administrative norms and time.
- Bejerano: no restriction without legal authority; Supreme Monitoring Committee: equality and primary arrangements; Wael: no general hearing right for regulations, and relative voidness in indirect attacks.
- Try interpretation before annulment, and choose between direct and indirect challenge deliberately.
