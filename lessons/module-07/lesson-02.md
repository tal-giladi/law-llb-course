# 07.2 · Secondary legislation: regulations, orders, directives, and parliamentary approval

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any rule that binds you, whether it is called a regulation, an order, a by-law, a circular or a procedure, and work out who made it, under which section of which law, whether it had to be published or approved by a Knesset committee, and whether it was allowed to say what it says. Most of the rules that touch daily life (fees, forms, licensing conditions, deadlines) are secondary legislation, not statutes.

## Why it matters

The Knesset passes a few hundred laws per term; ministers, authorities and local councils issue thousands of regulations, orders and by-laws. The Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 that govern every civil claim, the small-claims fee, the list of goods you may return to a shop: all of these are secondary legislation. A learner who stops at the statute misses most of the operative law.

Secondary legislation is also where many valid challenges lie. A regulation that goes beyond its enabling law, sets a primary arrangement the Knesset never made, creates an offence without committee approval, or was never published, may be void. Knowing the rules lets you check a rule before you obey it, and argue against it if it was made improperly.

## The law

### What a regulation is

> ”תקנה“ – הוראה שניתנה מכוח חוק והיא בת־פעל תחיקתי.

> ”הוראת מינהל“ – הוראה או מינוי – לרבות הודעה, מודעה, רשיון, היתר וכיוצא באלה – שניתנו בכתב מכוח חוק ואינם בני־פעל תחיקתי;

*Gist:* A regulation is a provision made under a statute that has legislative effect; an administrative instruction (a notice, licence, permit and the like) is made under a statute but has no legislative effect. "Enactment" (חיקוק) means a statute or a regulation. [Interpretation Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות#סעיף_3)

"Legislative effect" means a general norm addressed to the public or an undefined part of it, not to named persons (AG guideline 2.3100, ch. 2). Whatever its title (order, rules, by-law), a general binding norm made under a statute is a regulation in this sense.

### Two rules that survive from the Mandate ordinance

> לא תהא תקנה סותרת הוראותיו של כל חוק;

> תקנות בנות־פעל תחיקתי יפורסמו ברשומות, ותחילת תקפן ביום פרסומן, אם אין הוראה אחרת בענין זה.

*Gist:* A regulation may not contradict any statute; regulations with legislative effect must be published in Reshumot and take effect on publication unless they say otherwise. [Interpretation Ordinance ss. 16(4), 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הפרשנות#סעיף_16) These sections were kept in force by Interpretation Law s. 31.

The Interpretation Law adds that a power to make regulations includes the power to amend or revoke them "in the way they were made" (s. 15), and that when a statute is repealed, regulations under it fall with it unless the repealing law replaces the provisions (s. 23).

### Who makes regulations

> (א) שר הממונה על ביצועו של חוק מוסמך להתקין תקנות לביצועו.
> (ב) חוק יכול שיסמיך את ראש הממשלה או שר להתקין תקנות בענין שנקבע בהסמכה.

*Gist:* The minister in charge of a statute may make regulations to implement it, and a statute may authorise the Prime Minister or a minister to regulate a specified matter. [Basic Law: The Government s. 37](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_37)

### Approval by a Knesset committee

> (ב) תקנות שבהן נקבעו עבירות ועונשים, למעט חוקי עזר של רשות מקומית, טעונות אישור ועדה של הכנסת.

*Gist:* Regulations that create offences and penalties (except municipal by-laws) need the approval of a Knesset committee; s. 2(א) caps imprisonment set in a regulation at six months. [Penal Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_2)

Beyond s. 2(ב), approval is required whenever the enabling statute says so: the formula "באישור ועדת [...] של הכנסת" is common in fees, taxes, social-security and consumer regulations. Committee approval is a condition of validity: a regulation that needed approval and did not get it is void.

> [!NOTE]
> Until 2016, Basic Law: The Knesset s. 21א also required committee approval for regulations with criminal penalties, with approval deemed given after 45 days of committee silence. Section 21א was repealed by Amendment 45 (ס״ח 2573, 11.8.2016). The Attorney General's guideline 2.3100 (last updated 2003) still describes it; read that guideline with this change in mind.

### Emergency regulations

> (ג) תקנות שעת חירום כוחן יפה לשנות כל חוק, להפקיע זמנית את תוקפו או לקבוע בו תנאים [...]
> (ד) אין בכוחן של תקנות שעת חירום למנוע פניה לערכאות, לקבוע ענישה למפרע או להתיר פגיעה בכבוד האדם.
> (ו) תוקפן של תקנות שעת חירום יפקע כעבור שלושה חודשים מיום התקנתן, זולת אם הוארך תוקפן בחוק, או שבוטלו על ידי הכנסת בחוק או בהחלטה של רוב חברי הכנסת.

*Gist:* During a declared state of emergency the Government may make emergency regulations that can override statutes, but not block access to courts, punish retroactively or permit harm to human dignity; they lapse after three months unless extended by statute, and must be laid before the Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee. [Basic Law: The Government s. 39](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_39) The periods are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

Section 39(ה) adds a proportionality limit: emergency measures only "במידה שמצב החירום מחייב זאת". Under Rules s. 73 the committee or thirty MKs may propose that the Knesset revoke emergency regulations. Basic Law: The Knesset s. 44 forbids emergency regulations from changing that Basic Law.

### Directives and by-laws

Internal directives (נהלים, חוזרים, הנחיות) are not legislation: they guide officials and cannot by themselves impose duties on the public beyond what a law allows. They must still be made available: "רשות ציבורית תעמיד לעיון הציבור את ההנחיות המינהליות הכתובות שעל פיהן היא פועלת ושיש להן נגיעה או חשיבות לציבור" ([FOI Law s. 6(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע#סעיף_6)). Local authorities must publish their by-laws (s. 6(ג)), which appear in the חיקוקי שלטון מקומי series of Reshumot.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 3267/97 רובינשטיין נ׳ שר הביטחון (1998)

For decades the Minister of Defence deferred military service for yeshiva students under a general power to exempt "for other reasons". The Court held that the Knesset, not the executive, must make the fundamental decisions on issues that divide society: such a decision is a primary arrangement (הסדר ראשוני) that requires primary legislation. As the number of exempted students grew, "quantity became quality" and the arrangement exceeded the minister's power. The declaration of invalidity was suspended for twelve months to let the Knesset legislate.

Why it matters: it is the leading statement of the primary-arrangements doctrine. A general enabling clause does not let a minister settle a major policy question. The same question (how to regulate yeshiva students' service) has returned to the Knesset and the Court repeatedly since; module 6 (lesson 06.6) covers the constitutional side. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/323)

### בג״ץ 2109/20 בן מאיר נ׳ ראש הממשלה (19.3.2020; 26.4.2020)

In March 2020 the Government made emergency regulations letting the Israel Security Agency track the phones of COVID-19 patients, at a time when Knesset committees had not yet been formed. On 19.3.2020 the Court issued an interim order barring implementation unless a Knesset committee exercised oversight. The Government then acted through a decision under the ISA Law, with committee involvement. On 26.4.2020 the Court held that further use of the ISA for contact tracing would require primary legislation, as a temporary statute meeting the limitation clause, and that tracing journalists required their consent.

Why it matters: emergency and enabling powers do not replace parliamentary oversight, and a serious, continuing rights infringement belongs in a statute. [English translation (19.3.2020)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/88) · [English translation (26.4.2020)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/89)

## How it works in practice

### Reading the heading of a regulation

Every regulation published in Reshumot opens with a recital: "בתוקף סמכותי לפי סעיף __ לחוק __, ובאישור ועדת __ של הכנסת, אני מתקין תקנות אלה". That one line tells you:

- the enabling section, which you must open and read;
- whether committee approval was given;
- who made it (a minister, the Government, a regulator).

If approval is required and the recital does not mention it, check the committee's protocols before concluding anything.

### Three kinds of regulation

The AG guideline 2.3100 distinguishes regulations "in accordance with the law" (תקנות ביצוע, filling in details of arrangements the statute set), regulations "outside the law" (the statute names a subject and lets the regulation set the arrangement, as Courts Law s. 108 does for procedure), and regulations "contrary to the law" (rare, expressly authorised, like emergency regulations). The further a regulation goes from execution, the stricter the scrutiny of its authority.

### Primary arrangements test

Ask: is this a basic policy choice, affecting a large group or a fundamental right, that society disputes? If so, the statute must set at least the main lines. Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8 makes this explicit for protected rights: an infringement must be "בחוק [...] או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו".

### Where to find the text

- The current consolidated text: [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ויקיטקסט:ספר_החוקים_הפתוח) (unofficial) or the Knesset legislation database.
- The official text: Reshumot, קובץ התקנות.
- Committee approval: the committee's protocols on the Knesset site.

### Commencement

Regulations take effect on publication unless they set another date. The AG guideline recommends, absent urgency, a commencement date thirty days after publication, so that the public can prepare.

## Worked example

Yusuf Haddad runs a small falafel stand in Kafr Qasim. A new set of regulations under a food-safety law requires every food business to install a specific ventilation system within 60 days, on pain of a fine of up to ₪20,000 and six months' imprisonment.

1. **Classify.** A general binding norm, made under a statute: a regulation with legislative effect.
2. **Enabling section.** The recital cites a section allowing the Minister of Health to set "conditions for the sanitary handling of food". Yusuf reads it: it authorises sanitary conditions, which a ventilation requirement plausibly is.
3. **Offence and approval.** The regulations create an offence with imprisonment. Under Penal Law s. 2(ב) they need a Knesset committee's approval; s. 2(א) caps imprisonment at six months, so the penalty is within the cap. Yusuf checks the recital: "ובאישור ועדת הבריאות של הכנסת". He finds the protocol of the approval session.
4. **Publication.** Published in קובץ התקנות with a commencement date 30 days later; valid.
5. **Primary arrangement?** A sanitary standard for kitchens is an implementing detail, not a basic social choice. No primary-arrangements problem.
6. **Conclusion.** The regulations look valid. Yusuf's realistic options are to comply, ask the ministry about the transition period, or, when the next amendment is published as a draft, comment on it (lesson 07.5). Had the recital lacked committee approval, the criminal provisions would have been void.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One regulation, order or by-law that affects you (a fee, a licensing condition, a parking by-law, a benefit rule).
- Its text on Wikisource or in Reshumot, and the enabling statute.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find the regulation's full title and Reshumot reference (ק״ת number and page).
2. Copy the recital and identify the enabling section. Open that section and copy its key words.
3. Classify the regulation: implementing details, an arrangement "outside the law", or a regulation authorised to depart from the law.
4. Check committee approval: does the enabling section or Penal Law s. 2(ב) require it? Is it mentioned in the recital?
5. Check publication and commencement.
6. Ask the primary-arrangements question: does the regulation settle a basic policy choice the statute left open?
7. Ask whether it infringes a protected right, and if so whether the statute authorises it expressly (Basic Law s. 8).
8. Write a one-paragraph validity note: valid, doubtful (and why), or likely invalid (and why).

### Template

None. Lesson 07.4 extends this check into a full validity checklist.

### How you know it worked

- You quoted the enabling section, not just named it.
- Your approval conclusion is tied to a specific provision (the enabling section or Penal Law s. 2(ב)).
- Your note separates "I disagree with this rule" from "this rule was made without authority".

### Self-check

- [ ] I can define a regulation using Interpretation Law s. 3.
- [ ] I know that regulations must be published and may not contradict a statute.
- [ ] I can say when a Knesset committee must approve a regulation, and that s. 21א of the Basic Law was repealed in 2016.
- [ ] I can apply the primary-arrangements test from Rubinstein.
- [ ] I can list three limits on emergency regulations.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, the checking. Reading regulations, finding their enabling sections and protocols, and writing to the ministry or the Knesset committee about them are open to everyone in their own name. Raising invalidity as a defence in your own case (for example, against a fine) is also possible yourself (lesson 07.4).

### Where and how

- Text: Wikisource and the Knesset legislation database; official text in Reshumot.
- Committee approval: protocols on each committee's page on the Knesset site (⚠️ the Knesset site may block automated tools; open it in a normal browser).
- Directives: the authority's FOI publications page (FOI Law s. 6); if a directive you are subject to is not published, ask for it (module 2, lesson 02.7).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You want to petition to annul a regulation: that is a High Court petition with strict timing and standing rules (module 6, lesson 06.8).
- You face criminal charges under a regulation you believe is void.
- The regulation affects your licence or business so much that a mistake would be costly.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading only the statute | You miss the operative fees, forms and deadlines | Search for regulations under the statute | Always look for "תקנות" under every law you rely on |
| Treating an internal directive as binding law | You accept a duty no law imposes | Ask for the legal source of the duty | Check whether the norm is in Reshumot |
| Relying on AG guideline 2.3100 for the 45-day approval default | Wrong conclusion on validity | Apply Penal Law s. 2(ב) and the enabling section | Check the current Basic Law text: s. 21א was repealed |
| Assuming emergency regulations last as long as the emergency | Missing that they lapsed | Check the three-month period and any extending statute | Note the date of every emergency regulation |
| Arguing "primary arrangement" against every detailed rule | Weak argument | Reserve it for basic policy choices | Apply the Rubinstein test honestly |

## Review

- A regulation is a provision with legislative effect made under a statute (Interpretation Law s. 3); it must be published and may not contradict a statute (Interpretation Ordinance ss. 16(4), 17).
- Committee approval is required for regulations creating offences (Penal Law s. 2(ב)) and wherever the enabling statute says so; Basic Law s. 21א was repealed in 2016.
- Primary arrangements belong to the Knesset (Rubinstein); rights may be infringed by regulation only under express statutory authority (Basic Law: Human Dignity s. 8).
- Emergency regulations can override statutes but face limits on content and a three-month life (Basic Law: The Government s. 39); Ben Meir insisted on parliamentary oversight and, for continuing measures, primary legislation.
- Internal directives are not legislation, but authorities must publish those that matter to the public (FOI s. 6).
