# 23.10 · Practice lab: witness questions and cross-examination

This lab prepares you for the hearing itself. You will plan the order of witnesses, write a cross-examination of the other side's main witness and of its expert, prepare the objections you are likely to need, rehearse aloud with a friend playing the witness, and turn the result into a summation. It builds on lesson 15.7 (the trial) and on the affidavit and bundle from lesson 23.9. Use your own case (dry run) or the supplied case. Plan about two and a half hours, including rehearsal.

## Why it matters

Credibility is decided in the courtroom (Evidence Ordinance s. 53; lesson [23.1](lesson-01.md)), and cross-examination is where it is decided. Self-represented parties often waste their time arguing with the witness, asking "why", or repeating their own story. A short, prepared cross-examination that gets three admissions and stops is worth more than an hour of questions.

## The law

- **Order and continuity.** Plaintiff's evidence first, then the defendant's; rebuttal evidence needs leave (CPR reg. 66); cross-examination follows immediately after the main testimony (reg. 64(ב)); re-examination is limited to unclear matters arising from cross-examination (reg. 67(ג)).
- **Objections.** State the grounds at once; the other side answers at once; the court rules immediately unless it postpones (reg. 68).
- **The court's powers.** The court may ask its own questions, recall a witness and order documents produced (reg. 69).
- **Fair questioning.**

> בחקירת עד בבית משפט, לא ירשה בית המשפט חקירה אשר, לדעת בית המשפט, אינה לענין הנידן ואינה הוגנת; ובפרט לא ירשה בית המשפט חקירה שיש בה משום עלבון, הפחדה, התעיה או ביוש שאינם לענין הנידן ואינם הוגנים.

*Gist:* The court will not allow questioning that is irrelevant and unfair, in particular insulting, intimidating, misleading or shaming questions. ([Examination of Witnesses Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_הדין_(חקירת_עדים)))

- **Experts.** You may insist on cross-examining the other side's expert (Evidence Ordinance s. 26; CPR reg. 87(ח)); questions to a court expert have their own deadlines (reg. 91; lesson [23.7](lesson-07.md)).
- **Summations.** Oral, as soon as possible after the evidence, unless the court orders written ones; missing written summations is treated like non-appearance (reg. 74).

## Leading cases

### Key text: Examination of Witnesses Law s. 2

The limit on cross-examination is fairness and relevance, not politeness: hard questions are allowed, humiliating ones are not. Prepare questions that would be fair if asked of you. The deeper doctrine on what a witness may say (hearsay, opinion, privilege) is in lessons [23.2](lesson-02.md), [23.3](lesson-03.md) and [23.7](lesson-07.md).

## How it works in practice

### A cross-examination in three parts

1. **Lock in the good facts** the witness must admit ("You signed the quote on 12 January?").
2. **Confront with documents** where the witness's affidavit conflicts with the record ("Please read the message of 10 March aloud.").
3. **Stop.** Do not ask the one question too many ("So you admit you abandoned the job?"); say it in summations instead.

### Rehearsal

Ask a friend or family member to play the witness, once cooperative and once evasive. Time yourself. Cut every question that does not lead to an admission you need.

## Worked example

**Supplied case (continued from lesson [23.9](lesson-09.md)).** At Sapir Biton's hearing the contractor company calls its manager, Ofer Peretz, and files an opinion of a building engineer saying the work done was worth ₪31,000, not ₪25,000.

Sapir's cross-examination of the manager (8 minutes):

1. "On 12 January 2026 you signed a quote for ₪38,000 for the whole bathroom?" (exhibit 1) — "Yes."
2. "You received ₪25,000 by bank transfer on 20 January?" (exhibit 5) — "Yes."
3. "On 10 March you wrote to me: 'נחזור ביום ראשון, אל תדאגי'?" (exhibit 4) — "Yes."
4. "Nobody from your company came after 3 March?" — "We were busy." Sapir: "Is that a yes?" — "Yes."
5. She stops.

Her cross-examination of the engineer (7 minutes):

1. "You visited the flat on 2 June?" — "Yes."
2. "By then the second contractor had finished the bathroom?" — "Yes."
3. "So you did not see the work my contractor left on 3 March?" — "I relied on photos."
4. "The photos you received came from the defendant?" — "Yes."
5. "You did not see my photos of 3 March, exhibit 2?" — "No."

When the defendant's lawyer asked Sapir, "Isn't it true your neighbour told you she saw nothing?", Sapir objected: "The question asks me what someone else said; my neighbour will testify herself." The court allowed the question only as to what Sapir heard, and noted the neighbour would testify.

Her summation followed the table: contract (exhibit 1, admitted), payment (exhibit 5, admitted), abandonment (exhibit 4 and the manager's admission), cost to complete (second contractor's invoice), and the expert's weakness (no visit before completion, defendant's photos only).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your affidavit and exhibit bundle from lesson [23.9](lesson-09.md) (or Sapir's case).
- The other side's affidavits and expert opinion, real or imagined from their pleadings.
- The draft template: [witness examination plan](../../templates/witness-examination-plan.md).
- A friend to play the witness; a timer; about 150 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Order and time (10 min).** Fill in part א of the template: the order of witnesses and the time you expect for each.
2. **Your own witness (15 min).** If your witness testifies orally, write five open questions (part ב). If by affidavit, write one or two re-examination questions you might need (part ו).
3. **Cross-examination of the main witness (35 min).** On part ג, write ten to fifteen closed questions, each with the expected answer, the document that proves it and what you will do if the witness denies it. Order them: good facts, then confrontation.
4. **Cross-examination of the expert (20 min).** On part ד, write one or two questions for each target: qualifications, factual basis, method, independence, consistency. If there is a court expert, draft clarification questions instead and diary their deadline.
5. **Objections (10 min).** Read the other side's affidavits and mark three places where you would object. Practise saying each objection in one sentence (part ה).
6. **Rehearse (40 min).** Run both cross-examinations aloud with your friend: once with a cooperative witness, once with an evasive one. Cut questions that do not produce an admission. Time it.
7. **Summation (20 min).** Fill in part ז: every element, the evidence with transcript or exhibit references, the burden (lesson [23.6](lesson-06.md)) and why the other side's evidence does not displace yours. Rehearse a ten-minute oral version.

### How you know it worked

- Every cross-examination question can be answered yes or no and is tied to a document or a fact the witness must know.
- Your cross-examinations fit inside the time you planned.
- You can make each objection in one sentence, naming its ground.
- Your summation covers every element and says who bore the burden.

### Self-check

- [ ] I do not ask "why" in cross-examination.
- [ ] I know re-examination is only for matters arising from cross-examination.
- [ ] I know I may insist on cross-examining the other side's expert.
- [ ] I know what happens if I miss a written-summations deadline.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. A party may examine and cross-examine witnesses and argue in person (CPR reg. 170). No friend or relative may question witnesses for you in the civil courts; in small claims a helper may appear only with the court's leave (Courts Law s. 63; lesson [12.6](../module-12/lesson-06.md)).

### Where and how

- At the evidence hearing in the assigned courtroom, or by video if the court so orders (CPR reg. 72).
- Witness summonses: ask in time and pay expenses (regs 83–86; periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: court volunteer desks and information centre ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You must cross-examine a professional expert on a technical field you cannot master.
- The other side alleges fraud or forgery against you.
- A witness needs protection, or you are the complainant in a case involving violence or sexual offences.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking "why did you…?" | Witness gives a speech that helps him | Interrupt politely: "My question was whether…" | Closed questions only |
| Arguing with the witness | Judge stops you; time wasted | Move to the next document | Save argument for summations |
| Asking the one question too many | Witness retracts or explains away | Leave it; the transcript has the admission | Stop when you have the point |
| Not objecting to hearsay or opinion | Court may rely on it | Object at once; ask that it be recorded | Mark likely points in advance |
| Missing the written-summations date | Treated like non-appearance (reg. 74) | Ask at once for an extension with reasons | Diary the date the moment it is set |

## Review

- Plan the order and time of witnesses; cross-examination follows each main testimony.
- Cross-examine in three parts: lock in good facts, confront with documents, stop.
- Object at once with a one-sentence ground; the court rules on the spot (reg. 68); unfair and humiliating questions are not allowed (Examination of Witnesses Law s. 2).
- Test experts on qualifications, factual basis, method, independence and consistency.
- Summations connect every element to the evidence and the burden of proof.
