# 23.2 · Witnesses: competence, compellability and privilege

Before the court asks whether a witness is telling the truth, it asks whether the person may testify at all, whether they can be forced to, and whether some of what they know is protected. This lesson covers the three questions: competence (who may testify), compellability (who must) and privilege (what a witness, or a party, may refuse to reveal). Lesson 15.4 introduced privilege in civil disclosure; here you go to the full doctrine, including the criminal-law rules on spouses and family members, the professional privileges and their limits, the case-law journalist privilege, and the protections for children and complainants. By the end you can claim, waive or challenge a privilege in your own case.

## Why it matters

A witness who is incompetent cannot be heard; a witness who is not compellable may refuse; a privileged document can be withheld even though it is relevant and reliable. These rules decide what evidence exists in a case before weight is ever discussed.

For a self-represented party the practical stakes are high:

- **You may be asked for your medical file** in an injury claim, for your therapist's notes in a custody dispute, or for your correspondence with a lawyer you consulted. Knowing which privilege applies, and whether you have already waived it, decides what you must hand over.
- **You may need a witness who does not want to come**: a former employee, a relative. Knowing that almost everyone is compellable lets you ask for a summons with confidence (CPR regs 83–86; lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md)).
- **In criminal matters** the family rules matter: a spouse may not, as a rule, testify against the accused, except in violence and sexual offences within the family.

The doctrinal debate is about the price of privilege. Every privilege hides relevant truth from the court. Supporters justify it by the value of the protected relationship (lawyer and client, doctor and patient, the free press); critics answer that privileges should be narrow and relative, so a judge can lift them when justice requires. Israeli law has both kinds: the lawyer–client privilege is absolute (only the client may waive it), while the medical, psychological and social-work privileges are relative (the court may lift them).

## The law

### Competence: everyone, with family exceptions (ss. 2–8)

> הכל כשרים להעיד בכל משפט, בכפוף לאמור בסעיפים 3 ו־4, ואין אדם פסול להעיד מפני שהוא בעל דין בתובענה אזרחית, או מתלונן או נאשם במשפט פלילי, או מפני שהוא מעבידו, עובדו, בן זוגו או קרובו של התובע, המתלונן, הנתבע או הנאשם, או מפני שהורשע או נושא עונש על עבירה.

*Gist:* Everyone is competent to testify in every case, subject to ss. 3–4; being a party, a complainant, an accused, an employee, a relative or a convicted person is no bar. ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_2))

> במשפט פלילי אין בן זוג כשר להעיד לחובת בן זוגו, ואין כופים אותו להעיד לחובת אדם המואשם יחד עם בן זוגו בכתב אישום אחד.

*Gist:* In a criminal case a spouse is not competent to testify against the accused spouse, and cannot be compelled to testify against a co-accused in the same indictment. ([s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_3)) Section 4 applies the same rule to a parent and a child.

The exceptions are wide (s. 5): the family bar does not apply where the charge is bodily harm, violence or threats; listed sexual and abuse offences committed by a spouse against a spouse, a parent against a child or a child against a parent; breach of a protection order; related obstruction offences; and where the witness had complained to the authorities against the family member before the case. It makes no difference whether the witness is the victim or someone else (s. 5(ג)).

Testimony **for** the accused is always allowed, and once given it may be used against him, including what comes out in cross-examination (s. 6). "Spouse" includes a cohabitant known to the public as a spouse, and excludes a spouse living apart with intent to end the family life permanently, even under one roof (s. 8). In civil cases a party may testify for himself or be called by the opponent (s. 7).

A child below the age of criminal responsibility may testify; the court first warns the child, in language the child understands, to tell the truth (s. 55(א)), and a conviction may not rest on that testimony alone without something to strengthen it (s. 55(ב); lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md)).

### Compellability: s. 1

Anyone who can give admissible and relevant testimony may be summoned (s. 1(א); lesson [23.1](lesson-01.md)). Competence and compellability usually go together: a competent witness must come, take the oath or affirmation and answer, unless a privilege applies. In civil cases you ask the court for a summons in time and pay the witness's expenses (CPR regs 83–86).

### Privileges: chapter ג (ss. 44–52)

| Section | Privilege | Holder | Absolute or relative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 44 | State security and foreign relations, by certificate of the Prime Minister or the Defence or Foreign Minister | The State | Relative: a Supreme Court justice may order disclosure on a petition |
| 45 | Important public interest, by a minister's certificate | The State | Relative: the trial court decides the petition |
| 47 | Self-incrimination | The witness | Personal; does not apply to an accused who chooses to testify about the charge (47(ג)) |
| 48 | Lawyer–client communications | The client | Absolute: only the client waives |
| 49 | Doctor–patient | The patient | Relative: court may lift it for justice |
| 50 | Psychologist | The patient | Relative |
| 50א | Social worker | The client | Relative |
| 50ב | Therapy of victims in sex offences and serious family-violence cases | The victim | Special test in favour of the victim |
| 51 | Clergy, confession | The clergyman | As the religion forbids disclosure |

> דברים ומסמכים שהוחלפו בין עורך דין לבין לקוחו או לבין אדם אחר מטעם הלקוח ויש להם קשר ענייני לשירות המקצועי שניתן על ידי עורך הדין ללקוח, אין עורך הדין חייב למסרם כראיה, אלא אם ויתר הלקוח על החסיון…

*Gist:* Communications and documents between a lawyer and client (or someone on the client's behalf) connected to the professional service are privileged unless the client waives; the lawyer's employees are bound too, and the privilege survives the end of the relationship. ([s. 48](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_48))

> רופא אינו חייב למסור ראיה על דבר הנוגע לאדם שנזקק לשירותו… אלא אם ויתר האדם על החסיון או שמצא בית המשפט כי הצורך לגלות את הראיה לשם עשיית צדק עדיף מן הענין שיש לא לגלותה…

*Gist:* A doctor need not give evidence on what came to him in confidence while treating a patient, unless the patient waives or the court finds that the need to disclose for justice outweighs confidentiality; the claim is heard in camera. ([s. 49](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_49)) Sections 50 and 50א apply the same structure to psychologists and social workers.

> אין אדם חייב למסור ראיה אם יש בה הודיה בעובדה שהיא יסוד מיסודותיה של עבירה שהוא מואשם בה או עשוי להיות מואשם בה.

*Gist:* No one must give evidence that admits a fact forming an element of an offence he is or may be charged with; if the court rejects the claim and the evidence is given, it cannot be used against him in that prosecution without his consent. ([s. 47](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_47)) The privilege applies in civil cases too: a witness in a civil trial may refuse a specific question that would incriminate him.

Section 52 extends all these privileges to any body or person authorised by law to take evidence, such as a tribunal, a commission of inquiry or an investigator.

### Privileges outside the Ordinance

- **Mediation.** What was said in a court-referred mediation is not evidence in civil proceedings ([Courts Law s. 79ג(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_79ג)).
- **Journalist's privilege.** Recognised by case law, not statute (Citrin, below).
- **Documents prepared for litigation.** Courts recognise a privilege for documents whose dominant purpose was the litigation, such as an investigator's report commissioned for the case (lesson [15.4](../module-15/lesson-04.md)). (⚠️ The leading Supreme Court rulings could not be opened on a free host and are not cited.)

### Waiver in personal-injury claims

A plaintiff who sues for bodily injury must attach a medical confidentiality waiver in the prescribed form (CPR reg. 15(3), Form 1). By putting your health in issue you give the defendant access to the relevant records; you may still object to records that have nothing to do with the injury, and ask the court to limit the waiver.

### Protecting vulnerable witnesses

The Law of Procedure Amendment (Examination of Witnesses) Law 1957 lets the court stop questions that are irrelevant and unfair, especially insulting, intimidating, misleading or shaming ones (s. 2). It bars questions about a sexual-offence complainant's sexual past unless refusing would cause the accused a miscarriage of justice (s. 2א). It allows testimony out of the accused's sight (ss. 2ב–2ג), forbids an accused from personally cross-examining a child victim (s. 2ד) and extends some of these protections to related civil tort claims (s. 2ז). ([Examination of Witnesses Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_הדין_(חקירת_עדים)))

## Leading cases

### ב״ש 298/86 ציטרין נ׳ בית הדין המשמעתי של לשכת עורכי הדין במחוז תל אביב (7.4.1987)

Two journalists refused to reveal their sources to the Bar Association's disciplinary court and were fined. President Shamgar held that, although no statute grants it, journalists enjoy a **relative** privilege grounded in freedom of the press: the court may order disclosure only where the question is relevant, disclosure is necessary to do justice on an essential matter, and the matter is serious (grave offences or serious wrongdoing). A Bar disciplinary hearing did not justify lifting it, and the fines were cancelled.

**Why it matters:** privileges are not a closed list; courts can create relative privileges by weighing a public interest against the need for truth. The same three-part balancing appears whenever a court decides whether to lift a relative privilege. English translation: [Versa / LARC](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/110/).

### רע״פ 761/12 מדינת ישראל נ׳ מקור ראשון המאוחד (הצופה) בע״מ (29.11.2012)

Police sought a newspaper photographer's pictures of a violent night-time attack on an army brigade headquarters; she refused, saying they could identify her source. Justice Rubinstein held that information that may identify a source and was given with an expectation of confidentiality is covered by the journalist's privilege, but that the privilege is lifted under the three-part Citrin test where the information is relevant and significant and the authorities have no alternative way to obtain it; a promise of confidentiality is relevant but not decisive.

**Why it matters:** it shows the "no alternative source" factor that decides most applications to lift a relative privilege. If you can prove the fact another way, the court will rarely lift a privilege for you. English translation: [Versa / LARC](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/366/).

### Key text: s. 48 and the absolute lawyer–client privilege

Unlike ss. 49–50א, s. 48 contains no power for the court to lift the privilege "for justice". Only the client can waive it. The text limits it to communications that have a substantive connection to the professional service ("קשר ענייני לשירות המקצועי"): a contract you signed with the other side does not become privileged because you later gave a copy to your lawyer, while your email asking the lawyer what to do about it is privileged.

### Key text: s. 50ב (2022) and victims' therapy files

Added in 2022, s. 50ב changes the test when the defence in a sex-offence or serious family-violence trial seeks the victim's therapy records: disclosure only if the record may help the defence and its value outweighs the interest in confidentiality, including the harm to the victim and her rehabilitation, or if it is essential to the defence; the victim is heard first. It shows how privilege law balances the accused's right to a fair trial against the victim's dignity.

## How it works in practice

### Claiming a privilege

1. **Identify** each document or question. Privilege is claimed item by item, never as a blanket (lesson [15.4](../module-15/lesson-04.md)).
2. **Name the section** and the holder: "חסוי לפי סעיף 48 לפקודת הראיות – התכתבות עם עו״ד בקשר לייעוץ שקיבלתי ביום…".
3. **List it** in your disclosure affidavit, marked privileged, so the other side can challenge it.
4. **If challenged**, the court may inspect the document itself (CPR reg. 59(ב)) and, for ss. 49–50א, hears the claim in camera.

### Answering a request to lift a relative privilege

Argue the balance: how relevant is the record really, can the fact be proved another way, how much harm would disclosure cause, and can the order be narrowed (time period, a summary by the therapist, viewing by the court only).

### Before you call a witness

Ask: is the witness competent (criminal family bar)? Will they come voluntarily or do you need a summons? Will they claim a privilege (self-incrimination is common with business partners)? Plan the evidence without them if the answer is uncertain.

## Worked example

Moshe Gabay, a teacher in Beit Shemesh, is in a custody dispute in the family court. His former wife, Rivka, asks the court to order the psychologist Moshe saw after the separation to hand over his treatment notes, saying they will show he is unstable.

1. **Which privilege?** Psychologist privilege, s. 50: relative, held by Moshe. The family court may relax procedure, but never privileges (Family Court Law s. 8(א)).
2. **Waiver?** Moshe never sued for emotional harm and did not rely on his therapy, so he has not waived.
3. **The balance.** Moshe argues that his parenting can be assessed by the court's own expert (the welfare officer's report and a court-appointed expert, lesson [23.7](lesson-07.md)), which is less invasive; that the notes concern his grief, not his care of the children; and that disclosure would deter parents from seeking help.
4. **A narrower alternative.** He proposes that, if the court sees a need, the psychologist give a short letter confirming attendance and that no risk to the children was identified.
5. **Outcome.** The court hears the claim in camera, declines to order the notes, and asks the court-appointed expert to consider the issue.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A dispute of your own (or Moshe's case), and your list of documents from lesson [15.4](../module-15/lesson-04.md) if you made one.
- Evidence Ordinance ss. 2–8 and 44–52 open.
- About 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Witness check (10 min).** List every witness you might call. For each: competent? compellable? likely to claim a privilege? need a summons?
2. **Privilege scan (15 min).** Go through your documents. Mark each that may be privileged with the section and the holder. Mark the ones the other side may hold that are privileged (their lawyer's letters, their medical file).
3. **Waiver check (5 min).** For each privilege you hold, write whether anything you did (suing for injury, relying on advice, showing the document to others) may have waived it.
4. **Draft one paragraph (10 min).** Write the paragraph you would put in a disclosure affidavit to claim one privilege, and two sentences answering a request to lift it.

### Template

- [Written motion (בקשה בכתב)](../../templates/written-motion.md), for a request about privilege on the list of motions.

### How you know it worked

- Every witness on your list has a competence and compellability answer.
- Every privilege claim names a section and a holder.
- You know which of your privileges the court can lift and which only you can waive.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may claim a privilege you hold, object to questions and ask the court to protect a witness in your own case (CPR reg. 170). The privilege against self-incrimination is personal: only the witness can claim it, and the court decides. In a criminal case where you are a witness against a family member, the competence rules in ss. 3–5 apply whether or not you have a lawyer.

### Where and how

- Privilege claims in civil cases: in your disclosure affidavit and, if disputed, on the list of motions (regs 49, 59; lesson [15.4](../module-15/lesson-04.md)).
- Medical waiver in injury claims: Form 1 in the First Schedule to the 2018 Regulations, attached to the claim (reg. 15(3)).
- Free help: court information centre; Legal Aid where eligible ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A question in a civil case could expose you to a criminal charge (s. 47).
- The other side seeks your psychological or social-work file in a family case.
- A ministerial privilege certificate (ss. 44–45) blocks evidence you need.
- You are a complainant in a sexual-offence or family-violence case and the defence seeks your therapy records (s. 50ב).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Showing your lawyer's opinion to the other side in negotiations | Privilege arguably waived | Argue limited waiver; ask the court to restrict use | Never forward legal advice |
| Blanket "all medical records are privileged" in an injury claim | Rejected: you put your health in issue | Offer a waiver limited to relevant records | Sign the Form 1 waiver; object only to unrelated records |
| Calling your spouse to testify against your co-accused | Spouse cannot be compelled (s. 3) | Find other evidence | Check ss. 3–5 before relying on family witnesses |
| Answering an incriminating question in a civil trial | Answer may be used against you | Claim s. 47 before answering | Ask the court for a ruling first |
| Assuming the family court ignores privilege | Therapy notes protected anyway (s. 8(א)) | Prove your point by other evidence | Plan for court experts instead |

## Review

- Everyone is competent (s. 2); in criminal cases spouses, parents and children are not competent against each other, with wide exceptions for family violence and sexual offences (ss. 3–5).
- Competent witnesses are compellable and must answer unless a privilege applies.
- Privileges: state and public interest (ss. 44–46, by certificate), self-incrimination (s. 47), lawyer (s. 48, absolute), doctor, psychologist, social worker (ss. 49–50א, relative), victims' therapy (s. 50ב), clergy (s. 51); plus mediation (Courts Law s. 79ג) and the case-law journalist privilege (Citrin).
- Claim privilege item by item with the section; a bodily-injury plaintiff must sign a medical waiver.
- The Examination of Witnesses Law protects complainants and children from unfair questioning and confrontation.
