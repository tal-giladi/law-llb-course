# 23.4 · Documents, institutional records and digital evidence

Most modern disputes are proved by documents and data: a contract, a bank statement, a delivery log, a WhatsApp chat, an email, a signed PDF. This lesson covers how Israeli law lets a document in and what it proves: originals and copies, public documents, institutional records and computer output under ss. 35–39ב of the Evidence Ordinance, and digital evidence such as chats, screenshots and electronic signatures. It builds on lesson 12.2 (collecting evidence lawfully) and lesson 23.3 (hearsay), because a document is usually an out-of-court statement. By the end you can prepare a document bundle that survives the questions "who made this?", "has it been changed?" and "is it the whole thing?".

## Why it matters

A document raises three separate questions, and the other side can attack each:

1. **Authenticity.** Is it what it claims to be? Did the defendant really send this message, sign this contract, issue this invoice?
2. **Integrity and completeness.** Is it unaltered, and is it the whole conversation, not a cropped screenshot?
3. **Content.** Even if authentic, is it admissible to prove the truth of what it says, or is it hearsay (lesson [23.3](lesson-03.md))?

The law answers these questions with a mix of old rules written for paper (originals, certified copies, the 20-year rule for old documents) and rules added for computers in 1995, when the Computers Law amended the Evidence Ordinance to let computer output in as an institutional record. Digital evidence is now the norm, and courts deal with it through these provisions, the Electronic Signature Law 2001 and the 2018 Regulations' notice requirement for electronic evidence. The open debate is how much proof of authenticity to demand when editing tools and AI make fabrication cheap: courts generally admit chats and screenshots and fight about weight, but a party who cannot explain where a file came from risks having it disregarded.

## The law

### Originals and copies

The common-law **best-evidence rule** requires the original to prove the content of a document; a copy is admissible when the original is lost, destroyed or in the other side's hands. Israeli courts have relaxed it over the years, and in civil practice the question is usually weight, not admissibility. The Regulations reflect this: copies, not originals, are filed unless the court orders otherwise (CPR reg. 70; lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md)). Bring the original to the hearing in case it is challenged.

The Ordinance adds statutory routes:

- A **photocopy** made under the photocopy regulations, with a certificate, is prima facie evidence (s. 41).
- An **institutional record** may be proved by a photocopy (s. 41א), and computer output that is an institutional record counts as an **original**, not a copy (s. 41ב).
- A document at least **20 years old**, from proper custody, may be presumed to be in the handwriting and duly signed by the person it appears to come from (s. 43).

### Public documents: ss. 29–34ב

A public document (a legislative, judicial or executive act, or part of the official records of the State, a ministry, a local authority, a court, a notary or another official body, Israeli or foreign) may be proved by the original, an examined copy, a copy printed by the official printer, or a copy certified by the official who keeps it (s. 32). Official seals, signatures and custody are presumed genuine until the contrary is proved (s. 34), and what was published in Reshumot is presumed duly done (s. 34א). A document made abroad may be proved by certification before an Israeli diplomatic or consular representative, or by a foreign notary whose certificate is authenticated by such a representative (s. 30).

### Institutional records: ss. 35–36

> ”רשומה מוסדית“ – מסמך, לרבות פלט, אשר נערך על ידי מוסד במהלך פעילותו הרגילה של המוסד;

*Gist:* An institutional record is a document, including computer output, made by an institution in the ordinary course of its activity; "institution" means the State, a local authority, a business (including a profession) or anyone who provides a service to the public. ([s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_35))

> (א) רשומה מוסדית תהא ראיה קבילה להוכחת אמיתות תוכנה בכל הליך משפטי, אם נתקיימו כל אלה –
> (1) המוסד נוהג, במהלך ניהולו הרגיל, לערוך רישום של האירוע נושא הרשומה בסמוך להתרחשותו;
> (2) דרך איסוף הנתונים נושא הרשומה ודרך עריכת הרשומה יש בהן כדי להעיד על אמיתות תוכנה של הרשומה;
> (3) היתה הרשומה פלט – הוכח בנוסף, כי –
> (א) דרך הפקת הרשומה יש בה כדי להעיד על אמינותה;
> (ב) המוסד נוקט, באורח סדיר, אמצעי הגנה סבירים מפני חדירה לחומר מחשב ומפני שיבוש בעבודת המחשב.

*Gist:* An institutional record proves the truth of its content in any proceeding if the institution routinely records such events close to when they happen and its way of collecting data and making the record indicates truthfulness; for computer output, also that the way the output is produced indicates reliability and that the institution regularly takes reasonable measures against intrusion and disruption. ([s. 36(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_36))

Further rules:

- For output, the time the underlying data was entered counts as the time the record was made (s. 36(ב)).
- Records made by investigating or prosecuting authorities and offered by them in a criminal case cannot use this route (s. 36(ג)): the police must bring the officer.
- If a record is admitted, the other side may cross-examine witnesses it summons to rebut it, if they are connected to the party who offered the record (s. 36(ד)).
- Output that expresses an opinion on a scientific, professional or medical matter is admissible only under the rules for experts (s. 39א; lesson [23.7](lesson-07.md)).
- These sections cure only the hearsay problem; a record that is inadmissible for another reason stays inadmissible (s. 39ב).

**Bank records.** In a case where the bank is not a party, a banker cannot be compelled to produce its records or testify about accounts recorded in them except by a court order for special reason (s. 38); a party may ask the court for an order to inspect and copy entries in a bank's records (s. 39).

**Computer output** (פלט) is "data, signs, concepts or instructions produced in any way by a computer" ([Computers Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המחשבים)).

### Electronic signatures: Electronic Signature Law ss. 2–4

> (א) לא תישלל קבילותה של חתימה רק בשל היותה חתימה אלקטרונית.

*Gist:* A signature is not inadmissible merely because it is electronic. ([s. 3(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חתימה_אלקטרונית#סעיף_3))

A message signed with a **secure** electronic signature is admissible and is prima facie evidence that it was not changed after signing; with a **certified** signature (issued by a registered certifying authority), it is also prima facie evidence that the holder signed it (s. 3(ב)). Where a statute requires a signature, an electronic message satisfies it by a certified signature or by another electronic signature that fulfils the purposes of the requirement with sufficient certainty, except for document types listed in the First Schedule (s. 2).

> (א) גורם מעצב המבקש להסתמך על חתימה של צד לחוזה עמו על מסר אלקטרוני בקשר לאותו חוזה או לכריתתו, עליו הנטל להוכיח כי אותו צד חתם על המסר האלקטרוני…

*Gist:* The party that designed how the contract is signed electronically (typically the business with the online form) must prove that the other party really signed, whatever the contract says; the rule does not apply to a certified signature. ([s. 3א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חתימה_אלקטרונית#סעיף_3א))

### Notice of electronic evidence: CPR reg. 71

> בעל דין המבקש להציג ראיה במדיה אלקטרונית או דיגיטלית, יודיע לבית המשפט על כוונתו לעשות כן לא יאוחר משלושה ימים לפני מועד הדיון…

*Gist:* A party who wants to present evidence in electronic or digital media must notify the court no later than three days before the hearing; the Courts Director may set the format. ([reg. 71](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי)) A Courts Director's notice on submitting electronic evidence was published in 2025–2026 (י״פ תשפ״ו 1800; ⚠️ text not opened: check the format before you file).

## Leading cases

### Key text: s. 36 and the 1995 amendment

The institutional-record rules were rewritten by the Computers Law 1995. The explanatory notes, quoted in [Hebrew Wikipedia, פקודת הראיות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות) (secondary), say the amendment extended rules that had applied only to bank records to all institutions and simplified the conditions. That is why today a phone company's call log, a supermarket's till record or a hospital's electronic file can prove its content through a witness from the institution who explains how records are made and protected, without calling every clerk who typed an entry.

### Key text: Electronic Signature Law s. 3א (2018)

Added in 2018, s. 3א reverses the practical burden in online contracts: if a bank, phone company or online shop relies on "your" electronic signature on a form it designed, the business must prove you signed. For a consumer disputing a signature on a digital loan or subscription form, this is the starting point.

## How it works in practice

### Laying the foundation for a business record

Ask the institution for a certificate or affidavit from the person responsible for its records stating: (1) the institution routinely records such events close to when they happen; (2) how data is collected and entered; (3) for output, how it is produced and what protections against intrusion and disruption are in place. If the institution refuses, ask the court to summon a records officer (CPR reg. 83) or to order production (s. 39 for banks).

### Digital evidence checklist

| Item | How to preserve | How to show it is authentic | Common attack |
|---|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp chat | Export the chat (with media) and keep the phone; screenshots showing the number and date | Your testimony as recipient; the export file; the other side's own phone if disputed | "Cropped, out of context": file the whole relevant exchange |
| Email | Save as .eml or PDF with full headers | Headers show sender, server and time | "Forged": headers and the sender's server records |
| Screenshot of a website or post | Screenshot with the URL and date; a web archive copy | Your testimony when and how you captured it | "Edited": keep the original file and its metadata |
| Audio recording of your own call | Original file unedited; note date, time, participants | Your testimony; a transcript (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)) | "Spliced": produce the full original |
| Photo | Original file with metadata | Your testimony; metadata date | "Taken another day": send it to the other side at the time |
| E-signed PDF | Keep the signing platform's audit trail | Certificate or audit log; s. 3א if they designed the process | "Not my signature": burden under s. 3א |

### Presenting it

- File PDFs of chats and screenshots as numbered exhibits, and give reg. 71 notice for audio, video or any file to be played.
- Bring the device to the hearing; the court may want to see the original chat.
- Never edit a file; if you need to highlight, do it on a copy and say so.

## Worked example

Yusuf Darawshe, a contractor from Iksal, is sued by a homeowner, Liat Ben-Ami, for ₪22,000 for a delayed renovation. Liat relies on a WhatsApp screenshot in which Yusuf supposedly wrote "I promise to finish by 1 March or I pay ₪500 a day". Yusuf says he wrote "I hope to finish by 1 March" and nothing about a penalty.

1. **Authenticity and integrity.** Yusuf denies the text. He asks for the full chat export, not a single screenshot, and brings his own phone with the chat. The export shows "I hope to finish by 1 March"; the screenshot's font and spacing differ.
2. **Burden.** Liat must prove the message she relies on. The court asks her to produce her phone; the message on her phone matches Yusuf's version.
3. **Business record.** Yusuf also relies on the building-materials supplier's delivery log showing that tiles Liat chose arrived only on 20 February. He obtains a certificate from the supplier's logistics manager on how deliveries are logged in the system at the time of delivery (s. 36(א)(1)–(3)).
4. **Notice.** He gives reg. 71 notice that he will show the chat on his phone at the hearing.
5. **Outcome.** The screenshot is given no weight; the delay is attributed largely to the tile delivery; the claim is reduced to ₪4,000 for the remaining delay.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The documents and digital files in a dispute of your own (or Yusuf's case).
- Evidence Ordinance ss. 35–36 and the Electronic Signature Law ss. 2–3א open.
- About 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Inventory (10 min).** List every document and file you will rely on: type, source, date, where the original is.
2. **Three questions (15 min).** For each, write how you will show authenticity, integrity/completeness, and admissibility of content (own document, other party's admission, institutional record, public document).
3. **Preserve (10 min).** Export at least one chat with media, save one email with full headers, and record the metadata of one photo.
4. **Institutional record (10 min).** For one third-party record you need (bank, phone company, municipality), draft a three-line request for a records certificate covering the s. 36 conditions.
5. **Notice (5 min).** Draft a one-line reg. 71 notice listing the electronic evidence you will present.

### How you know it worked

- Every exhibit has a stated source and a way to prove it is authentic and complete.
- Each third-party record has a plan for the s. 36 foundation.
- Your electronic files are originals, unedited, with the device or export available.

- [ ] I know computer output can be an institutional record if s. 36(א)(3) is met.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may file your own documents and digital evidence, request records about yourself (Privacy Protection Law s. 13; lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)) and ask the court for orders to produce third-party records in your own case (CPR reg. 170). A request for a bank-records order under s. 39 is a motion you may file yourself.

### Where and how

- Copies are filed; originals brought to the hearing (reg. 70).
- Electronic evidence: notice at least three days before the hearing (reg. 71), in the format the Courts Director sets ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); in small claims, media files by the method the court allows (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)).
- Free help: court information centre ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side alleges forgery of a document or signature, or you suspect forgery.
- You need a forensic examination of a device or file.
- The key evidence is a large data set (accounts, logs) you cannot explain without an expert.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing a single cropped screenshot | Challenged as incomplete; low weight | File the full export and bring the phone | Export whole chats at once |
| Deleting the chat after taking screenshots | Cannot prove integrity | Ask the other side or the provider for its copy | Keep the device and the export |
| Filing a bank printout without any foundation | Objection under s. 36 | Get a records certificate or summon the bank officer | Request the certificate early |
| Presenting a video without notice | Court may refuse to view it | Ask leave; explain the delay | Give reg. 71 notice three days before |
| Accepting a business's claim that "you signed online" | You lose a dispute you could win | Put the business to proof under s. 3א | Ask for the audit trail |

## Review

- Every document raises authenticity, integrity and content questions; answer all three.
- Copies are usually enough (reg. 70; s. 41); public documents are proved by certified copies (s. 32) and presumed genuine (s. 34).
- Institutional records, including computer output, prove their content if the s. 36 conditions are shown; police records in criminal cases are excluded from this route.
- Electronic signatures are admissible; secure and certified signatures carry presumptions, and the business that designed an online signing process must prove the consumer signed (s. 3א).
- Preserve digital evidence as original files, export whole chats, keep the device, and give reg. 71 notice.
