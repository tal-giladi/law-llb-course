# 23.3 · Hearsay and its exceptions

"My neighbour told me the landlord admitted the boiler was broken" is the most common sentence in a self-represented party's affidavit, and the most common reason a paragraph is struck out. This lesson teaches the rule against hearsay (עדות שמיעה, also called עדות מפי השמועה): what it is, why it exists, and how Israeli law lets some out-of-court statements in, through the Evidence Ordinance's statutory exceptions and through case-law exceptions. By the end you can test any statement in your own case, take hearsay out of your affidavit, and find a lawful way to prove the same fact.

## Why it matters

The rule against hearsay is the central exclusionary rule of the common-law tradition Israel inherited. It is also the one non-lawyers break most often, because in daily life we rely on what others tell us. In court, a fact is proved by someone who perceived it with his own senses and can be cross-examined about it.

The rule rests on three worries about a statement made out of court by someone who does not testify:

1. **Sincerity.** The maker was not under oath and cannot be cross-examined, so he may have lied.
2. **Perception and memory.** He may have seen badly or remembered wrongly.
3. **Transmission.** The witness who repeats it may have misheard or misunderstood.

The debate is whether these worries justify exclusion or only a reduction in weight. Critics point out that the rule excludes reliable evidence (a business's routine log, a spontaneous cry for help) and that professional judges can discount weak hearsay. Defenders answer that the rule protects the right to confront one's accusers, especially in criminal trials. Israeli law keeps the rule but has many exceptions, mostly in criminal law, where the legislature added s. 10א in 1979 to deal with witnesses who change their story in court.

## The law

### The rule and the test

The rule itself is case law, not statute: a witness may testify to what he perceived; a statement made out of court by another person is inadmissible **to prove that its content is true**. In civil cases the same idea is written into affidavits: an affidavit in lieu of testimony contains only facts the deponent can testify to from his own knowledge ([CPR reg. 178(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי); lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md)).

The key question is always: **what is the statement offered to prove?**

- Offered to prove that its content is true ("the landlord said the boiler was broken" to prove the boiler was broken): hearsay.
- Offered to prove only that it was said, because saying it has legal effect or relevance: not hearsay. Examples: the offer and acceptance that formed a contract, a notice of cancellation, a threat, a defamatory statement, a warning that shows the defendant knew of a danger.

### Statement at the time of an offence: s. 9

> עדות על אמרה שנאמרה בשעה שנעשה, לפי הטענה, מעשה עבירה, או בסמוך לפניו או לאחריו, והאמרה נוגעת במישרין לעובדה השייכת לענין, תהא קבילה אם אמר אותה אדם שהוא עצמו עד במשפט.

*Gist:* Testimony about a statement made at the time of an alleged offence, or shortly before or after it, directly about a relevant fact, is admissible if the person who made it is himself a witness at the trial. ([s. 9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_9))

### Statement of a victim of violence: s. 10

> עדות על אמרה שאמר אדם שנעשה בו, לפי הטענה, מעשה אלימות, והאמרה נוגעת לאותו מעשה או לנסיבות־לואי שלו, תהא קבילה אף אם האדם שאמר אותה אינו נוכח כעד ואף אין להביאו למשפט משום שהוא נפטר או תשוש או חולה או נעדר מן הארץ, ובלבד שנתקיימה באותה אמרה אחת מאלה…

*Gist:* A statement by the alleged victim of violence about the act is admissible even if he cannot be brought to court because he died, is weak, ill or abroad, if it was made at the time or soon after (or at the first opportunity to complain), or is a link in the chain of events, or was made when he was dying or believed he was dying. ([s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_10))

### A witness's prior statement: ss. 10א–10ג

> (א) אמרה בכתב שנתן עד מחוץ לבית המשפט תהיה קבילה כראיה בהליך פלילי אם נתקיימו אלה:
> (1) מתן האמרה הוכח במשפט;
> (2) נותן האמרה הוא עד במשפט וניתנה לצדדים הזדמנות לחקרו;
> (3) העדות שונה, לדעת בית משפט, מן האמרה בפרט מהותי, או העד מכחיש את תוכן האמרה או טוען כי אינו זוכר את תכנה.

*Gist:* In a criminal trial, a witness's written out-of-court statement (usually his police statement) is admissible if its giving is proved, he testifies and can be cross-examined, and his testimony differs materially from it, or he denies it or says he does not remember. ([s. 10א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_10א))

The statement is also admissible if the witness refuses or cannot testify, is dead or cannot be found, provided the court is convinced that improper means were used to deter or prevent him from testifying (s. 10א(ב)). The court may base findings on the statement and prefer it to the testimony, giving reasons, in light of the circumstances, the evidence, the witness's conduct and the signs of truth (s. 10א(ג)). But:

> (ד) לא יורשע אדם על סמך אמרה שנתקבלה לפי סעיף זה אלא אם יש בחומר הראיות דבר לחיזוקה.

*Gist:* No conviction may rest on such a statement unless something in the evidence strengthens it. ([s. 10א(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_10א); the support requirements are in lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md))

A written statement is also admissible in a criminal case if both sides agree, its content is not disputed and the accused has a lawyer (s. 10ב). These sections do not make admissible what another rule excludes (s. 10ג).

### The accused's own statement: s. 11

> אמרתו של נאשם מותר להוכיח בעדותו של אדם ששמע אותה; נרשמה האמרה בכתב והנאשם חתם עליה או קיים אותה באופן אחר, מותר להוכיחה בעדות על כך ממי שהיה נוכח באותו מעמד…

*Gist:* The accused's statement may be proved by the person who heard it, and a signed written statement by someone who was present; a confession must also pass the "free and voluntary" test of s. 12 (lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md)). ([s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_11))

### Other statutory routes for out-of-court statements

- **Institutional records** (ss. 35–36): a business or public body's routine record proves its content (lesson [23.4](lesson-04.md)).
- **Public servant's certificate** (s. 23) and **public documents** (ss. 29–34).
- **A final criminal conviction** is prima facie evidence of its findings in a civil case against the convicted person (s. 42א).
- **The child investigator**: in listed offences a child's testimony recorded by a child investigator, and his notes, are admissible ([Child Protection Law s. 9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_דיני_הראיות_(הגנת_ילדים))), but a conviction on it requires corroboration (סיוע) in other evidence (s. 11 of that Law).

### Case-law exceptions

- **Admission of a party** (הודיית בעל דין): what the other party said against his own interest may be proved by anyone who heard it. The party is in court and can explain, so the main worry falls away. This is why your recorded call with the landlord admitting the debt is so valuable.
- **Spontaneous statements** (res gestae), discussed in the case below.
- **Small claims**: the court may admit hearsay ([Courts Law s. 62](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_62)), but it weighs it lightly.

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 7293/97 ז׳אפר עאמר נ׳ מדינת ישראל

A woman phoned a police volunteer while the robbers were driving away and gave the getaway car's number; she was never identified and did not testify. The Supreme Court held that s. 9, which requires the maker to testify, does not cover every spontaneous statement: where the maker cannot be brought to court for objective reasons, the common-law res gestae exception still allows a spontaneous statement made in the excitement of the event, close to it in time.

**Why it matters:** case law fills gaps in the Ordinance's list of exceptions, but the conditions are strict: a startling event, a statement made before the excitement fades, and a link to the event. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, רס גסטה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/רס_גסטה) (⚠️ secondary source; full text not opened).

### Key text: s. 10א and the "turncoat witness"

Section 10א answers a familiar problem: a witness tells the police one thing and, at trial, under pressure or loyalty, says another or claims to remember nothing. The statement goes in, the witness is cross-examined on both versions, and the court may prefer the statement, with written reasons, provided something else in the evidence strengthens it. Case law (summarised on [Hebrew Wikipedia, עדות מפי השמועה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/עדות_מפי_השמועה), ⚠️ secondary) has applied s. 10א(א) also to a witness who appears but will not give coherent testimony, and has read s. 10א(ב) flexibly about who applied the pressure.

## How it works in practice

### The two-question test

1. Is this a statement made out of court by someone other than the witness now testifying (or by the witness himself on an earlier occasion)?
2. Is it offered to prove that what it says is true?

Two yeses: hearsay. Then look for an exception: s. 9, 10, 10א–10ב, 11, 36, 42א, admission of a party, res gestae. No exception: find the person who perceived the fact and call him.

### Cleaning your affidavit

| You wrote | Problem | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "The neighbour told me the movers dropped the cabinet." | Hearsay of the neighbour | Ask the neighbour for her own affidavit; in yours: "On 3.3 I saw a dent on the cabinet." |
| "The landlord told me on the phone that he knew about the leak." | Not a problem: admission of a party | Keep it, attach the recording or note the date and time |
| "The technician said the boiler was installed wrong." | Hearsay and opinion | Get the technician's signed report or opinion; call him |
| "The company's website said delivery in 3 days." | Not hearsay if offered to prove the promise was made | Keep it, attach a dated screenshot |

### Objecting

Say at once: "I object, the witness is repeating what someone else told him; it is hearsay." If the other side says it is offered only to show what was said, ask the court to record that limit, and argue in summations that it proves nothing more.

## Worked example

Tigist Mengistu, a nurse from Rehovot, sues her former landlord for her ₪7,000 deposit. He defends: she left the flat damaged. Her draft affidavit says:

1. "When I moved out the flat was clean and undamaged." Own knowledge: keep.
2. "The new tenant told me the landlord said he would keep my deposit anyway." Double hearsay (new tenant, landlord). Strike out; but the landlord's statement, if proved first-hand, would be an admission of a party. She asks the new tenant to testify about what the landlord said to him in his presence; the tenant heard the landlord directly, so the tenant can testify to the admission.
3. "My friend Ruth saw the flat on the last day and said it was spotless." Hearsay of Ruth. Rewrite: Ruth gives her own affidavit about what she saw.
4. "The landlord wrote to me on WhatsApp: 'I'll return the deposit after I check the boiler'." An admission of a party in writing: keep, with the chat export (lesson [23.4](lesson-04.md)).
5. "The handyman's invoice shows the landlord painted the flat before I moved in." The invoice is a business document; if the handyman is a business that keeps such records routinely, it may be proved as an institutional record (s. 36) or, more simply, by asking the landlord about it in cross-examination.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A draft affidavit of your own (from lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md) or the module 15 project), or Tigist's paragraphs.
- Evidence Ordinance ss. 9–11 open.
- About 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Mark (10 min).** In your affidavit, underline every sentence that reports what someone else said or wrote.
2. **Test (10 min).** For each, write what it is offered to prove. Mark it "not hearsay" (offered for the fact it was said, or an admission of the other party) or "hearsay".
3. **Fix (15 min).** For every "hearsay" sentence, choose a fix: the person who perceived it gives his own affidavit; a document proves it as an institutional record; you delete it.
4. **Object (5 min).** Read the other side's affidavit (or imagine one) and write two objections to hearsay you would make, in one sentence each.

### How you know it worked

- No sentence in your affidavit relies on what an absent person said, unless it is the other party's admission or is offered only to prove it was said.
- Every fact you removed has another route to proof.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may draft your own affidavit, object to hearsay in the other side's evidence and argue an exception in your own civil case (CPR reg. 170). In small claims the rule is relaxed (Courts Law s. 62). As a witness in a criminal case, you do not decide what is admitted; the prosecution and defence argue it.

### Where and how

- Affidavits: signed before a lawyer or another authorised person who confirms the warning (Evidence Ordinance s. 15; lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md)).
- Witnesses who will not come: ask for a court summons in time (CPR reg. 83(ב); periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: court information centre ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The key fact can only be proved through a statement of someone who is dead, abroad or refuses to testify.
- You are an accused and the prosecution relies on a witness's police statement under s. 10א.
- The other side's case rests on a chain of documents and summaries whose makers will not testify.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affidavit repeats what friends and neighbours said | Paragraphs struck or given no weight | Get the friends' own affidavits; ask leave to file them | Write only what you saw, heard or did |
| Treating the other party's own words as hearsay and leaving them out | Lose your best evidence | Add the admission with date, place and recording | Remember: a party's admission is admissible against him |
| Not objecting when the other side's witness repeats gossip | Court may rely on it | Object at once; ask that the limit be recorded | Listen for "he told me that…" |
| Assuming a police statement is evidence in your civil case | It is hearsay there (s. 10א is criminal only) | Call the person who gave it | Plan first-hand witnesses |
| Relying on small-claims flexibility for a central fact | Admitted but outweighed | Bring the source in person | Use hearsay only for side issues |

## Review

- Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove its content is true; the same words offered to prove they were said are not hearsay.
- Statutory exceptions: s. 9 (maker testifies), s. 10 (victim of violence unavailable, incl. dying declarations), ss. 10א–10ב (a witness's prior statement in criminal cases, with support needed for conviction), s. 11 (accused), s. 36 (records), s. 42א (conviction), Child Protection Law s. 9 (child investigator, with corroboration).
- Case-law exceptions: a party's admission and spontaneous statements (Amar).
- In civil affidavits, own knowledge only (reg. 178); find the person who perceived the fact.
- Small claims admits hearsay but weighs it lightly.
