# 23.7 · Expert opinions and medical evidence

When a case turns on medicine, engineering, valuation or any other professional field, the judge cannot decide alone, and an ordinary witness may not give an opinion. Expert evidence fills the gap, and it is usually the most expensive and most decisive evidence in a civil case. This lesson covers the opinion rule, how an expert opinion is admitted in writing under ss. 20–26 of the Evidence Ordinance, the strict filing rules of CPR regs 87–93, court-appointed experts, the special regimes for road accidents and family cases, and how to test an expert in cross-examination. Lesson 15.7 gave you the deadlines; this lesson gives you the doctrine and the strategy.

## Why it matters

Without an expert opinion, a medical matter simply cannot be proved in a civil court, save for special reasons (CPR reg. 93(א)). Many self-represented claims fail on this one rule: the plaintiff tells the court how much his back hurts, but no doctor's opinion links the pain to the accident or sets the disability percentage.

At the same time, expert evidence has a known weakness. Each side pays its own expert, and the opinions often mirror the payer. Supreme Court justices have criticised the "battle of experts" (below), and the system leans increasingly on court-appointed experts, who are neutral but whose opinions are hard to displace. Knowing when to hire your own expert, when to ask for a court expert and how to challenge either is a core trial skill.

## The law

### The opinion rule

An ordinary witness testifies to facts he perceived; he may not give opinions on matters requiring expertise. This is a common-law rule applied by Israeli courts. A lay witness may still describe impressions that ordinary people form from what they see ("he looked drunk", "the car was going fast"). Opinions on professional matters come only from experts, and the Ordinance lets them be given in writing.

### Written expert opinions: ss. 20–26

> בית המשפט רשאי, אם אין הוא רואה חשש לעיוות דין, לקבל כראיה, בכתב, חוות דעתו של מומחה בשאלה שבמדע, שבמחקר, שבאמנות או שבידיעה מקצועית (להלן – חוות דעת), ותעודה של רופא על מצב בריאותו של אדם (להלן – תעודת רופא).

*Gist:* The court may admit in writing an expert's opinion on a question of science, research, art or professional knowledge, and a doctor's certificate on a person's health, if it sees no risk of a miscarriage of justice. ([s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_20))

- **Signature presumed genuine** for opinions made in Israel (s. 21); opinions made abroad need certification by an Israeli consular or diplomatic representative that the signature is trustworthy and that the expert faces penalties for falsehood under local law (s. 22).
- **Form**: as in the First Schedule or as close as possible (s. 24(א)); the Minister may regulate (s. 24(ב)).
- **Treated as sworn testimony** for the perjury offence; every opinion must contain the expert's declaration that he knows this (s. 25).
- **Cross-examination**: the court may order the expert to be examined orally, and **must** do so if a party asks; a vexatious request may cost the requester (s. 26).
- Computer output that expresses a professional or medical opinion is admissible only through these rules (s. 39א; lesson [23.4](lesson-04.md)).

### Party experts in civil cases: CPR reg. 87

> (א) רצה בעל דין להוכיח עניין שברפואה, יצרף לכתב טענותיו חוות דעת של מומחה בתחום מומחיותו.

*Gist:* A party who wants to prove a medical matter attaches an opinion of an expert in the relevant field to its pleading. ([reg. 87(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

| Rule | Content |
|---|---|
| 87(ב) | After a plaintiff's medical opinion, the defendant may demand in writing that the plaintiff be examined by its expert; the plaintiff must agree without delay unless refusal is justified |
| 87(ג) | The plaintiff answers a medical opinion attached to the defence with a counter-opinion within the set period |
| 87(ד) | The court may exempt a party from attaching a medical opinion or postpone it; ask with the claim or defence |
| 87(ה)–(ו) | Non-medical opinions: filed a set period before the evidence hearing; the answering opinion later |
| 87(ז) | The opinion follows s. 24 and attaches a list of the sources it relies on |
| 87(ח) | You bring your expert for cross-examination unless the other side waives |
| 87(ט) | No additional opinions without the court's approval |
| 93(א) | No medical opinion attached and no exemption: you cannot prove the medical matter, save special reasons |
| 93(ב) | Failure to comply with the experts chapter without reasonable justification: the court will not consider your expert proof |

All periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Court-appointed experts: regs 88–92 and the Experts Regulations

> (א) … בית המשפט רשאי בכל עת למנות מטעמו מומחה, כמה מומחים או צוות של מומחים למתן חוות דעת בעניין שבמומחיות…

*Gist:* The court may at any time appoint one or more experts of its own, weighing the value of the claim, its subject and the scope of the dispute between the parties or their experts. ([reg. 88(א)–(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

- Once a court expert is appointed, party experts are not cross-examined unless a party asks (reg. 88(ג)).
- If the parties **agreed** to the court expert, by default no party opinions are filed and earlier ones are treated as not admitted (reg. 88(ד)).
- The court expert may require a party to submit the subject of the opinion for examination (reg. 90).
- Clarification questions and cross-examination of the court expert have their own deadlines (reg. 91); the court decides who pays and may order a deposit (reg. 92).
- A court expert may not be appointed if there is a conflict of interest, for example a family relationship with a party, a continuing work relationship, or an opinion given to one of the parties in the past year ([Experts Regulations reg. 16](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_המשפט_(רשימת_מומחים_מטעם_בית_המשפט))).

### Special regimes

- **Road accidents.** Where a court medical expert is appointed under the Road Accident Victims Compensation Law, the parties may summon him for cross-examination and send written clarification questions, but may not bring other expert evidence on the matter without leave for special reasons ([s. 6א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_פיצויים_לנפגעי_תאונות_דרכים#סעיף_6א)).
- **Family court.** Where the court appoints an expert or adviser, a party may not bring its own expert without leave ([Family Court Law s. 8(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_8)).
- **National Insurance.** Disability is decided by NII medical committees, not by party experts in court; appeals go to an appeals committee and then, on law only, to the labour court (lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md)).

### Getting your medical records: Patient's Rights Law s. 18

> (א) מטופל זכאי לקבל מהמטפל או מהמוסד הרפואי מידע רפואי מהרשומה הרפואית, לרבות העתקה, המתייחסת אליו.

*Gist:* A patient is entitled to receive his medical information from the medical record, including a copy, from the caregiver or institution, subject to a narrow exception for information that could cause serious harm, reviewed by an ethics committee. ([s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_החולה#סעיף_18)) Your expert needs these records; ask early.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 4330/07 מוזס נ׳ מדינת ישראל

In a medical-negligence appeal Justice Rubinstein criticised the system in which each side files its own medical opinions, observing that in many cases the content of the opinion seems connected to who ordered it, and pointing to the gap between the parties' opinions.

**Why it matters:** judges know party experts are partisan. A party expert who concedes reasonable points and explains his method is more persuasive than one who never gives ground. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, עד מומחה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/עד_מומחה) (⚠️ secondary source; full text not opened).

### ע״א 10508/08 דור זהב חברה לקבלנות בנין והשקעות בע״מ נ׳ הוועדה המקומית לתכנון ובניה הרצליה (4.2.2010)

Justice Rubinstein returned to the theme and called for wider use of deciding experts whose income comes only from advising the court, independent of the parties.

**Why it matters:** together with reg. 88, this explains why courts often appoint their own expert where party opinions are far apart, and why a court expert's opinion carries great weight. If you cannot afford a party expert, ask the court to appoint one and to decide how the cost is shared (reg. 92). Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, עד מומחה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/עד_מומחה) (⚠️ secondary source).

## How it works in practice

### Choosing and briefing an expert

1. **Field.** The right speciality: an orthopaedist for a fracture, a neurologist for nerve damage, a structural engineer for cracks, a licensed appraiser (שמאי) for valuation.
2. **Independence.** No family or business ties to you; disclose any.
3. **Brief in writing.** Give the facts, the documents, and the questions ("Is the damage consistent with the leak? What does repair cost?"). Do not tell the expert what to conclude.
4. **Check the draft** for: qualifications and experience; documents relied on; examination performed; reasoning; answers to each question; the s. 25 declaration; the list of sources (reg. 87(ז)); signature and date.
5. **Diary** the filing deadline and make sure the expert will attend cross-examination (reg. 87(ח)).

### Cross-examining an expert

| Target | Typical questions |
|---|---|
| Qualifications | "Your speciality is orthopaedics, not neurology?" |
| Factual basis | "You did not see the X-rays from the day of the accident?" "You relied only on what the defendant told you?" |
| Method | "You did not test the moisture of the wall?" "Which guideline did you apply?" |
| Independence | "How many opinions have you written for this insurer in the last year?" |
| Consistency | "In your opinion page 3 you wrote X; today you say Y?" |

Prepare by sending written clarification questions where allowed (court experts, reg. 91; road accidents, s. 6א(ב)(2)) before the hearing.

## Worked example

Hila Azoulay, a kindergarten teacher from Dimona, slipped on a wet floor in a supermarket and broke her wrist. She sues for ₪120,000 in the magistrates' court.

1. **Medical opinion with the claim.** Hila attaches an orthopaedist's opinion (reg. 87(א)) assessing 10% permanent disability. She signs the medical confidentiality waiver (reg. 15(3); lesson [23.2](lesson-02.md)).
2. **Defence examination.** The supermarket's insurer demands she be examined by its expert (reg. 87(ב)); she attends. Its opinion: 2%, "mostly pre-existing arthritis".
3. **Counter-opinion?** Within the reg. 87(ג) period Hila considers a counter-opinion but, given the cost, asks the court to appoint its own expert, sharing the cost.
4. **Court expert.** The court appoints a hand surgeon from the list (reg. 88), checks there is no conflict (Experts Regulations reg. 16), and sets clarification-question deadlines (reg. 91). His opinion: 7%, of which 1% pre-existing.
5. **Clarification questions.** Hila asks the court expert whether her pre-accident X-ray showed arthritis; he answers that it showed none. The insurer cross-examines him; he holds his view.
6. **Result.** The court adopts the court expert's 6% net disability.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A dispute of your own that turns on a professional question (or Hila's case).
- CPR regs 87–93 and Evidence Ordinance ss. 20–26 open.
- About 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Need test (10 min).** List every issue in your case. Mark each "fact" (lay witnesses can prove it) or "expertise" (needs an expert). For each expertise issue, name the right field.
2. **Records (10 min).** List the records your expert will need and draft a one-paragraph request for your medical records (Patient's Rights Law s. 18) or technical documents.
3. **Brief (15 min).** Write a two-page brief for an expert: neutral facts, list of documents, and three to five questions.
4. **Deadline plan (5 min).** Write the reg. 87 deadlines that apply to your case, using [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
5. **Cross-examination (5 min).** For the other side's expected expert, write five questions, one for each target in the table.

### Template

- [Written motion (בקשה בכתב)](../../templates/written-motion.md), for a request to appoint a court expert or for an exemption under reg. 87(ד).

### How you know it worked

- Every expertise issue has an expert field and a plan (party expert, court expert, or a reason none is needed).
- Your brief asks questions and does not dictate answers.
- You know the date by which your opinion must be filed and what happens if you miss it (reg. 93).

### Self-check

- [ ] I know a medical matter cannot be proved without an expert opinion attached to the pleading, unless exempted.
- [ ] I know a party may insist on cross-examining the other side's expert (s. 26; reg. 87(ח)).
- [ ] I know that agreeing to a court expert usually excludes party opinions (reg. 88(ד)).
- [ ] I know the special rule for court medical experts in road-accident cases (s. 6א).

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may hire your own expert, file the opinion, ask for a court expert, send clarification questions and cross-examine experts in your own case (CPR reg. 170). The expert himself must be qualified in his field; you may not write the opinion for him.

### Where and how

- Medical records: from the hospital or clinic under Patient's Rights Law s. 18; fees for copies may apply ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Court experts are appointed from the courts' list; the court decides the fee and who pays (reg. 92).
- Free help: court information centre; Legal Aid in eligible personal-injury cases ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The claim is a medical-negligence claim, or the disability assessment is worth tens of thousands of shekels.
- You missed a reg. 87 deadline and need an exemption.
- The other side's expert raises technical issues you cannot frame questions about.
- The case is a road-accident claim against an insurer and the court medical expert's assessment decides the value.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing for bodily injury with no medical opinion | Cannot prove the medical matter (reg. 93(א)) | Ask for an exemption or postponement with reasons (reg. 87(ד)) | Attach the opinion to the claim |
| Filing a non-medical opinion late | Opinion may be ignored (regs 87(ה), 93(ב)) | Ask leave with reasons | Diary the period before the evidence hearing |
| Expert does not come to cross-examination | Opinion not considered | Ask to set another date | Confirm attendance and pay the fee in advance |
| Agreeing to a court expert without thinking | Your own expert's opinion falls away (reg. 88(ד)) | Ask the court to order otherwise | Decide strategy before agreeing |
| Treating the doctor's sick note as an expert opinion | No disability assessment, no causation | Obtain a proper opinion | Brief an expert with questions |

## Review

- Lay witnesses give facts; professional opinions come from experts, admitted in writing under ss. 20–26, with the expert's perjury declaration and a right to cross-examine.
- In civil cases medical opinions go with the pleadings; non-medical ones by a set period before trial; without them the matter cannot be proved (regs 87, 93).
- Courts may appoint their own experts (reg. 88); agreeing to one usually excludes party opinions; conflicts are barred (Experts Regulations reg. 16).
- Road-accident and family cases restrict party experts once a court expert is appointed (s. 6א; Family Court Law s. 8(ג)).
- Get your records (Patient's Rights Law s. 18), brief the expert neutrally, and cross-examine on qualifications, basis, method, independence and consistency.
