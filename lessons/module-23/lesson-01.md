# 23.1 · The structure of evidence law: admissibility and weight

A court never sees what happened; it sees evidence about what happened, and evidence law decides which of it the judge may hear and how much it may count. This lesson gives you the map for the whole module: the four layers of evidence law (competence, admissibility, weight and sufficiency), where Israeli evidence rules come from, how strictly they apply in each forum, and why the modern trend moves questions from "may the court hear it?" to "how much is it worth?". It builds on lessons 15.7 (the civil trial) and 12.2 (lawful evidence). By the end you can draw an evidence map for your own case and see its weak points before the other side does.

## Why it matters

Most cases are lost on facts, not law. A self-represented party usually knows what happened and what the law says, and still loses because the proof is missing, inadmissible or too weak. Evidence law is also a Bar exam subject, taught as a full course at every law faculty (TAU דיני ראיות 1411-9334, HUJI 62606, Bar-Ilan 99309), because it is where procedure and truth-finding meet.

The rules serve several goals that pull against each other:

- **Finding the truth.** Hear everything relevant and let the judge weigh it.
- **Reliability.** Keep out evidence that is cheap to fabricate and hard to test, such as what an absent person supposedly said (hearsay, lesson [23.3](lesson-03.md)).
- **Fairness and rights.** Exclude evidence obtained by violating rights, even if it is true (lesson [23.5](lesson-05.md)), and protect relationships through privileges (lesson [23.2](lesson-02.md)).
- **Efficiency.** Proof by affidavit, certificates and records instead of calling every clerk and doctor (lessons [23.4](lesson-04.md) and [23.7](lesson-07.md)).

The academic debate is about the balance. One view holds that rigid admissibility rules were designed in England for lay juries, which Israel does not use, so a professional judge can hear almost anything and discount what is weak; this is the "from admissibility to weight" trend described in the literature. The other view answers that some evidence, once heard, cannot be "un-heard", and that clear exclusionary rules protect the accused and discipline the police. Both views are reflected in the current law: civil procedure and the small-claims, family and labour courts lean towards weight; criminal law keeps firmer admissibility rules and adds corroboration requirements (lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md)).

## The law

### The four layers

Evidence questions come in a fixed order. Ask them in this order every time:

1. **Competence (כשרות).** May this person testify at all? Almost everyone may (s. 2), with narrow exceptions for spouses, parents and children in criminal cases (ss. 3–5; lesson [23.2](lesson-02.md)).
2. **Admissibility (קבילות).** May the court receive this item? It must be relevant, and no exclusionary rule (hearsay, privilege, illegality, opinion) may bar it.
3. **Weight (משקל).** How much does the admitted item prove? The judge decides on credibility, circumstances and "signs of truth" (s. 53).
4. **Sufficiency (דיות).** Is the admitted, credited evidence enough for a finding? Usually yes; in some cases the law requires more: corroboration in criminal cases (lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md)) and written reasons when a civil judgment rests on a single uncorroborated witness of a listed kind (s. 54).

A separate question, who must prove and how much, is the burden and standard of proof (lesson [23.6](lesson-06.md)).

### Relevance and the power to summon: s. 1

> (א) מותר להזמין כל אדם ליתן עדות שהיא קבילה ושייכת לעניין; והוא, כשאין הוראה אחרת בפקודה זו.
> (ב) בית המשפט רשאי, לפי שיקול דעתו, לסרב להוציא הזמנה אם אין בה צורך או אם ראה שנתבקשה למטרה שאיננה גילוי האמת.

*Gist:* Any person may be summoned to give admissible and relevant testimony, and the court may refuse a summons that is unnecessary or sought for a purpose other than finding the truth. ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_1))

Relevance has no statutory definition. It is a matter of logic and experience: an item is relevant if it makes a fact in issue more or less likely. A fact "in issue" is one the pleadings or the indictment put in dispute; what both sides admit needs no proof.

### Weight: s. 53 and s. 57

> ערכה של עדות שבעל־פה ומהימנותם של עדים בהם ענין של בית המשפט להחליט בו על פי התנהגותם של העדים, נסיבות הענין ואותות האמת המתגלים במשך המשפט.

*Gist:* The value of oral testimony and the credibility of witnesses are for the court, judged by the witnesses' conduct, the circumstances and the signs of truth that emerge during the trial. ([s. 53](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_53))

> סתירות בעדותם של עדים אין בהן, כשלעצמן, כדי למנוע את בית המשפט מקביעת עובדות שלגביהן חלו הסתירות.

*Gist:* Contradictions between witnesses do not, by themselves, prevent the court from finding the facts they concern. ([s. 57](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_57)) The court may believe part of a witness's testimony and reject the rest.

### Inadmissible evidence heard by mistake in a criminal case: s. 56

> ראיה שאינה קבילה במשפט פלילי ונתקבלה בטעות או בהיסח הדעת, לא תשמש הוכחה לאשמה ואין לבסס עליה שום פסק־דין…

*Gist:* Inadmissible evidence received by mistake in a criminal case may not support a conviction; the judgment stands only if the accused would have been convicted without it on other sufficient evidence. ([s. 56](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_56))

### Judicial notice of the law: s. 57ב

> כל דין הוא מן המפורסמות שאינן צריכות ראיה, אם אין הוראה אחרת משתמעת.

*Gist:* Israeli law need not be proved; the court knows it. ([s. 57ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_57ב)) Foreign law, by contrast, is a fact that must be proved, usually by an expert opinion (module 30). Facts so well known that no reasonable person disputes them (the date of a holiday, that it is dark at midnight) are also taken without proof under case law.

### Where the rules are relaxed

| Forum | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Small claims | The court may admit evidence that would be inadmissible elsewhere, except privileged material, and is not bound by ordinary procedure | [Courts Law s. 62](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_62) |
| Family court | Where the Family Court Law has no specific rule, the court proceeds in the way it thinks best for doing justice, but privileges stay | [Family Court Law s. 8(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_8) |
| Labour court | Not bound by the laws of evidence, except in the listed criminal and appeal proceedings | [Labour Court Law s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_הדין_לעבודה#סעיף_32) |
| Civil courts (magistrates', district) | The Evidence Ordinance and case law apply in full; the 2018 Regulations govern the form (affidavits, experts, digital evidence) | Lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md) |
| Criminal courts | Full rules, plus corroboration requirements and exclusion of illegally obtained evidence | Lessons [23.5](lesson-05.md), [23.8](lesson-08.md) |

> **Family Court Law s. 8(א):** "בכל ענין של דיני ראיות וסדרי דין, שאין עליו הוראה אחרת, לפי חוק זה, ינהג בית המשפט בדרך הנראית לו הטובה ביותר לעשיית משפט צדק; אין בהוראה זו כדי לגרוע מכללי חסיונות עדים או כללי ראיות חסויות."

*Gist:* In the family court, the court may depart from evidence rules to do justice, but never from privileges. ([s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_8))

A relaxed forum changes **admissibility**, not **weight**. A judge in small claims may read a neighbour's unsigned note, but will give it little weight if the neighbour could have come and did not.

### Where the rules come from

- **The Evidence Ordinance [New Version] 1971**: a 1971 consolidation of the Mandate Evidence Ordinance and Oaths Ordinance, amended many times since. Its chapters: oral testimony (competence, statements, testimony abroad), written evidence (affidavits, experts, public documents, institutional records, photocopies, convictions, old documents), privileges, the weight of evidence, and miscellaneous provisions.
- **Other statutes**: the Criminal Procedure Law 1982 (lesson [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md) and module 24), the Eavesdropping Law 1979 and Protection of Privacy Law 1981 (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)), the Evidence Law Amendment (Protection of Children) Law 1955, the Law of Procedure Amendment (Examination of Witnesses) Law 1957, the Electronic Signature Law 2001.
- **Regulations**: the Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 (affidavits, experts, digital evidence).
- **Case law**: the common-law rules Israel inherited (hearsay, the best-evidence rule, the opinion rule) live mostly in Supreme Court rulings, which bind lower courts (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20; lesson [01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md)).

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 4844/09 מסעאד נ׳ מדינת ישראל (31.5.2010)

Justice Vogelman explained that findings of credibility require direct observation of the witness: reactions to questions, body language and the manner of testifying, together with considerations of logic. This is why s. 53 gives the trial court, not the appeal court, the task of deciding whom to believe.

**Why it matters:** in your own case, credibility is built in the courtroom, not on paper. A calm, consistent witness who admits what is true and unhelpful is believed more than one who has an answer for everything. Source: summarised in [Hebrew Wikipedia, פקודת הראיות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות) (⚠️ secondary source; the Supreme Court site could not be opened).

### ע״פ 8703/96 עמר נ׳ מדינת ישראל, פ״ד נא(4) 764

Justice Dorner held that a judge who did not see the witness directly may still make credibility findings on the basis of logic alone, for example by comparing the testimony with documents and with what is inherently likely.

**Why it matters:** "signs of truth" (אותות האמת) in s. 53 are not only demeanour. Documents written at the time, consistency with objective facts, and plausibility often beat a confident witness. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, פקודת הראיות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות) (⚠️ secondary source).

### ע״פ 524/81 ששון נ׳ מדינת ישראל, פ״ד לח(4) 271

The rule that credibility is for the trial court means an appeal court rarely interferes with findings of who told the truth; it does so only in exceptional cases.

**Why it matters:** your evidence has to win at first instance. An appeal is not a second chance to be believed (lesson [15.8](../module-15/lesson-08.md)). Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, פקודת הראיות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות) (⚠️ secondary source).

## How it works in practice

### The evidence map

Lawyers prepare a table before the trial; you should too. One row per element of the cause of action (or of the defence):

| Element to prove | Fact | Evidence | Admissibility risk | Weight risk | Enough? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A contract was made | Quote accepted on 3.3 by WhatsApp | Chat export, exhibit 2 | Authentication of the chat (lesson 23.4) | Low: sent by the defendant | Yes |
| Breach | Work stopped on 15.4 | My testimony; photos | None | Medium: my own interest (s. 54(2)) | Better with the neighbour's testimony |
| Damage | ₪9,400 to finish the work | Second contractor's invoice | None (own document) | Medium: is the price reasonable? | Add a quote or an expert opinion |

### Objections are made at the time

In a civil trial an objection to a question or exhibit is stated at once, with reasons, and decided at once (CPR reg. 68; lesson [15.7](../module-15/lesson-07.md)). A party who keeps silent while inadmissible evidence goes in may find the objection treated as waived. Say "I object, this is hearsay" or "I object, the witness is giving an opinion" when it happens, not in summations.

### Weight beats admissibility in everyday cases

In the magistrates' and small-claims courts most fights are about weight: whether a WhatsApp message really came from the defendant, whether a photo shows the flat on the handover date, whether the plaintiff's own testimony is enough. Plan for weight: documents made at the time, a neutral witness, an objective record (bank, phone company, municipality).

## Worked example

Nadia Khoury, a pharmacist from Haifa, sues a garage for ₪14,000 in small claims after her car's engine failed two weeks after a repair. She lists her evidence and classifies it by layer:

1. **Her own testimony** that the garage replaced the timing belt. Competent (s. 2: a party may testify; s. 7). Admissible. Weight: she has an interest, so a document helps.
2. **The garage invoice** listing "החלפת רצועת תזמון". Admissible; strong weight, because the defendant wrote it.
3. **A second mechanic's message**: "The belt was installed wrong." In small claims the court may admit it (Courts Law s. 62), but its weight is low: he is not a witness, the message is short and the garage cannot question him. She asks him to write a signed, reasoned report and to attend.
4. **A recording of her call with the garage owner** in which he says "we may have made a mistake". She was a party to the call: lawful (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)). Admissible; she prepares a transcript.
5. **A post in a Facebook group** by an anonymous person: "this garage ruined my car too". Little or no relevance to her car and no weight; she drops it.

Her map shows one weak element (causation), so she invests in the mechanic's report rather than in more photos.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A dispute of your own (or Nadia's case).
- Your facts file from lesson [12.1](../module-12/lesson-01.md), if you made one.
- The Evidence Ordinance open on Wikisource.
- About 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Elements (10 min).** Write the cause of action (contract, negligence, a statutory right) and list its elements, one per row.
2. **Facts and evidence (15 min).** For each element, write the fact you need and every piece of evidence you have or could get.
3. **Classify (10 min).** For each piece, mark the four layers: competent? admissible (and under which rule)? likely weight? enough alone?
4. **Forum check (5 min).** Write which forum will hear the case and whether its rules are relaxed (Courts Law s. 62, Family Court Law s. 8, Labour Court Law s. 32).
5. **Gaps (5 min).** Mark every element where the answer to "enough?" is no. Write one action that would fix each gap.

### Template

- [Clinic case file](../../templates/clinic-case-file.md), part ד (evidence index); add the four-layer columns from the table above.

### How you know it worked

- Every element of your claim has at least one admissible piece of evidence.
- You can name, for each risky item, the rule that might exclude it.
- You know whether your forum relaxes the rules and which rules it never relaxes (privileges).

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain the difference between admissibility and weight with an example from my own case.
- [ ] I know that Israeli law needs no proof but foreign law does.
- [ ] I know that small claims relaxes admissibility but not privilege.
- [ ] I know that objections are made when the evidence is offered.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. A party may present evidence, testify, call and question witnesses and object to the other side's evidence in person in the civil courts (CPR reg. 170) and as a matter of course in small claims. Planning your evidence needs no lawyer. What you may not do is help another person prepare their evidence as a service (Bar Association Law s. 20; lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)).

### Where and how

- Statute texts: Wikisource and the Knesset legislation database (lesson [02.1](../module-02/lesson-01.md)).
- Court information centres and volunteer desks explain procedure, not your evidence strategy ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).
- Forum-by-forum rules on acting alone: [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The case turns on a disputed admissibility question (privilege, an illegally obtained recording, a confession).
- You are a suspect or defendant in a criminal case (module 24).
- The other side relies on expert evidence you cannot answer (lesson [23.7](lesson-07.md)).
- Your evidence map shows a gap you cannot fill and the stakes are high.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bringing "everything" without a map | Key element left unproved among irrelevant exhibits | Ask leave to add the missing evidence, with reasons | Build the evidence map before the pleadings close |
| Relying on small-claims flexibility for a hearsay note | Admitted but given little weight | Bring the author as a witness | Prefer first-hand witnesses and documents |
| Objecting only in summations | Objection treated as waived | Raise it as soon as you notice; ask the court to note it | Object when the evidence is offered (reg. 68) |
| Trying to "prove" Israeli law with printouts | Wasted pages | Cite the section in argument | Law is judicially noticed (s. 57ב) |
| Hoping the appeal court will believe you | Appeal dismissed on credibility | Appeal only on law or clear error | Win credibility at trial |

## Review

- Ask the four questions in order: competence, admissibility, weight, sufficiency; burden and standard come separately.
- Evidence rules come from the Evidence Ordinance, other statutes, the 2018 Regulations and case law that inherited common-law rules.
- Small claims, family and labour courts relax admissibility (Courts Law s. 62; Family Court Law s. 8; Labour Court Law s. 32), never privilege, and weight still decides.
- Credibility is for the trial court (s. 53), which judges demeanour, circumstances and signs of truth; appeals rarely change it.
- An evidence map links every element to admissible proof and shows the gaps early.
