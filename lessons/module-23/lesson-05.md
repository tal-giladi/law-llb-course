# 23.5 · Illegally obtained evidence

Should a court use true evidence that was obtained by breaking the law? For decades Israeli law mostly said yes: relevance and reliability mattered, not how the evidence was found, except where a specific statute said otherwise. In 2006 the Supreme Court, in Yissacharov, adopted a relative exclusionary doctrine, and in 2022 the Knesset wrote it into the Evidence Ordinance as s. 56א. This lesson covers that doctrine, the older statutory exclusions for eavesdropping, privacy infringement and involuntary confessions, and the debate behind them. Lesson 12.2 taught you how to collect evidence lawfully; here you learn what happens to evidence that was not collected lawfully, in criminal and civil cases.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson discusses police investigations and criminal trials. If you are a suspect or have been questioned, do not rely on this lesson to decide what to say or do: ask for a lawyer (the Public Defender if you are eligible) and see module 24.

The exclusion question arises in two very different settings:

- **Criminal cases.** The police search without a warrant, fail to warn a suspect of his right to a lawyer, or pressure him to confess. Excluding the result may free a guilty person; admitting it may reward illegality and taint the trial.
- **Civil and family cases.** One spouse copies the other's emails, a neighbour films into a flat, an employer reads a worker's private mailbox. Excluding the result may hide the truth; admitting it may encourage people to spy on each other.

The debate has strong arguments on both sides, and the current law reflects a compromise:

- **For a broad exclusionary rule:** constitutional rights (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty; lesson [06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)) are meaningless without a remedy; a court that uses illegal evidence becomes a partner to the illegality; only exclusion deters investigators.
- **Against it:** the trial's purpose is to find the truth; victims and the public pay when reliable evidence is lost; investigators can be disciplined or sued instead; and a far-reaching rule should be made by the Knesset rather than by courts (the view of the dissent in Yissacharov).

Israel chose a **relative** doctrine: no automatic exclusion, but judicial discretion guided by stated factors, first by case law and since 2022 by statute.

## The law

### The general rule since 2022: Evidence Ordinance s. 56א

> (א) בית המשפט הדן במשפט פלילי רשאי שלא לקבל ראיה שהושגה שלא כדין, ובכלל זה הודעת נאשם או עד, חפץ או כל ראיה אחרת, אם שוכנע שקבלתה במשפט תפגע באופן מהותי בזכות להליך הוגן, בשים לב לאופייה ולחומרתה של ההפרה, למידת ההשפעה של ההפרה על הראיה שהושגה ולעניין הציבורי שבקבלת הראיה או באי־קבלתה; בסעיף קטן זה, ”הפרה“ – השגת ראיה שלא כדין.
> (ב) אין באמור בסעיף קטן (א) כדי לפגוע בכללים לפסילת ראיות הקבועים בהוראות חיקוק אחרות.

*Gist:* A criminal court may refuse illegally obtained evidence, including a statement of the accused or a witness, an object or any other evidence, if admitting it would substantially harm the right to a fair trial, considering the nature and seriousness of the breach, how much the breach affected the evidence, and the public interest in admitting or excluding it; other statutory exclusions remain. ([s. 56א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות#סעיף_56א))

**Status.** Section 56א was added by Amendment No. 19 (ס״ח תשפ״ב, 984), in 2022, according to the Wikisource consolidated text. No later amendment to it appears in that text as last revised on 17 September 2025. (⚠️ The Reshumot PDF was downloaded but could not be machine-read; confirm the wording, commencement and any newer bills on the Knesset legislation database before relying on this.) The section is limited by its words to **criminal** trials.

### Eavesdropping: Eavesdropping Law s. 13

> (א) דברים שנקלטו בדרך של האזנת סתר בניגוד להוראות חוק זה… לא יהיו קבילים כראיה בבית משפט, אלא באחד משני אלה:
> (1) בהליך פלילי בשל עבירה לפי חוק זה;
> (2) בהליך פלילי בשל פשע חמור, אם בית משפט הורה על קבילותה לאחר ששוכנע, מטעמים מיוחדים שיפרט, כי בנסיבות הענין הצורך להגיע לחקר האמת עדיף על הצורך להגן על הפרטיות…

*Gist:* What was captured by unlawful secret eavesdropping is inadmissible in any court, except in a prosecution for the eavesdropping offence itself, or in a prosecution for a serious felony where the court, for special reasons, finds the need for truth outweighs privacy; an unlawful tap by an authority that could have obtained a warrant is admissible under the second exception only if made by mistake in good faith. ([s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_האזנת_סתר#סעיף_13)) Lawful wiretaps are admissible only in criminal proceedings that are not private complaints (s. 13(ג)).

Remember the definition (lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md)): recording a conversation **you take part in** is not secret eavesdropping.

### Privacy: Protection of Privacy Law s. 32

> חומר שהושג תוך פגיעה בפרטיות יהיה פסול לשמש ראיה בבית משפט, ללא הסכמת הנפגע, זולת אם בית המשפט התיר מטעמים שיירשמו להשתמש בחומר, או אם היו לפוגע, שהיה צד להליך, הגנה או פטור לפי חוק זה.

*Gist:* Material obtained by infringing privacy is inadmissible in any court without the victim's consent, unless the court allows it for recorded reasons or the infringer, being a party, has a defence or exemption under the Law. ([s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_32)) This applies in civil, family and religious courts, and in criminal cases.

### Confessions: Evidence Ordinance s. 12

A confession is admissible only if the prosecution proves it was "free and voluntary" (s. 12(א)); the full rules are in lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md). Yissacharov held that its general doctrine applies to confessions too, alongside s. 12.

### Mistakes: s. 56

Inadmissible evidence received by mistake cannot support a conviction unless other evidence is sufficient (s. 56; lesson [23.1](lesson-01.md)).

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 5121/98 יששכרוב נ׳ התובע הצבאי הראשי, פ״ד סא(1) 461 (4.5.2006)

A soldier found with drugs was interrogated without being told of his right to consult a lawyer, and confessed to using drugs on three occasions; the trial court found the omission illegal and intentional. An expanded panel of nine (Justice Beinisch for the majority) held that, after the Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty and in the absence of legislation, Israel adopts a **relative** case-law exclusionary doctrine: evidence is excluded if admitting it would substantially violate the accused's right to a fair trial. The doctrine is **preventive**, protecting the fairness of the trial, not mainly deterrent. The factors: the character and seriousness of the illegality (including whether it was intentional), the degree to which it affected the evidence, and the social cost and benefit of exclusion (including the seriousness of the offence). It applies to all types of evidence, confessions included. The confession was excluded. Justice Grunis dissented: a broad doctrine should come from the legislature, and on the facts, since the soldier had been told of his right to silence, exclusion was not justified.

**Why it matters:** it is the foundation of s. 56א, whose factors track the majority's test almost word for word, and the main Bar-exam case on exclusion. English translation: [Versa / LARC](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/411/).

### Later case law on the doctrine

Summaries in [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פסק_דין_יששכרוב_נגד_התובע_הצבאי_הראשי) (⚠️ secondary source; full texts not opened) describe three developments:

- **Derivative evidence.** Yissacharov did not adopt the American "fruit of the poisonous tree" rule; evidence found through illegal evidence is assessed case by case. In ע״פ 4988/08 פרחי נ׳ מדינת ישראל, a DNA sample taken illegally was excluded, but other evidence found later was not, and the conviction stood.
- **Timing.** In דנ״פ 5852/10 מדינת ישראל נ׳ שמש, פ״ד סה(2) 377, a further hearing held that exclusion is decided by the trial court once an indictment is filed, not by a court at the investigation stage.
- **Practical effect.** Confessions obtained after investigators deterred a suspect from meeting his lawyer have been excluded in serious cases.

### בג״ץ 6650/04 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי בנתניה (14.5.2006)

A rabbinical court relied on photographs a husband took while infringing his wife's privacy. The High Court of Justice held that the Privacy Law, including its exclusion of privacy-infringing material, applies in all branches of law and in all courts, rabbinical courts included, and that marriage does not waive one spouse's privacy against the other.

**Why it matters:** in family disputes, evidence gathered by spying on a spouse (photos inside the home, copied emails, a tracking app) is presumptively inadmissible under s. 32 and may expose the gatherer to a privacy suit. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, חוק הגנת הפרטיות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות) (⚠️ secondary source).

## How it works in practice

### In a criminal case

The defence raises admissibility when the evidence is offered; for a confession, a "trial within a trial" (משפט זוטא) is held on whether it was free and voluntary and whether s. 56א applies (lesson [23.8](lesson-08.md)). The court decides on the evidence about how the item was obtained: interrogation recordings, officers' testimony, warrants.

### In a civil or family case

- **If the other side offers material obtained by infringing your privacy:** object under s. 32 at once, say why it infringes privacy (s. 2 of the Privacy Law lists the forms), and that you do not consent. The other side must then show a defence or ask the court to allow it for recorded reasons.
- **If you are tempted to obtain such material:** don't. Use the green-light routes from lesson [12.2](../module-12/lesson-02.md), and ask the court for disclosure orders instead (lesson [15.4](../module-15/lesson-04.md)).
- **Courts weigh** the importance of the evidence to finding the truth, the seriousness of the infringement, whether it was a crime, and whether the fact could be proved another way.

### A four-step analysis for any doubtful item

1. Was it obtained unlawfully? Which law was broken (Eavesdropping, Privacy, Computers Law, the Criminal Procedure Law on searches, the right to a lawyer)?
2. Is there a specific statutory exclusion (s. 13, s. 32, s. 12)? Apply its own test.
3. In a criminal case, apply s. 56א: seriousness of the breach, effect on the evidence, public interest.
4. If admitted, what weight? Illegality can also affect credibility.

## Worked example

Avraham and Michal Cohen-Levi, from Petah Tikva, are divorcing. In the family court Michal seeks a larger share of a business account. Avraham's lawyer files printouts of Michal's private Gmail messages to her sister about "hiding" money abroad. Avraham obtained them by logging into Michal's laptop with a password he guessed.

1. **Unlawful?** Copying writings not meant for him (Privacy Law s. 2(5)) and possibly unauthorised access to computer material (Computers Law). The privacy infringement is clear.
2. **Statutory exclusion.** Privacy Law s. 32: inadmissible without Michal's consent unless the court allows it for recorded reasons or Avraham has a defence. Marriage does not waive Michal's privacy (בג״ץ 6650/04).
3. **Avraham's argument.** The emails reveal hidden assets; truth requires them. Michal answers that the facts can be proved lawfully: the family court can order disclosure of her bank records and appoint an accountant (lesson [18.4](../module-18/lesson-04.md)).
4. **Result.** The court excludes the printouts because the fact can be proved by lawful means, and orders Michal to disclose her foreign accounts on affidavit. Michal considers a privacy claim; the court warns both sides about costs.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your evidence map from lesson [23.1](lesson-01.md), or Avraham and Michal's case.
- Evidence Ordinance s. 56א, Eavesdropping Law s. 13 and Privacy Law s. 32 open.
- About 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Screen (10 min).** For each item in your map, note how it was obtained and by whom. Flag any item obtained from someone else's device, account, home or private conversation.
2. **Classify (10 min).** For each flagged item, apply the four-step analysis: which law, which exclusion, which test, which weight.
3. **Replace (10 min).** For each item likely to be excluded, write a lawful substitute: a disclosure request, a witness, a records request, a court order.
4. **Argue both sides (10 min).** Write three sentences for admitting one flagged item and three for excluding it, using the statutory factors. This is the Bar-exam skill.

### Template

- [Written motion (בקשה בכתב)](../../templates/written-motion.md), for a disclosure request that replaces unlawfully obtained material.

### How you know it worked

- No item in your plan depends on material obtained by infringing someone's privacy or by unlawful eavesdropping.
- For every excluded item you have a lawful substitute.
- You can state the s. 56א factors and the s. 32 exception from memory.

## Without a lawyer

> [!WARNING]
> Secret eavesdropping and computer intrusion are crimes. If you already hold material obtained that way, do not use or share it before taking legal advice.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. In a civil or family case you may object to the other side's privacy-infringing evidence and argue s. 32 yourself (CPR reg. 170). In a criminal case where you are the accused, exclusion arguments are technical and often decisive: ask for a lawyer, and the court may appoint a public defender where the law allows (module 24).

### Where and how

- Objections: when the evidence is offered, or in a written motion before the hearing if it was attached to a pleading (lesson [15.11](../module-15/lesson-11.md)).
- Privacy guidance: the Privacy Protection Authority at the Ministry of Justice (gov.il, ⚠️ manual check).
- Free help: Legal Aid for eligible civil matters; the Public Defender for eligible criminal defendants ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are a suspect or accused and evidence was taken from you without a warning, a warrant or access to a lawyer.
- Your key evidence came from someone else's device, account or home.
- The other side threatens a privacy or computer-crime complaint over evidence you gathered.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copying a spouse's emails to prove hidden assets | Excluded (s. 32); privacy claim; possible crime | Ask the court for disclosure orders instead | Use only messages sent to you |
| Leaving a phone recording after leaving the room | Crime; inadmissible in civil cases (s. 13) | Do not use or share; take advice | Record only conversations you take part in |
| Assuming illegal evidence is always excluded | Surprise when it is admitted under s. 56א's balance | Argue the factors, not a slogan | Learn the relative test |
| Not objecting to privacy-infringing material | Court may rely on it | Object at once, stating the infringement | Read the other side's exhibits for their source |
| Arguing exclusion in a civil case under s. 56א | Wrong section: s. 56א covers criminal trials | Rely on s. 32 or s. 13 | Match the rule to the forum |

## Review

- Since Yissacharov (2006), and by statute since 2022 (s. 56א), a criminal court may exclude illegally obtained evidence if admitting it would substantially harm the right to a fair trial, weighing the breach, its effect on the evidence and the public interest.
- The doctrine is relative and preventive; derivative evidence is assessed case by case.
- Unlawful eavesdropping is inadmissible except in narrow criminal exceptions (Eavesdropping Law s. 13); privacy-infringing material is inadmissible unless the court allows it or a defence applies (Privacy Law s. 32), in every court.
- Confessions must also be free and voluntary (s. 12; lesson 23.8).
- In civil and family cases, prevent the problem: collect lawfully and ask the court for disclosure.
