# 06.7 · Practice lab: a proportionality analysis of a real law

In this lab you analyse a real Israeli statutory provision the way the High Court of Justice would, from the right it affects to the remedy, and then compare your answer with what the Court actually decided. The supplied provision is mandatory retirement at 67; you may instead choose a provision that affects you. The result is a two-page memo you can reuse in a letter, an objection or a petition.

## Why it matters

Lessons 06.3 and 06.4 gave you the structure. Using it on a real law teaches what the structure hides: finding the purpose, finding evidence, deciding how much room the legislature deserves, and choosing a remedy. Mandatory retirement is a good first case: it affects almost every employee, it touches a derived right (equality on grounds of age), and it involves no security or religious controversy, so you can concentrate on the method.

## The law

> הגיל שבהגיעו אליו ניתן לחייב עובד לפרוש מעבודתו בשל גילו, הוא גיל 67 לגבר ולאישה (בחוק זה – גיל פרישת חובה).

*Gist:* An employee may be required to retire because of age at 67, for men and women alike. [Retirement Age Law, s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_גיל_פרישה#סעיף_4)

Distinguish s. 3 (the age of entitlement to a pension: 67 for men; for women, a different age set in the law and raised in 2022) from s. 4 (the age at which you can be made to retire). The lab concerns s. 4 only.

The limitation clause, Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8, is in lesson 06.3. Equality on grounds of age is a right derived from human dignity (lesson 06.4).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 9134/12 גביש נ׳ הכנסת (2016)

Employees challenged s. 4 as age discrimination. A seven-justice panel dismissed the petition. It held that compulsory retirement because of age does infringe equality to a degree that amounts to an infringement of human dignity; but the infringement meets the limitation clause. The purposes (uniform rules, protecting employees' interests and social security, opening jobs to younger workers, workforce planning) are proper; the means are rationally connected; and in a complex, polycentric area of social and economic policy the Court reviews "with extreme care", leaving the Knesset a wide margin. Read the opinion for how it handled the least-harmful-means and narrow-sense stages.

Why it matters: it is your answer key, and the clearest example of restraint in social and economic legislation. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/165)

### בג״ץ 5239/11 אבנרי נ׳ הכנסת (2015)

The Boycott Law 2011 made a public call to boycott Israel (including areas under its control) a civil wrong and allowed administrative sanctions. The Court upheld most of it but struck the provision allowing compensation without proof of damage, holding it disproportionate.

Why it matters: an example of a mixed result, upholding a law but removing its most far-reaching provision. Use it as a model if you choose a provision with several parts. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/71)

## How it works in practice

A real analysis takes four kinds of material:

1. **The text** of the current provision and its history (Wikisource "תיקון" notes; Knesset legislation database).
2. **The purpose**: the bill's explanatory notes and the committee protocols (Knesset website), and any government reports.
3. **Facts**: who is affected and how much (for example, labour-force data from the Central Bureau of Statistics, pension rules, exceptions in collective agreements).
4. **The case law**: the ruling on this provision if there is one, and the leading cases on the right.

Write the memo with the [research memo template](../../templates/research-memo.md) and the analysis with the [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md).

## Worked example

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The Retirement Age Law on Wikisource, or a provision of your choice enacted or amended after March 1992 that limits a right of yours.
- The [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md) and the [research memo template](../../templates/research-memo.md).
- Your rights analysis from lesson 06.4, if you used your own matter.
- 2.5 hours: 30 minutes to collect material, 60 to analyse, 40 to write, 20 to compare.

### Steps

1. **Fix the provision.** Copy s. 4 (or your provision) and note its date. Check that it is reviewable (lesson 06.2: date, override).
2. **Purpose.** Find the explanatory notes of the Retirement Age Bill (2003–2004) on the Knesset website, or rely on the purposes stated in Gavish. Write the purpose as its strongest supporter would.
3. **Stage 1.** Which right? Equality on grounds of age (derived from dignity); possibly freedom of occupation (Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation s. 3). Explain how strong the infringement is.
4. **Stage 2.** Fill every row of the worksheet. For the least-harmful-means test, consider individual assessment of capacity, a higher age, or a right to continue with the employer's consent. Ask: does each alternative achieve the purposes equally?
5. **Narrow sense.** List the benefits (planning, jobs for younger workers, dignified exit without individual "unfitness" tests) and the harms (loss of income and status, especially for those with small pensions). Decide how much margin the Knesset deserves in this field.
6. **Stage 3.** If the provision fails, propose the narrowest remedy (for example an exception for employees without sufficient pension).
7. **Memo.** Two pages: question, short answer, analysis by stages, the strongest counter-argument, conclusion.
8. **Compare.** Read the Gavish abstract and the majority's proportionality analysis. Note where you agree, where you differ, and why.

### Template

[Proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md); [research memo (תזכיר משפטי)](../../templates/research-memo.md).

### How you know it worked

- Every stage has a conclusion and a reason; the memo says where the provision stands or falls.
- Your least-harmful-means alternatives are concrete and tested against the purposes.
- You can explain any difference between your result and Gavish by pointing to a specific stage.

<details><summary>Model answer for s. 4 of the Retirement Age Law</summary>

**Question.** Does s. 4 of the Retirement Age Law, allowing employers to require retirement at 67, unconstitutionally infringe equality?

**Short answer.** It infringes equality as part of human dignity, but on the current case law it meets the limitation clause (Gavish, 2016), mainly because of the wide margin the Knesset has in social and economic policy.

**Reviewability.** Enacted 2004; no override. Reviewable.

**Stage 1.** Treating people differently because of age alone, and ending their employment on that basis, infringes equality; where it affects livelihood and self-worth it reaches the core of dignity. Freedom of occupation is also affected.

**Legality and values.** In a statute; befits Israel's values (no contrary argument raised).

**Purpose.** Uniform and predictable retirement rules; protecting employees as a group (a dignified exit without individual tests of decline); coordinating with pension and social security; workforce planning; opportunities for younger workers. All proper.

**Rational connection.** A fixed age advances uniformity and planning. Met.

**Least harmful means.** Individual assessment would be less harmful to those able to continue, but would not achieve uniformity or a dignified exit to the same degree, and would require employers to prove decline. A higher age achieves less of the planning purpose. So no alternative achieves the purposes equally. Met (the strongest counter-argument: other countries manage without a mandatory age).

**Narrow sense.** Benefits: system-wide certainty and pension coordination. Harm: serious for those who want and need to work, especially with small pensions. Given the polycentric nature of the issue and the legislature's margin, the balance is within the range. Met, with the caveat that harm to low-pension employees is the weakest point.

**Remedy.** None needed. If it had failed, the narrowest remedy would be an exception for employees without a sufficient pension, not voiding s. 4.

**Comparison with Gavish.** Same result. The Court stressed restraint and caution in social and economic legislation; a memo reaching the opposite result would need strong evidence of harm and a working alternative.

</details>

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, as analysis and argument in your own matter. A challenge to a statute itself is a High Court of Justice petition against the Knesset; you may file it in your own name (lesson 06.8), but such petitions rarely succeed without expert help. Preparing such analyses for others as an occupation or for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Use the memo in a letter to the body applying the law to you (for example, an employer that is a public body, or a ministry), asking it to use any discretion the law leaves.
- Labour matters, including disputes over retirement, go to the labour courts; ask whether the dispute is really about the law or about how your employer applied it.
- Fees and deadlines via [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are about to be retired and have a short window to act.
- Your employer is a private company and your claim depends on a collective agreement or your contract.
- You want to challenge the statute itself rather than its application.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysing the pension age (s. 3) instead of mandatory retirement (s. 4) | Wrong provision | Re-read the sections | Copy the exact provision in step 1 |
| Using an alternative that achieves less at the least-harmful-means stage | Wrong stage | Move it to the narrow-sense balance | Ask "equally effective?" |
| Ignoring the margin of appreciation in economic policy | Overconfident conclusion | Explain why the Court would or would not defer | Read the Gavish holding on restraint |
| Memo with no counter-argument | One-sided, unpersuasive | Add the strongest opposing view | Use the worksheet columns |
| No source for the purpose | Analysis rests on guesswork | Use the explanatory notes or the ruling | Collect material first |

## Review

- A full analysis moves through reviewability, infringement, the four limitation-clause conditions, and remedy.
- The purpose comes from primary sources (explanatory notes, protocols, rulings), stated at its strongest.
- In social and economic policy the Court leaves the Knesset a wide margin (Gavish).
- Mixed outcomes are common: the Court may uphold a law but remove one provision (Avneri).
- Compare your analysis with the Court's by stage, not only by result.
