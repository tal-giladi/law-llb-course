# 06.8 · Practice lab: a self-represented HCJ petition (dry run)

In this lab you take a decision of a public body that harms you, or the supplied case, and turn it into a complete petition to the High Court of Justice, as a dry run: the forum check, the threshold check, the prior demand, the petition with its requested orders, the affidavit and the list of attachments, and a decision on whether to ask for an interim order. Nothing is filed. At the end you will know whether your matter belongs in the HCJ at all, and if it does, what a petition the registry would accept looks like.

## Why it matters

Anyone may petition the High Court of Justice in their own name, and every year many people do. Most self-represented petitions fail before the merits: the matter belonged in another court, the petitioner never wrote to the authority first, waited too long, or asked for an order the Court cannot give. Each of those is a threshold doctrine you can check yourself in an hour. The constitutional tools of this module (proportionality in 06.3, rights in 06.4, the grounds of review in 06.6) only matter once the petition passes the threshold.

## The law

### What the HCJ can order

> לתת צווים לרשויות המדינה, לרשויות מקומיות, לפקידיהן ולגופים ולאנשים אחרים הממלאים תפקידים ציבוריים על פי דין, לעשות מעשה או להימנע מעשות מעשה במילוי תפקידיהם כדין [...]

*Gist:* The HCJ may order State and local authorities, their officials and others performing public functions under law to do, or refrain from doing, an act in the lawful performance of their duties. [Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 15(ד)(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה#סעיף_15). Section 15(ג), quoted in lesson 06.6, makes the HCJ a residual court: it hears matters that are not within another court's jurisdiction.

### When the Administrative Affairs Court comes first

> עתירה נגד החלטה של רשות או של גוף המנוי [...]

*Gist:* The Administrative Affairs Court (the district court sitting in administrative matters) hears petitions against decisions of authorities on the matters listed in the First Schedule. [Administrative Affairs Courts Law 2000, s. 5(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מנהליים). The schedule is long and keeps growing: arnona, freedom of information, education, tenders, business licensing, local authorities, planning and building, population administration and entry into Israel, public housing, transport, health, the Israel Land Authority and dozens more. Because s. 15(ג) makes the HCJ residual, a petition on a scheduled matter filed in the HCJ will be sent away.

### Form and content of the petition

> העתירה תפרט את פרטיו האישיים של העותר ואת הנימוקים [...]

> [...] וכתובה על צד אחד של הגליון עם שוליים של חמישה סנטימטרים לפחות.

*Gist:* The petition sets out the petitioner's personal details and the factual and legal grounds, follows Form 1 of the schedule, and is written on one side of the page with margins of at least 5 cm. [HCJ Procedure Regulations 1984, reg. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק). Reg. 3 requires an address for service in Israel.

> כל עתירה תהא נתמכת בתצהיר לפי טופס 2 שבתוספת

*Gist:* Every petition is supported by the petitioner's affidavit in Form 2. [reg. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק). The Supreme Court President's registry guidelines require the original affidavit for an HCJ petition. An affidavit is valid only if the person before whom it is made warned you to tell the truth and confirmed that on the document; that person may be a lawyer, a judge or another person listed in [Evidence Ordinance s. 15(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות). A false affidavit is treated as false swearing (s. 16).

### What happens after filing

The petition goes before a single justice, who may issue an order nisi, call the petitioner, or refer it to a panel of three (reg. 5). If the Court dismisses the petition without calling the respondent, "רשאי הוא להטיל על העותר הוצאות לטובת המדינה": it may order the petitioner to pay costs to the State (reg. 6). Once an order nisi or interim order is given, the petitioner must promptly supply copies of the petition and affidavits for service (reg. 8). The Court may grant, cancel or vary interim orders as it sees fit (reg. 19).

### Fee

A petition carries a court fee (Courts (Fees) Regulations, Supreme Court schedule), updated every January by the consumer price index (reg. 11). Regulation 14 allows a full or partial exemption for a person who cannot pay, on an affidavit about their and their spouse's property and income in the six months before the request, filed with the petition; the Court also asks whether the proceeding shows a cause of action. See [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) for the amount.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 9029/16 אבירם נ׳ שרת המשפטים (1.2.2017)

Two petitioners, appearing for themselves, argued that a Knesset member elected to the Judicial Selection Committee as an opposition member had to leave it once his party joined the coalition. The Court dismissed the petition for delay, without deciding the constitutional question. It applied the three-part test: subjective delay (the petitioners waited about six months without a good explanation), objective delay (in the meantime the committee had worked intensively, including on Supreme Court candidates, so a ruling would affect others' reliance), and whether dismissing the petition would seriously harm the rule of law (it would not). The Court stressed that six months alone is not a bar; delay is weighed against its consequences.

Why it matters: it shows the delay doctrine applied to self-represented petitioners on an important constitutional question. A good argument does not survive a late petition. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/aviram-v-minister-justice)

### בג״ץ 910/86 רסלר נ׳ שר הביטחון (1988)

Covered in lesson 06.6. For your petition, the point is the distinction it draws: a person whose own interest is harmed has standing as of course; a public petitioner, with no personal interest, is heard only where the issue is of public importance and concerns the rule of law. If you petition about your own matter, say so in the first paragraph. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/312)

### Key text: the prior-demand requirement

The rule that you first write to the authority and only then go to court is judge-made and flexible. A 2024 article by Gal Rosilio sets out the four reasons the Court gives (saving resources, building a factual record, narrowing the dispute, letting the authority form a position) and the exceptions for urgent cases, using petitions filed after October 2023. [Rosilio, "Exhaustion of remedies in 'urgent and special' cases" (Hebrew, free)](https://journal.lawforum.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/ILLF-Rosilio-Threshold.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### The order of checks

1. **Is it a public body exercising a public power?** Ministries, the IDF, the police, statutory corporations, local authorities, and private bodies performing public functions under law. A private company acting commercially is not.
2. **Which court?** If the matter is in the First Schedule of the Administrative Affairs Courts Law, go to the district court sitting in administrative matters. If a statute gives a specific appeal (to a tribunal, an appeals committee, the labour court), use it. Only what is left goes to the HCJ.
3. **Threshold.** Standing (your own interest), a prior written demand and a final answer or a reasonable time without one, no alternative remedy, no unexplained delay, full and honest disclosure of all relevant facts including those against you.
4. **Grounds.** Authority, procedure (hearing, reasons), considerations, reasonableness, proportionality where a right is limited.
5. **Relief.** A narrow, specific order: cancel and reconsider after a hearing, decide within a fixed period, give reasons.

### Filing and timetable

- Filing is at the Supreme Court registry in Jerusalem. Under the President's registry guidelines (consolidated version from 1.1.2021), the first pleading that opens a proceeding is filed by hand at the registry or by post, not by fax or Net HaMishpat. Check the Judiciary's site or call the information centre (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) for any newer online channel before filing.
- In practice the Court usually asks the respondents for a preliminary response within a set period; State respondents answer through the State Attorney's HCJ department. Many petitions end at this stage: the authority reconsiders, the petition is withdrawn, or it is dismissed.
- Costs are a real risk if the petition is plainly unfounded (reg. 6) and, at the end, if it fails after the respondents were put to work.

### What the 2023–2026 dispute changes for a petitioner

| Issue | Current law (17 October 2026) | Strongest argument for limiting | Strongest argument for keeping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasonableness as a ground against ministers | Available: the 2023 amendment was voided (HCJ 5658/23) | Judges should not substitute their balance for an elected minister's | It is often the only ground that reaches an arbitrary decision |
| Public-petitioner standing | Ressler: allowed in rule-of-law cases | It draws the Court into political controversies | Without it, some unlawful acts harm no one specifically and escape review |
| Who sits on your panel | Judges appointed under the present s. 4; the 2025 amendment applies from the 26th Knesset and is under challenge | Elected representatives should have a decisive say | Independence requires that no political side control appointments |

None of this changes the threshold rules above. A petition about your own matter that is well founded in procedure and reasons rarely turns on the constitutional dispute.

## Worked example

The supplied case (invented facts):

Fadi Halabi, 38, a reservist from Daliyat al-Karmel and the sole carer of his eight-year-old son after his wife's death in July 2026, received a call-up for 40 days of reserve duty starting 15 November 2026. On 1 October he applied in writing to his unit's deferral committee, attaching the death certificate and a letter from his son's school. On 8 October he received a two-line answer: "הבקשה נדחתה". No hearing, no reasons. On 9 October he emailed the committee asking for reasons and a hearing; no answer by 17 October.

1. **Forum.** A decision of the IDF about reserve service. Not in the First Schedule (item 36 covers career soldiers only), and no statutory appeal is named in the decision. HCJ.
2. **Threshold.** Standing: his own call-up. Prior demand: the 9 October email asks for reasons and a hearing; he should give the committee a short, stated time to answer (say seven days, given the start date) and then file. Delay: none. Clean hands: he must attach the refusal and mention that he served all previous call-ups, and any facts against him.
3. **Grounds.** Procedure: no hearing and no reasons (the duty to give reasons, Decisions and Reasons Law, lesson 02.4). Considerations: the refusal ignores a central relevant fact. Reasonableness and proportionality: the harm to a child left without a carer against the unit's need, with less harmful alternatives (deferral, a shorter or split service).
4. **Relief.** An order nisi asking why the decision should not be cancelled and the request reconsidered after a hearing within 14 days; not "exempt me from service", which the Court would not give.
5. **Interim order.** Yes, because the call-up date would make the petition pointless: a short paragraph on irreparable harm, the balance of convenience (one reservist deferred for weeks against a child alone), and the petition's prospects.
6. **Likely outcome.** In a case like this the State often agrees to reconsider after the petition is filed, and the petition is withdrawn or dismissed as moot. That is a success.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This lab is a dry run: nothing is filed. If you decide to file a real petition about your own matter, re-check every step against the current regulations and the registry's instructions first.

### You need

- A decision of a public body that harms you (refusal, failure to answer, a condition, a cancellation), with the documents; or the supplied Halabi case.
- The [petition to the High Court of Justice](../../templates/hcj-petition.md) template, the [letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md) template and your [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md) from 06.3 or 06.7.
- The First Schedule of the [Administrative Affairs Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מנהליים) and the [HCJ Procedure Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_בבית_המשפט_הגבוה_לצדק).
- 3 hours: 30 minutes forum and threshold, 30 prior demand, 90 petition, 30 affidavit, attachments and review.

### Steps

1. **Chronology (15 minutes).** Dated, numbered events, each tied to a document. Mark the date of the decision and of the last letter.
2. **Forum check (15 minutes).** Identify the decision-maker and the statute that empowers it. Search the First Schedule for the subject; read the item in full, because items name specific statutes. Look for a statutory appeal or a specialised tribunal. Write one sentence: "The forum is ___ because ___."
3. **Threshold table (15 minutes).** One row each: standing, prior demand, final answer or reasonable time, alternative remedy, delay, clean hands. Mark each "met", "fixable" or "fatal", with the fix.
4. **Prior demand (30 minutes).** If you have not written yet, write now with the letter template: the facts, the decision, why it is unlawful in two or three sentences, what you want, and a date by which you will go to court. In a dry run, assume the authority refused or did not answer.
5. **Requested orders (15 minutes).** Write the order nisi as "מדוע לא..." questions: narrow, specific, achievable. Delete any request the Court could not grant (damages, an order against a private person, an order to legislate).
6. **Facts section (20 minutes).** One fact per paragraph, numbered, each with its attachment (ע/1, ע/2...). Include facts against you.
7. **Exhaustion section (10 minutes).** The demand, the answer or silence, why no alternative remedy exists, why there is no delay.
8. **Legal argument (40 minutes).** Use the grounds in order: authority, procedure, considerations, reasonableness, proportionality (summarise your worksheet). Cite statute sections; cite at most two rulings, and only rulings you have read.
9. **Interim order (10 minutes).** Decide whether you need one. If yes, write three short paragraphs: irreparable harm, balance of convenience, prospects. If no, write why not.
10. **Affidavit and attachments (20 minutes).** Fill Form 2: which paragraphs you know personally, which to the best of your knowledge and from what source. Prepare the attachments list. Note who would certify the affidavit (Evidence Ordinance s. 15).
11. **Fee and filing plan (10 minutes).** Note the current fee from [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md), whether you would ask for an exemption (reg. 14), the filing method you confirmed, and the number of copies the registry asks for.
12. **Red-team review (20 minutes).** Read the petition as the State Attorney would. Write the three strongest threshold or merits answers they would give, and fix what you can.

### How you know it worked

- The forum sentence names the schedule item you checked, or says why none applies.
- Every threshold row is "met", or "fixable" with the fix done.
- Each requested order is something the Court can actually order against the respondent.
- Every fact has an attachment; the affidavit covers every factual paragraph.
- A reader who has never seen the case understands in two minutes who you are, what was decided, why it is unlawful and what you want.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, in your own name and about your own matter: any person may petition the HCJ and conduct the petition. A parent may petition for a minor child as the child's guardian. Representing anyone else, or drafting petitions for others as an occupation or for pay, is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20). A company or association is usually represented by a lawyer; check with the registry. Prisoners' petitions about prison conditions go to the district court, not the HCJ.

### Where and how

- **Where:** the Supreme Court registry in Jerusalem, by hand or by post as the President's guidelines describe; confirm the current filing method and the number of copies with the Judiciary's information centre (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) before you go. Matters in the First Schedule go to the district court sitting as an Administrative Affairs Court instead.
- **Fee:** see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); ask for an exemption under reg. 14 with the petition if you cannot pay.
- **Deadline:** no fixed period, but delay can defeat the petition (Aviram). File promptly after the final answer, or after a stated reasonable time without one.
- **Free help:** the Judiciary's information centre for procedure; Legal Aid (Ministry of Justice) in some matters, on means and merits; university law clinics, several of which handle public-law petitions; Kol-Zchut for the background rights (⚠️ check pages manually).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The matter is urgent and an interim order is needed within days, and you have not drafted one before.
- The State's preliminary response raises a threshold argument you cannot answer, or attaches classified or expert material.
- The case challenges a statute or a Basic Law rather than a decision about you.
- Other people are affected and want to join; you may not represent them.
- You face a costs order or the Court calls a hearing before a panel.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petitioning the HCJ on a First Schedule matter (arnona, planning, FOI) | Dismissed or transferred; time lost | Refile in the Administrative Affairs Court | Do step 2 before writing anything |
| No prior written demand | Dismissed in limine, possibly with costs | Write to the authority, wait, refile | Do step 4 and attach it |
| Waiting months after the final answer | Dismissed for delay | Explain the delay honestly in the petition | Diary the date of the final answer |
| Asking for damages or a broad declaration | Relief the Court will not give | Narrow to cancel and reconsider | Write orders as "מדוע לא..." questions |
| Hiding an unfavourable fact | Dismissed for lack of clean hands | Disclose it with your explanation | Include facts against you in step 6 |
| Affidavit not properly certified, or a copy instead of the original | Registry refuses to register | Sign a new original before an authorised person | Plan the certification in step 10 |

## Review

- The HCJ is residual: check the Administrative Affairs Court's First Schedule and any statutory appeal first.
- Most petitions fail on threshold doctrines: no prior demand, delay, an alternative remedy, or missing disclosure; each can be checked in advance.
- Ask for narrow, achievable relief: an order nisi to cancel and reconsider, with an interim order only when the harm is irreversible.
- The petition is supported by an original affidavit in Form 2; every fact needs an attachment.
- The 2023–2026 dispute has not changed the threshold rules; reasonableness review of ministers remains available as of October 2026.
