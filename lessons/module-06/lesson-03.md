# 06.3 · The limitation clause and proportionality

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a law or a government decision that limits a right and test it the way an Israeli court does: is a protected right infringed, does the infringement meet each condition of the limitation clause, and if not, what remedy follows. Proportionality is the single most used tool in Israeli public law; you will meet it again in administrative law, criminal procedure and labour law.

## Why it matters

The limitation clause is how the Basic Laws allow rights to be limited. Almost every law limits someone's rights: a tax limits property, a licence limits occupation, an arrest limits liberty. The question is never "is a right affected?" but "is the limit justified?". Israeli courts answer it with a structured test borrowed from German and Canadian law and adopted in the 1990s.

Knowing the structure matters in practice for two reasons. First, it tells you what to argue and in what order; a petition that skips straight to "this is unfair" is weak. Second, it tells you what is really in dispute. Most laws pass the first conditions easily, and cases are won or lost at the last sub-tests, where courts weigh benefits against harm. Critics argue that this last stage is where judges substitute their values for the legislature's; supporters say it is where the real justification must be shown. Both views are about the same, identifiable step.

## The law

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* Rights in the Basic Law may be infringed only by a law, or under a law with express authorisation, that befits the values of Israel, serves a proper purpose, and goes no further than required. [Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_8)

> אין פוגעים בחופש העיסוק אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש, או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

*Gist:* The same four conditions for freedom of occupation. [Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation, s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_חופש_העיסוק#סעיף_4)

Read the clause as four conditions, all of which must be met:

1. **Legality** — "בחוק או לפי חוק ... מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו": the infringement is in a statute, or in a regulation or decision made under express statutory authority. A minister cannot limit a right on general powers alone.
2. **Values** — "ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל": the law fits Israel's values as a Jewish and democratic state. In practice this condition is rarely decisive.
3. **Proper purpose** — "שנועד לתכלית ראויה": the aim is legitimate and important enough to justify limiting a right.
4. **Proportionality** — "במידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש": the courts split this into three sub-tests:
   - (a) **Rational connection**: the means actually advance the purpose.
   - (b) **Least harmful means**: no other means would achieve the purpose to the same degree with less harm.
   - (c) **Proportionality in the narrow sense**: the benefit of the law is in reasonable relation to the harm it causes. This is the balancing stage.

Before the limitation clause comes a threshold stage: the challenger must show that a protected right is infringed. After it comes a remedy stage: if the law fails, what should the court do (strike, sever, read down, suspend)? The burden is on the challenger at the first stage and on the State at the second.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 7052/03 עדאלה – המרכז המשפטי לזכויות המיעוט הערבי בישראל נ׳ שר הפנים (2006)

The Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law (Temporary Provision) 2003, passed after terror attacks in which some attackers had entered through family-unification permits, barred most residents of the West Bank and Gaza in certain age groups from receiving status in Israel through marriage to Israelis. An eleven-justice panel upheld it 6–5. President Barak, for five justices, held that it infringed the rights to family life and equality and failed proportionality in the narrow sense: a blanket ban added a small security gain over individual checks at a large cost to rights. Vice-President Cheshin, for the majority on the outcome, held that the Basic Law does not include a constitutional right to bring a foreign spouse to live in Israel, and that in any case the law was proportionate given the security risk. Justice Levy joined the result but warned that the law could not be extended in the same form.

Why it matters: it is the classic illustration that cases are decided at the narrow-sense stage, and that judges applying the same test can reach opposite results. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/26)

### בג״ץ 10662/04 חסן נ׳ המוסד לביטוח לאומי (2012)

The Income Support Law treated anyone who owned or used a car as having income equal to the benefit, so they lost the benefit entirely. The Court held that the right to minimum dignified existence derives from human dignity; the absolute presumption passed the rational-connection test "somewhat barely" but failed the least-harmful-means test, because an individual examination of the car's real value and use was possible. The provision was declared void.

Why it matters: a clear case decided at sub-test (b), which leaves the Knesset room to pursue the same purpose by narrower means. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/187)

### בג״ץ 2605/05 המרכז האקדמי למשפט ולעסקים נ׳ שר האוצר (2009)

A 2004 amendment to the Prisons Ordinance allowed a private, profit-making company to run a prison. Before it opened, an expanded panel held 8–1 that transferring the power to imprison to a profit-making body infringed the inmates' liberty and dignity by its very nature, beyond the infringement of imprisonment itself, and that the infringement was disproportionate. The amendment was declared void. Justice Levy dissented, holding that the harm was speculative and the Court should wait to see how the prison operated.

Why it matters: it shows the narrow-sense balance applied to the structure of state power rather than to measurable harm, and it is often cited by critics as an example of review based on principle rather than evidence. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/20)

### בג״ץ 8665/14 דסטה נ׳ הכנסת (2015)

The Prevention of Infiltration Law allowed holding people who entered Israel irregularly in custody for up to three months and in an open "residency centre" for up to twenty months. A nine-justice panel unanimously upheld the three-month custody, reading it narrowly as tied to identification and removal; a majority held the twenty-month maximum stay disproportionate and set a shorter interim limit while the Knesset legislated. This was the third ruling on successive versions of the law (Adam, 2013; Eitan, 2014).

Why it matters: a "constitutional dialogue" in action. Each version was tested, the Knesset re-legislated, and the Court adjusted. Supporters see a working check; critics see the Court repeatedly overriding a policy chosen by the elected branches. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/125)

## How it works in practice

### The order of analysis

| Stage | Question | Who bears the burden | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Infringement | Is a right protected by a Basic Law (or derived from dignity) infringed? | Challenger | Often contested for derived rights |
| 2a. Legality | Is it in a law, or under express authority in a law? | State | Decisive against regulations or decisions without clear authority |
| 2b. Values | Does the law befit Israel's values? | State | Almost always met |
| 2c. Proper purpose | Is the purpose legitimate and important enough? | State | Usually met; may fail if the real purpose is improper |
| 2d(i). Rational connection | Do the means advance the purpose? | State | Usually met |
| 2d(ii). Least harmful means | Is there a less harmful means that achieves the purpose equally? | State | Sometimes decisive (Hassan) |
| 2d(iii). Narrow sense | Is the gain worth the harm? | State | Where most hard cases are decided (Adalah) |
| 3. Remedy | Strike, sever, read down, suspend? | Court | Usually narrow, often suspended |

### Practical tips

- **Find the stated purpose** in the bill's explanatory notes (דברי הסבר) and the committee protocols on the Knesset website. Courts look at both the declared purpose and how the law works.
- **Least harmful means** is about alternatives that achieve the purpose *to the same degree*. Proposing a gentler measure that achieves less is an argument for stage (iii), not (ii).
- **Evidence matters.** Claims of harm and of benefit need data: numbers of people affected, security assessments, costs.
- **Margin of appreciation.** In social and economic policy (Gavish, lesson 06.7) the Court gives the Knesset wide room; in core liberty (custody, imprisonment) much less.

## Worked example

A municipality in a mixed city passes a by-law, under a general power to regulate public order, banning loudspeakers on all private balconies after 21:00 to reduce noise complaints. Avraham Toledano, who plays recorded prayers on a holiday evening, and Samira Khoury, who hosts family celebrations, both object.

1. **Right infringed.** Freedom of expression and freedom of religion (derived from dignity, lesson 06.4), and property (use of one's home). Stage 1 met.
2. **Legality.** The by-law limits rights under a *general* power. Section 8 requires express authority for an infringement made "under a law". This is the weakest point for the municipality: courts have held that general powers do not authorise significant rights infringements.
3. **Values and purpose.** Reducing noise for neighbours' rest and health is a proper purpose.
4. **Rational connection.** A ban on loudspeakers reduces noise. Met.
5. **Least harmful means.** A volume limit in decibels, or exemptions for a set number of holiday or family events, would reduce noise almost as much with less harm. Likely fails here.
6. **Narrow sense.** Not needed if it fails (5), but the analysis would weigh rest against occasional religious and family expression.
7. **Remedy.** Most likely the by-law (or the blanket part) is set aside, and the municipality can adopt a volume rule. Note that both objectors, from different communities, win on the same analysis.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md).
- One of: Hassan (car presumption), Academic Center (private prison), or a law or by-law that affects you.
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the provision in one sentence, with its source (law, section).
2. Write the purpose as the State would put it, in its strongest form, using the explanatory notes if available.
3. Stage 1: name the right and its source (Basic Law section, or derived from dignity with a case).
4. Stage 2a–2c: legality, values, proper purpose. One or two lines each.
5. Stage 2d: for each sub-test, write the State's best argument, then the challenger's, then your conclusion.
6. Stage 3: propose the narrowest remedy that would cure the defect.
7. Compare with the Court's actual result if you used a decided case, and note at which sub-test it was decided.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, as an argument. A proportionality analysis is how you argue against any public decision that limits your rights: in a letter to an authority, an objection, an appeal, a petition to the Administrative Affairs Court or to the High Court of Justice. Writing it for yourself is lawful; writing it for others as an occupation or for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- **Before going to court**, send the authority a written demand that sets out your proportionality argument and asks it to change the decision (template: [letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md)). Courts expect this (lesson 06.8).
- **Decisions of local authorities and many ministries** go to the Administrative Affairs Court; **laws and national-level decisions** to the High Court of Justice (lesson 06.8).
- Fees via [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your argument depends on evidence you cannot get yourself (security data, economic studies).
- The State responds with expert opinions.
- You are challenging a statute rather than a decision made under it.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing "unfair" without naming a protected right | Fails at stage 1 | Identify the right and its source | Start every analysis with stage 1 |
| Attacking the purpose when it is plainly legitimate | Lost credibility | Concede the purpose; move to the means | State the purpose in its strongest form |
| Proposing an alternative that achieves less as "least harmful means" | Court rejects it at stage (ii) | Use it at the narrow-sense stage instead | Ask: does the alternative achieve the purpose equally? |
| No facts on harm or benefit | Balancing cannot be done in your favour | Gather numbers, documents, examples | Collect evidence before writing |
| Asking only to strike the whole law | Court prefers narrow remedies | Ask for a reading-down or a specific provision | Plan stage 3 in advance |
| Ignoring the legality condition | Missing the easiest winning argument against regulations | Check the authorising section for express authority | Always read the enabling law |

## Review

- The limitation clause has four conditions: by or under a law with express authority; befitting Israel's values; proper purpose; no more than required.
- "No more than required" is three sub-tests: rational connection, least harmful means, proportionality in the narrow sense.
- The challenger proves the infringement; the State justifies it.
- Hard cases are decided at the last sub-tests: Hassan at least harmful means, Adalah (6–5) and Academic Center at the narrow-sense balance.
- Remedies are usually narrow and often suspended, leaving the Knesset room to legislate again (Desta).
