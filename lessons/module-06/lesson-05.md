# 06.5 · Institutions: Knesset, Government, President, State Comptroller

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain how Israel's main constitutional institutions are formed and how each is checked, choose the institution that can actually deal with a problem you have, and write a complaint to the Ombudsman, the State Comptroller's free complaints service. The lesson also covers how far the High Court of Justice reviews the Knesset's internal decisions and the Government's appointments, a central issue in the 2023–2026 dispute.

## Why it matters

Israel is a parliamentary democracy with a single chamber. The Government holds office by the Knesset's confidence, and in practice the coalition controls both the executive and the legislature, including the power to amend Basic Laws by a simple majority. There is no upper house, no federal structure, no presidential veto and no regional courts of equal standing. That is why the institutions that do check power — the State Comptroller, the Attorney General, the courts, the President's limited powers, the opposition's procedural rights — carry so much weight, and why disputes over them are so intense.

For a citizen, the practical question is simpler: who can fix my problem? A minister's policy is changed by the minister, the Government or the Knesset; an unfair act by an office can be investigated by the Ombudsman without cost; a pardon is the President's; an unlawful decision goes to a court.

## The law

### The Knesset

> הכנסת היא בית הנבחרים של המדינה.

*Gist:* The Knesset is the State's house of representatives. [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הכנסת#סעיף_1)

> תקופת כהונתה של הכנסת תהיה ארבע שנים מיום היבחרה.

*Gist:* The term is four years. [s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הכנסת#סעיף_8) The Knesset may dissolve itself early only by a law passed by a majority of members (s. 34). The 25th Knesset passed such a law on 17 July 2026, and elections are set for 27 October 2026.

Section 7א bars a list or candidate whose aims or acts include denying Israel's existence as a Jewish and democratic state, incitement to racism, or support for armed struggle by an enemy state or terror organisation; a Central Elections Committee decision to bar a candidate needs Supreme Court approval. [s. 7א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הכנסת#סעיף_7א)

### The Government

> הממשלה היא הרשות המבצעת של המדינה.

> הממשלה מכהנת מכוח אמון הכנסת.

*Gist:* The Government is the executive and holds office by the Knesset's confidence. [ss. 1, 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_1)

> (ב) הבעת אי־אמון בממשלה תיעשה בהחלטה של הכנסת, ברוב חבריה, להביע אמון בממשלה אחרת [...]

*Gist:* No-confidence is "constructive": 61 members must express confidence in an alternative government. [s. 28](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_28)

> הממשלה מוסמכת לעשות בשם המדינה, בכפוף לכל דין, כל פעולה שעשייתה אינה מוטלת בדין על רשות אחרת.

*Gist:* The Government has residual power to act for the State in anything not given by law to another authority. [s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה#סעיף_32) Remember from lesson 06.3: residual power does not authorise infringing rights.

Section 39 allows emergency regulations, which may even change laws, while a state of emergency declared by the Knesset is in force; they cannot change the Basic Laws on rights beyond what Human Dignity and Liberty s. 12 permits.

### The President

> לנשיא המדינה נתונה הסמכות לחון עבריינים ולהקל בעונשים על ידי הפחתתם או המרתם.

*Gist:* The President may pardon offenders and reduce or commute sentences. [s. 11(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_נשיא_המדינה#סעיף_11)

> חתימתו של נשיא המדינה על מסמך רשמי טעונה חתימת קיום של ראש הממשלה או של שר אחר [...]

*Gist:* The President's official acts need a minister's countersignature, except those relating to forming a government or dissolving the Knesset. [s. 12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_נשיא_המדינה#סעיף_12) The President also signs laws, appoints judges chosen by the Judicial Selection Committee, and assigns the task of forming a government.

### The State Comptroller and the Ombudsman

> במילוי תפקידיו יהיה מבקר המדינה אחראי בפני הכנסת בלבד ולא יהיה תלוי בממשלה.

*Gist:* The Comptroller answers only to the Knesset and is independent of the Government. [s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_מבקר_המדינה#סעיף_6)

> (א) מבקר המדינה ייבחר בידי הכנסת בהצבעה חשאית; [...] (ב) תקופת כהונתו של מבקר המדינה תהיה שבע שנים. (ג) מבקר המדינה יכהן תקופת כהונה אחת בלבד.

*Gist:* Elected by the Knesset by secret ballot, for a single seven-year term. [s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_מבקר_המדינה#סעיף_7)

> מבקר המדינה יברר תלונות מאת הציבור על גופים ואנשים כפי שייקבע בחוק או לפיו; בתפקידו זה יכהן מבקר המדינה בתואר "נציב תלונות הציבור".

*Gist:* The Comptroller investigates public complaints and in this role is the Ombudsman. [s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_מבקר_המדינה#סעיף_4)

The State Comptroller Law sets the complaint rules:

> כל אדם רשאי להגיש תלונה אל נציב תלונות הציבור.

*Gist:* Anyone may complain. [s. 33](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_מבקר_המדינה#סעיף_33) The complaint must be signed with name and address (s. 34); it must concern an act (including an omission or delay) that harms the complainant directly and is unlawful, without authority, contrary to proper administration, or shows excessive rigidity or flagrant injustice (s. 37). Not investigated: complaints about the President, the Knesset or MKs in their role, the Government as a body, judicial acts, and matters pending before or decided by a court (s. 38). Complaints filed more than a year after the act, or where an appeal route exists, are investigated only for a special reason (s. 39). A complaint does not extend any statutory deadline (s. 45(א)(2)).

## Leading cases

### ע״ב 1/88 כך נ׳ ועדת הבחירות המרכזית לכנסת השתים־עשרה (1988)

The Central Elections Committee barred the Kach list under the newly added s. 7א for incitement to racism. The Supreme Court upheld the disqualification, holding that the evidence of racist aims and acts was clear, convincing and unequivocal, and rejected the argument that s. 7א itself was invalid for infringing equality of elections.

Why it matters: it sets the standard for disqualifying lists and candidates, which the Court applies narrowly before every election. Supporters see a democracy defending itself; critics worry about excluding voters' choices. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/283)

### בג״ץ 2144/20 התנועה למען איכות השלטון בישראל נ׳ יושב ראש הכנסת (2020)

After the March 2020 election, the acting Speaker, serving under the continuity rule, refused to put the election of a permanent Speaker on the agenda, although 61 members asked for it. The Court ordered that the vote be held by a set date, holding that the Speaker could not block the majority's will in a way that undermined the Knesset's basic functioning. The Speaker resigned rather than hold the vote.

Why it matters: the strongest example of the Court intervening in internal Knesset procedure. Supporters say it protected the Knesset majority itself; critics say it breached the separation of powers by ordering the legislature's presiding officer. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/270)

### בג״ץ 232/16 התנועה למען איכות השלטון בישראל נ׳ ראש הממשלה (2016)

The petition challenged Aryeh Deri's appointment as Minister of the Interior after a past corruption conviction for acts committed in that ministry. The majority dismissed it, holding that a criminal record is a relevant consideration in an appointment, weighed by the offence's seriousness, its relation to the office, time elapsed and other factors, but that the decision was within the range of reasonableness. Justice Hendel dissented.

Why it matters: it shows the review of ministerial appointments by reasonableness that the 2023 amendment tried to remove (lesson 06.6). In January 2023 a later appointment of the same minister was annulled as extremely unreasonable; that ruling is one background to the amendment. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/268)

## How it works in practice

### Who fixes what

| Your problem | Institution | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| An office ignored you, delayed, or treated you unfairly | Ombudsman (State Comptroller) | Signed complaint, online or by post | Free |
| A policy you think is wrong | Minister, Government, Knesset committee | Letter; Knesset committee participation (module 7) | Free |
| A criminal sentence you ask to reduce | President (via the Ministry of Justice pardons department) | Pardon request | Free |
| An unlawful decision affecting you | Court (Administrative Affairs Court or HCJ) | Petition (lesson 06.8) | Court fee ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) |
| Systemic failure in a ministry | State Comptroller audit | Complaint, or information to the audit units | Free |

### Current state of affairs (October 2026)

- **Knesset and elections.** The Knesset dissolved by law on 17 July 2026; elections are on 27 October 2026. The Government continues in office until a new one is formed (Basic Law: The Government s. 30(ב)), subject to the restraint expected of a transitional government.
- **State Comptroller.** The Comptroller's seven-year term ended in July 2026. The Knesset's vote on a successor in June 2026 was invalidated by a five-justice panel in July 2026 because some members photographed their ballots, breaching the secret ballot required by s. 7(א); as of early October 2026 the repeat vote had not been held and a petition asked the Court to order the Speaker to convene it. (News-based; ruling text not yet checked.) The Ombudsman's complaint unit continues to operate.
- **Attorney General.** The Government voted in August 2025 to end the Attorney General's term under a new procedure; in December 2025 a seven-justice panel annulled both the procedure and the dismissal, and she remains in office. Bills to split the office passed a first reading in June 2026 but were not enacted before the Knesset dissolved; under the continuity rule a new Knesset may resume them.

## Worked example

Daniel Avraham, a reservist from Kiryat Gat, applied to the National Insurance Institute for a reservist's grant. Five months later he has had no decision, despite two emails.

1. **Which institution?** A delay by a public body that harms him directly: the Ombudsman (s. 37 covers "פיגור בעשיה"). A court petition would be slower and cost a fee.
2. **Is it excluded?** NII is an audited body; no court case is pending; he complains within a year (s. 39).
3. **Before complaining.** He sends a final written request citing the duty to decide within the statutory period ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)), and keeps copies.
4. **The complaint.** One page: who he is, what he asked for and when, what happened, documents attached, what he wants (a decision within a set time). Signed, with address (s. 34).
5. **What to expect.** The Ombudsman asks NII to respond (s. 41) and tells Daniel the result (s. 43). The decision is not binding in court, but bodies usually act on it. If NII then refuses the grant, his appeal route is the one the NII decision states, and its deadline runs regardless of the complaint (s. 45).

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. An Ombudsman complaint is low-risk and free; you may file it for real in your own matter.

### You need

- A real or recent problem with a public body (a ministry, local authority, NII, the Israel Land Authority, a government company).
- Your correspondence and dates.
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Check the conditions: audited body (ss. 9, 36), direct harm to you, an act or delay contrary to law or proper administration (s. 37), not excluded (s. 38), within a year (s. 39).
2. If you have not yet written to the body, write first ([letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md)) and wait for the statutory period.
3. Draft the complaint: heading (נושא התלונה), your details, the body and department, a dated chronology, what you want, list of attachments.
4. State the ground in one sentence: for example "עיכוב בלתי סביר במתן החלטה, בניגוד לכללי המינהל התקין".
5. Note any appeal deadline that is running and diarise it separately; the complaint does not stop it.
6. File through the Ombudsman's online form on the State Comptroller's website, or by post, and keep the confirmation.

### Template

None; the Ombudsman's online form has the required fields. Use the [letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md) for the prior request.

### How you know it worked

- Your complaint meets ss. 34 and 37 and is not caught by s. 38.
- It fits on one page with a dated chronology and a specific request.
- You have diarised every running deadline.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain constructive no-confidence (s. 28).
- [ ] I know which matters the Ombudsman will not investigate.
- [ ] I can name the cases on disqualifying lists, internal Knesset decisions and ministerial appointments.
- [ ] I know the current status of the Comptroller's office and the Attorney General.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Anyone may complain to the Ombudsman (s. 33), write to ministers and Knesset members, and request a pardon in their own matter. A friend or relative may help you write, but drafting such documents for others as an occupation or for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Ombudsman: online form on the State Comptroller's website (https://www.mevaker.gov.il), or by post. Free.
- Pardon requests: through the Ministry of Justice pardons department, by the prisoner or a relative; check the current form on gov.il.
- Elections: the Central Elections Committee publishes polling-station and voter-registry information before 27 October 2026.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need a binding remedy quickly; the Ombudsman cannot order anything enforceable.
- A statutory appeal deadline is close.
- You are asking for a pardon in a serious criminal case, where the content of the request matters greatly.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complaining to the Ombudsman about a court decision | Not investigated (s. 38(4)–(5)) | Use appeal or the Judicial Ombudsman (conduct only) | Check s. 38 first |
| Waiting for the Ombudsman while an appeal deadline runs | Appeal right lost (s. 45) | File the appeal in time and the complaint in parallel | Diarise deadlines |
| Unsigned or anonymous complaint | Not processed (s. 34) | Sign and give an address | Use the online form |
| Writing to the President to overturn a policy | No power | Write to the minister or Knesset committee | Use the "who fixes what" table |
| Treating news reports as settled law | Wrong advice to yourself | Check the ruling and its date | Mark news-based items |

## Review

- The Knesset (single chamber, four years, dissolution by law, s. 34) gives the Government its confidence; no-confidence must be constructive (s. 28).
- The Government has residual power (s. 32), which cannot infringe rights; emergency regulations are limited by s. 39 and the rights Basic Laws.
- The President pardons and performs formal acts, mostly with countersignature (ss. 11–12).
- The State Comptroller is independent, elected by secret ballot for one seven-year term, and as Ombudsman investigates complaints free of charge (State Comptroller Law ss. 33–45).
- The Court has reviewed Knesset internal acts (2144/20) and appointments (232/16); critics see this as a breach of the separation of powers.
