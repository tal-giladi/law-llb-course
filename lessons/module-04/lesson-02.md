# 04.2 · Negligence: duty of care, breach, the reasonable person

By the end of this lesson you will be able to test any accident against the four elements of negligence the way an Israeli court does, spot when the law shifts the burden of proof to the defendant, and recognise the cases where courts refuse to impose a duty at all.

## Why it matters

Negligence (רשלנות) is the workhorse of Israeli tort law. Falls on broken pavements, injuries in schools and shops, medical errors, damaged cars, leaking ceilings, collapsed balconies, careless advice: almost all are negligence claims. If you ever sue or are sued in tort, the odds are high that the case turns on whether someone behaved as a "reasonable and prudent person" and whether the law expected them to protect you at all.

The doctrine looks simple in the statute and is subtle in the cases. Israeli courts ask not only "was the harm foreseeable?" but also "should the law make this defendant answer to this plaintiff for this kind of harm?" That second, policy question is where most hard cases are decided, and where judges and scholars still disagree.

## The law

### The definition

> עשה אדם מעשה שאדם סביר ונבון לא היה עושה באותן נסיבות או לא עשה מעשה שאדם סביר ונבון היה עושה באותן נסיבות, או שבמשלח יד פלוני לא השתמש במיומנות, או לא נקט מידת זהירות, שאדם סביר ונבון וכשיר לפעול באותו משלח יד היה משתמש או נוקט באותן נסיבות – הרי זו התרשלות; ואם התרשל כאמור ביחס לאדם אחר, שלגביו יש לו באותן נסיבות חובה שלא לנהוג כפי שנהג, הרי זו רשלנות, והגורם ברשלנותו נזק לזולתו עושה עוולה.

*Gist:* Careless conduct is doing what a reasonable and prudent person would not do, or failing to do what they would, or, in a profession, falling below the skill and care of a reasonable member of that profession; it becomes the tort of negligence when the careless person owed the injured person a duty and caused them damage. ([Civil Wrongs Ordinance, s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_35))

> החובה האמורה בסעיף 35 מוטלת כלפי כל אדם וכלפי בעל כל נכס, כל אימת שאדם סביר צריך היה באותן נסיבות לראות מראש שהם עלולים במהלכם הרגיל של דברים להיפגע ממעשה או ממחדל המפורשים באותו סעיף.

*Gist:* The duty is owed to every person and every property owner whom a reasonable person should have foreseen could be harmed in the ordinary course of things. ([s. 36](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_36))

Read together, ss. 35–36 give four elements: (1) a duty of care, (2) breach of the standard of care, (3) damage, (4) a causal link between breach and damage. Causation and damage are lesson 04.3.

### When the defendant must prove it was not negligent

> בתובענה שהוגשה על נזק והוכח בה כי לתובע לא היתה ידיעה או לא היתה לו יכולת לדעת מה היו למעשה הנסיבות שגרמו למקרה אשר הביא לידי הנזק, וכי הנזק נגרם על ידי נכס שלנתבע היתה שליטה מלאה עליו, ונראה לבית המשפט שאירוע המקרה שגרם לנזק מתיישב יותר עם המסקנה שהנתבע לא נקט זהירות סבירה מאשר עם המסקנה שהוא נקט זהירות סבירה – על הנתבע הראיה שלא היתה לגבי המקרה שהביא לידי הנזק התרשלות שיחוב עליה.

*Gist:* Res ipsa loquitur: if you could not know what actually caused the accident, the damage was caused by property under the defendant's full control, and the event fits negligence better than care, the defendant must prove it was not negligent. ([s. 41](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_41))

Three more burden-shifting rules: damage caused by a **dangerous thing** or something that escaped (s. 38), by **fire** (s. 39), and by a **wild animal** or an animal known to be dangerous (s. 40). In each, once you prove the basic facts, the owner or person in charge must prove there was no negligence.

### Trespassers

The occupier's duty about the condition of land does not extend to a trespasser unless the trespasser proves they entered in good faith and without intent to commit an offence or a tort (s. 37).

## Leading cases

### ע"א 145/80 ועקנין נ' המועצה המקומית בית שמש, פ"ד לז(1) 113 (1982)

The Supreme Court set out the structure Israeli courts still use. The duty question has two stages: a **notional duty** (does this kind of defendant owe a duty to this kind of plaintiff for this kind of harm, as a matter of legal policy?) and a **concrete duty** (was this plaintiff's harm foreseeable in these circumstances?). Foreseeability has two sides: what a reasonable person *could* foresee (a technical question) and what they *should* foresee (a normative one). Not every foreseeable risk is unreasonable: everyday, ordinary risks are left with the person who meets them. Why it matters: almost every negligence judgment in Israel walks through these stages. Full text is not freely available online; the structure is quoted in many later rulings and in free academic writing, for example [Gilead's analysis of the debate on negligence models](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/models-of-negligence-the-debate-in-israels-supreme-court/).

### ע"א 3510/99 וואלאס נ' אגד (6.8.2001)

A man waiting at Egged's central bus station in Jerusalem was beaten by a group of young men. He sued the attackers and Egged, claiming Egged was negligent and in breach of statutory duty by not securing the station. The magistrates' court struck out the claim against Egged at the outset. The Supreme Court reversed: a spontaneous criminal attack by a third party does not automatically fall outside tort law, and whether an operator of a crowded public place must take reasonable precautions against crime depends on foreseeability and legal policy, decided on evidence. Why it matters: the duty to protect against third parties' crimes is decided case by case, not ruled out. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/wallace-v-egged).

### ע"א 4493/05 ירושלמי נ' פולריס יבוא מכונות בע"מ (7.3.2007)

A sponsor sent a rider to a motorbike race in Egypt, where he was seriously injured. No one had taken out personal-accident insurance for him, and no statute required it. The Court (Justice Hayut, with Justices Grunis and Naor) held that, as a rule, one party to a joint venture has no duty in negligence to insure the other or to check that he insured himself, unless there is a special relationship or reliance; there was none. Why it matters: courts limit negligence where the harm is a purely financial "missed protection" and the plaintiff could protect himself. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yerushalmi-v-polaris-imports-ltd).

### The debate

Since the 2010s some justices and scholars have argued that the two-stage duty test is artificial and that the real work is done by asking whether the defendant's conduct was unreasonable in light of the risk, the cost of precautions and policy. Others defend a separate duty stage as the only way to keep liability within limits (pure economic loss, public authorities, omissions). The structure in ועקנין remains the one courts apply; the debate affects how much weight policy carries at each stage.

## How it works in practice

### The four questions, in court order

1. **Duty.** Is there a recognised relationship (driver and road user, shop and customer, school and pupil, doctor and patient, landlord and visitor)? If it is new, argue foreseeability and why policy supports a duty. Pure economic loss, omissions and harm caused by third parties are the hard categories.
2. **Breach.** What would a reasonable person, or a reasonable member of the profession, have done? Courts weigh the likelihood of harm, its severity, the cost and practicality of precautions and the value of the activity. Professional and safety standards, regulations and the defendant's own procedures are strong evidence.
3. **Damage.** What did you lose? Bodily injury, property, expenses, pain and suffering (lesson 04.3).
4. **Causation.** Would the harm have happened without the breach? Was it within the risk that made the conduct careless (lesson 04.3)?

### Evidence that wins negligence cases

- Photos of the hazard, with date and scale, taken as soon as possible.
- Witness names and phone numbers, collected on the spot.
- Earlier complaints showing the defendant knew (municipal 106 hotline reference numbers, emails to the building committee).
- Repair quotes and receipts; medical records from the first visit.
- The rule or standard the defendant ignored (safety regulation, standard, internal procedure).

### Shifting the burden

If you cannot know why something happened (a lift dropped, a ceiling collapsed, a bottle exploded), check s. 41 and s. 38. If the defendant controlled the thing and this kind of accident usually means carelessness, plead the presumption expressly. It does not win the case automatically; it moves the burden.

## Worked example

Tigist Mekonen, a nurse from Netanya, trips at night on a raised paving slab on her street and breaks her wrist. The slab had been lifted by a tree root for months; her neighbour had reported it to the municipal hotline eight weeks earlier and has the reference number.

1. **Duty.** A local authority responsible for pavements owes pedestrians a duty for their condition: a recognised notional duty. Concretely, a pedestrian tripping on a raised slab is the very risk a reasonable authority foresees.
2. **Breach.** A reasonable authority that received a report would inspect and mark or repair the slab within a reasonable time. Eight weeks without even a warning sign is strong evidence of breach. The municipality may argue limited budget and thousands of kilometres of pavement; the court weighs this, but a specific report narrows the defence.
3. **Damage.** Fracture, treatment, days off work, pain and suffering.
4. **Causation.** She fell because of the slab; the hospital record says "נפילה על מדרכה".
5. **Weak points.** Was it dark? Was she looking at her phone? That raises contributory fault (lesson 04.5), not a full defence. A bodily injury with lasting effects also means she should take advice before settling (see below).

Tigist photographs the slab with a ruler the next morning, gets the neighbour's hotline reference, and keeps all medical and pay records.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An accident that happened to you or your family, or the supplied case.
- The text of ss. 35–41 open.
- 50 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Yusuf Abu Rabia, a student from Rahat, parks at a shopping centre in Be'er Sheva. A loose metal sign falls from the centre's façade in a moderate wind and smashes his windscreen. The centre's management says: "Act of God, the wind did it."

### Steps

1. Write the facts in dated sentences.
2. **Duty:** name the relationship. Is it recognised? If not, write two sentences on foreseeability and policy.
3. **Breach:** write what a reasonable person in the defendant's position would have done, and the evidence that they did not.
4. **Burden:** check ss. 38–41. Could you plead that the defendant must prove it was not negligent? Which facts do you need for that?
5. **Damage and causation:** list each loss and how it flows from the breach.
6. **The defence's best answer:** write it in the defendant's voice ("act of God", "you parked in a marked no-parking spot", "the sign was inspected last month").
7. Write a three-line conclusion: strong, arguable or weak, and why.

### Template

None. A four-column table (element, your facts, evidence, defence answer) is enough.

### How you know it worked

- Each of the four elements has facts and evidence next to it.
- You have stated the defence's best answer and your reply to it.
- For the supplied case, you identified s. 41 (property under the centre's full control, cause unknown to you) and s. 64(1) (an unusual natural event) as the battleground.

### Self-check

- [ ] I separated notional duty from concrete foreseeability.
- [ ] I described breach by what a reasonable person would do, not by the outcome.
- [ ] I checked the burden-shifting sections.
- [ ] I wrote the defence's strongest point honestly.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for property damage and minor injuries in your own name: small claims up to the ceiling, or the magistrates' court above it (Courts Law ss. 60, 63; Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). A claim against a company or a municipality is still yours to bring; they will usually have a lawyer or a claims department.

### Where and how

- Small claims: an individual's claim up to the ceiling in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); filing is covered in lesson 04.9.
- Magistrates' court above that ceiling; limitation: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) and lesson 04.5.
- Before suing: a demand letter (lesson 04.8) and, if the defendant is insured, a claim to the insurer.
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The injury may be permanent or you lost work for weeks: medical-expert evidence and future losses are hard to prove alone.
- The defendant is a hospital or doctor: medical negligence needs an expert opinion before filing.
- The insurer offers a settlement that includes a waiver of all future claims.
- The limitation period is close and the facts are still unclear.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing "I got hurt, so they were negligent" | The court finds no proof of breach | Add evidence of what a reasonable defendant would have done | Prove the standard, not just the result |
| No photos or witnesses from the day | The hazard is repaired; the defendant denies it existed | Look for hotline records, CCTV requests, neighbours' photos | Photograph with date and scale at once |
| Ignoring s. 41 when the cause is unknown | You carry a burden you cannot meet | Plead the presumption and the facts that trigger it | Check ss. 38–41 whenever the cause is in the defendant's hands |
| Suing for purely financial loss as if it were physical damage | Court finds no duty (Yerushalmi) | Reframe as contract or show a special relationship and reliance | Ask whether the law protects this kind of loss |
| Settling a bodily-injury claim early | Future medical costs are waived | Rarely fixable after signing | Take advice before signing any waiver |

## Review

- Negligence has four elements: duty, breach, damage, causation (ss. 35–36).
- Duty has a notional (policy) stage and a concrete (foreseeability) stage, following ועקנין; ordinary risks of life stay with the plaintiff.
- Breach is measured against a reasonable person or a reasonable professional, weighing risk, severity and the cost of precautions.
- Sections 38–41 shift the burden to the defendant for dangerous things, fire, animals and res ipsa loquitur.
- Duties to prevent third-party crime are decided on evidence (Wallace v. Egged); duties to protect another's finances need a special relationship (Yerushalmi v. Polaris).
