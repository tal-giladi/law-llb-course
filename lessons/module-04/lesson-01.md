# 04.1 · The Civil Wrongs Ordinance: structure and the list of torts

By the end of this lesson you will be able to open the Civil Wrongs Ordinance, find the tort that fits a set of facts, list its elements, and tell whether the wrong lives in the Ordinance itself or in another statute that borrows the Ordinance's rules.

## Why it matters

Tort law (דיני נזיקין) decides when one person must compensate another for harm that does not come from a broken contract: a fall on a broken pavement, a neighbour's noisy generator, a dog bite, a car scratched in a car park, a medical error, a false accusation. It is the law most self-represented people meet first, usually as plaintiffs in a small claim and sometimes as defendants.

Israeli tort law has an unusual shape. It is not a list of principles but a **closed list of named torts** in one Mandate-era ordinance, translated and re-enacted in 1968, interpreted for decades through English common law and now developed by Israeli courts. Above all, one tort, negligence, has grown so wide that it covers most everyday cases. If you understand the structure, you know where to look; if you skip it, you may sue under the wrong tort and miss an element the court will insist on.

## The law

### One ordinance, a closed list

> הדברים המנויים להלן בפקודה זו הם עוולות, ובכפוף להוראות הפקודה – כל הנפגע או הניזוק על ידי עוולה שנעשתה בישראל יהא זכאי לתרופה המפורשת בפקודה מידי עושה העוולה או האחראי לה.

*Gist:* Only the wrongs listed in the Ordinance are torts, and anyone injured or damaged by one of them in Israel is entitled to the remedies the Ordinance provides, against the wrongdoer or whoever is responsible for them. ([Civil Wrongs Ordinance [New Version], s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_3))

The Ordinance was enacted by the Mandate in 1944 and re-enacted in Hebrew as a "new version" in force from 1 October 1968 (s. 93). Section 1 still tells the reader to interpret it by English principles; in practice Israeli courts develop it independently, and rarely cite that instruction.

### What counts as damage

> ”נזק“ – אבדן חיים, אבדן נכס, נוחות, רווחה גופנית או שם־טוב, או חיסור מהם, וכל אבדן או חיסור כיוצאים באלה;

*Gist:* "Damage" is broad: loss of life, property, comfort, physical welfare or reputation, or any reduction of them. ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_2))

The narrower **pecuniary damage** (נזק ממון) is "actual loss or expense that can be valued in money and particularised". Several torts (trespass to land, trespass to chattels, deceit, injurious falsehood, inducing breach of contract) pay damages only if you prove pecuniary damage, and s. 76(2) requires you to give its details in the statement of claim.

### The list, by chapter 3 of the Ordinance

| Tort | Section | Core idea | Needs proof of damage? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assault (תקיפה) | 23–24 | Intentional use of force against a person without consent, or a credible threat of it | No |
| False imprisonment (כליאת שווא) | 26–27 | Total, unlawful deprivation of liberty for any time | No |
| Trespass to land (הסגת גבול במקרקעין) | 29–30 | Unlawful entry to land, or damage or interference with it | Pecuniary damage for compensation |
| Trespass to chattels (הסגת גבול במיטלטלין) | 31–32 | Unlawfully taking goods from someone's possession, or violent interference with them | Pecuniary damage for compensation |
| Negligence (רשלנות) | 35–41 | Careless conduct, a duty of care, damage caused | Yes |
| Injury by a dog (היזק על ידי כלב) | 41א–41ג | Owner strictly liable for bodily injury by a dog | Yes (bodily injury) |
| Public nuisance (מיטרד לציבור) | 42–43 | Endangering the public or obstructing a public right | Private plaintiff: pecuniary damage |
| Private nuisance (מיטרד ליחיד) | 44–47 | Substantial interference with reasonable use and enjoyment of another's land | Yes |
| Blocking sunlight; withdrawing support | 48, 48א | Narrow land torts | — |
| Unlawful detention; conversion (עיכוב שלא כדין; גזל) | 49–55 | Keeping or taking someone else's goods | Remedy can be return of the goods |
| Deceit; injurious falsehood (תרמית; שקר מפגיע) | 56–58 | Knowingly false statements causing loss | Pecuniary damage |
| Malicious prosecution (נגישה) | 60 | Malicious, baseless criminal or insolvency proceedings that ended in your favour | — |
| Inducing breach of contract (גרם הפרת חוזה) | 62 | Knowingly causing a party to breach a contract with you | Pecuniary damage |
| Breach of statutory duty (הפרת חובה חקוקה) | 63 | Breach of a duty in another enactment meant to protect you | Damage of the kind the enactment meant to prevent |

Chapter 2 sets the general rules: trivial acts are not torts (s. 4); voluntary assumption of risk (s. 5) and statutory authority (s. 6) are defences; public servants are immune for acts in their governmental role, while the state answers instead (ss. 7–7ו); no suit for a tort committed under the age of 12 (s. 9(א)); joint tortfeasors are liable jointly and severally (s. 11); employers answer for employees (s. 13). Chapter 4 deals with fault and contributory fault (ss. 64–70), chapter 5 with remedies (ss. 71–89). You will meet each of these in this module.

### Torts outside the Ordinance

Other statutes create torts and then plug them into the Ordinance:

> פגיעה בפרטיות היא עוולה אזרחית, והוראות פקודת הנזיקין [נוסח חדש] יחולו עליה בכפוף להוראות חוק זה.

*Gist:* Invasion of privacy is a civil wrong to which the Ordinance's rules apply, subject to the Privacy Protection Law. ([Privacy Protection Law 1981, s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות))

The same technique appears in the [Prohibition of Defamation Law 1965](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע) (defamation is a civil wrong and, in some cases, a crime), the Consumer Protection Law 1981 (s. 31: misleading a consumer is a tort), the Commercial Torts Law 1999 and the Defective Products Liability Law 1980. Defamation and privacy have their own defences and their own compensation-without-proof-of-damage rules; this course treats them only as pointers and returns to them in a later year.

## Leading cases

### דנ"א 5712/01 ברזני נ' בזק חברה ישראלית לתקשורת בע"מ (11.8.2003)

A consumer sued Bezeq over a misleading advertisement and sought to lead a class action. In a further hearing of seven justices, the majority (Justice Cheshin, with President Barak and Deputy President Or) held that a breach of the Consumer Protection Law's ban on misleading conduct is a tort, so the Ordinance's general rules apply to it, including the need to prove damage and a causal link: a consumer who never saw the advertisement was not misled by it and has no personal claim. The dissenters would have allowed recovery without proof of reliance. Why it matters: once a statute says "this is a tort", you inherit the Ordinance's elements, defences and remedies, not just the statute's prohibition. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/barazani-v-bezeq).

### ע"א 1326/07 המר נ' עמית (28.5.2012)

Seven justices reconsidered the claims that arise when negligent prenatal testing leads to the birth of a child with a severe disability. They held that the child's own "wrongful life" claim can no longer be recognised, because life with a disability cannot be treated as legal damage compared with not being born, while the parents' "wrongful birth" claim, a regular negligence claim, remains and covers the extra costs of raising the child. Why it matters: negligence is a single section of the Ordinance, yet the Supreme Court can recognise, reshape or abolish whole categories of claim within it. That is how most modern Israeli tort law is made. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/hammer-v-amit).

## How it works in practice

### Reading a set of facts the tort-lawyer's way

1. **Who was harmed, and how?** Body, property, money, reputation, privacy, comfort. The kind of harm points to the tort: bodily injury from a car is a road accident (lesson 04.6), not ordinary negligence; harm to reputation is defamation.
2. **What did the defendant do?** An intentional act (assault, conversion, deceit), a careless act or omission (negligence), use of land (nuisance), breach of a statute (s. 63).
3. **Which tort fits best?** Usually negligence; often negligence plus breach of statutory duty; sometimes a specific tort that is easier to prove (for example, s. 41א makes a dog's owner liable for bodily injury without proof of negligence).
4. **Who is responsible?** The wrongdoer, an employer (s. 13), the owner or occupier of land, the state or a public authority instead of the employee (s. 7א), an insurer that must pay (lessons 04.7, 04.8).
5. **What remedy?** Damages (s. 76), an injunction (ss. 71–74), return of goods (ss. 51, 55).
6. **Which forum and when?** Small claims for an individual's claim up to the ceiling, otherwise the magistrates' or district court; the general limitation period applies (lesson 04.5). Ceilings and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Pleading more than one tort

Lawyers routinely plead negligence and breach of statutory duty side by side, and an intentional tort together with negligence in the alternative. You may do the same in your own claim; but you can recover only once for the same damage (s. 77).

### Contract or tort?

If you have a contract with the defendant (a garage, a mover, a contractor), the same facts may also be a breach of contract (module 3). Tort is often still useful: it reaches people you had no contract with (the subcontractor, the employee), and the Ordinance gives presumptions such as res ipsa loquitur (s. 41). Section 77(ב)–(ג) prevents double recovery.

## Worked example

Rania Haddad, a teacher in Nazareth, finds that the neighbouring workshop runs a diesel generator under her bedroom window every night from 10 p.m. to 2 a.m. Last month, the workshop's forklift also backed into her garden wall and cracked it. The workshop owner says: "It's my land, I can do what I want."

Rania maps the facts:

| Fact | Harm | Possible tort | Elements to prove | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generator every night | Loss of sleep and enjoyment of her home | Private nuisance (s. 44) | Substantial interference with reasonable use and enjoyment, considering the place and its character; damage | Injunction limiting hours or requiring a silencer (s. 71); damages |
| Generator noise above limits | Same | Breach of statutory duty (s. 63), if a noise regulation protects neighbours | Duty in an enactment; meant to protect her class; breach; damage of that kind | Same |
| Forklift hits wall | Cracked wall: repair cost | Negligence (s. 35); trespass to land (s. 29) | Careless driving, duty, causation, damage; or unlawful interference with land plus pecuniary damage | Repair cost |
| Forklift driver is an employee | — | Employer's vicarious liability (s. 13) | Act in the course of employment | Sue the workshop, not only the driver |

Her conclusion: the wall claim is a straightforward property-damage claim for the repair quote (lesson 04.9 shows how to file it); the generator calls for an injunction, which small claims court cannot give, so she first writes a demand letter (lesson 04.8) and will consider the magistrates' court if it fails. "It's my land" is not a defence: nuisance is precisely about how one uses one's own land.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A harm you or your household suffered in the last few years that was not caused by a breach of contract, or the supplied case below.
- The Ordinance on Wikisource, open at chapter 3.
- 45 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Moshe Friedman, a yeshiva student in Bnei Brak, parks his bicycle in his building's lobby. A delivery company's courier, rushing, knocks it over, bends the wheel, then argues with Moshe and holds his arm for a minute so he cannot photograph the van. The building's management committee had removed the lobby's bicycle rack without notice.

### Steps

1. Write the facts in five to eight dated sentences, without adjectives.
2. List every harm: body, property, money, liberty, comfort, reputation.
3. For each harm, find the candidate torts in the table above. Open each section and copy its elements as a checklist.
4. Tick the elements you can prove and note the evidence for each (photo, receipt, witness, message).
5. Name every possible defendant and why each is responsible (wrongdoer, employer, occupier).
6. Write the remedy for each harm: an amount with a basis, an injunction, or return of goods.
7. Mark which harm is too small to pursue and which is worth a demand letter.

### Template

None. Use a table like the worked example; the [forum check worksheet](../../templates/forum-check-worksheet.md) helps with step 7.

### How you know it worked

- Every harm is matched to at least one tort with its section number.
- Every element has a piece of evidence next to it, or a note that it is missing.
- You can say in one sentence why each defendant is responsible.

### Self-check

- [ ] I used the Ordinance's own list, not a general idea of "unfair".
- [ ] I checked whether the tort needs pecuniary damage.
- [ ] I considered the employer, not only the person who acted.
- [ ] I separated body injury in a road accident from other harms.
- [ ] I noted where a contract claim might also exist.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Mapping your own dispute to the Ordinance is research in your own matter. An individual may sue in tort in their own name in the small claims, magistrates' and district courts (Civil Procedure Regulations 2018, reg. 170; small claims: Courts Law s. 63). Drafting claims or opinions for others as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- The law: the [Ordinance on Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין); the official version in the Knesset database (lesson 02.1).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut guides, Legal Aid where eligible, university clinics; see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
- Ceilings and limitation periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The harm is a bodily injury with lasting effects, or a death in the family.
- The defendant is the state, a hospital or a large insurer and liability is disputed.
- The facts may also be a crime by or against you (s. 88 lets the civil court pause the case).
- You are being sued for defamation or invasion of privacy: these have special defences and compensation rules.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing for "unfairness" without naming a tort | The claim is dismissed for failing to show a cause of action | Amend the claim to name the tort and plead its elements | Start from the Ordinance's list and copy the elements |
| Suing under trespass without pecuniary damage | No compensation even if trespass is proved | Add negligence or nuisance if they fit, or seek an injunction | Check which torts require pecuniary damage |
| Forgetting the employer | A judgment against an employee who cannot pay | Add the employer as a defendant (s. 13) | Always ask "who was he working for?" |
| Treating a road-accident injury as negligence | The negligence claim fails; the special law applies exclusively | Re-file under the road accidents law (lesson 04.6) | Ask whether a motor vehicle was in use for transport |
| Claiming the same damage twice, in contract and tort | Only one award; the rest is struck out | Choose the stronger basis or plead in the alternative | Plead alternatives, not cumulative claims |

## Review

- Israeli tort law is a closed list of torts in the Civil Wrongs Ordinance [New Version], plus statutory torts in other laws that adopt the Ordinance's rules.
- "Damage" is broad (s. 2), but several torts require pecuniary damage and its details in the claim.
- Negligence is the dominant tort; courts shape whole categories of claim within it (Hammer v. Amit).
- A statute that declares a wrong a tort brings in the Ordinance's elements, including damage and causation (Barazani).
- Map every harm to a tort, every element to evidence, every defendant to a reason, and every claim to a remedy.
