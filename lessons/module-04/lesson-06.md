# 04.6 · Road accidents: the no-fault compensation law

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell whether a bodily injury falls under Israel's no-fault road-accident law, find the insurer that must pay, spot the exclusions, and assemble the documents for a claim and for interim payments.

## Why it matters

Road accidents are the largest source of bodily-injury claims in Israel, and they are governed by a law that works differently from everything else in this module. Since 1975, a person injured in a road accident is compensated **without proving anyone's fault**, by the compulsory insurer of the vehicle, and **cannot** sue in negligence instead. Fault does not reduce the payment; contributory negligence does not exist here. In exchange, damages for pain and suffering are capped and calculated by formula.

Getting the basics wrong is costly: claiming against the wrong insurer, missing an exclusion, suing the other driver in negligence for an injury (the claim fails), or signing a settlement with a lawyer whose fee exceeds the legal cap. Property damage to cars is not covered by this law at all; that is ordinary tort and the subject of lesson 04.9.

## The law

### What a road accident is

> ”תאונת דרכים“ – מאורע שבו נגרם לאדם נזק גוף עקב שימוש ברכב מנועי למטרות תחבורה; …ואולם לא יראו כתאונת דרכים מאורע שאירע כתוצאה ממעשה שנעשה במתכוון כדי לגרום נזק לגופו או לרכושו של אותו אדם, והנזק נגרם על ידי המעשה עצמו ולא על ידי השפעתו של המעשה על השימוש ברכב המנועי;

*Gist:* A road accident is an event in which a person suffers bodily injury as a result of the use of a motor vehicle for transport purposes; an event caused by an act intended to harm that person's body or property is excluded where the act itself, not its effect on the use of the vehicle, caused the harm. ([Road Accident Victims Compensation Law 1975, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_פיצויים_לנפגעי_תאונות_דרכים))

The definition is extended to explosions or fires caused by components essential to driving, harm from a vehicle parked illegally, and some uses of the vehicle's mechanical power. "Use" includes driving, getting in and out, parking, pushing, towing and roadside repairs by the user (s. 1). A "motor vehicle" includes trains and tractors but not wheelchairs (s. 1).

### Liability without fault, and exclusivity

> המשתמש ברכב מנועי (להלן – הנוהג) חייב לפצות את הנפגע על נזק גוף שנגרם לו בתאונת דרכים שבה מעורב הרכב.

*Gist:* The user (driver) of a motor vehicle must compensate the victim for bodily injury in a road accident involving the vehicle. ([s. 2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_פיצויים_לנפגעי_תאונות_דרכים))

> האחריות היא מוחלטת ומלאה, ואין נפקא מינה אם היה או לא היה אשם מצד הנוהג ואם היה או לא היה אשם או אשם תורם של אחרים.

*Gist:* Liability is absolute and complete, regardless of anyone's fault or contributory fault. (s. 2(ד))

> מי שתאונת דרכים מקנה לו עילת תביעה על פי חוק זה… לא תהיה לו עילת תביעה על פי פקודת הנזיקין בשל נזק גוף, זולת אם נפגע בתאונה שנגרמה על־ידי אדם אחר במתכוון.

*Gist:* A person with a claim under this Law has no claim under the Civil Wrongs Ordinance for bodily injury, unless another person caused the accident intentionally. (s. 8(א))

In practice the claim is paid by the vehicle's compulsory insurer, because every vehicle must carry a policy covering liability under this Law (Motor Vehicle Insurance Ordinance ss. 2–3).

### Several vehicles

Each driver is liable for the people travelling in **his own** vehicle (s. 3(א)). A person outside the vehicles (a pedestrian, a cyclist hit by two cars) may claim from all drivers involved, jointly and severally (s. 3(ב)). Special sharing rules apply between insurers of motorcycles and other vehicles (s. 3(ג)).

### Who is excluded

Section 7 excludes, among others: a person who caused the accident intentionally; someone driving a vehicle without permission, or travelling in it knowing so; a driver without a licence for that vehicle (except a licence lapsed only for non-payment of the fee or an execution-office restriction); a person who used the vehicle to commit a felony; a driver without valid insurance, and an owner who let someone drive uninsured. Dependants of an excluded victim may still claim (s. 7ב).

### When no insurer can pay: Karnit

The fund (Karnit) compensates an entitled victim who cannot claim from an insurer because the driver is unknown, has no insurance or the insurance does not cover the use, or the insurer is in liquidation or under a special administrator (s. 12). A permitted driver who did not know and could not reasonably know the vehicle was uninsured may also claim from Karnit (s. 7א).

### Damages and interim payments

The Ordinance's rules on damages apply (s. 4(א)), with limits: lost earnings are counted only up to three times the national average wage; non-pecuniary damage (pain and suffering) is capped and calculated under regulations (the Road Accident Victims Compensation (Calculation of Compensation for Non-Pecuniary Damage) Regulations 1976); see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Within 60 days of a written demand, the insurer must make **interim payments** for medical expenses and living needs (s. 5); if it does not, you may ask the magistrates' court for an order (s. 5א). A disability degree already set under another law (for example by National Insurance) binds the court unless it allows contrary evidence for special reasons (s. 6ב).

### Lawyers' fees are capped

Section 16 caps a lawyer's success fee in road-accident claims as a percentage of the amount paid: a lower cap for settlements without court proceedings and a higher one where proceedings were held; anyone who paid more may recover the excess (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### Limitation

The Law has no special period: the general seven years of the Prescription Law apply, with the minority rule (s. 10).

## Leading cases

### רע"א 444/87 אלסוחה נ' עיזבון המנוח דוד דהאן (30.7.1990)

Combined appeals asked whether a person who suffers mental injury because a close relative was killed or badly injured may recover, both under the Ordinance and under the road-accidents Law. The Court (President Shamgar, Justices Barak and Levin) held that such damages are recoverable under both laws by the same standards: the duty generally extends to first-degree relatives (parents, children, spouses); the relative need not have witnessed the accident; injury from prolonged caring for the victim may count; but only severe and substantial mental reactions are compensated, not ordinary grief and distress. Why it matters: the road-accidents Law protects not only the person in the vehicle but, within limits, the family whose lives are shattered by the injury. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/alsoucha-v-estate-david-dehan).

## How it works in practice

### Which insurer?

| You were | Claim from | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Driver or passenger in a vehicle | The compulsory insurer of **that** vehicle (s. 3(א)) | Even if the other car was entirely to blame |
| Pedestrian or cyclist hit by one vehicle | That vehicle's compulsory insurer | |
| Pedestrian hit by two or more vehicles | Any or all of their insurers, jointly and severally (s. 3(ב)) | |
| Hit by an unknown or uninsured vehicle | Karnit (s. 12) | Police report is important evidence |
| Hit by a car while on an electric bicycle or scooter | Depends on whether your vehicle is a "motor vehicle" and insured; get advice | Fast-changing area |

### The claim file

1. Police report or confirmation that the accident was reported (essential for Karnit and useful always).
2. Details of the vehicles: licence numbers, the compulsory insurance certificate (תעודת ביטוח חובה) and insurer.
3. Medical records from the first visit onwards, discharge letters, HMO referrals.
4. Proof of income before the accident (payslips, tax returns) and of absence after.
5. Receipts for expenses: medication, travel to treatment, help at home.
6. National Insurance decisions, if you claimed a work-injury or general disability benefit.
7. A written demand for interim payments (s. 5), with copies of receipts; diary day 60.

### Property damage is different

Damage to your car in the same accident is not covered by this Law. It is an ordinary negligence claim against the other driver, usually paid by their third-party property insurance or by your own comprehensive insurance, and it **does** depend on fault. Lesson 04.9 is about exactly that claim.

## Worked example

Nadia Halabi, a pharmacist from Daliyat al-Karmel, is a passenger in her friend Rim's car when a truck runs a red light and hits them. Nadia breaks her collarbone and misses five weeks of work. The truck driver was clearly at fault.

1. **Is it a road accident?** Bodily injury from the use of a motor vehicle for transport: yes.
2. **Whom does Nadia claim from?** The compulsory insurer of **Rim's car**, the vehicle she was travelling in (s. 3(א)), not the truck's insurer, even though the truck was at fault. Who was to blame does not change whom she claims from.
3. **Exclusions?** Rim had a licence and insurance; Nadia did not know of any problem. None apply.
4. **Can she sue the truck driver in negligence?** No: s. 8 makes this Law exclusive for bodily injury.
5. **Interim payments.** She sends a written demand to Rim's insurer with medical receipts and a payslip, and diaries day 60 (s. 5).
6. **Lawyer or not?** Her injury may leave a permanent limitation of movement. She considers representation, knowing the fee is capped by s. 16, and asks any lawyer for a written fee agreement within the cap.
7. **Rim's car damage** is Rim's own property claim against the truck's insurer, under ordinary negligence (lesson 04.9).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A road accident you or a family member had, or the supplied case.
- The text of ss. 1, 2, 3, 7, 8 and 12 of the Law.
- 50 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Yoni Mizrahi, 19, from Holon, borrows his uncle's car without asking, while his uncle is abroad. A pedestrian, Esther Cohen, steps out between parked cars and is hit; Yoni is also hurt when he brakes into a pole. The uncle's compulsory insurance covers "any driver aged 24 and over".

### Steps

1. For each injured person, decide whether it is a "road accident" and why.
2. For each, name the insurer or fund that must pay, with the section.
3. Check every exclusion in s. 7 against each person and write yes or no with a reason.
4. Identify any person who must go to Karnit (s. 7א, s. 12).
5. Write the list of documents each injured person needs.
6. Draft a short written demand for interim payments (s. 5) for one injured person.
7. Note what is **not** covered: property damage, and whether any negligence claim remains.

### Template

None. A demand for interim payments is a short letter; the structure of the [demand letter to a company](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md) works with the facts and s. 5 as the legal basis.

### How you know it worked

- For the supplied case you found that Esther, a pedestrian, claims from the car's insurer regardless of fault, and her stepping out does not reduce her compensation.
- You tested Yoni against s. 7(2) (use without permission) and the policy's age condition (s. 7(5)), and saw that he is likely excluded.
- Your document list matches the claim file above.

### Self-check

- [ ] I did not use fault to decide who pays for bodily injury.
- [ ] I applied s. 3(א) to passengers and s. 3(ב) to people outside the vehicles.
- [ ] I tested every exclusion in s. 7.
- [ ] I separated property damage from bodily injury.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may claim from the insurer and, if needed, sue in your own name (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). Many victims use lawyers because medical evidence and future losses decide the amount; s. 16 caps their fee. Helping another victim with their claim for payment is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- The compulsory insurer's claims department: a written claim with documents; keep proof of delivery.
- Karnit: for unknown or uninsured vehicles; check its current procedure (⚠️ verify on gov.il or the fund's own site before filing).
- Court: magistrates' or district court by amount; interim-payment requests to the magistrates' court (s. 5א). Ceilings and caps: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The injury may be permanent, or you are unable to work for more than a few weeks.
- The accident was also a work accident and National Insurance is involved.
- The insurer argues an exclusion under s. 7 (no licence, no permission, uninsured use).
- Someone died: dependants' claims are technical.
- An insurer's representative offers a quick settlement in exchange for a full waiver.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing the at-fault driver in negligence for bodily injury | The claim fails under s. 8 | Claim under the 1975 Law from the right insurer | Ask first: was a motor vehicle used for transport? |
| Claiming from the other car's insurer as a passenger | Rejected; wrong insurer | Claim from the insurer of the vehicle you were in | Apply s. 3(א) |
| Not demanding interim payments | You finance treatment yourself for months | Send a written demand under s. 5 | Send it as soon as you have first receipts |
| Paying a lawyer above the cap | You overpay | Demand the excess back (s. 16) | Sign a written fee agreement that states the cap |
| Mixing car repairs into the bodily-injury claim | Confusion and delay | Run the property claim separately (lesson 04.9) | Keep two files from day one |

## Review

- A road accident is bodily injury from the use of a motor vehicle for transport; liability is absolute and fault is irrelevant (s. 2).
- The Law is exclusive for bodily injury: no negligence claim (s. 8), except against someone who caused the accident intentionally.
- Passengers claim from their own vehicle's insurer; people outside claim from the vehicles involved (s. 3).
- Section 7 excludes certain victims; Karnit pays when no insurer can (s. 12).
- Damages are capped for high earnings and pain and suffering; interim payments are due within 60 days of a written demand; lawyers' fees are capped (s. 16).
