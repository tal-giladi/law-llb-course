# 04.7 · Liability of the state, employers and professionals; insurance

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find the defendant who will actually pay: the employer behind the employee, the state behind the public servant, the hospital behind the doctor, and the insurer behind them all, and you will know the special limits that apply when the defendant is the state.

## Why it matters

The person who harmed you is often not the one you should sue. A bus driver, a nurse, a municipal inspector or a soldier is rarely worth suing personally, and some cannot be sued at all. The law channels liability to employers, public bodies and their insurers, which can pay and which have the information you need. Choosing the right defendant decides whether a judgment is worth anything.

Suing the state also has its own rules: public servants are personally immune for governmental acts, the state is exempt for combat actions and some other situations, and claims about security-force activity in Judea and Samaria have short notice and filing periods. Professional defendants, especially doctors, are measured by a professional standard and by the patient's right to decide, which the Supreme Court has recognised as a separate head of damage.

## The law

### Employers, principals and contractors

> (א) לענין פקודה זו יהא מעביד חב על מעשה שעשה עובד שלו –
> (1) אם הרשה או אישרר את המעשה;
> (2) אם העובד עשה את המעשה תוך כדי עבודתו;

*Gist:* An employer is liable for an employee's act it authorised or ratified, or that the employee did in the course of employment. ([Civil Wrongs Ordinance, s. 13(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_13))

An act is in the course of employment if done while performing the employee's ordinary duties, even in an improper way, but not if done for the employee's own purposes and not the employer's (s. 13(ב)). A principal is liable for an agent's acts within the agency (s. 14). A person who hires an **independent contractor** is generally not liable for the contractor's torts, unless they were negligent in choosing the contractor, interfered, authorised the act, were under a statutory duty to do the work, or the work was itself unlawful (s. 15). The employee remains personally liable too (s. 16).

### Public servants: immunity, and the state answers

> (א) לא תוגש תובענה נגד עובד ציבור על מעשה שעשה תוך כדי מילוי תפקידו השלטוני כעובד ציבור, המקים אחריות בנזיקין; הוראה זו לא תחול על מעשה כאמור שנעשה ביודעין מתוך כוונה לגרום נזק או בשוויון נפש לאפשרות גרימתו במעשה כאמור.

*Gist:* No tort claim may be brought against a public servant for an act done in performing their governmental function, unless it was done knowingly with intent to harm or with indifference to that possibility. ([s. 7א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_7א))

The immunity protects the servant, not the body: the state or the public authority remains liable (s. 7א(ב)), and procedures in ss. 7ב–7ג let the state or authority take the servant's place in the suit. "Public authority" means a local authority and the statutory corporations listed in the Ordinance's schedule (s. 7).

### The state as defendant

> דין המדינה, לענין אחריות בנזיקים, כדין כל גוף מואגד, פרט לאמור להלן בחוק זה.

*Gist:* For tort liability, the state is treated like any corporate body, except as this Law provides. ([Civil Wrongs (State Liability) Law 1952, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הנזיקים_האזרחיים_(אחריות_המדינה)))

The exceptions:

- No liability for an act within lawful authority or in reasonable good-faith belief in it, **but** liability for negligence in doing it (s. 3).
- No liability for a **combat action** of the IDF (s. 5), defined broadly to include fighting terror and hostilities and actions to prevent them that are combat in character (s. 1).
- For damage caused by the IDF in Judea, Samaria and Gaza: written notice within 60 days and a claim within two years, extendable by the court in limited cases (s. 5א).
- No liability towards nationals of an enemy state, residents of territory declared enemy territory (Gaza has been so declared since 2014), or members and activists of a terrorist organisation, except as the Law's schedule allows (s. 5ב).
- No tort liability for injury or illness of a soldier caused by military service; compensation comes through the rehabilitation laws instead (s. 6).

### Liability insurance: going straight to the insurer

> בביטוח אחריות רשאי המבטח – ולפי דרישת הצד השלישי חייב הוא – לשלם לצד השלישי את תגמולי הביטוח שהמבטח חייב למבוטח, ובלבד שהודיע על כך בכתב למבוטח 30 ימים מראש והמבוטח לא התנגד תוך תקופה זו; אולם טענה שהמבטח יכול לטעון כלפי המבוטח תעמוד לו גם כלפי הצד השלישי.

*Gist:* In liability insurance, the insurer must, at the injured third party's demand, pay the third party what it owes the insured, after giving the insured 30 days' written notice without objection; the insurer keeps its defences against the insured. ([Insurance Contract Law 1981, s. 68](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חוזה_הביטוח))

In practice you may name the wrongdoer's liability insurer (third-party property insurance for cars, a business's third-party liability policy, a building's policy) as a defendant alongside the wrongdoer.

## Leading cases

### בג"ץ 8276/05 עדאלה – המרכז המשפטי לזכויות המיעוט הערבי בישראל נ' שר הביטחון (12.12.2006)

A 2005 amendment (s. 5ג of the State Liability Law) exempted the state from liability for almost any act of the security forces in areas the Minister of Defence declared "conflict zones", not only for combat actions. **The state** argued that the security forces faced continuous hostilities in which ordinary tort rules and evidence could not work, and that the Palestinian Authority did not cooperate in investigating claims. **The petitioners** argued that the exemption denied compensation for harm with no connection to combat, such as abuse of detainees or damage to property far from any fighting. A panel of nine (President Barak writing) held s. 5ג unconstitutional because it exempted the state for torts unrelated to combat; whether damage results from a combat action must be decided claim by claim. In 2012 the Knesset amended the Law again, widening the definition of "combat action" and adding s. 5ב (enemy territory and terrorist organisations); the current text should be read as it stands, and its application is still litigated. Why it matters: it shows that the state's immunity from tort is a matter of statute, and that the Basic Laws limit how far that statute may go. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/adalah-legal-center-arab-minority-rights-israel-v-minister-defense).

### ע"א 2781/93 דעקה נ' בית החולים "כרמל", חיפה (29.8.1999)

A patient admitted for an operation on her leg was asked, after receiving sedatives on the operating table, to sign consent to a biopsy on her shoulder; the biopsy left her shoulder stiff. The Court found no negligence in the decision or the procedure, but found that her informed consent was not obtained. Because she would probably have agreed had she been properly informed, there was no causal link to the physical harm; but the Court recognised **violation of autonomy** as a separate head of damage and compensated her for it. Why it matters: a professional's duty includes respecting the client's right to decide with full information, and breaching it is compensable even when the outcome would have been the same. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/daaka-v-carmel-hospital).

### Employer and employee together

In ע"א 4530/91 רשות הנמלים והרכבות נ' צים (lesson 04.5), the port authority answered vicariously for its pilot. That is the ordinary pattern: sue the employer, which answers for its employee and is usually insured.

## How it works in practice

### Finding the paying defendant

| Who harmed you | Sue | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An employee at work (driver, cleaner, guard) | The employer, and the employee if you wish | s. 13 |
| A subcontractor's worker on a building site | The subcontractor; the main contractor or owner if an exception in s. 15 applies | s. 15 |
| A municipal worker acting in their governmental role | The municipality, not the worker | s. 7א |
| A police officer, soldier or civil servant | The state (through the State Attorney's civil department) | s. 7א; State Liability Law |
| A doctor in a hospital | The hospital (and its insurer); the doctor if private | s. 13; s. 35 professional standard |
| A driver who damaged your car | The driver and their third-party property insurer | s. 68 Insurance Contract Law |

### Suing the state in practice

- The defendant is "מדינת ישראל", usually with the ministry named (for example "משטרת ישראל"); it is represented by the State Attorney's office or a district attorney's civil department.
- The state may also pay certain claims ex gratia through a committee set up under regulations to the State Liability Law (⚠️ check current procedure).
- Check the s. 5א notice period at once if the harm occurred in Judea, Samaria or Gaza.

### Professional defendants

The standard is the reasonable member of the profession (s. 35): the reasonable doctor, lawyer, accountant, engineer. Expert evidence is almost always needed. In medical cases, ask for your full medical file in writing first (the Patient's Rights Law gives you a right to it; detail in a later year), and separate two questions: was the treatment careless, and was your informed consent obtained?

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## Worked example

Rivka Goldstein, from Jerusalem's Ramot neighbourhood, parks her car near a construction site. A worker employed by a scaffolding subcontractor drops a steel pole from the third floor and smashes her windscreen. The main contractor's sign on the fence names the developer and the contractor.

1. **Direct wrongdoer:** the worker; he is liable but not worth suing alone.
2. **Employer:** the scaffolding subcontractor, liable under s. 13 for an act in the course of work.
3. **Main contractor:** generally not liable for an independent contractor (s. 15), but safety-at-work regulations put duties on the site's contractor; if the duty to secure the area is statutory, s. 15(4) and breach of statutory duty (s. 63) may bring it in.
4. **Insurers:** construction sites usually carry third-party liability insurance (a "contractor's all risks" policy); Rivka asks the contractor in writing for the insurer's details and, under s. 68, may claim from it directly.
5. **Claim:** windscreen and a day's car rental, with quotes and receipts; small claims, naming the subcontractor and the main contractor (lesson 04.9).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An incident where the person who harmed you worked for someone, or the supplied case.
- The text of ss. 7א, 13–16 of the Ordinance and s. 68 of the Insurance Contract Law.
- 45 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Omar Khatib, a student from Umm al-Fahm, is pushed by a municipal parking inspector during an argument over a ticket and falls, breaking his phone and bruising his arm. A week later, a private physiotherapist treating the arm uses an electrical device without explaining its risks; Omar suffers a small burn.

### Steps

1. List each person who harmed you and the act.
2. For each, identify their employer or principal and whether the act was in the course of employment (s. 13(ב)).
3. Check whether the person is a public servant performing a governmental function (s. 7א) and whether the exception for intentional harm might apply.
4. For any professional, write the professional standard and, separately, the informed-consent question.
5. For each defendant, find out whether it is insured and how to reach the insurer.
6. Write the final list of defendants and why each is responsible.
7. Note any special notice or time limit.

### Template

None. A short table (person, employer, legal basis, insurer) is enough.

### How you know it worked

- For the supplied case you named the municipality, not the inspector alone, unless the push was intentional harm outside the immunity.
- You separated the physiotherapist's treatment quality from the failure to explain risks (Daaka).
- Every defendant on your list has a legal basis and a likely payer.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, in your own name against an employer, a company, a municipality or the state; the state and authorities will be represented by lawyers (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). Claims against hospitals and professionals are allowed but usually need expert opinions.

### Where and how

- Small claims for property damage and small injuries within the ceiling; magistrates' or district court above it. [Current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Claims against the state: statement of claim naming "מדינת ישראל" and the ministry; served on the State Attorney's office as the court directs.
- Free help: Legal Aid in some matters; university clinics; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The claim is against the state or security forces and may involve s. 5, 5א or 5ב.
- It is a medical-negligence claim: you need an expert opinion to file.
- A professional's insurer denies cover and you must decide whom to sue.
- You are a public servant being sued personally: ask your employer to apply s. 7ב or 7ג first.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing only the employee | Judgment you cannot collect; immunity for public servants | Add the employer, authority or state | Always ask "who employs this person?" |
| Suing the main contractor for a subcontractor's act with no s. 15 exception | Claim against it dismissed with costs | Drop it or plead the exception with facts | Check s. 15(1)–(5) before naming |
| Missing the s. 5א notice for damage in the Area | The court may refuse to hear the claim | Ask the court to extend for special reasons | Send written notice within 60 days |
| Medical claim without an expert opinion | Struck out or lost | Obtain an opinion; ask the court for time | Get the medical file and an opinion before filing |
| Not asking for the insurer's details | You chase an uninsured individual | Demand them in writing; use s. 68 | Ask for the insurer in the first letter |

## Review

- Employers answer for employees' acts in the course of employment (s. 13); hiring an independent contractor is generally not a basis for liability, with five exceptions (s. 15).
- Public servants are immune for governmental acts unless they acted with intent or indifference to harm; the state or authority answers instead (s. 7א).
- The state is liable like a corporation, except for combat actions, for some claims by enemy nationals and terror operatives, and soldiers' service injuries; claims about the Area have short notice periods.
- Professionals are judged by the standard of their profession; failure to obtain informed consent is compensable as violation of autonomy (Daaka).
- A liability insurer must pay the injured third party at their demand (Insurance Contract Law s. 68).
