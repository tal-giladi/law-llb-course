# 04.4 · Specific torts: trespass, nuisance, false imprisonment, breach of statutory duty

By the end of this lesson you will be able to recognise the named torts other than negligence that ordinary people meet most often, prove their elements, anticipate their special defences, and use breach of statutory duty to turn a safety or consumer rule into a claim for compensation.

## Why it matters

Negligence asks whether someone was careless. The specific torts ask narrower questions, and that can make them easier to win. A neighbour's nightly noise is a nuisance whether or not he is careless. Being held in a shop's back room is false imprisonment unless the shop shows lawful authority. A restaurant that lets customers smoke breaks a statute that was written to protect you, and s. 63 lets you sue for that breach.

These torts also bring remedies negligence rarely does: an injunction to stop the noise, an order to return your goods. Knowing which tort fits tells you which court to go to, because small claims court can order payment but cannot stop a nuisance.

## The law

### Trespass to land

> הסגת גבול במקרקעין היא כניסה למקרקעין שלא כדין, או היזק או הפרעה בידי אדם למקרקעין שלא כדין; אך אין תובע יכול להיפרע פיצויים על הסגת גבול במקרקעין אלא אם סבל על ידי כך נזק ממון.

*Gist:* Trespass to land is unlawful entry, damage or interference; compensation requires pecuniary damage. ([Civil Wrongs Ordinance, s. 29](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_29))

Once you prove the entry or interference, the defendant must prove it was lawful (s. 30). A mistake about ownership, even in good faith, is no justification (s. 33). Trespass to goods (s. 31) works the same way.

### Private nuisance

> (א) מיטרד ליחיד הוא כשאדם מתנהג בעצמו או מנהל את עסקו או משתמש במקרקעין התפושים בידו באופן שיש בו הפרעה של ממש לשימוש סביר במקרקעין של אדם אחר או להנאה סבירה מהם בהתחשב עם מקומם וטיבם; אך לא ייפרע אדם פיצויים בעד מיטרד ליחיד אלא אם סבל ממנו נזק.

*Gist:* Private nuisance is conduct, a business or a use of land that substantially interferes with the reasonable use or enjoyment of another's land, judged by the place and its character; damages require damage. ([s. 44](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_44))

"Of the place and its character" matters: noise expected next to a port is not the noise expected in a quiet residential street. Coming to the nuisance is no defence (s. 46); an agreement binding you is (s. 45). A use needed for the public good is not a nuisance if the harm is within the tolerable and reasonable steps were taken, though pecuniary damage may still be compensated (s. 48ב). Public nuisance (s. 42) can be sued by a private person only if they suffered pecuniary damage; otherwise it is for the Attorney General (s. 43).

### Assault and false imprisonment

> כליאת שוא היא שלילת חירותו של אדם, שלילה מוחלטת ושלא כדין, למשך זמן כלשהו, באמצעים פיסיים או על ידי הופעה כבעל סמכות.

*Gist:* False imprisonment is a total, unlawful deprivation of a person's liberty for any length of time, by physical means or by appearing to have authority. ([s. 26](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_26))

Assault (s. 23) is intentional use of force against a person without consent, or a threat of it that the victim reasonably believes. Both are actionable without proof of damage. The defences are specific: reasonable self-defence or defence of others, reasonable force to remove a trespasser after asking them to leave, acting under a valid warrant, and a few more (ss. 24, 27). "Total" deprivation means there is no reasonable way out; blocking one door of a room with an open second door is not imprisonment.

### Conversion and detention of goods

Taking, keeping, destroying or handing to a third party goods that someone else is entitled to possess is conversion (s. 52); refusing to return them is unlawful detention (s. 49). The court may order the goods returned (ss. 51, 55). A buyer in good faith under s. 34 of the Sale Law has a defence (s. 53).

### Breach of statutory duty

> (א) מפר חובה חקוקה הוא מי שאינו מקיים חובה המוטלת עליו על פי כל חיקוק – למעט פקודה זו – והחיקוק, לפי פירושו הנכון, נועד לטובתו או להגנתו של אדם אחר, וההפרה גרמה לאותו אדם נזק מסוגו או מטבעו של הנזק שאליו נתכוון החיקוק; אולם אין האדם האחר זכאי בשל ההפרה לתרופה המפורשת בפקודה זו, אם החיקוק, לפי פירושו הנכון, התכוון להוציא תרופה זו.

*Gist:* Anyone who fails to perform a duty imposed by any enactment (other than the Ordinance) that was meant to benefit or protect another person, causing that person damage of the kind the enactment meant to prevent, commits a tort, unless the enactment meant to exclude this remedy. ([s. 63](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_63))

Courts break s. 63 into five elements: (1) a duty imposed by an enactment on the defendant; (2) the enactment was meant to protect the plaintiff or a class to which they belong (s. 63(ב)); (3) the defendant breached it; (4) the breach caused damage; (5) the damage is of the kind the enactment meant to prevent. Typical enactments: work-safety regulations, building and planning safety rules, road traffic regulations, the smoking restrictions, consumer protection rules.

### Pointers: defamation and privacy

Defamation is governed by the [Prohibition of Defamation Law 1965](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_איסור_לשון_הרע): a publication that may humiliate a person or make them a target of hatred or contempt (s. 1), with defences of truth in the public interest and good faith (ss. 14–15), and compensation without proof of damage up to a statutory sum (s. 7א; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Invasion of privacy is a civil wrong under the [Privacy Protection Law 1981](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות), s. 4. Both have their own rules and are covered in a later year; get advice before suing or answering a claim under them.

## Leading cases

### ע"א 176/53 עטיה נ' רוזנבאום (24.9.1954)

A worker's hand was caught while cleaning a wool-teasing machine. The trial court dismissed his claim because the employer had fitted the usual guard. The Supreme Court (Justices Goitein, Landau and Berinson) allowed the appeal: the Factories Ordinance's duty to fence dangerous machinery securely was absolute, and following the common practice did not discharge it if the worker was in fact not protected. Why it matters: under s. 63 the question is whether the statutory duty was performed, not whether the defendant was careless by general standards. A strict safety statute can win a case negligence would lose. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/atia-v-rosenbaum).

### רע"א 9615/05 שמש נ' פוקצ'טה בע"מ (5.7.2006)

A pregnant woman dining with her children complained that another customer was smoking; the restaurant did nothing. Her small claim succeeded on breach of statutory duty under the Restriction of Smoking in Public Places Law, but she received only the price of the meal. Justice Rubinstein, sitting alone on a further appeal, held that the law protects the health of people in public places, that civil suits are an important way to enforce it given weak public enforcement, and that damages should reflect that purpose; he added compensation. Why it matters: breach of statutory duty is a practical tool in small claims, and courts may consider deterrence when assessing non-pecuniary harm in such cases. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/shemesh-v-focaccetta-ltd).

## How it works in practice

### Which tort, which remedy, which court

| Situation | Best tort | Remedy | Court |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbour's nightly noise, smells, smoke | Private nuisance (s. 44); breach of statutory duty if a regulation is broken | Injunction plus damages | Magistrates' court (small claims cannot give an injunction) |
| Neighbour builds a fence 30 cm into your land | Trespass to land (s. 29) | Removal order; damages for pecuniary loss | Magistrates' court; land disputes may involve the land registry and the condominium superintendent |
| Shop guard holds you in a back room | False imprisonment (s. 26); assault if touched (s. 23) | Damages | Small claims or magistrates' court |
| Ex-partner keeps your belongings | Conversion or detention (ss. 49, 52) | Return of the goods or their value | Small claims for value; magistrates' court for return |
| Restaurant allows smoking; unsafe workplace; missing fence at a construction site | Breach of statutory duty (s. 63), with negligence | Damages | Small claims or above |

Small claims court can award money and order replacement or repair of a product or cancellation of a transaction (Courts Law s. 60), but it cannot order a neighbour to stop a nuisance or remove a fence.

### Proving the specific torts

- **Nuisance:** a noise or smell log with dates and times, recordings, other neighbours' statements, and if possible a measurement by the municipality's environmental unit.
- **Trespass:** a survey map (מפת מדידה) or the registered plot boundaries.
- **False imprisonment:** the time, who stopped you, what they said, any witnesses or CCTV; write it down the same day.
- **Statutory duty:** the exact regulation, its text, and why it protects people like you.

## Worked example

Ahmad Masarwa, an engineer from Taibe, is stopped at a supermarket exit by a guard who says he saw him pocket batteries. The guard takes his arm, walks him to a back room, closes the door and tells him he cannot leave until the manager arrives. Forty minutes later the manager checks the CCTV, sees no theft and apologises. In front of other shoppers, the guard had called out "גנב".

| Element | Facts | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| False imprisonment: total deprivation of liberty | Closed door, told he could not leave, 40 minutes | His written account the same day; receipt with time; request for CCTV |
| Unlawful | No theft; no statutory authority shown for detention | The manager's apology (keep the message) |
| Assault | Guard took his arm without consent | Same; any witness |
| Employer's liability | Guard employed by the supermarket or a security contractor | Name on uniform; ask which company |
| Defamation (pointer) | Called "thief" in front of customers | Witness names; this is a separate statute with its own rules |

Ahmad's remedies: damages for false imprisonment and assault, which do not require proof of financial loss. He sends a demand letter to the supermarket chain (lesson 04.8) and asks it to preserve the CCTV recording. If the guard was employed by a security contractor, the supermarket may argue it is not responsible (s. 15, independent contractors); Ahmad names both companies.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A situation of your own involving a neighbour, a shop, a building site, an employer or a business, or the supplied case.
- The Ordinance and, for breach of statutory duty, the text of the relevant law or regulation.
- 45 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Miriam Levi, who lives on the second floor in Haifa, has a ground-floor neighbour who opened a falafel stand in his front yard. The fryer's extractor blows directly into her bedroom window from 11 a.m. to midnight. The stand has no business licence. Last week his worker also took Miriam's bicycle from the shared entrance "to make room" and has not returned it.

### Steps

1. List each wrong separately.
2. For each, write the tort and section, and copy its elements as a checklist.
3. For each element, note the evidence you have and what you must still collect.
4. For any statutory duty, find the enactment, quote the duty, and write one sentence on why it protects people like you.
5. Choose the remedy for each wrong: money, an injunction, return of goods.
6. Choose the court for each remedy (small claims or magistrates').
7. Write a one-paragraph plan: what you will send first, and to whom.

### Template

None. The [forum check worksheet](../../templates/forum-check-worksheet.md) helps with step 6.

### How you know it worked

- Each wrong has a tort, a section, elements with evidence, and a remedy.
- You have spotted that an injunction against the extractor cannot come from small claims court.
- For the bicycle, you chose between asking for its value (small claims) or its return.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, in your own name: small claims for money and, in the magistrates' court, claims for injunctions and return of goods (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170). An injunction claim in the magistrates' court involves more procedure; module 8 covers it.

### Where and how

- Before suing over a nuisance: the municipality's environmental or licensing unit, and the police for night-time noise; their reports are evidence.
- Shared-property disputes in a registered condominium may belong to the condominium superintendent (מפקח על בתים משותפים); covered in year 2.
- Small claims ceiling and fees: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); filing is lesson 04.9.
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need an urgent temporary injunction (s. 73): it requires an affidavit and, often, a guarantee.
- The dispute concerns land boundaries or registered rights.
- You are accused of assault or false imprisonment yourself, or the facts are also a criminal complaint (module 5).
- You want to sue for defamation or invasion of privacy, or are sued under them.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking small claims court to stop a nuisance | The court cannot give the injunction | Sue for damages there, or file in the magistrates' court | Match the remedy to the court before filing |
| Nuisance claim with no log or measurement | "Ordinary noise of a city" defence succeeds | Start a dated log and request an inspection | Keep a log for at least two weeks before acting |
| Breach of statutory duty without the text of the duty | The court cannot see who the law protects | Quote the section and explain its purpose | Find and quote the enactment before writing |
| Using force to recover your goods | You may commit assault yourself | Stop; demand in writing, then sue | Use s. 24's conditions strictly: ask first, minimal force, or better, none |
| Suing for defamation on impulse | Costs, counterclaims, and publicity | Take advice; consider a demand for correction | Treat defamation and privacy as specialist areas |

## Review

- Trespass and conversion protect possession; trespass needs pecuniary damage for compensation, and the defendant must prove lawfulness.
- Private nuisance is substantial interference judged by the place and its character; coming to the nuisance is no defence.
- False imprisonment and assault are actionable without damage; their defences are specific and narrow.
- Breach of statutory duty has five elements and can succeed where negligence would not (Atia v. Rosenbaum); it is a practical small-claims tool (Shemesh v. Focaccetta).
- Choose the remedy first: small claims gives money, not injunctions.
