# 04.5 · Defences, contributory negligence and limitation

By the end of this lesson you will be able to predict the defences a defendant will raise, estimate how far your own carelessness will reduce your damages, and calculate the last day on which you can sue.

## Why it matters

Every tort claim meets a defence. Insurers and defendants rarely argue "we did nothing wrong" alone; they argue "you took the risk", "you were careless too", "the storm did it", "you signed a waiver", and, most often in claims by self-represented people, "it is too late". Contributory fault is the commonest reason a strong claim pays only part of the damage, and limitation is the commonest reason a good claim pays nothing.

Knowing the defences also matters when you are the defendant: if your child, your dog or your car is involved, these are your arguments.

## The law

### Defences in the Ordinance

> (א) בתובענה שהוגשה על עוולה תהא הגנה שהתובע ידע והעריך, או יש להניח שידע והעריך, את מצב הדברים שגרמו לנזק וכי חשף עצמו או רכושו למצב זה מרצונו.

*Gist:* It is a defence that the plaintiff knew and appreciated the situation that caused the damage and voluntarily exposed himself or his property to it. ([Civil Wrongs Ordinance, s. 5(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_5))

The defence does not apply to breach of statutory duty (s. 5(ב)), and a child under 12 cannot assume a risk (s. 5(ג)). Courts read it narrowly: knowing a risk exists is not agreeing to bear its legal consequences, so in practice it rarely succeeds and the argument usually becomes contributory fault.

Other defences:

- **Trivial acts** are not torts (s. 4).
- **Statutory authority:** an act done under an enactment, or within lawful authority, or in reasonable good-faith belief in it, is a defence to every tort *except negligence* (s. 6).
- **Self-defence and protection of property:** reasonable and proportionate force (s. 24).
- **Minors:** no suit for a tort committed under the age of 12 (s. 9(א)); parents may still be liable for their own negligence in supervision.
- **Unusual natural event** or **another's decisive fault:** the fault is not a cause (s. 64(1)–(2), lesson 04.3).

### Contributory fault

> (א) סבל אדם נזק, מקצתו עקב אשמו שלו ומקצתו עקב אשמו של אחר, לא תיכשל תביעת פיצויים בעד הנזק מחמת אשמו של הניזוק, אלא שהפיצויים שייפָּרעו יופחתו בשיעור שבית המשפט ימצא לנכון ולצודק תוך התחשבות במידת אחריותו של התובע לנזק;

*Gist:* A plaintiff partly at fault still recovers, but damages are reduced by the share the court finds just in view of the plaintiff's responsibility. ([s. 68(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנזיקין#סעיף_68))

The court must record the full amount before the reduction (s. 68(ב)). "Fault" includes carelessness towards oneself (s. 64), and a person's fault includes that of someone they are responsible for, such as their employee (s. 67). Between defendants, each may claim contribution from the others in the proportion the court finds just (s. 84). The plaintiff may sue all of them for the whole (s. 11).

### Limitation

> התקופה שבה מתיישנת תביעה שלא הוגשה עליה תובענה (להלן – תקופת ההתיישנות) היא –
> (1) בשאינו מקרקעין – שבע שנים;

*Gist:* A claim not concerning land is time-barred after seven years. ([Prescription Law 1958, s. 5(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ההתיישנות#סעיף_5); see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

The period runs from the day the cause of action arose (s. 6). For torts, s. 89 of the Ordinance defines that day: the day of the act or omission (or the day it stopped, if continuing), or, where the cause is damage, the day the damage occurred; damage not discovered when it occurred counts from discovery, but the claim is barred in any case ten years after the damage occurred.

> נעלמו מן התובע העובדות המהוות את עילת התובענה, מסיבות שלא היו תלויות בו ושאף בזהירות סבירה לא יכול היה למנוע אותן, תתחיל תקופת ההתיישנות ביום שבו נודעו לתובע עובדות אלה.

*Gist:* If the facts of the claim were unknown to the plaintiff for reasons beyond his control, which reasonable care could not have prevented, the period starts when he learned them. ([Prescription Law, s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ההתיישנות#סעיף_8))

Further rules: time while the plaintiff is under 18 is not counted (s. 10); a written acknowledgement of the claim, or partial performance, restarts the period (s. 9); the period is suspended while the defendant knowingly misleads, threatens or exploits the plaintiff's distress to keep him from suing (s. 7). Limitation is not automatic: the defendant must raise it at the first opportunity (s. 3), and it bars the court from hearing the claim without cancelling the right itself (s. 2). Insurance claims have their own, shorter period (lesson 04.8).

## Leading cases

### רע"א 1272/05 כרמי נ' סבג (2.12.2007)

A man suffering from mental illness attacked and seriously wounded an eight-year-old boy; in the criminal case he was found not responsible because of his illness. The boy sued him in tort. The Court (Justice Rubinstein, with Justices Naor and Arbel) held that tort law has no insanity defence in the criminal-law sense; the only defence is lack of control over one's actions, as in automatism. Why it matters: defences do not move automatically from criminal to civil law, because tort law's purpose is compensating the victim, not blaming the wrongdoer. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/carmi-v-sabag-0).

### ע"א 4530/91 רשות הנמלים והרכבות נ' צים חברת השיט הישראלית בע"מ (10.10.2000)

A ship was damaged during pilotage in Haifa port by the joint fault of the port's pilot and the ship's captain. The Court (Justice Zamir, for a panel of five) held the port authority vicariously liable for its pilot, and the shipowner answerable for its captain's contributory fault, reducing its recovery to half. Why it matters: contributory fault is apportioned by responsibility, and an employer both answers for its employee's fault towards others and bears that fault when it is the plaintiff (s. 67). English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ports-and-railways-authority-v-zim-integrated-shipping-services-ltd).

### ע"א 461/62 צים חברת השיט הישראלית בע"מ נ' מזיאר (26.6.1963)

A passenger fell ill from food on a ship; the ticket contained a clause exempting the company from liability for negligence. The Court held the clause void as contrary to public policy for personal injury, drawing on Jewish law's high value for life and health. Why it matters: an exemption clause is a contract defence (s. 68(א) keeps such defences), but courts will not let a business contract out of liability for bodily harm through a printed form; today the Standard Contracts Law and consumer law (module 3) do much of this work. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/zim-israel-navigation-co-v-maziar).

## How it works in practice

### Estimating contributory fault

Courts do not use a formula; they compare the parties' blameworthiness and the causal weight of each fault. Some patterns from practice, as rough orientation only:

- A pedestrian looking at a phone while walking into a clearly visible hazard: a meaningful reduction.
- A worker who ignored a safety instruction: often a modest reduction, because the employer's duty is to protect workers also from their own inattention.
- A child under 12: no contributory fault in the usual way, and the defendant cannot rely on the child's assumption of risk.

When you calculate your claim, decide honestly what a court might deduct and use that range in settlement talks.

### Computing the limitation date

1. Find the start date: the act, the damage, or discovery (Ordinance s. 89; Prescription Law s. 8).
2. Add seven years (non-land claims).
3. Add any time the plaintiff was under 18.
4. Check for a written acknowledgement or partial payment that restarted the period.
5. Diary a target filing date well before the last day, and a reminder months earlier.

## Worked example

Daniel Shapiro, 16, from Kiryat Arba, was hit on his bicycle in 2021 by a falling branch from a tree in a privately managed park; he was riding on a path marked "no bicycles". His fracture healed; in 2025 an orthopaedist found a growth-plate injury from the accident that will need surgery.

| Question | Analysis |
|---|---|
| Defence: assumption of risk? | Riding on a "no bicycles" path is not knowingly accepting the risk of a falling branch; s. 5 is unlikely to succeed. |
| Contributory fault? | Arguable: the sign may have been there to prevent exactly this kind of accident, or for other reasons (pedestrian safety). If it was unrelated to falling branches, a court may give little weight to it. |
| Unusual natural event? | A branch falling in a mild wind is not unusual; a storm of record strength might be. |
| Limitation start | The accident (2021) for the original injury; the growth-plate damage discovered in 2025 may count from discovery (s. 89(2)), within the ten-year cap. |
| Minority | Daniel was 16; the years until he turns 18 are not counted (s. 10). The seven years run from his 18th birthday, at the earliest. |
| Acknowledgement | The park's insurer wrote in 2022 "we accept the incident in principle"; if that is an acknowledgement of the right (s. 9), the period restarted. |

Conclusion: the claim is not close to being time-barred, but it involves bodily injury and future surgery; Daniel and his parents should take advice before negotiating.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your incident from lessons 04.2–04.3, or Daniel's case above.
- A calendar and the Prescription Law.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. List every defence the other side could raise, with the section. Write each as one sentence in their voice.
2. For each, write your reply and the evidence behind it.
3. Estimate a range for contributory fault (for example, 0–20%) and the reason.
4. Recalculate your damages table from lesson 04.3 at the top and bottom of that range.
5. Compute the limitation date step by step and write each step down.
6. Check for any document from the other side that might be an acknowledgement.
7. Put the limitation date and a filing target in your diary.

### Template

None. Write the defences and replies as a two-column table under your damages table.

### How you know it worked

- Every defence has a section number and your reply.
- Your damages show a range, not a single figure.
- The limitation date is computed with each rule applied in order, and diarised.

### Self-check

- [ ] I treated assumption of risk as narrow and moved the argument to contributory fault where it belongs.
- [ ] I checked minority and discovery for the start of limitation.
- [ ] I looked for an acknowledgement in emails and letters from the defendant or its insurer.
- [ ] I know insurance claims have a different period (lesson 04.8).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Assessing defences and computing limitation is part of preparing your own claim; as a defendant you may raise these defences in your own statement of defence (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170).

### Where and how

- Limitation periods and the small-claims ceiling: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- If you are sued, raise limitation in your first defence document (Prescription Law s. 3), or it is lost.
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The limitation date is near and you have not yet filed: a lawyer can file a holding claim quickly.
- The claim involves a minor's bodily injury or future surgery.
- The defendant relies on a signed waiver or an exemption clause in a significant claim.
- You are being sued and the other side claims you acknowledged the debt.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the insurer's answer while the period runs | The claim becomes time-barred | File before the last day; negotiation can continue | Diary the limitation date on day one |
| Forgetting to raise limitation as a defendant | The defence is lost (s. 3) | Raise it at once if any opportunity remains | Check limitation before writing any defence |
| Claiming 100% when your own fault is obvious | Credibility suffers and settlement stalls | Offer a realistic reduction in negotiation | Estimate your share honestly from the start |
| Assuming a signed waiver ends the claim | You abandon a valid claim | Check public policy and standard-contract rules | Read lesson 03.6 on standard contracts |
| Assuming the period runs from the accident when damage appeared later | You think it is too late when it is not | Apply s. 89(2) and s. 8 | Separate act, damage and discovery dates |

## Review

- Assumption of risk (s. 5) is narrow; statutory authority (s. 6) does not cover negligence; there is no insanity defence in tort (Carmi v. Sabag).
- Contributory fault reduces damages by the plaintiff's share of responsibility (s. 68) and includes the fault of those the plaintiff answers for (s. 67; Ports v. Zim).
- Exemption clauses against bodily harm meet public policy limits (Zim v. Maziar) and standard-contract law.
- Most tort claims are barred after seven years from the act or the damage; discovery, minority and acknowledgement change the date.
- Limitation must be raised by the defendant at the first opportunity.
