# 08.5 · Administrative procedure in brief: petitions and appeals

When a public authority decides against you, the route to a judge runs through administrative procedure: a prior demand to the authority, then usually the Administrative Affairs Court, and only in residual cases the High Court of Justice. This lesson gives you the procedural map, the time limits and the contents of a petition, building on lesson [06.8](../module-06/lesson-08.md), which taught the HCJ petition and when the Administrative Affairs Court comes first.

## Why it matters

Most disputes with the State are not HCJ cases. They are decisions of a municipality on arnona or a business licence, of the Population Authority on a visa, of a planning committee on a permit, of a ministry on a tender or a freedom-of-information request. The Administrative Affairs Courts Law 2000 moved most of these to the district courts sitting as Administrative Affairs Courts, with the same grounds of review and remedies as the HCJ (s. 8). Many other decisions have a statutory appeal to a committee or a court instead.

Administrative procedure is also faster and stricter than civil procedure. A petition must usually be filed within weeks, not years; a court can dismiss even a timely petition for delay; and a petition filed without first asking the authority to reconsider is likely to be struck out. A self-represented petitioner who knows these three rules avoids most of the losses that happen before the merits are reached.

Academically, the 2000 law reflects a long debate about the HCJ's workload and the proper level of court for routine administrative disputes. Moving cases to district courts brought review closer to the citizen and allowed evidence to be heard, but it also created an extra layer of appeal and boundary disputes over what is in the law's schedules.

## The law

### What the court hears: s. 5

> בית משפט לענינים מינהליים ידון באלה – (1) עתירה נגד החלטה של רשות או של גוף המנוי בתוספת הראשונה בענין המנוי בתוספת הראשונה…; (2) ערעור המנוי בתוספת השניה…; (3) תובענה המנויה בתוספת השלישית…

*Gist:* The Administrative Affairs Court hears petitions against decisions on matters listed in the First Schedule (not petitions whose main remedy concerns making or cancelling regulations), appeals listed in the Second Schedule, claims listed in the Third Schedule, and anything another law assigns to it. ([Administrative Affairs Courts Law s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

### Grounds and remedies: s. 8

The court decides petitions and appeals "according to the grounds, powers and remedies" of the HCJ, with necessary changes. *Gist:* the doctrines of module 6 (authority, reasonableness, proportionality, natural justice) apply here. ([s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

### Appeals to the Supreme Court: ss. 11–12

A judgment in an administrative petition or claim may be appealed to the Supreme Court as of right (s. 11). A judgment in an administrative appeal, and listed interim decisions (interim orders, privilege, jurisdiction, fees and others), need leave of a Supreme Court justice (s. 12). ([ss. 11–12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים)) These appeals carry the prefix עע״מ (or בר״ם for leave).

### Time limits: regs 3, 4 and 23

> (ב) לא נקבע מועד כאמור, תוגש העתירה בלא שיהוי, לפי נסיבות הענין, ולא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום שההחלטה פורסמה כדין, או מיום שהעותר קיבל הודעה עליה או מיום שנודע לעותר עליה, לפי המוקדם.

*Gist:* Unless a law sets another period, a petition is filed without delay and no later than the set period from the earliest of publication, notice to the petitioner, or the petitioner learning of the decision; the court may extend after hearing the respondent. ([reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין)); period in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

> בית המשפט רשאי לדחות עתירה אם ראה כי בנסיבות הענין היה שיהוי בהגשתה, אף אם הוגשה בתוך אחד המועדים לפי תקנה 3.

*Gist:* Even a petition filed within the period can be dismissed for delay. ([reg. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין))) An administrative appeal with no period set by its law is filed within the default period (reg. 23; see current numbers).

### Contents of a petition: reg. 5

A petition states the petitioner's and respondents' details and each respondent's interest; the decision and the date it was published, notified or learned of; the source of the court's subject-matter and territorial jurisdiction, including the enabling provision and the schedule item; the petitioner's interest; the factual and legal grounds; the remedy; the fee and fee item; and any related proceeding. It must also include:

> (6) נעשתה פניה מוקדמת בכתב לרשות – יצורפו העתקים של ההתכתבות לענין זה; לא נעשתה פניה מוקדמת – הנימוקים להגשת העתירה בלא פניה מוקדמת;

*Gist:* attach the written prior demand and the correspondence, or explain why there was none. The petition follows Form 1, and its facts are verified by an affidavit in Form 2 that separates personal knowledge from belief and states its source (reg. 5(ג)). Attach the decision and the relevant documents (reg. 5(ד)). ([reg. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין)))

### After filing: regs 8 and 10

The court may hold a preliminary hearing and, at that stage, strike out or dismiss the petition, or with the respondent's consent decide it as if an answer had been filed (reg. 8). If it orders an answer, the respondent has a set period (reg. 10). Interim orders follow reg. 9.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 9029/16 אבירם נ׳ שרת המשפטים (1.2.2017)

Recap from lesson 06.8. Self-represented petitioners lost on delay: the Court weighed subjective delay (no good reason for waiting), objective delay (others relied on the situation in the meantime) and whether dismissal would seriously harm the rule of law. Why it matters here: the same three-part delay test is applied in the Administrative Affairs Court under reg. 4, often more strictly, because its periods are short. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/70)

### עע״מ 3908/11 מדינת ישראל – הנהלת בתי המשפט נ׳ דה מרקר – עיתון הארץ (22.9.2014)

A newspaper's FOI request for data on pending cases by judge was refused in part; the Administrative Affairs Court ordered disclosure, and the State appealed. An expanded bench of the Supreme Court, by majority, dismissed the appeal, holding that the public's right to information about the courts outweighed the claimed harm to their functioning. Why it matters: it shows the full route of administrative procedure, from a refused request through an administrative petition (FOI Law s. 17) to an appeal as of right under s. 11, and that the same rules bind even the courts' own administration. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/371)

### Key text: the prior demand

Lesson 06.8 cites Rosilio's 2024 article on why courts require a prior demand and when they excuse it. In the Administrative Affairs Court the requirement is written into reg. 5(ב)(6): attach the demand or explain its absence.

## How it works in practice

### Which route?

1. **Is there a statutory objection or appeal?** Arnona (objection to the director, then the appeals committee, then an administrative appeal), planning (objection to a plan, appeals board), national insurance (claim, committees, labour court), tax (objection, then the district court). Use it; a petition will be dismissed for failure to exhaust it.
2. **Is the matter in the First Schedule?** Then an administrative petition in the district court sitting in administrative matters for the right district (reg. 2). The schedule covers dozens of subjects; check the item number and write it in the petition (reg. 5(ב)(3א)).
3. **Is it a claim for money from an authority listed in the Third Schedule** (for example restitution of unlawful charges)? Then an administrative claim.
4. **Nothing fits?** The HCJ, as a residual forum (lesson 06.8).

### The time line

- Decision received: diary the reg. 3 period from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge.
- Prior demand: send it at once, ask for an answer within a short time, and say you will petition if there is none. Do not let the demand eat up the whole period; you may file and attach the demand if the authority has not answered.
- File: petition (Form 1), affidavit (Form 2), the decision, the correspondence, the fee or a fee exemption request ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Preliminary hearing or order to answer; possible interim order.
- Judgment; appeal to the Supreme Court as of right (petition) or by leave (administrative appeal).

### Filing and representation

Filing is at the registry of the district court or through Net HaMishpat where available for unrepresented parties (⚠️ manual check; lesson 08.8). An individual may file in person; whether the Administrative Affairs Court accepts unrepresented petitioners has not been confirmed in a free official source, though reg. 170 of the civil regulations is the general rule and petitions by individuals are common (Unverified; see [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)).

## Worked example

Amira Nassar, a café owner in Nazareth, applied to the municipality to renew her business licence. On 1 September 2026 she received a letter refusing it because of "planning deviations". She had built a pergola in 2019 with a permit she can show.

1. **Route.** Business licensing is a First Schedule matter: an administrative petition in the Nazareth district court sitting in administrative matters (s. 5(1)). No statutory appeal applies.
2. **Time.** The period runs from 1 September, when she received the letter (reg. 3(ב)). She diaries the last day and a safety date a week earlier.
3. **Prior demand (2 September).** She writes to the licensing department and the city's legal adviser: the decision, the permit (attached), a request to reconsider within 10 days, and notice that she will petition otherwise.
4. **No answer by 14 September.** She drafts the petition: parties and their interest; the decision and date; jurisdiction (the schedule item on business licensing and the Business Licensing Law provision); her interest (her livelihood); grounds (the decision rests on a factual error and ignores the permit, so it is unreasonable and lacks a proper factual basis); the remedy (an order to reconsider or to grant the licence); the fee; no related proceedings. She attaches the letter, the permit, her demand and the proof it was sent, and an affidavit in Form 2.
5. **Interim relief.** If the municipality threatens closure before the hearing, she asks for an interim order (reg. 9). She considers getting a lawyer for that step.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A decision of a public authority that affected you, or Amira's case above.
- The First Schedule of the [Administrative Affairs Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים) and regs 3–5 of the [procedure regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין)).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Route (10 min).** Is there a statutory objection or appeal? If not, find the First Schedule item. Write the route and the provision.
2. **Clock (5 min).** Write the date of publication, notice and knowledge, take the earliest, and compute the last day under reg. 3 using [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
3. **Prior demand (15 min).** Draft a one-page demand on the [letter to a public authority template](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md): the decision, why it is wrong, what you ask, a short deadline, and that you will go to court.
4. **Petition checklist (15 min).** List every item in reg. 5(ב)–(ד) and fill in what you have; mark what is missing.

### Template

- [Letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md) for the prior demand.
- [Petition to the High Court of Justice](../../templates/hcj-petition.md) as a model for structure; adapt the heading and jurisdiction for the Administrative Affairs Court.

### How you know it worked

- You named the route with a section or schedule item.
- Your last day is computed from the earliest trigger, with a safety margin.
- Your demand asks for something specific by a date.
- Your checklist covers all reg. 5 items.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know the court can dismiss a timely petition for delay (reg. 4).
- [ ] I know I must attach the prior demand or explain its absence (reg. 5(ב)(6)).
- [ ] I know when a statutory appeal comes before any petition.
- [ ] I know the difference between an appeal as of right (s. 11) and by leave (s. 12).

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may write the prior demand and use statutory objections yourself. Filing an administrative petition in your own name is generally possible under the general rule that a party may act for itself (Unverified for the Administrative Affairs Court specifically). Interim orders and contested evidence are harder alone.

### Where and how

- The district court sitting in administrative matters for the right district (reg. 2); registry or Net HaMishpat (⚠️ manual check).
- Fee and fee exemption, petition period, appeal period: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: Legal Aid in some administrative matters, university clinics, NGOs in the field (immigration, housing, welfare); see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need an interim order to stop an imminent act (closure, demolition, deportation).
- The authority's answer raises jurisdiction or delay arguments you cannot rebut.
- The matter is a tender, planning, or immigration status, where procedure is technical and the stakes high.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the authority's answer past the reg. 3 period | Petition late; possible dismissal | Ask to extend with reasons (reg. 3(ג)) | File in time even if the demand is unanswered |
| Filing in the HCJ on a First Schedule matter | Sent to the Administrative Affairs Court; delay | Ask for transfer | Check the schedule first |
| Skipping a statutory objection | Dismissed for not exhausting remedies | Use the objection now if still in time | Ask "is there a specific appeal?" first |
| No affidavit, or one that mixes knowledge and belief | Order to amend; weak evidence | File a proper Form 2 affidavit | Separate what you saw from what you were told |
| Counting the period from when you "fully understood" the decision | Late | Argue knowledge date with evidence | Count from the earliest trigger |

## Review

- The Administrative Affairs Court hears petitions on First Schedule matters, listed appeals and listed claims, with HCJ grounds and remedies (ss. 5, 8).
- Petitions have a short default period from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge, and can still fail for delay (regs 3–4).
- A petition must attach the prior demand or explain its absence, and be verified by affidavit (reg. 5).
- Statutory objections and appeals come first; the HCJ is residual.
- Appeal to the Supreme Court: as of right from a petition, by leave from an administrative appeal (ss. 11–12).
