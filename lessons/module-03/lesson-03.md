# 03.3 · Form requirements: land transactions (s. 8 Land Law), guarantees, gifts

By the end of this lesson you will know the few Israeli contracts where form matters, what "in writing" means for each, and what happens when the writing is missing. You will be able to tell whether an apartment deal, a promise of a gift or a guarantee for a relative's loan actually binds, and you will check a real guarantee form for the protections the law gives private guarantors.

## Why it matters

The default in Israeli law is freedom of form: a handshake, a click or a WhatsApp message can make a contract (s. 23, lesson 03.1). The exceptions are few but they cover the biggest decisions in a private person's life: buying or leasing land, promising a gift for the future, guaranteeing someone else's debt. In those areas a missing document can turn a "deal" into nothing, or leave you liable for far more than you thought. Knowing the exceptions also protects you from the opposite mistake: refusing to honour an oral contract because "nothing was signed".

## The law

### Land: registration completes, writing is required for the promise

> עסקה במקרקעין טעונה רישום; העסקה נגמרת ברישום, ורואים את השעה שבה אישר הרשם את העסקה לרישום כשעת הרישום.

*Gist:* A land transaction is completed only by registration; until then it is treated as an undertaking to make the transaction (s. 7(ב)). [Land Law s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_7)

> התחייבות לעשות עסקה במקרקעין טעונה מסמך בכתב.

*Gist:* An undertaking to make a land transaction requires a written document. [Land Law s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_8)

"Transaction in land" means transferring ownership or another right in land by the will of the transferor, other than by will (s. 6): sale, gift of land, mortgage, long lease, easement. A lease of more than five years is a transaction that is registered; shorter leases are not, but a promise to grant one is still a promise to make a transaction in land.

> התחייב אדם לעשות עסקה במקרקעין ולפני שנגמרה העסקה ברישום חזר והתחייב כלפי אדם אחר לעסקה נוגדת, זכותו של בעל העסקה הראשונה עדיפה, אך אם השני פעל בתום־לב ובתמורה והעסקה לטובתו נרשמה בעודו בתום־לב – זכותו עדיפה.

*Gist:* If an owner promises the same land twice, the first buyer wins, unless the second bought in good faith, for value, and registered while still in good faith. [Land Law s. 9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_9) This is why buyers register a caveat (הערת אזהרה) immediately (year 2, module 13).

### Gifts: a present gift needs no form, a future gift does

> התחייבות לתת מתנה בעתיד טעונה מסמך בכתב.

*Gist:* A promise to give a gift in the future must be in writing. [Gift Law s. 5(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המתנה#סעיף_5)

> כל עוד מקבל המתנה לא שינה את מצבו בהסתמך על ההתחייבות, רשאי הנותן לחזור בו ממנה, זולת אם ויתר בכתב על רשות זו.

*Gist:* Even a written promise of a gift may be withdrawn until the recipient has relied on it, unless the giver waived that right in writing; it may also be withdrawn for disgraceful conduct of the recipient or a significant worsening of the giver's finances (s. 5(ג)). [Gift Law s. 5(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המתנה#סעיף_5) A gift handed over now (s. 2, s. 6) needs no document, but a gift of land still needs registration and the s. 8 document.

### Guarantees: no general writing rule, strong rules for private guarantors

> הערבות נוצרת בהסכם בין הערב לבין הנושה או בהתחייבותו של הערב שהודעה עליה ניתנה לנושה; [...]

*Gist:* A guarantee is created by agreement between guarantor and creditor, or by the guarantor's undertaking notified to the creditor; the Guarantee Law sets no general writing requirement. [Guarantee Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הערבות#סעיף_3)

Chapter 2 of the law protects an **individual guarantor** (ערב יחיד: a person, not the debtor's spouse or partner) who guarantees a debt to a **professional lender** (a creditor who lends in the ordinary course of business):

> (א) ערב יחיד ערב רק לסכום הנקוב בחוזה הערבות ולתוספות כמפורט בסעיף 25.
> (ב) לא נקוב בחוזה הערבות סכום קצוב – פטור ערב יחיד מערבותו.

*Gist:* An individual guarantor is liable only up to the amount stated in the guarantee contract; if no fixed amount is stated, he is released. [Guarantee Law s. 21](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הערבות#סעיף_21)

The lender must disclose the loan's key terms before signing (s. 22), notify the guarantor of the debtor's default within a fixed period (s. 26), and, for a **protected guarantor** (ערב מוגן: an individual guarantor for amounts up to a linked ceiling, or a higher ceiling for a loan to buy the debtor's or family's home, s. 19), sue the debtor and exhaust execution first (s. 27). The ceilings are linked to the index; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Residential leases: writing is a duty, not a condition

> חוזה שכירות למגורים ייערך בכתב וייחתם בידי המשכיר והשוכר, ואולם אין באי־קיום הוראה זו כדי לגרוע מתוקפו של החוזה; העתק חתום מהחוזה יימסר לכל אחד מהצדדים.

*Gist:* A residential lease must be in writing and signed by both, but an oral lease is still valid; each party gets a signed copy. [Rental and Borrowing Law s. 25ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_השכירות_והשאילה#סעיף_25ב) Compare s. 23 of the Contracts Law: this is a writing *duty* with no invalidity, unlike s. 8 of the Land Law.

## Leading cases

### Key text: the softening of s. 8 in the case law

The Supreme Court has long treated s. 8 as a substantive requirement: without a document there is no binding promise to make a land transaction. Over time it softened this in two ways. First, a short document can be enough if it contains the essential terms (parties, property, price) and shows intent to be bound; missing secondary terms can be filled (Contracts Law s. 26). Second, in exceptional cases the court has allowed good faith (ss. 12 and 39) to stop a party from relying on the missing writing, where the other side fully performed and justice demands it. The free article by Avi Weinroth reviews this line of cases and its limits. [Weinroth, Mishpat ve-Asakim 22 (2018)](https://runilawreview.org/2018/08/22/vol22_weinroth/)

Why it matters: do not assume an oral land deal is enforceable, and do not assume a short signed memorandum is harmless.

### ע״א 692/86 בוטקובסקי ושות׳ – חברה לייבוא ושיווק בע״מ נ׳ גת, פ״ד מד(1) 57 (1989)

Intent to be bound by a preliminary document is judged objectively (lesson 03.2). Combined with s. 8, this means that a signed זיכרון דברים for an apartment that names the parties, the flat, the price and the payment plan can be both the "written document" s. 8 requires and a binding contract. Free text: search the case number at [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il); the holding is quoted in many later free rulings.

## How it works in practice

### Form check

| Agreement | Writing needed? | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Sale, gift, mortgage or long lease of land | Yes, a document (s. 8); completion by registration (s. 7) | No binding promise, except rare good-faith cases |
| Lease of a flat to live in | Yes, as a duty (s. 25ב) | Contract still valid; the standard terms and mandatory rules apply |
| Promise of a future gift | Yes (Gift Law s. 5(א)) | Not binding; even if written, revocable until reliance |
| Gift handed over now (money, a car) | No | Valid on delivery with agreement |
| Personal guarantee to a bank or lender | No general rule; but no stated amount = released (s. 21) | Check chapter 2 protections |
| Most other contracts (services, sale of goods, loans) | No (s. 23) | Prove the terms by messages, transfers, witnesses |

### What a s. 8 document must show

At least: the parties, an identification of the land (address, block and parcel if known), the price or other consideration, and words that show a commitment rather than a negotiation. A signature by the party being held to the promise is the usual proof. Payment schedule and delivery date strengthen it; a missing date can be filled by s. 41 (reasonable time).

### Signing a guarantee

Before you sign as guarantor for a relative's loan, ask the lender for the disclosure under s. 22 in writing, check that a fixed amount appears in the guarantee, and keep a copy. If you are a protected guarantor, the lender generally cannot come to you before exhausting the debtor (s. 27).

## Worked example

Rivka and Haim Friedman from Bnei Brak promise their daughter Esti, orally at a family dinner, that they will "give her the small flat in Petah Tikva when she marries". Esti marries a year later. Haim then decides to sell the flat. Separately, Haim signed a guarantee at the bank for his son-in-law's business loan; the form has no amount, only "all debts of the borrower".

Step by step:

1. **The flat.** A promise to transfer land in the future is a promise to make a transaction in land (s. 6) and a promise of a future gift (Gift Law s. 5). Both need writing; there is none. Esti has no enforceable right. Even with a document, the parents could withdraw before she relied on it (s. 5(ב)).
2. **Reliance.** Suppose Esti, relying on the promise, renovated the flat with ₪80,000 of her own money and her parents knew. She might argue that withdrawing now is in bad faith; at most this gives her a claim for her expenses, and only in exceptional cases could a court prevent reliance on missing writing. She should get advice.
3. **The guarantee.** Haim is an individual guarantor (not the borrower's spouse or partner) to a professional lender. No fixed amount is stated: under s. 21(ב) he is released from the guarantee. He should keep the form and, if the bank demands payment, answer in writing citing s. 21.
4. **Result.** The family promise is morally weighty but legally weak; the bank guarantee is legally weaker than the bank thinks.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One document from your own life that is, or should be, in writing: a lease, a guarantee, a family promise about property, a memorandum. If you have none, use the Friedman case.
- The statutes linked above.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Classify the agreement with the form-check table.
2. If writing is needed, check what your document contains against the list for its type (for land: parties, property, price, commitment, signature).
3. Decide: is writing a condition of validity (Land Law s. 8, Gift Law s. 5(א)) or a duty (Rental Law s. 25ב)?
4. For a guarantee: write down whether you are an individual or protected guarantor, whether an amount is stated, and whether you received disclosure.
5. Write a three-line conclusion: what binds, what does not, and what you would fix or ask for now.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- You can state whether your agreement needs writing and quote the section.
- You identified any missing essential term.
- For a guarantee, you can say whether s. 21 and s. 27 protect you.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may read and check your own documents, sign your own residential lease, give or receive a gift of money, and answer a lender's demand as guarantor. Land transactions in practice need a lawyer: Land Registry deeds must be signed before a lawyer or the Registrar, and online filing is for lawyers (see [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)).

### Where and how

- Land Registry (טאבו): forms and the option to sign before the Registrar; year 2, module 13 covers registration and caveats.
- Bank guarantees: ask the bank in writing for the s. 22 disclosure; complaints go to the Bank of Israel's Banking Supervision public enquiries unit.
- Free help: Kol-Zchut guides on guarantors and gifts; Legal Aid for eligible people (see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are about to sign any document about buying, selling, gifting or mortgaging land.
- A relative's promise of land is the basis of a family dispute and you relied on it financially.
- A lender demands payment from you as guarantor for a large debt.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on an oral promise of a flat | No enforceable right | Get advice on reliance and good faith; document expenses | Ask for a signed document and, for land, register a caveat |
| Signing a land memorandum as "just a reservation" | It may satisfy s. 8 and bind you | Get advice immediately | Never sign land papers without a lawyer |
| Signing a guarantee without reading the amount | Exposure to all the debtor's debts (if not protected) | Check s. 21: no stated amount releases an individual guarantor | Insist on a fixed amount and disclosure |
| Refusing to sign a written lease thinking it protects you | The lease is valid anyway; you lose clarity | Sign a written lease now | Use the written form; s. 25ב requires it |
| Assuming a written gift promise cannot be taken back | The giver withdraws before you rely | Document reliance; ask for a written waiver of withdrawal | Ask for the waiver under s. 5(ב) at the outset |

## Review

- Form is free in Israeli contract law (s. 23) with a few important exceptions.
- A promise to make a transaction in land requires a written document (Land Law s. 8); the transaction completes only on registration (s. 7).
- A promise of a future gift requires writing and may be withdrawn until reliance (Gift Law s. 5).
- Guarantees need no form, but an individual guarantor to a professional lender is released if no fixed amount is stated (s. 21) and has disclosure and exhaustion protections.
- A residential lease must be written, but an oral one is still valid (s. 25ב).
