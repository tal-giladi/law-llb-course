# 03.6 · Illegal contracts, standard contracts and consumer contracts

By the end of this lesson you will be able to spot the clauses in a phone, gym, bank, insurance or rental contract that Israeli law presumes to be unfair, decide which of them a court will strike out, and know what happens to a contract that breaks the law. This is where freedom of contract meets its limits, and where private people win most often.

## Why it matters

Most contracts you sign are not negotiated. You click "I agree" or sign a pre-printed form. Israel's Standard Contracts Law, one of the earliest of its kind (first enacted in 1964, re-enacted in 1982), lets a court cancel or rewrite any clause in such a contract that unfairly deprives the customer. A list of presumptions tells you which clauses are suspect: exemption from liability, one-sided rights to change prices, forced arbitration, choice of a distant court. Some clauses are simply void. Alongside it, the Consumer Protection Law bans misleading consumers and gives specific rights. And the general rule of s. 30 voids contracts that are illegal or against public policy, with a flexible relief power in s. 31.

## The law

### Illegal and immoral contracts

> חוזה שכריתתו, תכנו או מטרתו הם בלתי חוקיים, בלתי מוסריים או סותרים את תקנת הציבור – בטל.

*Gist:* A contract whose making, content or purpose is illegal, immoral or against public policy is void. [s. 30](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28חלק_כללי%29#סעיף_30)

> [...] בבטלות לפי סעיף 30 רשאי בית המשפט, אם ראה שמן הצדק לעשות כן ובתנאים שימצא לנכון, לפטור צד מהחובה לפי סעיף 21, כולה או מקצתה, ובמידה שצד אחד ביצע את חיובו לפי החוזה – לחייב את הצד השני בקיום החיוב שכנגד, כולו או מקצתו.

*Gist:* For a contract void under s. 30, the court may, where justice requires, release a party from restitution, or order the other party to perform its side where one side already performed. [s. 31](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28חלק_כללי%29#סעיף_31) Section 31 prevents a party from using the illegality as a shield to keep what it received. Section 19 (partial invalidity) also applies: an illegal clause can fall while the rest stands.

### Standard contracts: what they are and what courts do

> "חוזה אחיד" – נוסח של חוזה שתנאיו, כולם או מקצתם, נקבעו מראש בידי צד אחד כדי שישמשו תנאים לחוזים רבים בינו לבין אנשים בלתי מסויימים במספרם או בזהותם;

*Gist:* A standard contract is a form whose terms one party set in advance for use in many contracts with an open number of people. [Standard Contracts Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_האחידים#סעיף_2) A term specially negotiated for your contract is not a "term" under the law.

> בית משפט ובית הדין יבטלו או ישנו, בהתאם להוראות חוק זה, תנאי בחוזה אחיד שיש בו – בשים לב למכלול תנאי החוזה ולנסיבות אחרות – משום קיפוח לקוחות או משום יתרון בלתי הוגן של הספק העלול להביא לידי קיפוח לקוחות (להלן – תנאי מקפח).

*Gist:* Courts and the Tribunal shall cancel or change a term that, considering the whole contract and the circumstances, deprives customers or gives the supplier an unfair advantage. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_האחידים#סעיף_3) Note "יבטלו": once a term is depriving, the court must act.

### The presumptions (s. 4) and the void terms (s. 5)

Section 4 lists terms presumed depriving. The supplier may rebut the presumption, but the burden is on it. Among them:

> (1) תנאי הפוטר את הספק, באופן מלא או חלקי, מאחריות שהיתה מוטלת עליו על פי דין אילולא אותו תנאי [...]

*Gist:* A term exempting the supplier from liability it would have by law. [s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_האחידים#סעיף_4)

Others in s. 4: an unreasonable right for the supplier to cancel, suspend or change its main obligations (2); a right to change the price after signing at its sole discretion, unless the change comes from factors outside its control (4); a term tying the customer to the supplier unreasonably (5); a term limiting the customer's rights or remedies or setting unreasonable notice conditions (6); unreasonable agreed damages in the supplier's favour (6א); shifting the burden of proof to the customer (7); forcing arbitration (8), or arbitration on the supplier's terms (10); choosing the forum against the law or giving the supplier the sole choice (9); one-way index linkage (11); a term in which the customer "confirms he read the contract" or declares facts about himself (12).

> (א) תנאי בחוזה אחיד השולל או המגביל את זכות הלקוח לפנות לערכאות משפטיות – בטל.
> (ב) תנאי בחוזה אחיד הפוטר את הספק, באופן מלא או חלקי, מאחריות לנזק גוף או למעשה זדון המוטלת עליו על פי דין – בטל.

*Gist:* Terms barring the customer from going to court, or exempting the supplier from liability for bodily injury or wilful acts, are void outright. [s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_האחידים#סעיף_5)

What the law does not touch: a plainly worded price term, terms that copy a statute or an international treaty (s. 23). You cannot use the law to argue the price was too high.

### Two routes

- **In your own case:** "מצא בית משפט, בהליך שבין ספק ולקוח, שתנאי הוא מקפח, יבטל את התנאי בחוזה שביניהם או ישנה אותו" (s. 19). Any court, including the small claims court, can strike the term in your dispute.
- **For everyone:** the Attorney General, the Consumer Protection Commissioner and recognised consumer organisations may ask the Standard Contracts Tribunal to cancel a term (s. 16); the Tribunal's decision then applies to the supplier's similar contracts (s. 18). Individuals cannot apply to the Tribunal directly.

### Consumer law in one paragraph

The Consumer Protection Law forbids a dealer to do anything that may mislead a consumer on a material matter, including after the transaction (s. 2), and makes a breach a tort (s. 31), which opens the way to damages and class actions. It also gives specific cancellation rights (door-to-door, distance sales, the cancellation regulations), which lesson 03.10 turns into a practice lab.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 337/62 ריזנפלד נ׳ יעקובסון (1963)

A married man bought an apartment and registered it in the name of a woman he intended to marry after his divorce, on the understanding that it would return to him if they did not marry. When they separated she argued the arrangement was immoral and unenforceable. The majority held the agreement over the apartment was not void: it had to be distinguished from the promise of marriage, and the sanctity of contracts weighed against using public policy to let her keep the flat. Justice Silberg, dissenting, held the arrangement inseparable from an immoral relationship.

Why it matters: decided before the 1973 Law, it shows the core tension that s. 30 and s. 31 now manage: public policy versus the risk that the party who invokes illegality simply keeps the benefit. Section 31 gives today's courts tools to avoid that unjust result. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/313)

### ע״א 10085/08 תנובה מרכז שיתופי נ׳ עזבון ראבי (2011)

In 1995 Tnuva added silicone to long-life milk without listing it on the label, in breach of the binding standard. A consumer class action succeeded. The Supreme Court upheld liability for misleading consumers and recognised compensation for violation of autonomy (the consumer's right to choose what to consume), while adjusting the trial court's award.

Why it matters: it shows that misleading a consumer on a material matter (Consumer Protection Law s. 2) is actionable even when no physical harm is proved, and that small individual harms add up through class actions. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/386)

## How it works in practice

### Reading a standard contract in ten minutes

1. Find the liability section ("אחריות", "פטור", "הגבלת אחריות"): check for exemptions (s. 4(1)) and void exemptions for bodily injury (s. 5(ב)).
2. Find changes and cancellation ("שינוי תנאים", "ביטול"): one-sided rights to change price or terms (s. 4(2), (4)).
3. Find penalties and fees ("פיצוי מוסכם", "דמי ביטול", "קנס"): unreasonable agreed damages (s. 4(6א)) and consumer-law caps on cancellation fees.
4. Find disputes ("סמכות שיפוט", "בוררות"): forced arbitration or a distant court (s. 4(8)–(10)).
5. Find declarations ("הלקוח מצהיר כי קרא"): presumed depriving (s. 4(12)).
6. Note anything specially negotiated: it is outside the law.

### What to do with a depriving term

- Before signing: ask to delete or change it; many suppliers agree for individual customers.
- In a dispute: write that the term is depriving under s. 3, name the presumption in s. 4, and ask the court to cancel it under s. 19.
- For a systemic problem: complain to the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority, which can apply to the Tribunal.

## Worked example

Shai Peretz, a student from Be'er Sheva, joined a gym chain on a 12-month standard contract. After two months he broke his leg. The contract says:

- (a) "The member may not cancel during the commitment period for any reason."
- (b) "The gym is not liable for any injury on its premises."
- (c) "Any dispute will be decided by an arbitrator appointed by the gym, in Tel Aviv."
- (d) "The member confirms he read and understood all terms."
- (e) "The monthly price is ₪249."

Step by step:

1. **(a)** Limits the customer's right to cancel even for frustration-type events (injury) and ties him unreasonably: presumed depriving (s. 4(5), (6)). Also check consumer-law rules on cancelling continuing transactions (year 3).
2. **(b)** Exemption from liability: void as to bodily injury (s. 5(ב)) and presumed depriving for other liability (s. 4(1)).
3. **(c)** Arbitration with the supplier choosing the arbitrator and the place: presumed depriving (s. 4(8), (10)); a clause barring court access entirely is void (s. 5(א)).
4. **(d)** Presumed depriving (s. 4(12)); in any case it does not prove he understood.
5. **(e)** Plainly worded price: outside the law (s. 23(1)).
6. **Result.** Shai writes to cancel, citing the injury, ss. 3–5 and s. 19. If the gym insists, he may sue in small claims in Be'er Sheva and ask the judge to cancel (a) and (c).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One standard contract you have signed: phone, internet, gym, insurance, bank account terms, an online platform's terms of service. Download the current version.
- A highlighter or comment tool.
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Confirm it is a standard contract (s. 2): pre-set, for many customers.
2. Read it with the ten-minute checklist above. Highlight every candidate term.
3. For each, write: the clause number, the presumption in s. 4 or the rule in s. 5, and one sentence on why it deprives you.
4. Remove from your list terms that were specially negotiated or that only state the price (s. 23).
5. For each remaining term, decide: void (s. 5), presumed depriving (s. 4) or "depriving on the whole" (s. 3).
6. Check for misleading statements in the ads or the sales call (Consumer Protection Law s. 2) and note them.
7. Write a one-page objection letter for the two strongest terms, ending with what you ask for (cancel the term, waive a fee, refund).

### Template

None; the [contract review checklist](../../templates/contract-review-checklist.md) used in lesson 03.9 covers standard terms in its section D.

### How you know it worked

- Each term on your list has a section number and a reason.
- You did not attack the price itself.
- Your letter asks for a specific result.

- [ ] I can define a standard contract and a depriving term.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Any court hearing your dispute with a supplier, including the small claims court, may cancel a depriving term (s. 19). You cannot apply to the Standard Contracts Tribunal yourself; a consumer organisation or the Commissioner can.

### Where and how

- Small claims court for disputes within the ceiling (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Complaints: the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority (gov.il) and the relevant regulator (Ministry of Communications for phone and internet, Capital Markets Authority for insurance, Bank of Israel for banks).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut consumer guides; Legal Aid's consumer service for eligible people (see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are thinking of a class action: it requires a lawyer in practice and a court's certification.
- The contract is a business contract between companies; the Standard Contracts Law still applies, but stakes and arguments are larger.
- The illegality in s. 30 involves criminal exposure for you (for example an off-the-books payment to avoid tax).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing the price is unfair under the Standard Contracts Law | Rejected: price terms are excluded (s. 23) | Look for misleading conduct or hidden fees | Attack terms, not the price |
| Citing the law against a term you negotiated | Not a "term" under s. 2 | Use general contract law (good faith, interpretation) | Keep a record of what was negotiated |
| Paying an "off-the-books" price and then suing | The court may refuse relief under s. 30 | Ask the court to use s. 31 | Never agree to illegal arrangements |
| Writing "the contract is illegal" without detail | The judge sees an empty claim | Name the term, the presumption and the harm | Use the clause-by-clause table |
| Asking the Tribunal to hear your personal case | Not available to individuals | Raise the term in your own court case | Use s. 19 in court; complain to the Authority |

## Review

- Section 30 voids illegal, immoral or anti-public-policy contracts; s. 31 lets the court adjust restitution or order counter-performance to avoid injustice.
- A standard contract is a pre-set form for many customers; any court must cancel or change a depriving term (ss. 3, 19).
- Section 4 lists presumptions of unfairness; s. 5 voids terms barring access to courts or exempting liability for bodily injury or wilful acts.
- Price terms in plain language and terms that copy a statute are outside the law (s. 23).
- Consumer law adds a ban on misleading consumers (s. 2) and actionable damages (s. 31), as Tnuva shows.
