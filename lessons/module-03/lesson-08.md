# 03.8 · Breach and remedies: Contracts (Remedies for Breach) Law 1970

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide, for a real breach, which remedies are open to you, in what order to use them, and how much to claim. You will know when you may cancel at once and when you must first give an extension, when a court will order performance, and how Israeli law measures damages, including the sum you get without proving any loss.

## Why it matters

The Remedies Law is the statute a self-represented person uses most. It is short (25 sections), clear, and generous to the injured party: in Israel enforcement (specific performance) is a right, not an exceptional favour as in English law. But it is also full of traps. Cancel too quickly for a non-fundamental breach and you become the party in breach. Wait too long to cancel and you lose the right. Fail to reduce your loss and the damages shrink. This lesson gives you the sequence that avoids those traps; lesson 03.10 turns it into letters.

## The law

### The menu

> הופר חוזה, זכאי הנפגע לתבוע את אכיפתו או לבטל את החוזה, וזכאי הוא לפיצויים, בנוסף על אחת התרופות האמורות או במקומן, והכל לפי הוראות חוק זה.

*Gist:* The injured party chooses enforcement or cancellation, and may add damages to either or claim damages alone. [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_2) "Breach" is any act or omission contrary to the contract, including breach of one obligation (s. 1).

### Enforcement

> הנפגע זכאי לאכיפת החוזה, זולת אם נתקיימה אחת מאלה: (1) החוזה אינו בר־ביצוע; (2) אכיפת החוזה היא כפיה לעשות, או לקבל, עבודה אישית או שירות אישי; (3) ביצוע צו האכיפה דורש מידה בלתי סבירה של פיקוח [...]; (4) אכיפת החוזה היא בלתי צודקת בנסיבות הענין.

*Gist:* Enforcement is a right unless the contract cannot be performed, it would force personal work or service, it needs unreasonable supervision, or it would be unjust. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_3) "Enforcement" includes an order to pay money (s. 1), so a claim for the agreed price is an enforcement claim.

### Cancellation

> "הפרה יסודית" – הפרה שניתן להניח לגביה שאדם סביר לא היה מתקשר באותו חוזה אילו ראה מראש את ההפרה ותוצאותיה, או הפרה שהוסכם עליה בחוזה שתיחשב ליסודית; תניה גורפת בחוזה העושה הפרות להפרות יסודיות ללא הבחנה ביניהן, אין לה תוקף אלא אם היתה סבירה בעת כריתת החוזה.

*Gist:* A breach is fundamental if a reasonable person would not have made the contract had they foreseen it, or if the contract designates it as fundamental; a blanket clause making every breach fundamental is valid only if reasonable. [s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_6)

> (ב) היתה הפרת החוזה לא יסודית, זכאי הנפגע לבטל את החוזה לאחר שנתן תחילה למפר ארכה לקיומו והחוזה לא קויים תוך זמן סביר לאחר מתן הארכה, זולת אם בנסיבות הענין היה ביטול החוזה בלתי צודק; [...]

*Gist:* A fundamental breach allows immediate cancellation (s. 7(א)); for a non-fundamental breach you must first give an extension, and may cancel only if it is not cured within a reasonable time. [s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_7)

> ביטול החוזה יהיה בהודעת הנפגע למפר תוך זמן סביר לאחר שנודע לו על ההפרה; [...]

*Gist:* Cancel by notice within a reasonable time after learning of the breach, or after the extension ends. [s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_8) After cancellation both sides make restitution, or pay value where return is impossible or unreasonable (s. 9). Notices go in the agreed way, otherwise by registered mail or another customary way (s. 21).

### Damages

> הנפגע זכאי לפיצויים בעד הנזק שנגרם לו עקב ההפרה ותוצאותיה ושהמפר ראה אותו או שהיה עליו לראותו מראש, בעת כריתת החוזה, כתוצאה מסתברת של ההפרה.

*Gist:* Damages cover loss caused by the breach that the party in breach foresaw or should have foreseen at contracting as a probable result. [s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28תרופות_בשל_הפרת_חוזה%29#סעיף_10) "Damage" includes lost profit (s. 1).

- **Without proof of loss (s. 11):** after cancelling a contract to supply goods or services, the difference between the contract price and the value on the day of cancellation; for late payment of money, shekel interest under the Adjudication of Interest and Linkage Law (as amended in 2024), unless the court sets another rate.
- **Non-pecuniary loss (s. 13):** distress and inconvenience, at the court's discretion.
- **Mitigation (s. 14):** no damages for loss you could have prevented by reasonable means; reasonable costs of mitigating are recoverable even if they did not help.
- **Agreed damages (s. 15):** payable without proof, but the court may reduce them if they bear no reasonable relation to the loss foreseeable at contracting. Forfeiture of a deposit counts as agreed damages (s. 15(ג)).

### Anticipatory breach, frustration and self-help

- **Anticipatory breach (s. 17):** if a party says it will not perform, or it becomes clear it cannot or will not, remedies are available before the due date.
- **Frustration (s. 18):** if the breach results from circumstances the party did not and should not have foreseen and could not prevent, and performance became impossible or fundamentally different, there is no enforcement or damages; the court may order restitution and reimbursement of reasonable costs.
- **Lien (s. 19) and set-off (s. 20):** you may hold the other party's property received under the contract until paid, and set off debts arising under this law.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 1846/92 לוי נ׳ מבט בנייה בע״מ (1993)

Buyers of a new apartment suffered years of water penetration that the developer failed to fix. They cancelled and sued for restitution and damages. The district court ordered them to pay the developer $16,000 for their years of use of the flat. The Supreme Court agreed that, as a matter of restitution, they owed payment for use; but because damages put the injured party where it would have been had the contract been performed, the buyers were entitled to damages equal to that same sum, so the deduction was cancelled.

Why it matters: it shows the three remedies working together: cancellation, two-way restitution (s. 9) and expectation damages (s. 10). The injured party should not end up worse off because it chose to cancel. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/237)

### ע״א 6370/00 קל בנין בע״מ נ׳ ע.ר.מ. רעננה לבניה והשכרה בע״מ (2002)

Covered in lesson 03.4. For remedies its point is the distinction between reliance damages (restoring the position before the contract or negotiation) and expectation damages (the position after performance), and the possibility of expectation damages even for pre-contract bad faith. [English translation (portions)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/219)

## How it works in practice

### The sequence after a breach

1. **Collect proof:** the contract, the breach, the dates, your loss.
2. **Classify the breach:** fundamental (s. 6) or not. Check the contract for a list of fundamental breaches and whether it is reasonable.
3. **Decide: keep or end the contract?** Keep it: demand performance (enforcement) and damages. End it: cancel (immediately if fundamental, after an extension if not), demand restitution and damages.
4. **Mitigate:** take reasonable steps to reduce the loss (find a substitute, stop further spending), and keep receipts.
5. **Send the notice:** in writing, by registered mail and email (s. 21). One notice can give an extension and say that the contract will be cancelled if it is not cured.
6. **Quantify:** list each head of damage with proof, and the s. 11 alternatives.
7. **Watch the clock:** the general limitation period for contract claims (Prescription Law s. 5; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) and the much shorter "reasonable time" to cancel.

### Choosing between enforcement and cancellation

| You want | Choose | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| The thing itself (the flat, the car, the agreed work) | Enforcement | Barred for personal services (s. 3(2)) and where unjust |
| Your money back and to move on | Cancellation + restitution | Give an extension first if the breach is not fundamental |
| To be paid the agreed price | Enforcement (payment order) | Prove you performed your side |
| Compensation only | Damages | Prove foreseeability and mitigation |

## Worked example

Yael Ben-Ami, a teacher from Kfar Saba, hired a renovator, Kobi, to redo her kitchen for ₪60,000: ₪20,000 paid up front, the rest on completion, finish by 1 May. The contract says nothing about fundamental breaches. On 1 May only demolition is done and Kobi is working elsewhere. Yael has booked her daughter's bat mitzvah meal at home for 15 June.

Step by step:

1. **Classify.** Is a delay of an unfinished kitchen fundamental? Arguably yes, given the known event date; but to be safe Yael treats it as possibly non-fundamental.
2. **Notice with extension (s. 7(ב)).** On 2 May she writes: the work is in breach; she gives an extension until 16 May to complete specified stages; if not, she will cancel without further notice and claim damages.
3. **No cure.** On 17 May Kobi has done almost nothing. Yael sends a cancellation notice (s. 8) the same day.
4. **Mitigation (s. 14).** She promptly hires another contractor, who charges ₪62,000 for all the remaining work (prices rose and the job is now urgent). She keeps the quotes and receipts.
5. **Damages.** Kobi did only demolition, worth about ₪4,000. Restitution (s. 9): ₪20,000 paid minus ₪4,000 of work received = ₪16,000 back. Extra cost of completing (s. 10): she will pay ₪4,000 + ₪62,000 = ₪66,000 for a kitchen that was to cost ₪60,000, so ₪6,000 more. Check: she paid ₪82,000 in total instead of ₪60,000, a loss of ₪22,000 = ₪16,000 + ₪6,000. She may add modest non-pecuniary damages for the distress around the event (s. 13).
6. **Forum.** The total is within the small claims ceiling (check [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); she files in her area.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One breach from your own life (a late delivery, an unfinished job, an unpaid invoice) or Yael's case.
- The contract, the timeline, receipts.
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the breach in one sentence: which obligation, when it was due, what happened.
2. Decide fundamental or not, with reasons (s. 6). Check for a contract clause.
3. Choose your remedy path with the table above and write why.
4. If cancelling for a non-fundamental breach, write the extension: what must be done, by when.
5. List mitigation steps you took or will take.
6. Build a damages table: head of loss, amount, proof, section (10, 11, 13, 14(ב), 15).
7. Check the agreed-damages clause, if any, for reasonableness (s. 15(א)).
8. Note the deadlines: reasonable time to cancel and the limitation period.

### How you know it worked

- Your remedy path matches your classification of the breach.
- Every head of damage has a section, a number and a piece of proof.
- You can explain what you did to reduce your loss.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Notices of breach, extension and cancellation are steps you take in your own name, and damages claims within the small-claims ceiling are designed for self-represented people.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Where and how

- Small claims court (individuals, up to the ceiling) or the magistrates' court (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) and lesson 01.4 on venue).
- A judgment for money is enforced through the Execution Office (module 8).
- Free help: court information centres; Kol-Zchut guides on breach of contract (see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The contract is for an apartment purchase or land: cancellation has heavy consequences (deposit forfeiture, caveats, tax).
- You want to cancel and the breach may not be fundamental, with large sums at stake.
- The other side claims frustration (s. 18) in a large contract.
- The claim exceeds the small-claims ceiling and needs expert evidence of loss.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cancelling at once for a non-fundamental breach | Your cancellation is wrongful; you become the party in breach | Withdraw it promptly and give an extension | Classify first; if in doubt, give an extension |
| Waiting months before cancelling | Right to cancel lost (s. 8) | Claim damages instead | Cancel within a reasonable time after the breach or the extension |
| Letting losses grow | Damages reduced (s. 14) | Show reasonable steps you took later | Mitigate at once and keep receipts |
| Claiming unforeseeable losses | Rejected (s. 10) | Limit the claim to probable results | Tell the other side of special risks when contracting |
| Relying on a huge agreed-damages clause | Reduced by the court (s. 15(א)) | Prove a reasonable relation to foreseeable loss | Agree realistic sums |
| Asking a court to force a person to work for you | Barred (s. 3(2)) | Claim damages and hire someone else | Choose cancellation and damages for personal services |

## Review

- On breach you may enforce or cancel, and add damages to either (s. 2); enforcement is a right with four exceptions (s. 3).
- A fundamental breach allows immediate cancellation; a non-fundamental one needs an extension first (ss. 6–7); cancel by notice within a reasonable time (s. 8).
- Damages cover foreseeable loss (s. 10), with no-proof alternatives (s. 11), non-pecuniary loss (s. 13), a duty to mitigate (s. 14) and controlled agreed damages (s. 15).
- After cancellation both sides make restitution (s. 9); Levy v. Mabat shows damages can offset the injured party's restitution burden.
- Frustration (s. 18) excuses enforcement and damages but allows restitution.
