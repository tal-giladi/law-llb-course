# 27.2 · Plans: outline and detailed plans, and reading a plan online

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find every plan that applies to a plot, tell which of them governs, and read the parts that matter to you: what may be built, how high, how far from the boundary, and which land is reserved for the public. You will also know who may submit a plan and which committee approves it.

## Why it matters

In Israel nothing may be built, and no permit may be granted, unless it matches the plans that apply to the land (s. 145(ב), lesson 27.4). Plans decide the value of your flat, the view from your balcony, whether a school or a highway will be built next door, and whether your neighbour may add two floors. Most people discover a plan only when a crane appears. A learner who can open the national plan system, find the plan, and read its rights table can object in time (lesson 27.3), check a purchase (lesson [13.9](../module-13/lesson-09.md)) and plan their own permit.

Israeli planning is "plan-led": rights are created by approved plans, not by owners' wishes or by permits. The courts treat a plan as a kind of legislation; its provisions bind the committees, owners and the public alike. That is why procedure (deposit, objections, approval, publication) is taken so seriously: it is the public's only chance to take part in the making of that "legislation".

## The law

### The hierarchy of plans: ss. 129–131

> תכנית המיתאר הארצית – כוחה יפה מכל תכנית אחרת, אם לא נאמר אחרת בתכנית המיתאר הארצית.

*Gist:* A national plan prevails over every other plan, a district plan over local and detailed plans, and a local outline plan over a detailed plan, unless the higher plan says otherwise. [s. 131](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_131); [ss. 129–130](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_129)

### What each plan does: ss. 49, 55, 61, 69

The national plan sets land use for the whole country: farmland, industry, main roads, railways, power, water, nature and landscape, and population distribution (s. 49). A district plan sets the details needed to carry out the national plan in the district (s. 55). A local outline plan controls development in the local area, secures health, safety and transport, designates zones for housing, industry, commerce and public buildings, and protects heritage and open space (s. 61). A detailed plan may also divide land into plots and fix detailed building provisions (s. 69); it is usually the plan from which permits are issued.

> מטרות תכנית מיתאר מקומית הן: (1) פיקוח על פיתוח הקרקע במרחב התכנון המקומי [...]; (5) ייחוד שטחים ציבוריים פתוחים [...] הנותנים מענה לצורכי האוכלוסיה החזויה במרחב התכנון המקומי באופן הולם.

*Gist:* A local outline plan's purposes include controlling development and providing adequate public open space for the expected population. [s. 61](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_61)

### Who may submit a plan: s. 61א(ב)

Ministries, statutory authorities, government development companies, local committees and local authorities, and "an owner of land or anyone with an interest in the land" may prepare and submit a local or detailed plan. A holder of 66% or more of the rights in part of the land may submit a plan if it does not harm other owners (s. 61א(ב1)).

### Local or district power: s. 62א

> תכנית מפורטת או תכנית מיתאר מקומית, הכוללת אך ורק אחד או יותר מהנושאים המפורטים להלן, היא תכנית בסמכות הועדה המקומית: [...]

*Gist:* A plan that deals only with listed matters — such as reparcellation, widening a road on an approved route, enlarging public areas, changing building lines, height and number of floors without adding total area, redistributing floor area within limits, or adding housing units without adding main area — may be approved by the local committee; everything else needs the district committee. [s. 62א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_התכנון_והבניה#סעיף_62א)

A local committee with a comprehensive plan (תכנית כוללנית) and "independent" status (s. 31א) may approve more (s. 62א(א1), (ג)). This decides where you object (s. 103) and how you appeal (lesson 27.3).

### From preparation to deposit: ss. 77–78, 85, 98

When a plan is being prepared, the institution may publish a notice (s. 77) and impose interim conditions on permits in the area (s. 78). After it decides to deposit a plan it may restrict permits until approval (s. 98). Appeal against such restrictions goes to the appeals committee or the National Council (ss. 78(ב), 98(ב)–(ג)).

### Official information for a plot: s. 145(א1); reg. 20

> נקלטה בקשה לקבלת מידע להיתר, ימסור המהנדס את המידע להיתר על המקרקעין [...] בתוך 30 ימים מיום קליטת הבקשה: (1) התכניות החלות על המקרקעין [...]; (4) התכניות המופקדות החלות על המקרקעין; [...]

*Gist:* Before applying for a permit you ask the committee engineer for "information for a permit", which must list the approved and deposited plans, restrictions, designation, permitted areas and spatial guidelines for the plot within the period set in the regulations; the information is valid for two years (reg. 23). [Licensing Regulations reg. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_התכנון_והבנייה_%28רישוי_בנייה%29); all requests are made online through the Planning Administration's "רישוי זמין" system (reg. 2).

## Leading cases

### עע״ם 1446/06 הוועדה המחוזית לתכנון ולבניה מרכז נ' השתתפויות הנכסים בישראל (21.9.2010)

The Supreme Court read the 1995 list in s. 62א narrowly: local committees could approve only changes of limited local effect, and a plan that went beyond the list was void for lack of power even if it looked "minor". Why it matters: whenever you object to a local-committee plan, check every change against the s. 62א list; a change outside it is a strong legal ground. The list was widened in 2014, but the method of reading it strictly remains. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/ועדה_מקומית_לתכנון_ולבנייה) (secondary; the ruling's text on the Judiciary's site could not be reached).

### Key text: ss. 129–131 and "unless stated otherwise"

Most conflicts between plans are resolved by the hierarchy, but many national plans (for example the national plan for strengthening buildings, TAMA 38, now being phased out and replaced by the earthquake-resilience plans of ss. 70א–70ט added in 2022) expressly let local plans add or restrict. Why it matters: before arguing "the national plan prevails", read the higher plan's own provisions on its relation to local plans.

## How it works in practice

### Where plans live

| Source | What you find | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mavat (מבא״ת), the Planning Administration's plan system | Plan number, status (in preparation, deposited, approved), provisions, map, appendices, objections timeline, decisions | Search by plan number, block and parcel (גוש/חלקה) or address (⚠️ manual check) |
| GovMap | Plan boundaries layer over the map; block and parcel | Use it to find which plans cover your plot (⚠️ manual check) |
| The local committee's site | Older plans scanned, agendas, decisions, building files | Many committees use a municipal GIS |
| Reshumot (Official Gazette) | Deposit and approval notices | The legal date for deadlines |

### Reading a plan in 15 minutes

1. **Status.** Approved (מאושרת), deposited (מופקדת: you can still object), or in preparation.
2. **Area.** The map (תשריט): is your plot inside the blue line (the plan boundary) or next to it?
3. **Designation.** The colour and legend (check the legend; typical colours): residential (yellow and orange shades), commercial, public buildings (brown), open space (green), roads (red).
4. **Rights table.** The table of rights and building provisions (טבלת זכויות והוראות בנייה): main area, service area, number of units, floors, height, coverage (תכסית), setbacks.
5. **Conditions.** Conditions for permits (for example an approved development plan, parking, a shelter, preservation).
6. **Relations.** Which earlier plans this plan changes or cancels.
7. **Appendices.** Building (בינוי), traffic, landscape, environment: binding or "guiding" (מנחה) as the provisions say.

## Worked example

Daniel Abergil owns a ground-floor flat in an old four-storey building in Kiryat Yam. A neighbour tells him "the city approved six floors on our street".

1. **Find.** On GovMap Daniel turns on the plans layer and sees two plans covering his parcel: an old detailed plan from the 1970s and a new plan with a number in the current format.
2. **Status.** On Mavat the new plan shows "deposited" with a deposit date three weeks ago: it is not approved yet, and objections are still open (lesson 27.3).
3. **Power.** The plan adds floor area and housing units beyond the existing total: that is outside s. 62א(א), so it is a district committee plan (unless the local committee is independent with a comprehensive plan). The deposit notice confirms the Haifa district committee.
4. **Content.** The rights table allows six floors plus a roof floor, the building line moves 2 metres closer to the street, and a strip of the shared garden becomes a public path.
5. **Effect.** The plan may raise the flat's value (betterment levy, lesson 27.6) but takes part of the garden. Daniel diarises the objection deadline and starts lesson 27.9.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An address you care about (your home, a plot you might buy, a park near you).
- Access to Mavat and GovMap in a normal browser (⚠️ manual check of URLs: mavat.iplan.gov.il and govmap.gov.il).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Find the block and parcel of the address on GovMap.
2. Turn on the plans layer and list every plan that covers the parcel, with its number.
3. Open each plan on Mavat and write its status and date of approval or deposit.
4. Decide which plan governs each question (use ss. 129–131 and the plans' "relation to other plans" section).
5. From the governing plan, copy: designation, main and service area, number of units, floors, height, building lines and public areas.
6. Check for deposited plans or s. 77 notices that may change the picture.
7. Write a half-page "plan profile" with the plan numbers and links.

### Template

None. Use the seven-step "Reading a plan in 15 minutes" list as your headings.

### How you know it worked

- Your profile lists every plan covering the parcel and which one governs.
- You can state the permitted floor area and height and where they come from.
- You know whether any deposited plan affects the parcel and when its objection period ends.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can tell an outline plan from a detailed plan.
- [ ] I can tell whether a plan is in local or district power under s. 62א.
- [ ] I found the rights table and read the setbacks.
- [ ] I know the difference between a deposited and an approved plan.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Plans and their documents are public (ss. 96, 96א), and any person may read them. Requesting "information for a permit" is part of a permit process and is normally filed through the system by the applicant or the licensed designer (עורך הבקשה). Submitting a plan yourself is legally possible for an owner (s. 61א(ב)), but plans must be drawn by qualified planners and surveyors.

### Where and how

- Online: Mavat and GovMap (⚠️ manual check); the committee's site; the committee's public reception (קבלת קהל) for older files.
- FOI requests for documents not online (lesson [14.7](../module-14/lesson-07.md)).
- Free help: community planners or neighbourhood councils in some cities; environmental NGOs for open-space and nature plans.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are buying property and a deposited plan, an expropriation or a road reservation affects it.
- A plan changes your own rights (for example, reparcellation or a public designation on your plot).
- You consider submitting a plan as an owner or with neighbours.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading only the old detailed plan | A newer plan with different rights is missed | Re-check all plans on the parcel | Use the GovMap plans layer and Mavat |
| Treating a deposited plan as approved | Wrong assumptions about rights; missed objection | Check status and dates | Always write the status next to the plan number |
| Ignoring "guiding" vs "binding" appendices | Objecting to the wrong document | Read the provisions on appendices | Check the plan's list of documents |
| Assuming the local committee approved it | Objection sent to the wrong committee | Read the deposit notice | Apply s. 62א and s. 103 |

## Review

- Plans form a hierarchy (national, district, local outline, detailed); the higher plan prevails unless it says otherwise.
- Local committees approve only plans within s. 62א (wider for independent committees); other plans go to the district committee.
- Owners and those with an interest may submit plans; deposit opens the public's chance to object.
- Mavat and GovMap show plan status, provisions and maps; "information for a permit" gives the official list of applicable plans.
