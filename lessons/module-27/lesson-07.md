# 27.7 · Local government: structure, powers, bylaws and the council

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain how Israeli local authorities are built and supervised, test whether a municipal action is within its powers, read a bylaw and check its validity, and use the rights that residents have: public meetings, published minutes, the vote, and a written request that the authority must answer.

## Why it matters

The municipality is the government most Israelis deal with most often: arnona, parking, garbage, schools, welfare, permits, licences for businesses, and public spaces. Local authorities are creatures of statute; they have only the powers the law gives them, and every decision is subject to administrative law (module 14). Knowing the legal frame lets a resident tell the difference between a policy they dislike (a matter for the council and the next election) and an unlawful act (a matter for an objection, a petition or the Interior Ministry).

Israeli local government is centralised by international standards. The Interior Minister approves budgets and bylaws, may dissolve a failing council and appoint a committee to run the authority (s. 143), and many services are funded by the State. Supporters of central control point to the weakness of small authorities, corruption cases and the need for national standards; supporters of local autonomy point to accountability to residents and local knowledge. Courts usually respect the elected council's choices but insist on equality, relevant considerations and fair procedure.

## The law

### Three kinds of authority

Cities (עיריות) are governed by the Municipalities Ordinance. Local councils (מועצות מקומיות) are governed by the Local Councils Ordinance and the Local Councils Order (A); regional councils, made of villages with their local committees (ועדים מקומיים), by the Local Councils Order (B). Many provisions of the Ordinance apply to all of them through these orders, including the arnona rules of lesson 27.8.

### Powers: s. 249

The Ordinance lists the city's powers: roads and public places, water and sewage (now largely through water corporations), sanitation, education, culture, welfare, public order, markets, parks, transport and more, and the general power to provide public services. Every power is exercised within its purpose and subject to administrative law (lessons [14.1](../module-14/lesson-01.md), [14.2](../module-14/lesson-02.md)).

### Bylaws: ss. 250–258

> מועצה רשאית להתקין חוקי עזר כדי לאפשר לעיריה ביצוע הדברים שהיא נדרשת או מוסמכת לעשותם על פי הפקודה או כל דין אחר [...]

*Gist:* A council may make bylaws to carry out what it is required or empowered to do, and may require owners or occupiers to do work for that purpose. [s. 250](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_העיריות#סעיף_250)

Bylaws may set fees, levies and participation payments and licensing (s. 251), penalties (s. 254), and works at the defaulter's expense after notice (ss. 255–256).

> לא יפורסם חוק עזר כאמור בסעיף קטן (א), אלא כעבור ששים ימים מהיום שהביא ראש העיריה את חוק העזר לידיעת השר [...]

*Gist:* A bylaw approved by the council is signed by the mayor and published in Reshumot, but only after 60 days from its delivery to the Interior Minister, who may delay, disqualify or return it with comments. [s. 258](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_העיריות#סעיף_258)

A bylaw not published in Reshumot is not in force. Opening businesses on rest days needs the minister's consent (s. 258א).

### Public meetings and published information: Second Schedule s. 18; s. 248ב

> ישיבות מועצה יהיו פומביות; למעט ישיבה שראש העיריה הורה על קיומה, כולה או מקצתה, בדלתיים סגורות לאחר שראה כי טעמים שבביטחון המדינה או טעמים שבצנעת הפרט [...]

*Gist:* Council meetings are public, except parts that the mayor closes for reasons of state security or privacy. [Second Schedule s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_העיריות#תוספת_2_פרט_18)

Each city must run a free public website publishing, among other things, minutes of open council meetings within two working days of their approval, and recordings or transcripts (s. 248ב).

### Elections: Elections Law ss. 4, 7, 11; Mayor Election Law ss. 2–3

General local elections are held every five years on the date fixed in s. 4 (in the month of Heshvan); the 2023 round was postponed by special legislation and held in February 2024 (⚠️ secondary sources). The register includes every person aged 17 by election day registered in the population register as a resident of the authority (s. 11): residence, not citizenship, is the test, so permanent residents vote. Council candidates must be 21, registered and permanently resident (s. 7). The mayor is elected in a direct, personal election on the same day (Mayor Election Law ss. 2–3).

### Supervision: s. 143

The minister may order new elections, or appoint a mayor and council or an appointed committee, if the council has ceased to function, does not perform its duties (including as local planning committee), or a commission of inquiry so recommends; not within the year before regular elections.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 4112/99 עדאלה – המרכז המשפטי לזכויות המיעוט הערבי בישראל נ' עיריית תל אביב-יפו (25.7.2002)

Petitioners asked that mixed Jewish-Arab cities (Tel Aviv-Jaffa, Ramle, Lod, Acre, Nazareth Illit) add Arabic to municipal signs. President Barak held that the cities' signage power derives from the general services power in s. 249 of the Ordinance and must be exercised in light of its purpose and of basic values, including equality and the status of Arabic; the cities had to add Arabic to signs in Arab neighbourhoods, main roads and municipal institutions within set periods. Justice Dorner concurred; Justice Cheshin dissented, holding that collective language rights are for the legislature and government. Why it matters: a general municipal power is not a blank cheque; its exercise must take equality into account. The legal status of Arabic was later addressed by Basic Law: Israel – the Nation State of the Jewish People (2018), which gave Arabic a "special status"; how that affects such cases is debated. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/541)

### בג״ץ 953/87 פורז נ' ראש עיריית תל אביב-יפו (1988)

The Tel Aviv council decided not to include women among its representatives to the assembly electing the city's chief rabbi, arguing that a rabbi elected by women would not gain cooperation from the Chief Rabbinate. The High Court cancelled the decision: equality between men and women is a basic principle, and the council gave it no weight at all and relied on improper considerations. Why it matters: a council's vote is an administrative act; it can be struck down for discrimination even when it is a political choice. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/בג%22ץ_פורז_נגד_להט) (secondary).

## How it works in practice

### Where to find what

| You want | Where |
|---|---|
| A bylaw's current text | Reshumot (קובץ חוקי עזר), the city's website, Wikisource for some cities |
| Council agenda and minutes | The city's website (s. 248ב) |
| Budget and audit reports | The city's website; the State Comptroller's local-government reports |
| Your voter registration | The Interior Ministry's online check (Elections Law s. 15; ⚠️ manual check) |
| A decision affecting you | Written request to the department, then FOI (lesson [14.7](../module-14/lesson-07.md)) and Ombudsman (lesson [14.8](../module-14/lesson-08.md)) |

### Challenging a municipal act

1. **Is there a statutory route?** Arnona (lesson 27.8), planning (lessons 27.3–27.4), business licensing.
2. **Request to the authority**, with reasons, as a prior demand (lesson [14.10](../module-14/lesson-10.md)).
3. **Administrative petition** in the Administrative Affairs Court for most municipal decisions (First Schedule; lesson [14.9](../module-14/lesson-09.md)).
4. **Bylaw fines** (parking, cleanliness) are criminal fines: request to cancel or to be tried within the period on the notice (lesson [24.9](../module-24/lesson-09.md)).

## Worked example

Hadas Levi runs a small coffee cart in a public square in Ashdod. The municipality fines her ₪730 under a bylaw on "street trading", and orders her to stop.

1. **Find the bylaw.** Hadas finds the bylaw in Reshumot and checks its publication date: it was published in 2019, after the 60-day period (s. 258). It is in force.
2. **Power.** Street trading and public places are within the city's powers (s. 249) and bylaws (s. 250); licensing of street trading may be required (s. 251(2)).
3. **Licence.** The bylaw lets the city grant licences; Hadas never applied. She applies, citing equal treatment with two licensed carts in the same square.
4. **Fine.** Within the period on the notice she asks the prosecutor to cancel the fine on the ground that she has now applied; if refused, she decides whether to be tried.
5. **Council.** She reads the open council minutes where the licensing policy was approved, and finds the criteria; if the city refuses her on criteria it applied unequally, she sends a prior demand and considers an administrative petition.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your own local authority's website.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Identify your authority's type (city, local council, regional council) and, if regional, your village's local committee.
2. Find the agenda and minutes of the last open council meeting; note one decision that affects you.
3. Find one bylaw that affects you (parking, pets, signs, garbage, street trading) and its Reshumot publication.
4. Check: who may enforce it, what fines or works it allows, and whether it requires a licence.
5. Check your voter registration and the date of the next local elections (⚠️ manual check).
6. Draft a one-paragraph written question or request to the relevant department about the decision in step 2, citing the bylaw or the Ordinance.

### Template

[Letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md).

### How you know it worked

- You have links to a real bylaw and real minutes.
- Your request names the legal source of the authority's power.
- You know which route (statutory, request, petition, fine procedure) fits the matter.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Writing to the authority, attending meetings, reading minutes, FOI requests and Ombudsman complaints are all for residents to do in their own name. Petitions against a municipality may be filed in person (general rule of self-representation), and the fine procedures are open to the person fined. Lobbying councillors is political activity, not legal representation.

### Where and how

- The authority's website and public reception; the city's ombudsman (נציב תלונות הציבור העירוני) where one exists (⚠️ manual check).
- The Interior Ministry's district office for supervision complaints (⚠️ manual check).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You face a demolition, a closure order or a large debt claim from the authority.
- You consider a petition against a council decision on a tender, a large project or elections (election petitions have their own short periods).
- The authority sues you in a civil court.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on a bylaw draft from the city site | It may not be in force | Check Reshumot | Always confirm publication |
| Treating a policy disagreement as illegality | Petition dismissed | Use the council and elections | Ask "which legal ground?" first |
| Petitioning the High Court on a municipal decision | Transferred; time lost | File in the Administrative Affairs Court | Check the First Schedule |
| Ignoring a bylaw fine notice | Fine doubles; enforcement | Ask for late cancellation with reasons | Diarise the notice periods |

## Review

- Cities, local councils and regional councils are statutory bodies with only the powers the law gives them, under close ministerial supervision.
- Bylaws are made by the council, reviewed by the minister for 60 days and in force only after publication in Reshumot.
- Council meetings are public and minutes must be published; residents aged 17 vote, candidates must be 21.
- General powers must be used for their purpose and with equality (Adalah; Poraz).
