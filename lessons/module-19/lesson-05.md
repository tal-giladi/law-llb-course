# 19.5 · International courts and tribunals: the ICJ and the ICC (overview)

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain what the International Court of Justice and the International Criminal Court can and cannot do, read their documents correctly (an order is not a judgment; an advisory opinion is not binding in the same way), and write a short, neutral note on where a case involving Israel stands. This is an overview: the merits of the armed-conflict cases belong to international humanitarian law and international criminal law, year-4 electives.

## Why it matters

Since 2023 the ICJ and the ICC have been in Israeli news almost weekly, and both are routinely misdescribed. One headline says the ICJ "found Israel guilty"; another says it "rejected the case". Neither is accurate about a provisional-measures order. An Israeli law graduate is expected to read the documents themselves: who the parties are, where the jurisdiction comes from, what kind of decision was given, and what the next procedural step is. The same skill applies to any international case, from maritime boundaries to climate change.

These cases are also the hardest test of the course's neutrality rule. In this lesson each contested point is presented as "Israel's position" and "the opposing position", with attribution, followed by the procedural status as of October 2026. The course takes no side on the merits.

## The law

### The ICJ: consent is everything

The ICJ is the UN's principal judicial organ; all UN members are parties to its Statute. It hears disputes between States only, and only with their consent, given in one of three ways (Statute art. 36): a special agreement for the case; a compromissory clause in a treaty (for example, art. IX of the Genocide Convention, which gives the ICJ jurisdiction over disputes about the Convention); or a declaration accepting compulsory jurisdiction:

> The states parties to the present Statute may at any time declare that they recognize as compulsory ipso facto and without special agreement, in relation to any other state accepting the same obligation, the jurisdiction of the Court in all legal disputes concerning: …

*Gist:* States may accept the Court's jurisdiction in advance, on a reciprocal basis. ([ICJ Statute art. 36(2)](https://www.icj-cij.org/statute)) Israel has no such declaration in force; it is not among the States on the ICJ's list of declarations. Its only exposure to contentious cases is therefore through compromissory clauses in treaties it ratified without reservation to them, such as the Genocide Convention.

### Three kinds of ICJ decision

- **Judgments** on jurisdiction or merits bind the parties (art. 59), and UN members undertake to comply:

> Each Member of the United Nations undertakes to comply with the decision of the International Court of Justice in any case to which it is a party.

*Gist:* Compliance is a Charter obligation; under art. 94(2) the other party may go to the Security Council, where permanent members have a veto. ([UN Charter art. 94](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text))

- **Provisional-measures orders** (Statute art. 41) protect the rights claimed until the final judgment. The Court has held since *LaGrand* (2001) that they are binding. They require only *prima facie* jurisdiction, a link between the measures and rights that are "plausible", and urgency. They are not findings that a violation occurred.
- **Advisory opinions**:

> The Court may give an advisory opinion on any legal question at the request of whatever body may be authorized by or in accordance with the Charter of the United Nations to make such a request.

*Gist:* The General Assembly, the Security Council and authorised agencies may ask the Court for its view on a legal question. ([ICJ Statute art. 65(1)](https://www.icj-cij.org/statute); [UN Charter art. 96](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text)) An advisory opinion is not binding on States as such; it carries great legal authority as the Court's statement of the law, and the underlying rules it identifies bind States independently. States differ on how much weight to give it, and that difference is part of the Israeli debate below.

### The ICC: a criminal court for individuals

> It shall be a permanent institution and shall have the power to exercise its jurisdiction over persons for the most serious crimes of international concern, as referred to in this Statute, and shall be complementary to national criminal jurisdictions.

*Gist:* The ICC tries individuals, not States, for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and aggression, and only as a complement to national courts. ([Rome Statute art. 1](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/2024-05/Rome-Statute-eng.pdf))

**Preconditions (art. 12(2)).** Unless the Security Council refers a situation, the Court may act only if a State Party (or a State that accepted jurisdiction by declaration under art. 12(3)) is either:

> (a) The State on the territory of which the conduct in question occurred or, if the crime was committed on board a vessel or aircraft, the State of registration of that vessel or aircraft;
> (b) The State of which the person accused of the crime is a national.

*Gist:* Territory or nationality of a party State is enough; the consent of the accused's State is not required where the conduct occurred on a party's territory. ([Rome Statute art. 12(2)](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/2024-05/Rome-Statute-eng.pdf))

**Complementarity (art. 17(1)(a)).** A case is inadmissible where

> The case is being investigated or prosecuted by a State which has jurisdiction over it, unless the State is unwilling or unable genuinely to carry out the investigation or prosecution;

*Gist:* Genuine national proceedings against the same person for substantially the same conduct take precedence over the ICC. ([Rome Statute art. 17](https://www.icc-cpi.int/sites/default/files/2024-05/Rome-Statute-eng.pdf)) Article 18 lets a State ask the Prosecutor to defer to its own investigation after notification; article 19 lets a State challenge jurisdiction or admissibility.

**Official capacity (art. 27).** The Statute applies "equally to all persons without any distinction based on official capacity", including heads of State and government. Whether States not party to the Statute must respect that rule when asked to arrest another non-party's leader is disputed (compare the personal immunity in *Arrest Warrant*, lesson 19.4).

**Israel and the Rome Statute.** Israel signed on 31 December 2000 and on 28 August 2002 notified the UN Secretary-General that it "does not intend to become a party to the treaty" and has no legal obligations arising from its signature (the art. 18 VCLT exit, lesson 19.2). The State of Palestine acceded on 2 January 2015. The UN Treaty Collection lists about 125 parties (checked October 2026).

## Leading cases

Each entry: what the court decided, Israel's official legal position, the opposing legal position, and the procedural status as of October 2026. Statements of position are attributed; the course does not evaluate them.

### Legal Consequences of the Construction of a Wall (advisory opinion, 9.7.2004) and בג״ץ 7957/04 מראעבה נ׳ ראש ממשלת ישראל (15.9.2005)

*Decision.* At the General Assembly's request, the ICJ advised that the construction of the barrier in the West Bank and East Jerusalem was contrary to international law, that Israel should cease construction and dismantle it there, and make reparation. *Israel's position:* Israel argued in writing that the Court should decline to give the opinion and did not take part in the oral hearings; the barrier is a temporary security measure against suicide bombings, a right of self-defence, and its route is reviewed by the Israeli Supreme Court case by case. *Opposing position:* Palestine and most participating States argued that the route inside the West Bank serves settlements and annexation and breaches the right to self-determination and humanitarian law. *The Israeli court's answer:* in Mara'abe the Supreme Court held that it would give "full appropriate weight" to international-law norms as interpreted by the ICJ, but that the opinion, based on a different factual record, is not a judicial decision binding the Israeli court to find the whole barrier unlawful; it continued to review each segment for proportionality and ordered the State to reconsider the route around Alfei Menashe. [ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/131); [Mara'abe, English](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/252)

### Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the OPT (advisory opinion, 19.7.2024)

*Decision.* By majority, the Court advised that Israel's continued presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territory is unlawful; that Israel must end it as rapidly as possible, cease new settlement activity and make reparation; and that all States and the UN must not recognise the situation as legal. Vice-President Sebutinde dissented and several judges wrote separately. *Israel's position:* the Government rejected the opinion; the Prime Minister stated that the Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land; Israel argued that the questions were one-sided, that the Court lacked the security context, and that the issues must be resolved in negotiations under the Oslo framework and Security Council resolutions 242 and 338. *Opposing position:* Palestine, most participating States and the General Assembly treat the opinion as an authoritative statement of the law; in September 2024 the Assembly demanded that Israel end its presence within twelve months (resolution ES-10/24). *Status:* an advisory opinion has no further procedural stage; follow-up is political, in the UN organs. [ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/186)

### Obligations of Israel in relation to the UN and UNRWA (advisory opinion, 22.10.2025)

*Decision.* Requested by the General Assembly after Israel's 2024 laws restricting UNRWA's operations, the Court advised that Israel, as occupying power and UN member, must facilitate relief by the UN, including UNRWA, and respect UN privileges and immunities; it found that Israel's allegations that UNRWA lacked neutrality had not been substantiated. *Israel's position:* the Foreign Ministry categorically rejected the opinion, maintaining that UNRWA staff took part in the 7 October 2023 attack and that Israel is not obliged to cooperate with an organisation it considers infiltrated by Hamas, and that aid is delivered through other channels. *Opposing position:* the UN, UNRWA and most participating States see the opinion as confirming binding Charter and humanitarian obligations. *Status:* transmitted to the General Assembly; no further judicial stage. [ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/196)

### Application of the Genocide Convention in the Gaza Strip (South Africa v. Israel), contentious case, filed 29.12.2023

*Jurisdiction.* South Africa relies on art. IX of the Genocide Convention, to which both States are party. *Orders so far.* Provisional-measures orders of 26 January 2024, 28 March 2024 and 24 May 2024. In January 2024 the Court found that at least some of the rights South Africa claimed under the Convention were plausible and ordered Israel to take all measures within its power to prevent acts within art. II, to prevent and punish incitement, to enable humanitarian assistance, to preserve evidence and to report; it did not order a ceasefire and made no finding that genocide occurred. The May 2024 order addressed the military offensive in Rafah; Israel reads that order as limited to operations that could inflict on the group conditions of life bringing about its physical destruction, while South Africa and others read it as ordering the offensive to halt. *Israel's position:* the case is baseless; Israel acts in self-defence after the 7 October 2023 attack, in accordance with international law, against Hamas and not the Palestinian population; there is no genocidal intent; Hamas bears responsibility for civilian harm. *Opposing position:* South Africa alleges that Israel's conduct in Gaza violates the Convention, relying on the scale of death and destruction, restrictions on aid and statements by Israeli officials as evidence of intent. *Status (October 2026):* Israel's Counter-Memorial was due on 12 March 2026 (after two extensions) and, according to South African official statements, includes objections to jurisdiction; by Order of 21 May 2026 the Court fixed 22 November 2027 for South Africa's Reply and 22 May 2029 for Israel's Rejoinder. Many States have filed declarations of intervention under art. 63 (on interpretation of the Convention), including in 2026 the United States, the Netherlands, Hungary and others; Colombia withdrew its declaration in September 2026. No merits hearing is scheduled. [ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/192)

### ICC, Situation in the State of Palestine (ICC-01/18)

*History.* Palestine referred the situation in 2018; in February 2021 Pre-Trial Chamber I held by majority that the Court's territorial jurisdiction extends to Gaza and the West Bank, including East Jerusalem; the investigation opened in March 2021. On 21 November 2024 the Chamber rejected Israel's challenges under arts 18 and 19 (holding the jurisdiction challenge premature) and issued arrest warrants for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former Defence Minister Yoav Gallant. On 24 April 2025 the Appeals Chamber reversed the ruling on the jurisdiction challenge and remanded it for a decision on the merits. The Chamber declined to withdraw or suspend the warrants pending that decision (16 July and 17 October 2025), and on 15 December 2025 the Appeals Chamber, by majority, upheld the ruling that the post-7 October 2023 investigation falls within the existing situation and needed no new art. 18 notification. *Israel's position:* the ICC lacks jurisdiction because Israel is not a party and Palestine is not a sovereign State capable of delegating criminal jurisdiction, which under the Oslo Accords it never had over Israelis; Israel's independent legal system investigates allegations, so complementarity bars the case; and the procedure was flawed. Israel has also asked to disqualify the Prosecutor. The United States shares Israel's objection to jurisdiction over non-parties' nationals. *Opposing position:* the Prosecutor, Palestine and many States Parties hold that Palestine's accession was valid, that art. 12(2)(a) territorial jurisdiction does not depend on the accused's State, and that complementarity requires genuine national proceedings against the same persons for the same conduct. *Status:* as last confirmed from free sources (January 2026), the warrants remain in force, the remanded jurisdiction challenge and motions on Palestine's status and on the Prosecutor were pending; ⚠️ check the ICC's situation page for any 2026 ruling before relying on this. The ICC does not try accused persons in their absence. [Rome Statute status (UN)](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XVIII-10&chapter=18&clang=_en); [ASIL report, December 2025](https://asil.org/ilib/icc-appeals-chamber-upholds-article-181-decision-palestine-situation/); [Just Security analysis, January 2026](https://www.justsecurity.org/128238/complementarity-icc-israels-admissibility-challenge/)

## How it works in practice

### Reading an ICJ or ICC document in five questions

1. **Which court and which procedure?** ICJ contentious, ICJ advisory, ICC situation, ICC case.
2. **What kind of decision?** Judgment, order (provisional measures, time-limits), advisory opinion; ICC warrant, admissibility decision, appeal judgment.
3. **What exactly was decided?** Read the operative paragraph (the "dispositif") at the end, with vote counts; ignore headlines.
4. **Whom does it bind, and how?** Parties only (art. 59); provisional measures binding; advisory opinion authoritative but not binding as such; ICC warrants bind States Parties to cooperate.
5. **What is the next step and date?** Time-limit orders on the ICJ case page; ICC court records.

### Where to read

ICJ case pages (icj-cij.org/case/NNN) carry every order, judgment, press release and pleading, free. ICC court records are on icc-cpi.int (⚠️ the site sometimes blocks automated access; open it manually). The UN's UNISPAL site reproduces many documents. Israel's positions are published by the Foreign Ministry on gov.il (⚠️ manual check).

## Worked example

Dalia Cohen, a civics teacher in Modi'in, and Rami Nassar, a law student from Shfar'am, are asked by their community centre to explain "what the ICJ decided about Rafah". They apply the five questions to the 24 May 2024 order.

1. **Procedure:** contentious case, provisional measures, not a merits judgment.
2. **Decision type:** an order modifying earlier measures.
3. **Operative paragraph:** they read the Rafah paragraph in full, including its qualifying clause about conditions of life that could bring about physical destruction, and note the votes.
4. **Effect:** binding on Israel as a provisional measure; no finding of breach of the Convention.
5. **Next step:** written pleadings until 2029 (Order of 21 May 2026).

Their handout gives the text, then two attributed readings: Israel's (the order restricts only operations meeting the qualifying clause) and South Africa's (the order requires the offensive to stop), and adds that judges' declarations differed on the point. They end with the procedural stage. Members of the audience with opposite views both accept the handout as fair.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One pending international case (ICJ or ICC), involving Israel or not (other options: climate-change advisory opinion, a maritime-boundary case).
- The case page and the latest order or decision. 35 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Identify (5 min):** court, parties, filing date, basis of jurisdiction.
2. **Decisions so far (10 min):** list each with date and type; copy the operative words of the latest one.
3. **Positions (10 min):** one paragraph for each side, each sentence attributed ("Israel argues…", "South Africa argues…"), using their own filings or official statements.
4. **Status (5 min):** current stage and next date.
5. **Neutrality check (5 min):** delete every adjective that evaluates (e.g., "baseless", "brutal") unless it is inside a quotation attributed to a party.

### Template

None. Keep the note in your module 19 file.

### How you know it worked

- A reader cannot tell your own view from the note.
- You quoted the operative paragraph, not a press summary.
- You gave the next procedural date with its source.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can name the three routes to ICJ jurisdiction and say which applies to Israel.
- [ ] I can distinguish a provisional-measures order from a merits judgment and an advisory opinion.
- [ ] I can explain art. 12(2) and art. 17 of the Rome Statute.
- [ ] I can state Israel's jurisdiction argument against the ICC and the opposing argument.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Individuals cannot bring cases to the ICJ; only States (contentious) and authorised UN bodies (advisory) can. Individuals may send information to the ICC Prosecutor (art. 15 communications), but this course does not cover that. Where the skill shows up in practice: reading international decisions accurately in your own correspondence, teaching, journalism or advocacy, and recognising that an Israeli court treats an advisory opinion as weighty but not binding (Mara'abe, lesson 19.7).

### Where and how

- ICJ and ICC sites (free); the UN Audiovisual Library lectures (Learn more).
- For personal legal exposure abroad (travel and arrest warrants), see the next box.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You, as an individual, may be personally affected by proceedings abroad (a warrant, a universal-jurisdiction complaint, a travel concern related to service in the security forces): consult a criminal lawyer with international experience; the Ministry of Justice and the IDF Military Advocate General's office give guidance to service members (⚠️ manual check of current channels).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling a provisional-measures order a "ruling that Israel committed genocide" | A legally false statement | Describe it as an interim order with no finding of breach | Read the operative paragraph |
| Calling an advisory opinion "binding on Israel" or "meaningless" | Both overstate | Explain its authority and the competing views | Use the art. 65 and Mara'abe framing |
| Forgetting consent at the ICJ | Wrong prediction of whether a case can proceed | Find the compromissory clause | Check art. 36 routes first |
| Assuming the ICC needs Israel's consent | You misstate the jurisdiction debate | Explain art. 12(2)(a) and Israel's objection | Present both positions |
| Using a status from months ago | Outdated facts | Check the case page before writing | Date every status statement |

## Review

- The ICJ decides disputes between consenting States; Israel has no optional-clause declaration, so its cases come through treaty clauses (Genocide Convention art. IX) or advisory requests.
- Judgments bind parties; provisional measures bind but find no breach; advisory opinions are authoritative but not binding as such.
- The ICC tries individuals, needs territorial or nationality jurisdiction of a party State (or a Security Council referral), and yields to genuine national proceedings.
- On every Israel-related case, state Israel's position and the opposing position with attribution, then the procedural stage and date.
- As of October 2026: South Africa v. Israel is in written pleadings until 2029; the ICC warrants were in force with Israel's jurisdiction challenge on remand at last check.
