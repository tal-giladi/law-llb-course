# 19.8 · Practice lab: use a treaty in an Israeli court argument

In this lab you will write the international-law section of a real-format written argument to an Israeli court or tribunal, using one of three treaties Israel has ratified, and then say it aloud in two minutes. You choose one track: a child-abduction return (an incorporated treaty), a pupil's appeal against expulsion (the CRC through a statute's purpose clause), or an inclusion dispute (the CRPD through the presumption of compatibility). None of the tracks involves the armed conflict, by design.

## Why it matters

Lessons 19.2 to 19.7 gave you the parts: the treaty's status, its interpretation, the channel into Israeli law and the cases. Judges and hearing committees see many arguments that wave at "international conventions" and few that do it properly. A short, precise section, built on Israeli law and using the treaty in the way Israeli courts accept, is the difference between an argument that is ignored and one that is quoted back in the decision. This lab builds that section, in Hebrew, on the [treaty argument worksheet](../../templates/treaty-argument-worksheet.md).

## The law

### Track A: the Hague Abduction Convention (incorporated)

> לסעיפי האמנה שנוסחם מובא בתוספת יהיה תוקף של חוק, והם יחולו על אף האמור בכל דין, …

*Gist:* The Convention's articles, set out in the Schedule, have the force of law and apply notwithstanding any other law. ([Hague Convention Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אמנת_האג_(החזרת_ילדים_חטופים))) The Family Court has jurisdiction (s. 6); the Attorney General is the central authority (s. 4). The treaty itself is Israeli law, so you apply it directly.

> Where a child has been wrongfully removed or retained in terms of Article 3 and, at the date of the commencement of the proceedings before the judicial or administrative authority of the Contracting State where the child is, a period of less than one year has elapsed from the date of the wrongful removal or retention, the authority concerned shall order the return of the child forthwith.

*Gist:* Within one year of the wrongful removal or retention, return is mandatory; after a year, return is still ordered unless the child is settled in the new environment. ([Convention art. 12](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/full-text/?cid=24); Hebrew text in the Schedule)

> there is a grave risk that his or her return would expose the child to physical or psychological harm or otherwise place the child in an intolerable situation.

> The judicial or administrative authority may also refuse to order the return of the child if it finds that the child objects to being returned and has attained an age and degree of maturity at which it is appropriate to take account of its views.

*Gist:* The court need not order return where the other parent consented or acquiesced (art. 13(a)), where there is a grave risk of harm (art. 13(b)), or where a mature child objects (art. 13, second paragraph); art. 20 adds a narrow human-rights exception. ([Convention art. 13](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/full-text/?cid=24)) The Convention applies only to children under 16 (art. 4).

### Track B: the CRC through the Pupils' Rights Law

> חוק זה מטרתו לקבוע עקרונות לזכויות התלמיד ברוח כבוד האדם ועקרונות אמנת האומות המאוחדות בדבר זכויות הילד, …

*Gist:* The Law's stated purpose is to set pupils' rights in the spirit of human dignity and the principles of the CRC. ([Pupils' Rights Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_התלמיד))

> החלטה בדבר הרחקת תלמיד ממוסד חינוך לצמיתות והעברתו למוסד אחר תינתן רק לאחר שניתנה לתלמיד ולהוריו הזדמנות להשמיע את טענותיהם; …
> (ב) החלטה לפי סעיף קטן (א) תהיה בכתב ומנומקת.

*Gist:* A permanent expulsion may be decided only after the pupil and parents had an opportunity to be heard; the decision must be written and reasoned, and the parents and pupil may appeal in writing to a hearing committee within a short period ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); pending the appeal the pupil is not expelled, save for an immediate removal approved by the district supervisor. ([Pupils' Rights Law s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_התלמיד)) Section 10 adds that discipline must respect human dignity, with no physical or degrading measures. The CRC enters through s. 1 and the presumption: arts 3(1) (best interests), 12 (the child's own right to be heard, not only the parents'), 28(2) (school discipline consistent with dignity).

### Track C: the CRPD through the presumption

> 2. In realizing this right, States Parties shall ensure that: (a) Persons with disabilities are not excluded from the general education system on the basis of disability, … (c) Reasonable accommodation of the individual's requirements is provided;

*Gist:* No exclusion from general education because of disability; reasonable accommodation. ([CRPD art. 24(2)](https://www.un.org/disabilities/documents/convention/convoptprot-e.pdf)) Israel ratified the CRPD in 2012; it is not incorporated, so it works through the presumption of compatibility in reading the Special Education Law and the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law (Yated para. 8).

## Leading cases

### בע״מ 741/11 פלונית נ׳ פלוני (17.5.2011)

A mother kept her daughter in Israel; the Family Court and, by majority, the District Court ordered the girl's return to New Jersey. The Supreme Court (Arbel J., Melcer J. concurring; Vogelman J. dissenting) allowed the appeal. It restated the Convention's purposes (inter-State cooperation, the rule of law, deterring self-help and the child's interest in not being uprooted), held that the exceptions must be read narrowly and applied with caution, but found that the father had consented: a separation agreement showed that the parties agreed the mother and daughter would live in Israel. The Court set guides for consent and acquiescence: they need not be explicit, contract-law principles help, and the reasons for consent and the time that passed matter. Why it matters: it is a model of applying an incorporated treaty: the text, its purposes, narrow exceptions, and facts proved by documents. (Hebrew party names are anonymised; ⚠️ the "פלונית נ׳ פלוני" rendering is unconfirmed.) [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/136)

### בג״ץ 2599/00 יתד נ׳ משרד החינוך (14.8.2002) and ע״פ 4596/98 פלונית נ׳ מדינת ישראל (25.1.2000)

Recaps from lessons 19.7 and 19.6. Yated states the presumption of compatibility (para. 8) and used the ICESCR and the CRC to read the Special Education Law in favour of integrated children; CrimA 4596/98 used the CRC to give content to what force toward children is lawful. Why they matter here: they are the two authorities your Track B or C argument cites for using the CRC or CRPD in an Israeli forum. [Yated](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yated-v-ministry-education); [CrimA 4596/98](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/146)

## How it works in practice

### Where each track is heard

- **Track A:** a return petition in the Family Court of the district where the child is; it is heard urgently and is exempt from court fees ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). You may also apply through the central authority, the Ministry of Justice department acting for the Attorney General (⚠️ manual check of the current gov.il page). It is outside the family dispute-resolution stage (lesson [18.9](../module-18/lesson-09.md)).
- **Track B:** a written appeal (ערר) to the hearing committee under the Pupils' Rights Law, within the period in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); then, if needed, an administrative petition (lesson [14.11](../module-14/lesson-11.md)).
- **Track C:** a letter to the local authority and the ministry's district office; a placement committee appeal; or an administrative petition. In court, the same section goes into the petition.

### The structure of the section (any track)

1. **Heading:** "המסגרת הנורמטיבית: הדין הישראלי והמשפט הבינלאומי".
2. **Israeli norm first:** the statute and section the forum applies.
3. **The treaty and its channel:** name, Israel's ratification date, no relevant reservation; incorporated (Track A) or presumption (Tracks B, C).
4. **The articles:** short quotation, Hebrew where an official Hebrew text exists (the Schedule for Track A; Kitvei Amana for the CRC and CRPD, ⚠️ manual check), English otherwise.
5. **Interpretation:** VCLT art. 31 purpose, a general comment or leading foreign decision, and Israeli authority.
6. **Application to the facts:** one paragraph per element.
7. **The other side's best argument and your reply.**
8. **Relief requested,** in the forum's terms.

## Worked example

**Track B, illustrated.** Yara Haddad, 15, a pupil at a state school in Nazareth Illit (Nof HaGalil), is told by the principal that she will be permanently moved to another school after a fight. Her parents were invited to a meeting; Yara herself was told to wait outside. The decision letter is one line long.

1. **Israeli norm:** Pupils' Rights Law s. 6(א): opportunity "לתלמיד ולהוריו" to be heard; s. 6(ב): written, reasoned decision; s. 6(ג)–(ד): appeal and no expulsion pending appeal.
2. **Channel:** the CRC (ratified 1991, no reservation on arts 3 or 12) through s. 1 and the presumption (Yated para. 8).
3. **Articles:** CRC art. 12(1)–(2): the child's own right to be heard in administrative proceedings affecting her; art. 3(1): best interests; art. 28(2): discipline consistent with dignity.
4. **Application:** s. 6(א) names the pupil and the parents; read with CRC art. 12, the pupil must be heard herself, not only through her parents; excluding Yara from the meeting breaches s. 6(א). The one-line decision is not "מנומקת" (s. 6(ב)); lesson [14.4](../module-14/lesson-04.md) on reasons applies by analogy.
5. **Other side:** "the parents spoke for her". Reply: s. 6(א) lists both; the CRC purpose clause confirms that the child's voice is distinct.
6. **Relief:** cancel the decision, or return the matter for a new hearing with Yara present and a reasoned decision; meanwhile she stays in school (s. 6(ד)(1)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One track and its facts: your own situation (a child, a pupil, a family member), or the supplied facts below.
- The statute and treaty texts (links above), the [treaty argument worksheet](../../templates/treaty-argument-worksheet.md), and the cases. About 150 minutes.

**Supplied facts, Track A.** Ruth and Daniel Levi, Israelis, lived with their son Eitan (8) in Toronto from 2021. In May 2026 Ruth flew with Eitan to Israel "for the summer"; on 1 August 2026 she told Daniel she was staying. Daniel files a return petition on 20 September 2026. Ruth says Daniel had agreed by WhatsApp in June to "think about Israel", and that Eitan, who has started school in Ra'anana, does not want to return. Write Daniel's section (or Ruth's: choose one side and state it).

**Supplied facts, Track C.** Nadav, 9, from Kiryat Gat, has cerebral palsy and uses a walker. His school is on two floors and his class was put upstairs; the school proposes moving him to a special-education class elsewhere "for his safety". His parents want him to stay, with his class moved downstairs.

### Steps

1. **Facts and dates (15 min).** A dated chronology; for Track A, compute the one-year period from the wrongful retention date (not the date of travel).
2. **Israeli norm (15 min).** Copy the governing sections word for word.
3. **Worksheet part A (15 min).** Treaty status, ratification, reservations, incorporation.
4. **Worksheet part B (20 min).** The articles and their interpretation (purpose, general comment or the Court's case law).
5. **Worksheet part C (15 min).** Channel and, for Tracks B and C, the two readings and the presumption.
6. **Draft the section (45 min).** In Hebrew, 1–2 pages, using the eight-part structure above.
7. **Rebuttal (10 min).** Write the other side's best paragraph, then your reply.
8. **Oral version (15 min).** Two minutes aloud: Israeli norm, treaty, application, relief. Record yourself and listen once.

### How you know it worked

- Your section begins with an Israeli statute, not with the treaty.
- You used the right channel: "the Convention has force of law" (Track A) or "read in harmony" (Tracks B, C), never the other way round.
- You quoted at most three short treaty passages and applied each to a fact.
- You addressed the strongest counter-argument by name.
- Your oral version fits two minutes.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

- **Track A:** partly. A parent may file and appear in person in the Family Court ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)), and the central authority assists applicants. But return cases are fast, high-stakes and often cross-border; most parents use a lawyer, and Legal Aid may be available ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).
- **Track B:** yes. Parents and the pupil write the appeal themselves; no lawyer is needed before the hearing committee.
- **Track C:** yes for letters and committee appeals; an administrative petition may be filed in your own name (lesson 14.11).

### Where and how

- Track A: Family Court of the child's district; central authority (Ministry of Justice, ⚠️ manual check on gov.il); fee exemption ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Track B: the hearing committee under s. 6(ג); the ministry's district office; the period in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Track C: municipal education department, ministry district office, disability-rights organisations; the Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Track A: the other parent alleges violence or grave risk (art. 13(b)); a child has already been taken abroad; or proceedings are running in two countries.
- Track B: the school also reports the incident to the police (criminal exposure for a minor).
- Track C: the authority relies on a clear statutory rule (the presumption fails) and an interim order is needed.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counting the Hague one-year period from the trip, not the retention | Wrong conclusion on mandatory return | Recompute from the date retention became wrongful | Write the chronology first |
| Arguing "best interests" at large in a return case | The court says the Convention decides forum, not custody | Argue the art. 3, 12 and 13 elements | Remember the Convention's purposes (741/11) |
| Treating the CRC as directly enforceable before the hearing committee | Committee applies the Pupils' Rights Law | Use s. 1 and the presumption | Lead with s. 6 |
| Quoting long treaty passages | The section is skimmed | Quote the key words; apply them | Three quotations at most |
| Ignoring the other side's best argument | It wins by default | Add a rebuttal paragraph | Step 7 of the lab |

## Review

- Lead with Israeli law; bring the treaty through the right channel.
- Incorporated treaties (Hague Abduction Convention Law s. 2) are applied directly; unincorporated ones (CRC, CRPD) through purpose clauses and the presumption (Yated para. 8).
- In return cases, the art. 13 exceptions are narrow, and consent must be proved (LFA 741/11).
- The CRC gives the child an independent right to be heard (art. 12), which supports reading "the pupil and the parents" in s. 6 literally.
- A good section is short, quoted precisely, applied to facts, and answers the other side.
