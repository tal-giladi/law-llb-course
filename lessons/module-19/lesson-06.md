# 19.6 · Human-rights treaties and the UN system

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find out exactly which human-rights treaties bind Israel, read what the UN committees have said about Israel's compliance, and use that material properly: as authoritative interpretation and evidence in a letter or argument to an Israeli authority, not as a ruling that the authority must obey.

## Why it matters

Israel's Basic Laws protect dignity, liberty, property, occupation and (through interpretation) equality and expression (lesson [06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md)). Israel's human-rights treaties go further in several areas that Israeli statutes cover only partly: social rights such as education and health (ICESCR, CRC), the child's right to be heard (CRC art. 12), inclusive education and reasonable accommodation for people with disabilities (CRPD), and non-discrimination against women and minorities (CEDAW, CERD). Israeli courts cite these treaties often, mainly through the interpretive presumption explained in lesson 19.7.

The UN system around the treaties is also part of the picture: committees of independent experts review Israel's periodic reports and publish recommendations; the Human Rights Council and its special rapporteurs and commissions of inquiry report on Israel frequently; and Israel's relationship with these bodies is itself contested. A careful practitioner knows the difference between a treaty obligation, a committee's interpretation of it, and a political resolution.

| CRC Optional Protocol on sale of children | 14.11.2001 | 23.7.2008 |
| CRPD (persons with disabilities) | 30.3.2007 | 28.9.2012 |

Not ratified: the Convention on Enforced Disappearance, the Migrant Workers Convention, the Optional Protocol to CAT and the Second Optional Protocol to the ICCPR. **Israel has not accepted any individual-complaint procedure** (ICCPR First Optional Protocol, CEDAW, ICESCR, CRC and CRPD optional protocols; CAT art. 22, CERD art. 14), and it declared on ratifying CAT that it does not accept the CAT Committee's inquiry procedure (art. 20). So no person in Israel can bring an individual complaint against Israel to a UN treaty body. ([OHCHR ratification table](https://tbinternet.ohchr.org/_layouts/15/TreatyBodyExternal/Treaty.aspx?CountryID=84&Lang=EN)) Reservations are not shown in that table; read them on the UN Treaty Collection (lesson 19.2, CEDAW).

### The core obligations

> Each State Party to the present Covenant undertakes to respect and to ensure to all individuals within its territory and subject to its jurisdiction the rights recognized in the present Covenant, without distinction of any kind, such as race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, property, birth or other status.

*Gist:* Civil and political rights must be respected and ensured, without discrimination, for everyone within the State's territory and subject to its jurisdiction. ([ICCPR art. 2(1)](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-civil-and-political-rights))

> Each State Party to the present Covenant undertakes to take steps, individually and through international assistance and co-operation, especially economic and technical, to the maximum of its available resources, with a view to achieving progressively the full realization of the rights recognized in the present Covenant by all appropriate means, including particularly the adoption of legislative measures.

*Gist:* Social and economic rights are realised progressively within available resources, but the duty to take steps, and the duty not to discriminate, are immediate. ([ICESCR art. 2(1)](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/international-covenant-economic-social-and-cultural-rights))

> In all actions concerning children, whether undertaken by public or private social welfare institutions, courts of law, administrative authorities or legislative bodies, the best interests of the child shall be a primary consideration.

*Gist:* Every court, authority and legislature must treat the child's best interests as a primary consideration. ([CRC art. 3(1)](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))

> States Parties shall assure to the child who is capable of forming his or her own views the right to express those views freely in all matters affecting the child, the views of the child being given due weight in accordance with the age and maturity of the child.

*Gist:* A child able to form views has the right to express them in all matters affecting him or her, and to have them weighed by age and maturity; art. 12(2) extends this to judicial and administrative proceedings. ([CRC art. 12(1)](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))

> 2. In realizing this right, States Parties shall ensure that: (a) Persons with disabilities are not excluded from the general education system on the basis of disability, … (c) Reasonable accommodation of the individual's requirements is provided;

*Gist:* Children with disabilities may not be excluded from general education because of disability, and must receive reasonable accommodation. ([CRPD art. 24(2)](https://www.un.org/disabilities/documents/convention/convoptprot-e.pdf))

### Where Israeli law refers to a treaty in its own words

> חוק זה מטרתו לקבוע עקרונות לזכויות התלמיד ברוח כבוד האדם ועקרונות אמנת האומות המאוחדות בדבר זכויות הילד, …

*Gist:* The Pupils' Rights Law states that its purpose is to set principles for pupils' rights in the spirit of human dignity and the principles of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child. ([Pupils' Rights Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_התלמיד)) A purpose clause like this does not incorporate the CRC, but it makes the CRC a direct aid to interpreting every section of the Law (lesson 19.8).

### The reporting system

Each treaty requires periodic reports (ICCPR art. 40 and parallel articles). The cycle: Israel submits its report; NGOs submit shadow reports; the committee sends a list of issues; Israel's delegation appears in Geneva for a "constructive dialogue"; the committee adopts **concluding observations** with recommendations; Israel reports on follow-up. Committees also issue **general comments** interpreting the treaty for all parties. Neither is binding as such; both are the most authoritative reading of the treaty available, and Israeli courts sometimes cite them.

### The Charter-based system

- **The Human Rights Council** (47 elected States, Geneva) adopts resolutions, runs the **Universal Periodic Review** of every UN member, and appoints **special procedures** (special rapporteurs, working groups) and commissions of inquiry. Its agenda has a standing item 7 on "the human rights situation in Palestine and other occupied Arab territories", the only country-specific standing item.
- **Israel's relationship.** In February 2025 Israel announced it would stop participating in the Council, following the United States, citing item 7 and what the Foreign Minister called the Council's obsessive focus on Israel. Supporters of the Council's approach argue that the item reflects a prolonged occupation and that disengagement weakens Israel's ability to present its case. Israel continues to report to the treaty bodies, which are separate from the Council (⚠️ check the OHCHR country page for the latest Israeli report and review dates).
- **Special procedures communications.** Any person or group may send information about an alleged violation to a special rapporteur; this is not a complaint procedure and produces no binding decision.

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 4596/98 פלונית נ׳ מדינת ישראל (25.1.2000)

A mother convicted of assault and child abuse for hitting her children argued that the force was for educational purposes. The Supreme Court (Beinisch J., Barak P. concurring; Englard J. dissenting on the abuse count) held that corporal punishment for educational purposes is forbidden. In para. 28 Justice Beinisch relied on the CRC, noting its ratification by Israel in 1991, that it "expressly prohibits the use of physical or mental violence towards children", and that the child's status must be assessed "in the era after Israel became a signatory". Why it matters: an unincorporated treaty shaped the content of a criminal-law concept ("reasonable" parental force) through interpretation; it is the most cited example of the CRC's influence in Israeli courts. (The English translation gives the ratification date as 4 August 1991; OHCHR records 3 October 1991, see lesson 19.2.) [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/146)

### The territorial-scope dispute: ICJ Wall opinion (2004) and the treaty bodies

*Israel's position:* in its reports and dialogues Israel has maintained that the ICCPR and ICESCR do not apply beyond its sovereign territory, in particular to the West Bank and Gaza, and that in situations of armed conflict international humanitarian law is the governing *lex specialis*; Israel notes that its courts nonetheless review the military's actions and that the Palestinian Authority bears responsibility in areas under its control. *Opposing position:* the Human Rights Committee, the CESCR and the CRC Committee have consistently held that the treaties apply wherever Israel exercises effective control, and the ICJ in the 2004 Wall opinion advised that the ICCPR, ICESCR and CRC apply to acts done by a State in the exercise of its jurisdiction outside its own territory, alongside humanitarian law. *Status:* unchanged positions; the issue recurs at each review. [ICJ Wall case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/131)

## How it works in practice

### Using the UN material in an Israeli matter

1. **Confirm the treaty binds Israel** (table above) and check reservations.
2. **Find the article and the committee's reading:** the relevant general comment, and the latest concluding observations on Israel (OHCHR treaty body database, search "Israel", document type "Concluding observations").
3. **Find Israel's own statements:** Israel's periodic report often describes Israeli law as implementing the article. That is powerful in a letter: "the State told the CRC Committee that…".
4. **Translate into Israeli law:** link the treaty article to a statute or Basic Law the authority must apply, and ask for an interpretation consistent with the treaty (lesson 19.7).
5. **Avoid overstatement:** say "the Committee recommended" or "the Committee's view is", never "the UN ordered".

### What it costs and how long

All UN documents are free online. Treaty-body cycles run several years; a recommendation you cite may be five years old, so check whether Israel has reported since.

## Worked example

Aviva and Moshe Klein, a religious family in Petah Tikva, have a son, Ariel, 10, with an autism diagnosis. The local school will accept him only for half-days, saying it lacks an aide. They want to write to the municipal education department and the ministry's district office.

1. **Israeli law first:** the Special Education Law and its integration chapter govern the entitlement (module 27 deals with local authorities); the Supreme Court in Yated read that Law in light of the right to education (lesson 19.8).
2. **Treaty:** Israel ratified the CRPD in 2012 and the CRC in 1991, with no reservations relevant here (they check the UN Treaty Collection).
3. **Articles:** CRPD art. 24(2)(a) and (c) (no exclusion from general education; reasonable accommodation); CRC arts 3(1) and 28.
4. **UN material:** they find the CRPD Committee's general comment on inclusive education and its concluding observations on Israel (⚠️ check date and paragraph), which recommend expanding support in regular classes.
5. **Letter paragraph:** "Israel ratified the CRPD in 2012. Art. 24(2)(c) requires reasonable accommodation. The Special Education Law must be read, where possible, consistently with that obligation (HCJ 2599/00 Yated, para. 8). We ask that an aide be assigned so Ariel can attend a full day."

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One right that matters to you or your family (education, health, disability access, a child's right to be heard, equality at work).
- The OHCHR treaty body database and the treaty text. 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Pick the treaty and article (5 min).** Confirm ratification and reservations.
2. **Find interpretation (10 min).** The relevant general comment paragraph.
3. **Find Israel's review (15 min).** Israel's latest report on that treaty: what it said about your topic; then the concluding observations: what the committee recommended.
4. **Write the paragraph (10 min).** In Hebrew, four sentences: the treaty and ratification; the article; the committee's view or Israel's own statement; the request, linked to an Israeli statute.

### How you know it worked

- You cited a paragraph number of a general comment or concluding observations, with its date.
- Your request is addressed to an Israeli statute or power, not to "the UN".
- Nothing in the paragraph says the committee's view binds the authority.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Writing to an authority about your own or your child's rights and citing treaties needs no lawyer (lesson [14.4](../module-14/lesson-04.md) on reasons and hearings). Parents act for their minor children as natural guardians. Sending information to a UN special rapporteur needs no lawyer either; NGOs that write shadow reports welcome individual case information.

### Where and how

- OHCHR treaty body database and country page for Israel (free).
- Israeli human-rights and disability-rights organisations that prepare shadow reports; university human-rights clinics ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You plan to petition a court and the authority relies on a contrary statute (the presumption cannot override clear statutory language; lesson 19.7).
- Your matter is urgent (a child out of school), where an administrative petition with interim relief may be needed (lesson [14.11](../module-14/lesson-11.md)).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saying "the UN ordered Israel" | The authority dismisses the letter | Rephrase as treaty obligation plus committee view | Use exact status words |
| Citing a treaty Israel has not ratified (e.g. the Disappearances Convention) | No obligation | Find a ratified treaty or custom | Check the OHCHR table |
| Trying to file a UN individual complaint against Israel | Inadmissible: Israel accepted none | Use domestic remedies and NGO reporting | Read the complaint-procedure column |
| Citing old concluding observations as current | Outdated | Check for a later review | Date every UN citation |
| Arguing the treaty instead of the Israeli statute | The authority says it applies Israeli law | Use the treaty to interpret the statute | Always name the statute too |

## Review

- Israel has ratified the seven core treaties listed and two CRC protocols, with the CRPD in 2012, but accepted no individual-complaint procedure.
- Treaty bodies review Israel's reports and issue concluding observations and general comments: authoritative, not binding.
- The Human Rights Council, UPR and special procedures are Charter-based; Israel stopped participating in the Council in 2025, citing item 7.
- Israel's position is that the covenants do not apply outside its territory; the committees and the ICJ take the opposite view.
- In Israeli practice, treaties work mainly through interpretation (CrimA 4596/98; purpose clauses such as the Pupils' Rights Law s. 1).
