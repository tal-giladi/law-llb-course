# 19.2 · Treaties and Israel's treaty practice

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take any treaty, find out whether and from when it binds Israel, read Israel's reservations to it, interpret it the way a court would, and explain how the Government and the Knesset brought it into force. These are the facts you must have before you rely on a treaty in a letter or a court argument (lesson 19.8).

## Why it matters

Treaties are the source of international law that ordinary people meet most often. Israel is party to hundreds: human-rights covenants, the Hague conventions on child abduction and on apostilles, extradition and legal-assistance treaties, tax treaties, social-security agreements that let you add years of work abroad to your Israeli pension, aviation agreements and trade agreements. Each has a date from which it binds Israel, terms Israel may have excluded by reservation, and, crucially, a separate question of whether Israeli law has absorbed it.

Israel's practice also has a constitutional side that connects to lesson [06.5](../module-06/lesson-05.md): there is no statute on treaty-making. The Government signs and ratifies treaties under its general powers; the Knesset's role rests on custom, a Government procedure and, for treaties that give up territory, a Basic Law. Whether that is enough parliamentary control is a live debate, presented below with both sides.

## The law

### What a treaty is

> "treaty" means an international agreement concluded between States in written form and governed by international law, whether embodied in a single instrument or in two or more related instruments and whatever its particular designation;

*Gist:* The name does not matter (treaty, convention, covenant, protocol, agreement, exchange of letters); what matters is a written agreement between States intended to be governed by international law. ([VCLT art. 2(1)(a)](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf))

### Consent to be bound and the interim obligation

> "ratification", "acceptance", "approval" and "accession" mean in each case the international act so named whereby a State establishes on the international plane its consent to be bound by a treaty;

> A State is obliged to refrain from acts which would defeat the object and purpose of a treaty when: (a) it has signed the treaty or has exchanged instruments constituting the treaty subject to ratification, acceptance or approval, until it shall have made its intention clear not to become a party to the treaty; …

*Gist:* Signature subject to ratification does not bind a State to the treaty, but obliges it not to defeat the treaty's object and purpose until it makes clear it will not join; ratification or accession is the act that binds. ([VCLT arts 2(1)(b), 18](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf)) Israel used the art. 18 exit in 2002, when it notified the UN that it did not intend to become a party to the Rome Statute it had signed in 2000 (lesson 19.5).

### Reservations

> A State may, when signing, ratifying, accepting, approving or acceding to a treaty, formulate a reservation unless: (a) the reservation is prohibited by the treaty; (b) the treaty provides that only specified reservations, which do not include the reservation in question, may be made; or (c) in cases not failing under subparagraphs (a) and (b), the reservation is incompatible with the object and purpose of the treaty.

*Gist:* A State may exclude or modify parts of a treaty when it joins, unless the treaty forbids it or the reservation defeats the treaty's object. ([VCLT art. 19](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf))

Example that connects to modules 10 and 18. On ratifying CEDAW in 1991 Israel stated:

> The State of Israel hereby expresses its reservation with regard to article 16 of the Convention, to the extent that the laws on personal status which are binding on the various religious communities in Israel do not conform with the provisions of that article.

*Gist:* Israel did not accept CEDAW's equality-in-marriage article to the extent religious personal-status law conflicts with it; it made a parallel reservation on women serving as religious-court judges (art. 7(b)). ([UN Treaty Collection, CEDAW](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=IV-8&chapter=4&clang=_en)) The CEDAW Committee has repeatedly asked Israel to withdraw these reservations; Israel's position is that they reflect the religious-community structure of personal-status law described in lesson [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md).

### Performance and internal law

> Every treaty in force is binding upon the parties to it and must be performed by them in good faith.

> A party may not invoke the provisions of its internal law as justification for its failure to perform a treaty. This rule is without prejudice to article 46.

*Gist:* Pacta sunt servanda; a State cannot excuse a breach by pointing to its own law, except where its consent itself was given in manifest breach of a fundamental internal rule on competence (art. 46). ([VCLT arts 26, 27](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf))

### Interpretation

> 1. A treaty shall be interpreted in good faith in accordance with the ordinary meaning to be given to the terms of the treaty in their context and in the light of its object and purpose.
> …
> 3. There shall be taken into account, together with the context: (a) any subsequent agreement between the parties regarding the interpretation of the treaty or the application of its provisions; (b) any subsequent practice in the application of the treaty which establishes the agreement of the parties regarding its interpretation; (c) any relevant rules of international law applicable in the relations between the parties.

*Gist:* Text, context, object and purpose, together with the parties' later agreement and practice; preparatory work (art. 32) only confirms the meaning or resolves ambiguity or absurdity. ([VCLT arts 31, 32](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/conventions/1_1_1969.pdf)) Compare the Barak method of lesson [02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md): both start from the text and its purpose, but treaty interpretation gives more weight to what the parties jointly intended and later did.

### Israel and the VCLT

Israel is not a party to the VCLT: the UN Treaty Collection lists 119 parties and 45 signatories, and Israel is neither (it appears only as having objected in 1970 to declarations by two other States). That matters little in practice. The ICJ and the parties themselves treat the core rules (consent, pacta sunt servanda, interpretation, invalidity for jus cogens) as customary international law, which binds Israel anyway (lesson 19.3). The Israeli Foreign Ministry and courts use VCLT articles as the statement of the custom. The question to keep open is the edges: some VCLT details (for example, parts of the reservations regime) are less clearly customary.

### The Israeli side: who signs and who approves

> נשיא המדינה – … (5) יחתום על אמנות עם מדינות־חוץ שאושרו על ידי הכנסת;

*Gist:* The President signs treaties with foreign States "that were approved by the Knesset". ([Basic Law: The President s. 11(א)(5)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_נשיא_המדינה)) The provision does not say which treaties need Knesset approval, and the established reading is that it applies only where the Knesset was in fact asked to approve; most treaties are ratified by the Government alone, under its residual power (Basic Law: The Government s. 32, lesson [14.1](../module-14/lesson-01.md)).

> החליטה הממשלה לאשרר הסכם או לחתום על הסכם, שלפיו המשפט, השיפוט והמינהל של מדינת ישראל לא יחולו עוד על שטח שהם חלים בו, … יהא ההסכם, לאחר שאושר בכנסת ברוב חבריה, טעון אישור במשאל עם, אלא אם כן אושר ברוב של שמונים חברי הכנסת.

*Gist:* An agreement under which Israeli law, jurisdiction and administration would cease to apply to an area needs approval by a Knesset majority and then a referendum, unless 80 members approve it. ([Basic Law: Referendum s. 1(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_משאל_עם))

### The tabling procedure

There is no statute on ratification. The procedure is in the Government Work Regulations (תקנון עבודת הממשלה), s. 10, as described by the Knesset Legal Department and in the policy literature: a minister proposing ratification first delivers to the Knesset Secretariat the treaty, a Hebrew translation and a summary of its main points, **two weeks** before submitting the draft decision to the Government, so that members can examine it and raise it for debate. The minister may skip the tabling where, in his view and with the agreement of the Foreign Minister and the Justice Minister, urgency or confidentiality prevents it. The draft ratification decision then goes to the Government with an explanatory note and the positions of the Attorney General and the Foreign Ministry. The tabling rule began as a Government procedure in the 1980s (often dated 1984; the original decision was not checked for this lesson, ⚠️), and the text of the Regulations themselves is on gov.il (⚠️ manual check: gov.il blocks automated access).

Important political treaties go further, by custom: the Government asks the Knesset to vote. The peace treaties with Egypt (1979) and Jordan (1994) and the 2020 agreement with the United Arab Emirates were approved by the Knesset; the 2022 maritime-boundary agreement with Lebanon was tabled for the two-week period without a vote and then approved by the Government.

### Bringing a treaty into Israeli law

Ratification binds Israel internationally; it does not change Israeli law. If a treaty must create rights or duties inside Israel that existing law does not give, the Knesset legislates. Two common techniques:

- **Schedule technique:** the statute gives the treaty itself force of law. The Hague Convention Law (Return of Abducted Children) 1991 s. 2 provides that the Convention articles in its schedule "יהיה תוקף של חוק" (lesson 19.8).
- **Implementing technique:** the statute rewrites the obligations in Israeli terms (the Extradition Law 1954 together with bilateral treaties; the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law, which preceded the CRPD).

Lesson 19.7 explains what happens to treaties that were ratified but never incorporated.

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 2144/08 מונדרוביץ נ׳ מדינת ישראל (14.11.2010)

The United States sought the extradition of a man accused of sex offences against children in the 1980s. Israel had refused a 1985 request because the offences were not "extraditable offences" under the Israel–US extradition treaty; after a 2007 treaty amendment widened the list, the United States asked again and the District Court declared him extraditable. The Supreme Court (Procaccia J., Rubinstein and Naor JJ. concurring) held that applying the amended treaty to the new request was not retroactive punishment, since the amendment concerned the enforcement procedure and not criminal liability. But it allowed the appeal: the Extradition Law's own exception applied, because the offences had become time-barred under Israeli law. Why it matters: it shows the two layers working together. The treaty decides what the United States may ask for; the Israeli statute decides whether Israel may grant it, and the statute's protections apply whatever the treaty says. (Hebrew case name rendered from the English title, ⚠️.) [English translation and abstract](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/265)

### The Knesset's role: the competing views

There is no leading judgment that decides whether the Knesset must approve treaties; the courts have read Basic Law: The President narrowly, leaving the matter to practice. The debate:

- **For the present practice.** Treaty-making is an executive function in most parliamentary systems; flexibility and confidentiality matter in diplomacy; the Knesset already has the tabling period, the power to debate, and the final word whenever a treaty needs legislation to take effect inside Israel; and Basic Law: Referendum protects the most sensitive category.
- **For more Knesset control.** Treaties can bind the State for decades and shape domestic policy (human rights, trade, the environment); the tabling rule is a Government procedure that the Government itself can waive; several private bills have proposed a statutory requirement of Knesset approval for important treaties; and comparative practice (the British Constitutional Reform and Governance Act 2010, which put the old "Ponsonby rule" in statute) shows that statutory tabling is workable.

## How it works in practice

### Finding a treaty's status, step by step

1. **Multilateral treaties:** the UN Treaty Collection (treaties.un.org) shows signature and ratification dates, reservations and objections for treaties deposited with the UN Secretary-General. Human-rights treaties: the OHCHR ratification dashboard (lesson 19.6). Hague conventions: hcch.net status tables.
2. **Bilateral treaties and Israel's own record:** the Israel Treaty Series (כתבי אמנה) and the Foreign Ministry's treaty department (⚠️ manual check of current online access).
3. **Entry into force for Israel:** read the treaty's final clauses. Many multilateral treaties enter into force for a State 30 days after it deposits its instrument; that is why a ratification date and a "binding from" date can differ. (Israel ratified the CRC on 3 October 1991 per OHCHR; the Supreme Court in CrimA 4596/98 refers to ratification on 4 August 1991, probably the date of the Government's decision. Always say which date you mean.)
4. **Reservations:** read them in full; they may exclude the very article you want to rely on (CEDAW art. 16 above).
5. **Israeli implementing law:** search the Knesset database or Wikisource for a law naming the treaty ("חוק אמנת…", "חוק ליישום…"), and for regulations under it.

## Worked example

Tamar Abebe, an Ethiopian-born Israeli nurse in Rehovot, worked for eight years in Germany before immigrating. She wants to know whether those years count toward an old-age pension.

1. **Is there a treaty?** She searches "הסכם ביטוח לאומי גרמניה" and finds that Israel and Germany have a bilateral social-security agreement (the National Insurance Institute lists Israel's agreements; ⚠️ manual check on btl.gov.il).
2. **Status and date.** She checks the Israel Treaty Series entry: signature, ratification and entry into force, and any later protocol. Her German years predate her immigration; the agreement's transitional clause decides whether they count.
3. **Interpretation.** The agreement's ordinary meaning, read with its object (protecting migrant workers' contributions) and the two institutions' practice (art. 31(3)(b)), favours totalisation of periods.
4. **Domestic route.** The agreement is applied through the National Insurance Law and its regulations; her claim goes to the NII, and a dispute goes to the labour court (module 17).
5. **Result.** She files the NII claim in her own name, citing the agreement by name and article, and attaches her German insurance record.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One treaty that matters to you or your family (social security, tax, child abduction, apostille, disability rights, a human-rights covenant).
- Access to treaties.un.org, hcch.net or OHCHR, and the Knesset database or Wikisource. 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Identify (5 min).** Full name, date and place of conclusion, depositary.
2. **Status (10 min).** Israel's signature, ratification or accession date, entry into force for Israel, and the number of parties.
3. **Reservations (5 min).** Copy any reservation or declaration by Israel, and note objections by other States.
4. **Israeli law (10 min).** Find any Israeli statute or regulation incorporating or implementing it; note the technique (schedule or implementing law).
5. **Interpret one article (10 min).** Choose the article you would rely on and write three lines applying art. 31: ordinary meaning, context, object and purpose.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for the research: anyone may find and read a treaty and its status. Relying on a treaty in your own claim to the NII, a ministry or a court is part of acting for yourself; writing such arguments for others as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- UN Treaty Collection, OHCHR, HCCH, the Knesset legislation database and Wikisource (all free).
- The Foreign Ministry's treaty department answers public questions on Israel's treaties (⚠️ manual check of contact route on gov.il).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your case depends on which of several overlapping treaties applies (for example, a tax treaty and domestic anti-avoidance rules).
- A deadline under a treaty is running (the one-year period in the Hague Abduction Convention, lesson 19.8).
- The other side argues that Israel's reservation or a later protocol changes the outcome.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on a treaty Israel signed but never ratified | Israel is not bound (beyond art. 18) | Rely on custom or Israeli law instead | Check ratification, not signature |
| Ignoring Israel's reservation | Your argument rests on an excluded article | Address the reservation's scope | Read reservations in full |
| Using the ratification date as the start date | You miscalculate whether events are covered | Find entry into force for Israel | Read the final clauses |
| Arguing that a ratified treaty is Israeli law | The court applies Israeli law and rejects the framing | Argue incorporation or interpretation in harmony | Look for an implementing statute first |
| Interpreting by Israeli-style purposive reading alone | You miss the parties' joint practice | Add art. 31(3) material | Use the VCLT steps |

## Review

- A treaty binds from ratification or accession and entry into force; signature brings only the art. 18 duty.
- Israel is not a VCLT party, but its core rules apply to Israel as custom.
- Reservations can exclude articles (Israel's CEDAW arts 7(b) and 16 reservations); always read them.
- Israel ratifies by Government decision, after a two-week tabling in the Knesset under the Government Work Regulations; important treaties are put to a Knesset vote by custom; Basic Law: Referendum governs giving up territory.
- Ratification binds Israel internationally; only legislation makes a treaty part of Israeli law (Mondrowitz shows treaty and statute working together).
