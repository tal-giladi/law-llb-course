# 19.7 · International law in Israeli courts

By the end of this lesson you will know exactly what an Israeli judge does with an international rule you cite: apply it as law, use it to read a statute, weigh it as persuasive, or set it aside because a statute says otherwise. This is the lesson that turns the module into a litigation tool, and it prepares the practice lab (lesson 19.8).

## Why it matters

Israel is usually described as a "dualist" State: international law and Israeli law are separate systems, and international rules enter Israeli law only through recognised channels. But the picture is mixed. Customary international law enters automatically; treaties do not; and a powerful interpretive presumption lets ratified but unincorporated treaties influence almost every area of Israeli law. Knowing which channel applies to your rule tells you how to frame the argument, and how strong it is.

The subject also connects to the constitutional debates of lesson [06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md). Judges who rely heavily on international instruments are praised by some for aligning Israel with democratic human-rights standards and criticised by others for importing norms the Knesset never adopted. You will need to argue within the current law while understanding both critiques.

## The law

There is no statute or Basic Law on the status of international law in Israel. The rules are judge-made and consistent since the 1950s.

### Rule 1: customary international law is part of Israeli law, unless a statute says otherwise

In Edelson (1997), President Barak wrote: "Customary international law is part and parcel of the law of the State of Israel", citing Shimshon (1955), Eichmann (1962), Beit El (1979), Afu (1988) and other cases. He quoted President Shamgar that custom is part of the law of the land "subject to Israeli legislation providing otherwise", and Professor Dinstein that custom is incorporated "except in the case of direct contradiction between them and the written legislation, in which case the latter prevails". ([Edelson para. 12](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/her-majesty-queen-right-canada-v-edelson))

*Effect:* a proven customary rule can be the direct basis of a judgment (restrictive immunity in Edelson; the Hague Regulations in Beit El), but a clear Knesset statute prevails over it in an Israeli court, even though Israel may then be in breach internationally (VCLT art. 27 logic, lesson 19.2).

### Rule 2: a treaty is not part of Israeli law unless the Knesset incorporates it

Beit El held that a conventional rule (the Fourth Geneva Convention, absent legislation) cannot be enforced by an individual in an Israeli court, although it binds Israel internationally. The academic summary is that treaty provisions are binding domestically, in principle, "only if they pass a separate and express absorption by the Knesset" (Broude and Noam, 2008). The reason given is democratic: the Government ratifies treaties; only the Knesset legislates. Incorporated treaties (the Hague Abduction Convention Law 1991, lesson 19.8) are applied like any statute.

### Rule 3: the presumption of compatibility

In Yated (2002) the Court (Dorner J.) wrote:

> Another rule of interpretation is the presumption that the norms adopted by the State should be in accord with the norms of international law by which the State is bound. According to this presumption, all rules will, wherever possible, be interpreted in a manner consistent with the norms of international law.

*Gist:* Israeli legislation and administrative norms are read, wherever possible, consistently with Israel's international obligations, including unincorporated treaties. ([Yated para. 8](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yated-v-ministry-education))

And on its limit:

> These interpretive presumptions may be rebutted only when the language of the statute, or its particular purpose as specified in the law, cannot be reconciled with the general values of the legal system or with the international norm.

*Gist:* The presumption gives way only where the statute's words or specific purpose cannot be reconciled with the international norm. ([Yated para. 8](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yated-v-ministry-education))

The presumption fits the Barak method of lesson [02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md): Israel's international obligations are part of the "objective purpose" of every statute. It also binds administrative discretion: an authority exercising a broad power should, where possible, exercise it consistently with Israel's obligations.

### Rule 4: everything else is persuasive

Advisory opinions, treaty-body general comments and concluding observations, General Assembly resolutions, foreign judgments: courts may cite and weigh them, but they do not bind. Mara'abe (2005) is the model: "full appropriate weight" to the norms as interpreted by the ICJ, but no duty to follow its factual conclusions.

### The open question: Basic Laws and international law

Can a Basic Law be read in light of, or even limited by, international law? The courts have used international human-rights law to interpret the Basic Laws' rights (dignity read to include aspects of social rights, for example). Whether international law could limit the Knesset's constituent power has been argued in the debates over Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People (2018) and the 2023 amendments, but the Court has not held that it can. *Supporters* of a stronger role argue that a democracy that ratified human-rights treaties should be held to them at the constitutional level; *opponents* argue that this would let the Government (which ratifies) and international bodies bind the Knesset and the people without consent, contrary to Israel's dualist structure.

## Leading cases

### רע״א 7092/94 מלכות קנדה נ׳ אדלסון (3.6.1997)

Recap from lesson 19.4: the Court applied the customary rule of restrictive immunity directly, as part of Israeli law, to deny Canada immunity in a lease dispute. Why it matters here: para. 12 is the clearest modern statement of Rule 1 and its statutory limit, with the line of earlier authority. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/her-majesty-queen-right-canada-v-edelson)

### בג״ץ 2599/00 יתד – עמותת הורים לילדים עם תסמונת דאון נ׳ משרד החינוך (14.8.2002)

Parents of children with special needs integrated into regular schools claimed that the State must fund their special education there, as it did in special-education schools. The Court (Dorner J., Or and Levy JJ. concurring) held that the right to education is a fundamental right, recognised in Jewish law, Israeli law, international law (ICESCR art. 13, CRC arts 28–29 and the disabled child's right to special care) and comparative law, and read the Special Education Law 1988 in light of it: the State must provide special education free of charge to entitled children integrated into regular education. Why it matters: it states the presumption of compatibility (para. 8) in its general form and shows it working in favour of a family against a ministry, with no conflict context. (Full Hebrew party name ⚠️ unconfirmed; English title "Yated v. Ministry of Education".) [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yated-v-ministry-education)

### בג״ץ 7957/04 מראעבה נ׳ ראש ממשלת ישראל (15.9.2005)

Recap from lesson 19.5. The Court gave "full appropriate weight" to the norms of international law as interpreted by the ICJ's 2004 advisory opinion, but held that the opinion, based on a different factual record, does not bind it to find the whole barrier unlawful; it reviewed the segment before it for proportionality. Why it matters here: it is the Israeli courts' working rule for advisory opinions (Rule 4). [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/252)

### ע״פ 336/61 אייכמן נ׳ היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (29.5.1962)

Recap from lesson 19.1. The Court examined customary international law to answer the claim that the 1950 Law exceeded Israel's powers, found no prohibition and held that universal jurisdiction supported the prosecution. Why it matters here: it is one of the founding cases for Rule 1, and for the principle that Israeli courts identify custom themselves. [Case summary](https://www.internationalcrimesdatabase.org/Case/185)

## How it works in practice

### The four-channel test

| Your international rule | Channel | What you ask the court |
|---|---|---|
| Customary rule (proven) | Rule 1: part of Israeli law | "Apply the rule", unless a statute contradicts it directly |
| Treaty incorporated by statute | Statute | "Apply the statute (and the treaty in its schedule)" |
| Ratified treaty, not incorporated | Rule 3: presumption | "Prefer the reading of the statute or the exercise of discretion that is consistent with the treaty" |
| Signed-only treaty, soft law, AO, foreign judgment | Rule 4: persuasive | "Take this into account as persuasive" |

### Writing the argument

1. **Lead with Israeli law.** Name the statute, regulation or power the authority applied.
2. **Show two possible readings.** The presumption works only where the text allows more than one reading; if it allows one, you need a different argument.
3. **Bring the international rule.** Treaty, article, Israel's ratification (and no reservation), and an authoritative interpretation (general comment, ICJ, foreign courts).
4. **Ask for the compatible reading,** citing Yated para. 8.
5. **Anticipate rebuttal.** If the other side shows that the statute's words or specific purpose cannot be reconciled with the treaty, the presumption fails; have a fallback (Basic Law rights, proportionality, reasonableness).

## Worked example

Shlomo Mizrahi, 78, from Beersheba, uses a wheelchair. His bank's branch moved its counter service upstairs and tells him to use the app. The Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law and its accessibility regulations set the bank's duties; the bank says the regulations' transition periods have passed and it complies by offering online service.

1. **Israeli law first:** the Law's service-accessibility provisions and regulations (⚠️ he checks the exact sections on Wikisource).
2. **Two readings:** "accessible service" may mean any service channel (the bank's reading) or the same service in person on equal terms (his reading).
3. **International rule:** CRPD, ratified 2012; art. 9 (accessibility) and the CRPD Committee's general comment on accessibility; no Israeli reservation.
4. **Presumption:** the reading consistent with the CRPD is the second; Yated para. 8.
5. **Rebuttal check:** nothing in the Law's words or stated purpose requires the bank's reading; the presumption stands.
6. **Result:** his letter to the bank and his complaint to the Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities use this structure; if he sues in small claims, the same paragraph goes into the claim.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The dispute you used in lesson 19.6, or Shlomo's case.
- The Israeli statute and the treaty article. 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Classify the rule (5 min)** using the four-channel table.
2. **Find two readings (15 min)** of the Israeli provision, quoting its words.
3. **Apply the presumption (10 min):** which reading fits the treaty, and why.
4. **Test rebuttal (5 min):** can the statute's words or specific purpose be reconciled with the treaty?
5. **Write the paragraph (5 min)** in Hebrew, citing the statute, the treaty and Yated para. 8.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your argument requires proving a customary rule against the State's contrary view.
- The other side argues the statute is clear and you have no fallback constitutional argument.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking the court to "apply" an unincorporated treaty | Rejected under Rule 2 | Reframe as interpretation (Rule 3) | Run the four-channel test |
| Using the presumption against clear statutory text | Rebutted | Find a constitutional or administrative-law ground | Look for a real ambiguity first |
| Treating an advisory opinion as binding | Rejected under Mara'abe | Ask for "appropriate weight" | Use Rule 4 language |
| Citing custom without evidence | Not proven | Build the practice and opinio juris file (lesson 19.3) | Prefer a codifying treaty where possible |
| Ignoring a contrary statute | Custom yields (Edelson para. 12) | Address the statute directly | Search for legislation first |

## Review

- Custom is part of Israeli law unless a statute directly contradicts it (Edelson para. 12; Eichmann; Beit El).
- Treaties bind domestically only through Knesset legislation (Beit El).
- Ratified treaties shape Israeli law through the presumption of compatibility, rebuttable only where text or specific purpose cannot be reconciled (Yated para. 8).
- Advisory opinions and UN bodies' views are persuasive (Mara'abe).
- Whether international law can constrain Basic Laws is unresolved and contested.
