# 29.8 · Practice lab: check the pledges registry and answer a bounced-cheque demand

This lab turns lessons 29.1–29.4 into two documents you can use: a dated pledges check for a vehicle, with a safe payment sequence, and a written answer to a demand over a cheque that bounced, with an appeal outline in case the cheque restricted your account. Do it on your own facts if you have them; otherwise use the supplied cases.

## Why it matters

Both tasks are common, quick and costly to get wrong. A car bought without a search can be realised for the seller's loan; a bounced-cheque demand ignored for a few weeks becomes an execution file with interest and fees, and a restriction notice ignored for a few weeks loses the appeal. The lab drills the exact sequence.

## The law

No new law. You apply: Pledges Law ss. 4, 13, 15, 16–17 and the Registration Regulations regs 13–13א ([lesson 29.4](lesson-04.md)); Bills of Exchange Ordinance ss. 20, 28–29, 37 and Tal Trading ([lessons 29.1](lesson-01.md), [29.2](lesson-02.md)); Cheques Without Cover Law ss. 1–3ב, 7א, 10, 12 and the Procedure Regulations regs 1–5, 17 ([lesson 29.3](lesson-03.md)); Fair Credit Law s. 11 if the cheque came from a loan ([lesson 29.7](lesson-07.md)).

## Leading cases

Apply רע״א 8301/13 טל טרייד נ׳ בנק לאומי (2015) and its further hearing (2017): a holder for value who is not a holder in due course is subject to your defences ([lesson 29.2](lesson-02.md)). For the pledge, apply ע״א 2833/04 רגיס נ׳ טרבלסי: an unregistered or late-registered charge loses to the liquidator and other creditors ([lesson 29.4](lesson-04.md)).

## How it works in practice

Part A takes about 45 minutes and costs only the online search fee per criterion ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); you can stop before paying and simulate the result. Part B takes about 75 minutes and costs nothing until you file; the appeal carries a deposit and a court fee. Keep everything in one folder: printouts, letters, your diary of deadlines.

## Worked example

Dmitri Volkov of Karmiel is buying a 2020 van from a contractor in Nahariya for ₪78,000. In the same month, a ₪5,500 cheque he gave a kitchen supplier for cabinets never installed came back to him through a letter from a discount company's lawyer, and his bank's restriction notice arrived after a tenth refused cheque.

1. **Pledges.** Search by licence number: a pledge in favour of a leasing company. Search by the seller's company number at the Companies Registrar: a floating charge in favour of a bank (company charges are registered there, not at the Pledges Registrar). He asks for a payoff letter from the leasing company and a letter from the bank that the van is not caught by its charge or that it will release it.
2. **Payment sequence.** He pays the leasing company directly, waits for the cancellation to show, then pays the balance and transfers ownership at the post office the same day.
3. **Cheque demand.** The holder is a discount company, a remote party. It bought the cheque after its date and the endorsement lacks the supplier's ID number: it may fail the "complete and regular" and "not overdue" tests. He answers in writing: total failure of consideration, why the company is not a holder in due course (Tal Trading), and that he will oppose any execution file.
4. **Restriction.** Of the ten cheques, one was refused while his credit line had been cut without notice: a s. 10(א)(3) ground. He writes to the bank, then files form 1 within the period, asking that the restriction not start meanwhile.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- For part A: a vehicle (yours or one you might buy) with its licence number and the seller's ID; or the supplied case: Hadas Gabay of Kfar Yona is buying a 2019 scooter, licence number from the ad, seller a private person.
- For part B: a bounced-cheque demand you received, or the supplied case: Bashir Khoury of Haifa gave a gardener a ₪3,200 post-dated cheque for a year of maintenance; the gardener stopped coming after two months; Bashir stopped the cheque, but his account was overdrawn that day; the gardener's brother now demands payment "as holder"; Bashir's bank sent a warning after five refused cheques last month.
- The [pledge and vehicle purchase check](../../templates/pledge-and-vehicle-purchase-check.md) and the [bounced-cheque response kit](../../templates/bounced-cheque-response-kit.md).
- About 2 hours.

### Steps

**Part A — pledges (45 min)**

1. Open the Ministry of Justice pledges search service. Write down the search criteria offered and the fee per criterion.
2. Search (or simulate) by licence number. Print or screenshot the report with its date.
3. Search (or simulate) by the seller's ID or company number. If the seller is a company, note that company charges are at the Companies Registrar.
4. Check the vehicle record for restrictions and attachments (⚠️ manual check of the current service).
5. Fill sections A–C of the pledge check. If a pledge appears, write the payoff letter request and the payment sequence.
6. Write the two protective clauses (section D) into a short purchase agreement.

**Part B — the bounced cheque (75 min)**

7. Draw the cheque's journey (drawer → payee → endorsee) and apply the four-question test from lesson 29.2. Decide: pay, arrange, or defend.
8. Write Part A of the response kit: one page, facts with dates, the defence, and why the holder is or is not a holder in due course.
9. Count your refused cheques for the last twelve months under s. 1 (remember: a stopped cheque in an overdrawn account counts). Are you at warning stage, restricted, or clear?
10. If restricted, or likely to be: identify a s. 10(א) ground and its evidence; write the prior request to the bank; fill Part B of the kit; compute the last filing day from service of the notice.
11. Write a one-paragraph note to yourself: what you would do in the next 48 hours (deposit, call the payee, ask the bank, file).

### Template

[Pledge and vehicle purchase check](../../templates/pledge-and-vehicle-purchase-check.md); [bounced-cheque response kit](../../templates/bounced-cheque-response-kit.md); if an execution file is opened, the [execution debtor response kit](../../templates/execution-debtor-response-kit.md).

### How you know it worked

- Your pledges check has two dated searches and a payment sequence that never pays the seller first.
- Your reply to the demand names the holder's status and the section or ruling for each defence.
- Your cheque count separates counted from uncounted cheques with a reason for each.
- Your appeal outline has a ground from the closed list, an attached prior request to the bank and a computed deadline.

<details><summary>Model answer (Bashir)</summary>

The cheque counts toward restriction: it was refused while the account was overdrawn, even though a stop order was given (s. 1). The brother is a remote party; facts suggesting he is not a holder in due course: he is the payee's relative, he likely gave no value, and he took the cheque knowing the gardener had stopped working (notice of a defect, s. 28). If he is not a holder in due course, Bashir's defence of failure of consideration (ten months of the year unpaid for) is open against him (Tal Trading); at most the brother can claim for the two months worked, as the payee could. Reply in writing, refuse payment beyond the value of the work done, offer that sum, and say any execution file will be opposed. On restriction: after the warning, Bashir must keep the account covered; if a restriction follows and a cheque was refused by a bank mistake or while a credit line covered it, write to the bank and appeal within the period under reg. 5.
</details>

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes: searches, the purchase sequence, the reply to a demand and the s. 10 appeal are all done in your own name. Opposing an execution file and appearing in the magistrates' court are also open to an individual.

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- Pledges search: the Registrar of Pledges' online service (⚠️ manual check); fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Reply to a demand: registered mail or email with proof.
- Appeal: form 1, magistrates' court nearest the branch; deposit and fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A pledge appears and the seller cannot or will not arrange the payoff.
- The holder is a bank or regulated lender pressing a large cheque or note.
- You are close to aggravated restriction or the restriction comes from an execution file.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Searching only by licence number when the seller is a company | Missing a floating charge | Check the Companies Registrar | Search every register that applies |
| Answering a demand with "I owe nothing" only | No record of your defence | Send a reasoned letter with documents | Use the response kit |
| Stopping a cheque without covering the account | It counts toward restriction | Cover the account; ask the payee not to re-present | Check your balance before stopping |
| Missing the appeal period | Restriction runs for a year | Ask the court for an extension with reasons | Diary the date when the notice arrives |

## Review

- Search by every criterion and register that applies, date the printouts, and pay lenders before sellers.
- Classify the holder first; then choose payment, arrangement or defence.
- Count cheques under s. 1, look for a closed-list ground, ask the bank first, and file within the period.
