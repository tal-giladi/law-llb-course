# 29.2 · The holder in due course and defences against payment

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at a dishonoured cheque, identify who now holds it and how they got it, and say which of the drawer's defences still work against that holder. This is the single most tested topic in Israeli bills law, on the Bar exam and in the Execution Office: almost every opposition to the execution of a cheque ([lesson 28.3](../module-28/lesson-03.md)) turns on it.

## Why it matters

A bills obligation is abstract: the drawer promises to pay the cheque, not to pay "if the sofa arrives". Between the original parties (the drawer and the payee, called immediate parties, צדדים סמוכים) the abstraction is thin: the drawer may raise every defence arising from the deal, such as failure of consideration, fraud or a condition on delivery. The abstraction becomes real once the cheque is negotiated to a remote party (צד מרוחק). A remote holder who meets the conditions of a holder in due course takes the cheque free of the drawer's personal defences. The drawer then pays the stranger and is left to sue the payee, who may be insolvent or gone. Knowing which holder you face tells you whether to fight the file or negotiate.

## The law

### Who is a holder in due course

> אוחז כַּשורה הוא אוחז שנטל את השטר כשהוא שלם ותקין לפי מראהו ובתנאים אלה: (1) נעשה אוחז השטר לפני שעבר זמנו, ולא היתה לו כל ידיעה שהשטר חוּלל לפני כן, אם אמנם חוּלל; (2) נטל את השטר בתום לב ובעד ערך ובשעה שסיחרו לו את השטר לא היתה לו כל ידיעה שזכות קנינו של המסַחֵר פגומה.

*Gist:* A holder in due course took a bill that was complete and regular on its face, before it was overdue and without notice of a previous dishonour, in good faith, for value, and without notice of any defect in the title of the person who negotiated it. [s. 28(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_השטרות#סעיף_28)

Each condition is a line of attack:

- **Holder.** Only the payee or endorsee in possession, or a bearer (s. 1). A cheque with a broken chain of endorsements, as in Tal Trading, may leave the possessor without the status.
- **Complete and regular on its face.** Missing particulars, a visible alteration or an endorsement whose name does not match the payee's name defeat this.
- **Before it was overdue.** A cheque is overdue after an unreasonable time from its issue (s. 35(ג)); a post-dated cheque taken after its date may be overdue.
- **Good faith and no notice of a defect.** Defects include fraud, duress, an unlawful consideration and a breach of faith (s. 28(ב)). Wilful blindness counts as notice.
- **For value.** Any consideration sufficient for a simple contract, including an earlier debt (s. 26(א)).

A holder who takes from a holder in due course and is not party to the fraud gets the same rights (s. 28(ג)): the "shelter" rule.

### Presumptions and the burden of proof

> כל אוחז שטר, חזקה לכאורה שהוא אוחז כַּשׁוּרה; אך אם הודו או הוכיחו בתובענה שהקיבול או ההוצאה או הסיחור שלאחריה פגועים ברמאות, בכפיה, או באלימות ופחד, או באי־חוקיות, חובת הראיה מוחלפת [...]

*Gist:* Every holder is presumed to be a holder in due course, and every signatory is presumed to have signed for value; but once fraud, duress or illegality in the issue or a later negotiation is admitted or proved, the holder must prove that value was given in good faith after it. [s. 29](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_השטרות#סעיף_29) The drawer therefore starts behind, and must bring facts that shift the burden.

### What the holder in due course gets

> היה אוחז כַּשורה, הריהו אוחז השטר כשהוא נקי מכל פגם שבזכות קנינם של צדדים קודמים לו, וכן מכל טענות־הגנה אישיות גרידא שהיו להם בינם לבין עצמם [...]

*Gist:* A holder in due course holds the bill free of defects in the title of earlier parties and of their purely personal defences against each other, and may enforce it against every party liable on it. [s. 37(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_השטרות#סעיף_37)

### Defences that survive even against a holder in due course

- **Forgery or unauthorised signature** (s. 23(א)): it has no effect, unless the person is estopped (for example, he later confirmed it).
- **Material alteration** without consent (s. 64): the bill is void against parties who did not consent, except that a holder in due course of an invisibly altered bill may enforce it according to its original terms. Changes to the date, sum, time or place of payment are material (s. 64(ב)).
- **Incapacity**, such as a minor's signature (s. 21), and a bill that was never delivered by the drawer at all, though a holder in due course enjoys a conclusive presumption of valid delivery by all prior parties (s. 20(ב)).

### Defences between immediate parties

Between immediate parties and against a remote party who is not a holder in due course, the drawer may prove that delivery was conditional or for a special purpose only (s. 20(ב)(2)) and may raise defences from the underlying contract: total or partial failure of consideration, deceit, duress, exploitation, set-off. An accommodation party who lent his name is liable to a holder for value, even one who knew he was an accommodation party (s. 27(ב)).

### Loans: Fair Credit Law s. 11

> נפתח נגד הלווה הליך בבית משפט או בהוצאה לפועל לביצוע שטר שחתם הלווה בקשר לחוזה הלוואה, יהיו זכויות האוחז בשטר כפופות להוראות חוק זה כאילו היה המלווה, אלא אם כן הוכיח האוחז בשטר כי הוא אוחז בו בתום לב ובעד תמורה, וכי הוא לא ידע שמקור השטר בחוזה הלוואה כאמור.

*Gist:* When a borrower's note or cheque given under a loan is enforced, the holder is subject to the Fair Credit Law's protections as if he were the lender, unless he proves good faith, value and that he did not know the bill came from a loan; and every defence the borrower has under that Law is open to a guarantor too (s. 12). [ss. 11–12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אשראי_הוגן#סעיף_11) Note the reversed burden: here the holder must prove, not the drawer. Lesson 29.7 covers the Law itself.

## Leading cases

### רע״א 8301/13 טל טרייד בע״מ נ׳ בנק לאומי לישראל בע״מ (24.11.2015); דנ״א 8447/15 בנק לאומי לישראל בע״מ נ׳ טל טרייד בע״מ (11.9.2017)

Tal Trading bought diamonds and paid with a post-dated cheque for about $205,000 to "Zvi Or Diamonds Company Ltd." The supplier endorsed the cheque to Bank Leumi as security for its own debts, but the endorsement name did not match the payee's name, so the bank was not a holder in due course; the diamonds never came, and Tal stopped the cheque. The bank argued that as a holder for value (s. 26(ב)) it was immune to a total failure of consideration, under the 1962 rule in ע״א 333/61 גויסקי נ׳ מאיר. In 2015 the Supreme Court (Hendel J. writing) overruled Guisky: a holder for value who is not a holder in due course is subject to the drawer's defences, including total failure of consideration. A seven-judge further hearing in 2017 (Hayut J. for the majority, Sohlberg J. dissenting) left that ruling in place. Why it matters: only full holder-in-due-course status cuts off personal defences; banks and discount companies that take cheques with irregular endorsements or with knowledge of problems take them subject to the deal. [Versa (Cardozo), English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/bank-leumi-le-israel-v-tal-trading-corp)

### Key text: the Execution Office opposition (חוק ההוצאה לפועל s. 81א)

The procedure through which these defences are raised is the opposition to execution of a bill: an affidavit within the opposition period, after which the registrar stays execution and sends the matter to the magistrates' court, which treats the opposition as an application for leave to defend. The practical test the court applies is whether the affidavit discloses a defence that, if believed, would succeed. Lesson [28.3](../module-28/lesson-03.md) covers the steps; this lesson supplies the substance. [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ההוצאה_לפועל#סעיף_81א)

## How it works in practice

### The four-question test

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Is the holder my immediate party (the payee)? | All my defences from the deal are open | Go to 2 |
| 2. Is my defence real (forgery, alteration, incapacity)? | It works against anyone | Go to 3 |
| 3. Does the holder fail any s. 28 condition (irregular cheque, overdue, notice, bad faith, no value)? | Tal Trading: my personal defences are open | Go to 4 |
| 4. Did the holder take from a holder in due course without being party to fraud (s. 28(ג))? | The holder is protected; I pay and sue the payee | I can attack the holder's title |

### Facts that typically defeat holder-in-due-course status

- The discount company bought the cheque at a steep discount from a business it knew was failing.
- The cheque was taken after its date or after the drawer had stopped it.
- The endorsement does not match the payee's name, or the endorsement breaches the Cash Reduction Law (lesson 29.1).
- "לא סחיר" was written on a crossed cheque (s. 81): the taker cannot have better title than his transferor.
- The holder and the payee are related: same owners, same address, family.

### Evidence to gather

Your contract or order, proof of non-delivery or defective work, your stop order and the letter you sent the payee, the bank's printout of the refused cheque and the reason (Cheque Clearing Law s. 2(ג)), public information on the holder (Companies Registrar extract, its relationship to the payee), and any correspondence showing the holder knew.

## Worked example

Samira Haddad of Nazareth ordered a dining set for ₪14,000 from a furniture store and gave four post-dated cheques of ₪3,500, payable to the store, crossed but without "למוטב בלבד". The store closed after the first delivery date passed; nothing was delivered. A cheque-discounting company, which had bought dozens of the store's customers' cheques in its last month at a 12% discount, presented the second cheque; Samira had stopped it. The company opened an execution file.

1. **Immediate or remote?** Remote: the company is an endorsee, not the payee.
2. **Real defence?** No: the signature is hers and nothing was altered.
3. **Holder-in-due-course conditions.** The cheque was complete and regular, and taken before its date. Good faith and notice are the attack: buying many cheques from one store in its final weeks, at a steep discount, can support an inference that the company knew or shut its eyes to the store's collapse. If the endorsement lacked the store's name and ID number, or the cheque was endorsed more than once above the threshold, that also undermines regularity (Cash Reduction Law).
4. **Burden.** The company is presumed a holder in due course (s. 29(ב)). Samira's affidavit must give concrete facts that make the presumption doubtful; total failure of consideration is a personal defence that, after Tal Trading, works against any holder who is not in due course.
5. **Action.** Within the opposition period she files an opposition with an affidavit and attachments: the order, the store's closure notice, the stop order, the bank printout, a Companies Registrar extract showing the company's business and any link to the store, and a short statement of why the company is not a holder in due course.
6. **Fallback.** If the court finds the company is a holder in due course, Samira pays and claims from the store (or files a creditor's claim in its insolvency).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A cheque you gave that was stopped or bounced, or the supplied case: Uri Weiss of Ra'anana gave a garage a cheque for ₪6,200 for a repair that failed; the garage owner endorsed it to his brother-in-law, who presented it a month after its date.
- The Ordinance, ss. 20, 23, 26–29, 37, 64, and Fair Credit Law s. 11.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Draw the cheque's journey: drawer, payee, every endorsee, with dates.
2. Apply the four-question test, writing one line of reasoning per question with the section.
3. List the facts that could defeat holder-in-due-course status and the document that proves each.
4. Write a one-page draft affidavit section headed "מדוע המחזיק אינו אוחז כשורה" (why the holder is not a holder in due course), with numbered paragraphs.
5. Write a second paragraph stating your personal defence (for example, total failure of consideration) and why, after Tal Trading, it works against this holder.
6. Decide: fight, settle, or pay and sue the payee. Write one sentence with the reason.

[Execution debtor response kit](../../templates/execution-debtor-response-kit.md) (opposition section); [affidavit and exhibit bundle](../../templates/affidavit-and-exhibit-bundle.md).

### How you know it worked

- Each of your conclusions cites ss. 28, 29 or 37.
- Your affidavit draft contains facts, not adjectives ("bought 31 cheques of the same store between 1 and 28 May at a 12% discount", not "acted in bad faith").
- You can say who bears the burden at each step.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own cheque: an individual may file the opposition and conduct the leave-to-defend hearing in the magistrates' court in his own name. A company must normally be represented by a lawyer in court. The holder (a bank or discount company) will almost always have a lawyer.

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- Opposition: at the Execution Office branch, within the period in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md), with an affidavit signed before a lawyer (a lawyer verifying a signature is not representation).
- If the holder sues in court instead, the same defences go in your statement of defence or application for leave to defend ([lesson 15.2](../module-15/lesson-02.md)).
- Free help: court information centres, Legal Aid where eligible, Kol-Zchut on opposition to execution of a bill (⚠️ manual check).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The sum is large and the holder is a bank or regulated lender with a lawyer.
- Your defence depends on proving the holder's knowledge through documents you do not have (disclosure needed).
- The payee has gone into insolvency proceedings, so your fallback claim is weak.
- You suspect forgery or alteration: the case may involve a police complaint and expert evidence.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing "I owe nothing to the store" without addressing the holder | Opposition dismissed: holder presumed in due course | Add facts on the holder's status and knowledge | Always run the four-question test |
| Bare denials in the affidavit | No leave to defend | Amend or re-file within the time, with detailed facts | Write dates, sums, names and documents |
| Relying on Guisky (holder for value defeats failure of consideration) | Wrong law since 2015 | Cite Tal Trading | Check the current case law |
| Ignoring a loan origin | Losing Fair Credit Law protections | Raise s. 11 and attach the loan contract | Ask: did this cheque secure a loan? |
| Missing the opposition period | Execution goes ahead | Ask for an extension with reasons at once | Diary the period on the day of service |

## Review

- Immediate parties can raise every defence from the deal; a holder in due course takes free of personal defences and title defects (s. 37(2)).
- Status requires a complete and regular bill, taken before overdue, in good faith, for value and without notice (s. 28); it is presumed, but fraud or illegality shifts the burden (s. 29).
- Forgery, material alteration and incapacity survive even against a holder in due course.
- Tal Trading: a holder for value who is not in due course is subject to personal defences, including total failure of consideration.
- A note or cheque from a loan carries Fair Credit Law protections to any holder who cannot prove ignorance of its origin (s. 11).
