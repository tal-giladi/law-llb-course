# 26.3 · Validity: capacity, undue influence, defects and curing them

By the end of this lesson you will be able to test a formally valid will for the substantive grounds that void it, tell a curable mistake from a fatal one, read an ambiguous will the way the courts do, and decide whether a testator revoked an earlier will.

## Why it matters

Most will disputes in the Family Court are not about form. They are about the testator's mind and the people around the testator: an elderly parent with dementia who left everything to the child who lived with her, a new partner who drove the testator to the lawyer, a will that disinherits a child without explanation. The Succession Law gives the grounds in a few short sections; the courts fill them with tests and presumptions. If you write your own will, these sections tell you how to make it resistant to challenge. If you are an heir who suspects a will, they tell you what you must prove and how quickly.

The underlying tension is between freedom of testation and protection of the vulnerable. Section 27 makes the freedom strong: no one can bind a person to make, change or keep a will. But a freedom exercised by someone who no longer understands, or who is dominated by another, is not freedom. Courts must decide, after the testator's death and usually on conflicting family evidence, which it was.

## The law

### Capacity: s. 26

> צוואה שנעשתה על ידי קטין או על ידי מי שהוכרז פסול־דין או שנעשתה בשעה שהמצווה לא ידע להבחין בטיבה של צוואה – בטלה.

*Gist:* A will by a minor, by a person declared legally incompetent, or made when the testator could not understand the nature of a will, is void. [s. 26](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_26)

The test is functional and tied to the moment of making: did the testator understand that he was making a will, roughly what he owned, who his natural heirs were, and what the will did? A diagnosis of dementia is evidence, not a conclusion; a person with mild cognitive decline may have capacity on a good day. The person attacking the will carries the burden.

### Freedom and personal act: ss. 27–29

An undertaking to make, change or revoke a will, or not to, is not binding; a clause limiting the testator's right to change the will is void (s. 27). A will is made only by the testator personally, and a clause making its validity depend on someone else's wish is void (s. 28). The testator cannot leave it to another person to choose the beneficiaries or the shares, except to choose from a group or list the testator named (s. 29). Mutual wills are the statutory exception (s. 8א, lesson [26.4](lesson-04.md)).

### Defects of will: ss. 30–31

> (א) הוראת צוואה שנעשתה מחמת אונס, איום, השפעה בלתי הוגנת, תחבולה או תרמית – בטלה.

*Gist:* A provision made because of coercion, threat, undue influence, trickery or fraud is void. [s. 30(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_30)

A provision made by mistake is corrected if it is clear what the testator would have written without the mistake; otherwise it is void (s. 30(ב)). If a year passed after the pressure stopped, or after the testator learned of the fraud or mistake, and the testator could have revoked the will but did not, the defect no longer voids or corrects it (s. 31).

### Errors, uncertainty and illegality: ss. 32–34, 38

A clerical error or a mistake in describing a person or asset, a date, a number or a calculation is corrected by the Registrar (or the court) if the true intention can be clearly established (s. 32). A provision from which one cannot see to whom or what was given, or which cannot be understood, is void (s. 33). A provision whose performance is illegal, immoral or impossible is void (s. 34). An illegal or immoral condition falls away and the gift stands (s. 38(א)); a void part does not void the rest unless they are inseparable (s. 38(ב)).

### Interpretation: s. 54

> (א) מפרשים צוואה לפי אומד דעתו של המצווה כפי שהיא משתמעת מתוך הצוואה, ובמידה שאינה משתמעת מתוכה – כפי שהיא משתמעת מתוך הנסיבות.

*Gist:* A will is read according to the testator's intention as it appears from the will, and so far as it does not appear there, from the circumstances; an interpretation that upholds the will is preferred (s. 54(ב)). [s. 54](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_54)

### Revocation: s. 36

The testator may revoke a will expressly in one of the forms for making a will, or by destroying it; destruction by the testator is presumed to intend revocation (s. 36(א)). A later will revokes an earlier one to the extent they conflict, unless it only adds to it (s. 36(ב)).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 239/89 שורש נ׳ גלילי, פ״ד מו(1) 861 (1992)

The Supreme Court described a two-stage reading of s. 54: first the language of the will, and only if the content is unclear, the external circumstances. Justice Barak, concurring, favoured a broader purposive interpretation that may correct mistakes, fill gaps and carry out the testator's intention as nearly as possible. Why it matters: the two positions still frame will-interpretation arguments; the Barak line is closer to how contracts are read under the Contracts Law s. 25 ([3.7](../module-03/lesson-07.md)). Citation and summary from [Hebrew Wikipedia: צוואה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צוואה) (⚠️ secondary).

### דנ״א 7818/00 אהרון נ׳ אהרוני (2005)

In a further hearing, the Supreme Court held by four to three that where the testator's intention to revoke a will is clear, the will is revoked even though the revocation did not meet all the formal requirements of a will. A later decision refused to give up formal requirements where the alleged revocation rested on unclear statements. Why it matters: the curing logic of s. 25 extends to revocation, but only on clear evidence. Citation and summary from [Hebrew Wikipedia: צוואה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צוואה) (⚠️ secondary; full text on the Supreme Court site not reached).

### Key text: how courts approach undue influence

The statute does not define undue influence. Family Court and Supreme Court practice, as described in the professional literature, looks at the overall picture: whether the testator was dependent on the beneficiary or independent, whether he was isolated from other family members, whether the beneficiary was involved in making the will (choosing the lawyer, bringing the testator, being present, paying), and whether the will departs sharply from earlier wills or natural expectations. No single factor decides, and a beneficiary who cared for the testator is not suspect for that alone. (No leading ruling is cited here because its full text could not be checked from a free source; look up the current leading cases before relying on a specific test.)

## How it works in practice

### Who raises validity and when

Validity is raised by an objection to the application for a probate order, filed with the Registrar within the period published (at least two weeks, s. 67) and before the order is given; the file then goes to the Family Court (s. 67א). After an order is given, a party may ask to amend or cancel it on facts not before the Registrar (s. 72; lesson [26.6](lesson-06.md)). The objection must be supported by an affidavit and a fee (Succession Regulations reg. 19; amounts in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### Evidence that wins validity cases

- Medical records around the date of the will (the patient's right to his file passes to the heirs through the court; the objector usually asks the court to order them).
- A geriatrician's or psychiatrist's opinion, ideally from the time of making.
- The lawyer's notes: who asked for the will, who attended, what the testator said.
- Earlier wills and the reasons for changes.

### Protecting your own will

Make the will when you are well; explain unusual choices in a short letter; meet the lawyer or witnesses alone; avoid having a beneficiary arrange or pay for the will; if your health is fragile, attach a doctor's note on capacity from the same week; deposit the will.

## Worked example

Batya Levin, 88, of Haifa, made a witnessed will in January 2026 leaving her apartment to her granddaughter Maya, who had lived with her for two years, and her savings equally to her two sons. A 2015 will left everything to the sons equally. She died in June 2026. Her son Gadi objects.

1. **Form.** Witnessed by two of the lawyer's staff, signed together. Valid form.
2. **Capacity (s. 26).** Gadi points to a 2025 diagnosis of "mild cognitive impairment". The lawyer's file shows Batya explained that Maya cared for her and that the sons are well off. Her GP's note from December 2025 says she was oriented and managed her own money. A diagnosis alone does not show she failed to understand the nature of a will; Gadi bears the burden.
3. **Undue influence (s. 30(א)).** Maya drove Batya to the lawyer but waited outside. Batya still met friends and her sons regularly. Dependence existed (daily help), but isolation and involvement in drafting are weak. A court is likely to find no undue influence.
4. **Interpretation (s. 54).** The will gives Maya "my apartment on Hagefen Street". Batya had sold that apartment in 2024 and moved to a smaller one on Hatamar Street. Under s. 51(ג) a specific gift fails if the asset is not in the estate; but if the will's wording and circumstances show she meant "my home", the court may read it to fit, or correct a description error under s. 32. This is the real dispute.
5. **Outcome.** Gadi's objection on capacity and influence is weak; the argument on the apartment is strong. A sensible settlement uses the s. 32 correction risk to reach an agreed division.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The case of Batya, or a will in your own family that you are entitled to see (dry run only).
- A one-page table: ground, section, facts for, facts against, evidence to obtain.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Confirm the form (lesson [26.2](lesson-02.md)); if form fails, stop and consider s. 25.
2. Test capacity at the date of making: list the medical and lay evidence.
3. Test undue influence: dependence, isolation, involvement, departure from earlier wills.
4. Test each provision for uncertainty, illegality and mistake; decide whether s. 30(ב) or s. 32 can correct it, and whether s. 31's one-year bar applies.
5. Check for revocation: a later will, a destroyed original, a letter.
6. Write a one-paragraph conclusion: object or not, on which ground, with which evidence.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- Each conclusion cites a section and at least one fact on each side.
- You separated "the will is void" from "one provision is void" (s. 38).
- You listed evidence you would need and how you would get it.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. Any interested person may file an objection in his or her own name, and the Family Court hears it like a claim (s. 67א(ב)); self-representation is allowed. In practice validity cases turn on medical evidence, expert witnesses and cross-examination ([23.7](../module-23/lesson-07.md), [23.10](../module-23/lesson-10.md)), and the other side is usually represented.

### Where and how

- Before the order: an objection to the Registrar with an affidavit, the documents and the fee, served on the parties (reg. 19), within the published period.
- After the order: a request to amend or cancel under s. 72 (reg. 27), with an affidavit and fee.
- Free help: Legal Aid where eligible; family mediation in the court's assistance unit (18.9).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are objecting on capacity or undue influence: experts and medical records are needed.
- A large estate or the family home is at stake.
- You are the beneficiary being accused: early advice shapes the evidence.
- The objection period has passed and you need s. 72 or an extension.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objecting only because the will is "unfair" | Dismissed; costs | Base the objection on a statutory ground | Test each ground with evidence first |
| Relying on a diagnosis alone | Capacity claim fails | Obtain records and an expert opinion | Focus on the day of making |
| Missing the objection period | The order is given | Apply under s. 72 with new facts, or ask the Registrar to extend (reg. 19(ב)) | Check publications and the Registrar's online system |
| Beneficiary arranging and attending the will | Strong inference of influence | Independent evidence of the testator's wishes | Testator meets the lawyer alone |
| Describing assets too specifically | Gift fails if the asset changes | Correction under s. 32 or interpretation under s. 54 | Describe by category ("my home at my death") |

## Review

- A will is void if the testator was a minor, declared incompetent, or could not understand the nature of a will when making it (s. 26).
- A provision made under coercion, threat, undue influence, trickery or fraud is void; mistakes are corrected where clear (s. 30), subject to the one-year bar (s. 31).
- Clerical errors are corrected (s. 32); uncertain, illegal, immoral or impossible provisions are void (ss. 33–34), usually without voiding the rest (s. 38).
- Wills are read by the testator's intention, from the text and then the circumstances, preferring validity (s. 54).
- Revocation is by a form of will or by destruction; clear intention may suffice despite formal defects (Aharon v. Aharoni).
