# 26.1 · The Succession Law: heirs on intestacy and their shares

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say who inherits when a person dies without a will, work out each heir's fraction of the estate, and spot the three traps that most family calculations miss: the spouse's own half under the property-relations regime, the car and household goods, and the apartment rule in s. 11(א)(2).

## Why it matters

Most Israelis die without a will. For them the Succession Law 1965 writes the will: it decides who inherits and in what shares, and the Registrar of Inheritance issues a succession order (צו ירושה) that banks, the Land Registry and the pension funds rely on. Families often assume rules that are not the law ("the wife gets everything", "sons inherit and daughters do not", "the child who looked after mother gets the flat"). Knowing the statutory scheme lets you check a draft application before it is filed, decide whether a will is needed at all, and understand why relatives sometimes renounce in favour of one another.

The 1965 Law was one of the first Israeli civil codes and a deliberate break with the Ottoman and religious rules that applied before it. It treats sons and daughters, and children born in or out of marriage, equally (s. 3(ג), s. 13). It also applies to everyone, whatever their religion, unless all the parties consent to a religious court, which may then apply religious law within limits (s. 155; see [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md)). That compromise is still debated: some see consent-based religious jurisdiction as respect for communal autonomy and freedom of choice; others worry that "consent" inside a family can hide pressure on women or weaker heirs. The statute answers part of the concern by protecting minors and persons under guardianship (s. 155(ג)).

## The law

### Who inherits: ss. 1–2 and 10

> במות אדם עובר עזבונו ליורשיו.

*Gist:* On a person's death the estate passes to the heirs. [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_1)

> היורשים הם יורשים על פי דין או זוכים על פי צוואה; הירושה היא על פי דין זולת במידה שהיא על פי צוואה.

*Gist:* Heirs take either by law or under a will; inheritance is by law except to the extent a will provides otherwise. [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_2)

Section 10 lists the heirs by law: the person who was the deceased's spouse at the time of death, and the "relatives": children and their descendants, parents and their descendants (siblings, nephews and nieces), and grandparents and their descendants (uncles, aunts, cousins). [s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_10)

### The spouse: s. 11

> (א) בן־זוגו של המוריש נוטל את המיטלטלין, כולל מכונית נוסעים, השייכים, לפי המקובל ולפי הנסיבות, למשק הבית המשותף, ונוטל משאר העזבון – (1) אם הניח המוריש ילדים או צאצאיהם או הורים – חצי;

*Gist:* The spouse first takes the household movables, including a passenger car, and then half of the rest of the estate if the deceased left children, their descendants, or parents. [s. 11(א)(1)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_11)

Under s. 11(א)(2), if the closest relatives are siblings or their descendants, or grandparents, the spouse takes two-thirds of the rest; and if the couple had been married for three years or more and lived together in an apartment that is part of the estate, the spouse also takes the deceased's whole share in that apartment, and two-thirds of what remains. If there are no relatives in these classes the spouse takes everything (s. 11(ב)). What the spouse receives because of the marriage, including a ketubah, is deducted from the share, but not what the spouse receives under the Spouses (Property Relations) Law or a property agreement (s. 11(ג)).

### The relatives: ss. 12–16

> ילדי המוריש קודמים להוריו, הוריו קודמים להורי הוריו.

*Gist:* Children exclude parents, and parents exclude grandparents. [s. 12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_12)

Children share equally; so do parents, and so do grandparents (s. 13). A child who died before the deceased is represented by his or her children, and the same applies down each line (s. 14(א)); representation within the parents' and grandparents' classes does not apply where the deceased left a spouse together with parents or grandparents (s. 14(א) end). An adopted child inherits from the adopter as a child (s. 16). If there is no heir under ss. 10–16, the State inherits and must use the estate for education, science, health and welfare, with discretionary grants to dependants (s. 17).

### Capacity, disqualification and renunciation: ss. 3–6, 8

Anyone alive at the death may inherit, including a child born within 300 days of the death (s. 3). Disqualified are a person who caused the deceased's death intentionally or with indifference, a person convicted of attempting to cause it, and a person convicted of hiding, destroying or forging the last will (s. 5(א)). The 2024 amendment lets the court, for special reasons and if it was the deceased's wish, apply the disqualification only in part or not at all (s. 5(ג)), excludes heirs who prove a criminal-law defence such as self-defence (s. 5(ד)), and allows a freeze on the estate while the criminal case is pending (s. 5(ה)).

> מי שהסתלק מחלקו בעזבון, רואים אותו במידה שהסתלק כאילו לא היה יורש מלכתחילה; אין הסתלקות לטובת אדם אחר, אלא לטובת בן־זוגו, ילדו או אחיו של המוריש.

*Gist:* A renouncing heir is treated as if never an heir; renunciation may be in favour of only the deceased's spouse, child or sibling. [s. 6(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_6)

Renunciation is made in writing to the Registrar of Inheritance after the death and before distribution (s. 6(א)); a minor's renunciation needs court approval (s. 6(ג)); a conditional renunciation is void (s. 6(ד)). A war-time rule (s. 6א) widens the circle of permitted beneficiaries for the estates of people killed in the Iron Swords hostilities. An agreement about a living person's inheritance, or a waiver of it, is void (s. 8(א)).

### Cohabitants and religious courts

A surviving cohabitant (ידוע בציבור) who lived in a shared household, where neither partner was married to someone else, is treated as if the deceased had left them by will what a spouse would inherit, unless a will provides otherwise (s. 55; see [18.3](../module-18/lesson-03.md)). A religious court may grant succession orders only with the written consent of all parties (s. 155(א)).

## Leading cases

### Key text: s. 11 and the property-relations regime

The most common error in practice is to divide the whole family property as "the estate". For marriages from 1974, s. 5(א) of the Spouses (Property Relations) Law gives each spouse, on the marriage ending by death as well as by divorce, half the value of the balanceable assets ([18.4](../module-18/lesson-04.md)). That half belongs to the survivor; only the deceased's half is the estate. Section 11(ג) of the Succession Law confirms that what the spouse receives under the property-relations law is not deducted from the inheritance share. The survivor therefore usually ends with the own half plus half (or two-thirds) of the deceased's share. Why it matters: in a family with one home registered to both spouses, the children typically inherit a quarter of the home each between them, not a half.

### Key text: s. 155(ג) and the limits of religious law by consent

When all parties consent to a rabbinical, sharia, Christian or Druze court, that court may apply its own law, but a minor's or legally incompetent party's inheritance and maintenance rights may not be less than under the Succession Law (s. 155(ג)). [s. 155](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_155) Why it matters: an adult daughter may agree to a religious distribution that gives her less than the civil share; the law respects that adult choice but not at a child's expense. Supporters see the clause as a balance between pluralism and protection; critics argue that formal consent does not guarantee free choice within families, which is why many lawyers advise adult heirs to read the civil calculation before signing a consent form.

## How it works in practice

### Order of the calculation

1. **Separate the spouse's own property.** Apply the property-relations regime or the couple's property agreement (18.4). Jointly registered assets: half is already the survivor's.
2. **Identify the estate.** The deceased's assets at death, including the deceased's share of joint assets. Life insurance and pension benefits paid to named beneficiaries are generally outside the estate (s. 147).
3. **Household movables and the car** go to the spouse first (s. 11(א)).
4. **Apply s. 11 to the rest**: half, two-thirds, or the apartment rule and two-thirds, or all.
5. **Divide the relatives' part** by class and representation (ss. 12–14).
6. **Check debts** before anyone takes anything (lesson [26.5](lesson-05.md)).

### Tax

Israel has no estate or inheritance tax: the Estate Tax was abolished from 1 April 1981 (secondary source: [Hebrew Wikipedia, מס ירושה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/מס_ירושה)). Taxes arise later, when heirs sell inherited land or securities (module 25 covers the real-estate taxes).

### Who files and what it costs

Any heir may apply to the Registrar of Inheritance for a succession order, online or on paper; the online fee is lower. Fees, the publication charge and timings are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). The procedure is lesson 26.6 and the lab in 26.9.

## Worked example

Avraham Mizrahi, 72, of Be'er Sheva, died in March 2026 without a will. He married Ilana in 1985. They own an apartment registered to both (₪1,600,000), a joint bank account (₪200,000) and a car registered to him (₪60,000). He has a separate savings plan in his name (₪140,000) and no debts. Their children are Rachel and Eli; their son Daniel died in 2019, leaving two children, Noa and Itai. Avraham's mother, Simha, 94, is alive.

1. **Ilana's own half.** Married after 1974, no property agreement: half of the balanceable assets is hers. Apartment: her registered half (₪800,000) is hers anyway. Bank: ₪100,000 is hers. Car and savings plan, acquired during the marriage: half the value is hers by the balance (₪30,000 + ₪70,000). In practice families often simplify by agreement; the strict calculation is the starting point.
2. **The estate.** Avraham's half of the apartment (₪800,000), half the bank account (₪100,000), and the remaining value of the car and savings plan.
3. **The car** is a passenger car of the shared household: it goes to Ilana under s. 11(א).
4. **The rest.** Avraham left children, so Ilana takes half of the rest; the other half goes to the children.
5. **Simha.** Avraham's mother is excluded because children exclude parents (s. 12).
6. **Daniel's line.** Daniel predeceased, so Noa and Itai take his share equally (s. 14). The children's half is divided in thirds: Rachel ⅓, Eli ⅓, Noa ⅙, Itai ⅙.
7. **Result for the apartment.** Ilana: her own ½ + ½ of Avraham's ½ = ¾. Rachel and Eli: 1/12 each; Noa and Itai: 1/24 each. If Rachel and Eli want their mother to keep the flat, each may renounce in her favour (s. 6(ב)); the grandchildren, minors, may renounce only with court approval (s. 6(ג)), which is rarely given against a minor's interest.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A sheet of paper or a spreadsheet; the Succession Law (link above).
- Your own family (dry run, nothing filed), or the case below.
- 45 minutes.

**Practice case (if you prefer not to use your own family).** Samir Haddad, a Christian from Haifa, died at 58 without a will. He married Rania in 2024 (his second marriage) and they lived in his apartment, bought before the marriage and registered to him. He has no children; his parents died; he has a brother, Jiries, and a sister who died leaving one son, Fadi.

### Steps

1. Draw the family tree with the date of death and who was alive at that date.
2. Mark each person's class under s. 10 and who is excluded by s. 12.
3. Separate the surviving spouse's property-relations share (if there is a spouse and the asset is balanceable). For Samir's case: the apartment is a pre-marriage asset, excluded from the balance (Spouses (Property Relations) Law s. 5(א)(1)).
4. Apply s. 11: movables and car; then half or two-thirds; check the apartment rule (three years of marriage and living there).
5. Divide the relatives' part, with representation (s. 14).
6. Write the result as fractions that add up to 1 for each asset.
7. Note one renunciation that the family might want and whether s. 6(ב) allows it.

### Template

None. (The [succession-order application checklist](../../templates/succession-order-application-checklist.md), draft in lesson 26.9, starts from this tree.)

### How you know it worked

- Every fraction has a section number.
- The fractions for each asset add up to 1.
- In Samir's case you found that Rania takes two-thirds of the rest but not the whole apartment, because they had been married less than three years; Jiries takes ½ of the remaining third and Fadi the other ½ by representation.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Any heir may apply for a succession order in his or her own name, and the Registrar's online service is built for unrepresented applicants ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Preparing the application for a relative who is not applying with you, or charging for it, is legal work reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20); a family usually solves this by having one heir apply and the others receive notice.

### Where and how

- The Registrar of Inheritance for the area where the deceased was domiciled (Succession Regulations reg. 13), online through the Administrator General's site or on paper.
- A renunciation is an affidavit filed with the Registrar (Succession Regulations reg. 16); the Registrar's form expects it to be verified before a lawyer.
- Free help: the Registrar's service centre (\*2416), Kol-Zchut's inheritance pages (⚠️ manual check), Legal Aid for eligible applicants.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- There may be a will, even an informal letter, or a claim that the deceased made one.
- Property relations are disputed (a business, a pre-marriage asset mixed with joint money, a second marriage).
- An heir is a minor, under guardianship or abroad and unreachable, or someone may be disqualified under s. 5.
- The estate includes assets abroad (module 30) or a farm (s. 114).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating the whole family home as the estate | Children's shares overstated; the order is wrong | Ask to amend the order (s. 72) or agree on distribution | Separate the spouse's half first (18.4) |
| Forgetting a predeceased child's children | Grandchildren omitted; order can be set aside later | Apply to amend; notify all heirs | Draw the full tree with dates of death |
| Renouncing "in favour of" a nephew or friend | Void beyond the permitted circle; the share goes to the other heirs (s. 15) | Renounce generally, or gift after distribution | Check s. 6(ב) before signing |
| Signing a "family agreement" on a parent's estate while the parent is alive | Void (s. 8(א)) | Make it again after the death, or let the parent make a will | Plan through a will, not an agreement |
| Assuming religious-law shares apply automatically | Wrong expectations; dispute | Apply the civil law unless all consent to a religious court | Read s. 155 and 10.3 |

## Review

- Inheritance is by law unless and to the extent there is a will (s. 2); the heirs by law are the spouse and three classes of relatives (s. 10).
- The spouse takes household movables and the car, then half, two-thirds, the apartment plus two-thirds, or everything (s. 11).
- Children exclude parents, parents exclude grandparents; predeceased heirs are represented by their descendants (ss. 12–14).
- Calculate the survivor's own half under the property-relations regime before dividing the estate.
- Renunciation is possible only after the death, in writing to the Registrar, and in favour of only a spouse, child or sibling of the deceased (s. 6).
