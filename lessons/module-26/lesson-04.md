# 26.4 · Special cases: gifts on death, mutual wills and wills benefiting witnesses

By the end of this lesson you will be able to spot four arrangements that families use to plan an estate outside an ordinary will, and say which of them work: a gift that takes effect on death, mutual wills of spouses, gifts to the people who helped make the will, and "first to my wife, then to my children" clauses.

## Why it matters

Families improvise. Parents sign a "gift deed" for the flat to be transferred "after we are both gone", spouses write identical wills and promise each other not to change them, a helpful neighbour writes the will and is named in it, a father disinherits a son and names no one else. Each of these meets a specific rule of the Succession Law, and several are void. Because the testator is dead when the problem appears, nothing can be fixed then. These are also the classic bar-exam traps of succession law.

## The law

### Agreements on a future inheritance and gifts on death: s. 8

> (א) הסכם בדבר ירושתו של אדם וויתור על ירושתו שנעשו בחייו של אותו אדם – בטלים.
> (ב) מתנה שאדם נותן על מנת שתוקנה למקבל רק לאחר מותו של הנותן, אינה בת־תוקף אלא אם נעשתה בצוואה לפי הוראות חוק זה.

*Gist:* Agreements about a living person's inheritance, and waivers of it, are void; a gift meant to pass ownership only after the giver's death is valid only if made in a will. [s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_8)

The courts distinguish a gift on death (void unless in a will) from a present gift whose delivery is deferred: if the giver irrevocably binds himself now and only the transfer is postponed, the gift may be valid as a gift under the Gift Law, especially if a caveat is registered ([13.4](../module-13/lesson-04.md)). The line is fact-sensitive; the document's wording and conduct during life decide.

### Mutual wills: s. 8א (Amendment 12, 2005)

> (א) בני זוג רשאים לערוך צוואות מתוך הסתמכות של בן הזוג האחד על צוואת בן הזוג האחר [...]

*Gist:* Spouses may make wills in reliance on each other's will, whether each benefits the other or a third party, in two documents made at the same time or in one document. [s. 8א(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_8א)

Revocation (s. 8א(ב)), unless the wills provide otherwise (s. 8א(ג)):
- **While both are alive:** the revoking spouse gives the other written notice; on notice, both mutual wills are void.
- **After one dies, before distribution:** the survivor who wants to revoke must renounce everything he or she would receive under the deceased's mutual will (except in favour of the deceased's child or sibling).
- **After distribution:** the survivor must return what was inherited, or its value.

A clause that completely denies revocation while both are alive is void (s. 8א(ג)).

### Gifts to drafters and witnesses: s. 35

> הוראת צוואה, פרט לצוואה בעל־פה, המזכה את מי שערך אותה או היה עד לעשייתה או לקח באופן אחר חלק בעריכתה, והוראת צוואה המזכה בן־זוגו של אחד מאלה – בטלה.

*Gist:* A provision, other than in an oral will, benefiting the person who drafted the will, a witness, or anyone who otherwise took part in making it, or the spouse of any of them, is void. [s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_35)

Only the provision in that person's favour is void, not the whole will (s. 38(ב)); courts read "took part in making" by substance, not title.

### Successive, substitute and conditional heirs: ss. 41–45

A testator may name a substitute who takes if the first heir predeceases, is disqualified or renounces (s. 41), or a successive heir who takes after the first heir's death, a condition or a date (s. 42). The first successive heir may use what he received as his own, and the second takes only what is left; but the first cannot defeat the second by will (s. 42(ב)). A chain of more than two is void except in favour of someone alive when the will was made (s. 42(ד)). Gifts may be suspended or ended by a condition or date (ss. 43–44), and heirs may be given obligations that any interested person may enforce (s. 45).

### Outside the estate: s. 147 and s. 55

Sums payable on death under insurance, a pension fund, a provident fund or similar to a named beneficiary are not part of the estate, unless the beneficiary named is the estate (s. 147). A cohabitant's share under s. 55 operates as a deemed will and yields to an express or implied contrary provision ([18.3](../module-18/lesson-03.md)).

## Leading cases

### בע״מ 10807/03 זמיר נ׳ גמליאל and ע״א 4402/98 מלמד נ׳ סלומון (before Amendment 12)

Before s. 8א, the courts struggled with spouses who made matching wills and the survivor later changed his. In Melamed, Justice Barak described mutual wills as resembling a contract, because of the reliance and mutual commitment behind them. In Zamir, the Court held that the survivor may change his will unless an express or implied restriction in the will prevents it, a ruling criticised for weakening the reliance on which such wills rest. Why it matters: Amendment 12 (in force August 2005) answered this uncertainty with s. 8א's revocation rules; wills made before it are still read through these cases. Citations and summaries from [Hebrew Wikipedia: צוואה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צוואה) (⚠️ secondary).

### ע״א 122/86 שפיר נ׳ קליבנסקי, פ״ד מד(1) 738, and the Lishitzky litigation (ע״א 4660/94; דנ״א 1068/00)

Can a person write only "my son shall get nothing"? In Shapira v. Klibansky the Court held that a document containing only exclusions, without a positive gift, is not a will, because a will must show an intention to give rights in the estate. In the Lishitzky litigation, where a woman left her estate to a vaguely described beneficiary and expressly excluded her husband and adopted son, Justice Cheshin wrote, in obiter remarks, that Israeli law does not rule out a negative will. Why it matters: if you want to exclude an heir, make a positive gift of the whole estate to others as well; do not rely on exclusion alone. Citations and summaries from [Hebrew Wikipedia: צוואה שוללת](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צוואה_שוללת) (⚠️ secondary).

## How it works in practice

### The parents' flat: three routes

| Route | Effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Will (or mutual wills) | Passes on death; revocable | The parents can change it; it needs a probate order |
| Present gift with a caveat | Ownership passes now or is irrevocably promised; tax events now | The parents lose control; possible purchase or betterment tax consequences (module 25) |
| "Gift deed effective after death" | Void as a gift on death (s. 8(ב)) unless it is in will form | Family litigation; the flat passes by intestacy |

### Writing mutual wills

Say expressly that the wills are mutual and made in reliance on each other; state what the survivor may do with the property (sell, live in, spend) and what must remain for the final beneficiaries; state whether the default revocation rules of s. 8א(ב) apply or are modified; and make both wills at the same time. Two identical originals are deposited separately, and each spouse pays a deposit fee (Kol-Zchut, ⚠️ manual check).

## Worked example

Esther and Shlomo Ben-David, of Jerusalem, Haredi, married 45 years, have three children. Their neighbour Menachem, a retired teacher, drafted for them one document: "We both leave everything to the survivor, and after both of us to our three children equally; neither of us will change this." Menachem and his wife signed as witnesses. A clause gives Menachem "my books" from Shlomo.

1. **Mutual wills.** Made in one document, in reliance on each other: valid as mutual wills (s. 8א(א)).
2. **"Neither of us will change this."** As a complete ban on revocation while both are alive, void (s. 8א(ג)); the default rules apply. While both live, either may revoke by written notice to the other.
3. **The books.** Menachem drafted the will and witnessed it: the gift to him is void (s. 35); the rest stands.
4. **Witnesses.** Menachem and his wife are adults, but the s. 35 problem is limited to the gift. If they signed at the same occasion after the declaration, the form is valid (s. 20).
5. **After Shlomo dies.** Esther inherits everything. If she wants to leave the flat only to her daughter, she must, before distribution of Shlomo's estate, renounce what she gets under his will (s. 8א(ב)(2)(א)), or after distribution return it (s. 8א(ב)(2)(ב)).
6. **"After both of us to our children".** This is a successive-heir clause (s. 42): Esther may use the property, but she cannot defeat the children's gift by her own will.
7. **Forum.** If all heirs consent, the rabbinical court may grant the probate order and apply religious law, within s. 155(ג) (lesson [26.1](lesson-01.md)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The Ben-David document, or a family arrangement you know of (dry run, nothing filed).
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. Classify each clause: will provision, gift on death, agreement on a future inheritance, mutual-will clause, successive or conditional gift, obligation (s. 45).
2. For each, cite the section and decide: valid, void, or valid with limits.
3. For any person who helped draft or witnessed, apply s. 35.
4. Rewrite the arrangement in a lawful form: mutual wills with clear revocation and survivor-use clauses, or a present gift with a caveat.
5. List what must be done during life (deposit, caveat, notifying pension beneficiaries under s. 147).

### How you know it worked

- No provision in your rewrite benefits a drafter, a witness or their spouse.
- Your mutual-will clause states what the survivor may do and how revocation works.
- You can explain why a "gift deed after death" fails and what replaces it.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for your own will, and spouses may each write their own handwritten mutual wills. But a neighbour, friend or relative who drafts for you not only loses any gift (s. 35) but also performs work reserved to lawyers if done for another person (Bar Association Law s. 20). Mutual wills with conditions and successive heirs are a common source of litigation and justify paid advice.

### Where and how

- Your own handwritten or witnessed wills; deposit with the Registrar (each spouse separately).
- Present gifts of land: a gift deed and a caveat at the Land Registry, signed before a lawyer or the registrar (13.4).
- Named beneficiaries: update them with the insurer or pension fund (s. 147).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You want to restrict the surviving spouse or create a chain of heirs.
- You want to give the home to one child during your life.
- A second marriage, children from earlier relationships, or a cohabitant is involved.
- You want to exclude a child or spouse (consider maintenance claims under s. 56, lesson 26.5).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Gift effective after my death" in a contract | Void (s. 8(ב)) | Make a will | Use a will, or a present irrevocable gift with a caveat |
| Siblings signing an agreement on a living parent's estate | Void (s. 8(א)) | Re-sign after the death | Ask the parent to make a will |
| A helper drafts and benefits | Gift void (s. 35) | None after death | Independent drafting and witnesses |
| Mutual wills that ban revocation outright | Ban void; defaults apply | State reasonable conditions instead | Use s. 8א's framework explicitly |
| Excluding a child without a positive gift | May not be a will at all | Make positive gifts | Combine exclusion with a gift of the whole estate |

## Review

- Agreements on a living person's inheritance are void; a gift that passes only on death needs will form (s. 8).
- Mutual wills are recognised (s. 8א); revocation needs notice while both live, and renunciation or return after one dies.
- Gifts to drafters, witnesses, participants and their spouses are void (s. 35), but the rest of the will stands.
- Substitute, successive and conditional heirs, and obligations, are allowed within ss. 41–45.
- Insurance and pension sums with a named beneficiary bypass the estate (s. 147).
