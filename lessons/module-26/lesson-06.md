# 26.6 · Succession and probate orders: the Registrar, objections and the Family Court

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide where to apply for a succession order or a probate order, assemble an application that the Registrar will accept on the first try, and choose the right step when you disagree with an application or an order that was already given.

## Why it matters

Without an order, no one can touch the estate: banks freeze accounts, the Land Registry will not register heirs, and no one may claim under a will (s. 39). The Registrar of Inheritance, a unit of the Administrator General in the Ministry of Justice, issues the large majority of orders without a hearing. The procedure is designed for unrepresented families, with a cheaper online route. It becomes a court case only if someone objects or the Registrar transfers the file. Getting the application right, and knowing the short deadlines for objecting and appealing, decides most of what happens next.

The design reflects a policy of "administrative first, judicial if contested": the Registrar acts like a court registrar for uncontested matters (s. 73ב gives the Registrar independence), and every disputed point goes to the Family Court. Religious courts can grant the same orders only if everyone consents (s. 155), which preserves a choice for communities that want it and civil jurisdiction for everyone else.

## The law

### Who gives the order: ss. 65א–66, 67א, 151, 155

> (א) הרשם לעניני ירושה רשאי להצהיר על זכויות היורשים: בירושה על פי דין – על ידי צו ירושה; בירושה על פי צוואה – על ידי צו קיום צוואה (להלן – צו קיום).

*Gist:* The Registrar declares the heirs' rights by a succession order on intestacy and a probate order under a will; where only part of the estate is willed, a probate order covers that part and a succession order the rest. [s. 66](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_66)

The competent court under the Law is the Family Court (s. 151). A religious court that had jurisdiction over the deceased's personal status may give the orders if all parties consent in writing (s. 155(א)); see [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md) and [10.4](../module-10/lesson-04.md).

### Publication and objections: s. 67

> הוגשה בקשה לצו ירושה או לצו קיום, יודיע על כך הרשם לעניני ירושה ברבים ויקבע זמן מתאים שלא יפחת משבועיים להגשת התנגדויות; כל המעוניין בדבר רשאי להגיש התנגדות [...] תוך הזמן שנקבע וכל עוד לא ניתן הצו.

*Gist:* The Registrar publishes each application and sets at least two weeks for objections; any interested person may object within that time and as long as no order has been given. [s. 67](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_67)

Publication is on the Administrator General's website and in Reshumot (reg. 17(א), as amended in 2023); publication costs are paid by the applicant (reg. 17(ג)).

### Transfer to the court: s. 67א

The application is transferred to the Family Court if an objection is filed, if the State is a party, if the Attorney General chooses to intervene, if the Administrator General represents a person whose property it manages, or if the Registrar sees fit (s. 67א(א)). In court it is treated as a claim under the Family Court Law (s. 67א(ב)); the applicant becomes the claimant and the objector the defendant (reg. 21).

### Evidence and content: ss. 68–70

Death and its time are proved by a death certificate or declaration of death, unless permitted otherwise (s. 68(א)). A will (other than oral) is proved by the original; if it was destroyed in circumstances that do not revoke it, or cannot be produced, a copy may be allowed (s. 68(ב)). A succession order names the heirs and their fractional shares; a probate order declares the will valid except for provisions found void (s. 69).

### Force, correction and protection: ss. 71–73

Orders are effective against the whole world until amended or cancelled (s. 71). The Registrar or court that gave an order may amend or cancel it on facts or arguments not before it; the Registrar transfers to the court a request based on facts the applicant could have raised earlier (s. 72(א)). A person who acquired a right in good faith and for value relying on a valid order keeps it even if the order is later changed (s. 73).

### Duties concerning wills: ss. 75–76

> (א) מי שיש בידו צוואה חייב למסרה, במקור או בהעתק מאושר, לרשם לעניני ירושה מיד לאחר שנודע לו על מות המצווה.

*Gist:* Anyone holding a will must deliver it, original or certified copy, to the Registrar immediately on learning of the death; failure is an offence. [s. 75](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_75)

If a deposited or delivered will is not brought for probate within three months, the Registrar notifies the beneficiaries and publishes (s. 76).

### The application: reg. 14

An application is made on Form 1 (succession) or Form 2 (probate), or online; facts are verified by the applicant's declaration. It will not be accepted unless (reg. 14(ב)): the form is complete; a death certificate is attached; for probate, the original will is attached (or a request to prove a copy); proof that notice was sent by registered mail or delivered personally to the heirs (Form 3א) or beneficiaries (Form 3); for probate where none of the beneficiaries is a child, parent or spouse, notice also to the children and spouse, or failing them to the parents or siblings (Form 3ב); a power of attorney if represented; the fee and publication costs paid; and no earlier application recorded for the same estate. Online filing is open for applications by children, parents and their descendants, or the spouse, and for probate (Second Schedule); a represented applicant must file online; the original will is delivered to the Registrar within seven days (reg. 14(ד)).

### Appeal: s. 151א and reg. 46

A person aggrieved by a Registrar's decision may appeal to the Family Court, heard by a single judge (s. 151א), within fifteen days of service of the decision (reg. 46).

## Leading cases

### Key text: s. 72 and the limits of re-opening an order

Section 72 is not a second chance to argue what you could have argued before. The Registrar may refuse facts that the applicant could have raised before the order or at the first reasonable opportunity after it, and must then send the request to the court (s. 72(א)); the court too may refuse such facts (reg. 27(ג)). Why it matters: if you know of a will, a disqualified heir or a missing relative, raise it at the objection stage, not after the order.

### Key text: s. 73 and buyers relying on an order

A buyer who purchases an inherited apartment in good faith, for value, relying on a succession order valid at the time, is protected even if a will is found later and the order is cancelled (s. 73). Why it matters: the heir who sold may have to account to the true heir, but the buyer keeps the flat. This is one reason to move quickly with an objection.

## How it works in practice

### Choosing the route

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| No will; heirs agree; you are a child, parent or spouse | Registrar, online (lower fee) |
| No will; heirs include siblings, nephews, grandparents, uncles or cousins | Registrar on paper (Kol-Zchut says these files are manual only; ⚠️ manual check) |
| A will; uncontested | Registrar, probate application online or on paper; original will delivered |
| A will that leaves part of the estate undisposed | Probate for the will and succession for the rest (s. 66(ב)), one combined order (Form 6) |
| All parties want religious law | Religious court with everyone's written consent (s. 155; reg. 48) |
| Anyone objects | The file moves to the Family Court (s. 67א) |

### Fees and timing

The application fee is lower online (85% of the paper fee, reg. 1 of the Fees Regulations) and a separate publication charge is paid; the 2026 amounts are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Kol-Zchut reports that an order is usually given within about 50 days of a complete application, including the publication period (⚠️ manual check). Orders are issued digitally and sent directly to banks, insurers, the Tax Authority and the Land Registry; you also receive the order by email and in the government personal area (⚠️ manual check). Status can be tracked in the Registrar's online system.

### Objecting

An objection is filed with the Registrar (online through the Registrar's system, by post or at reception) within the published period and before the order; it states the grounds, attaches the documents, is supported by an affidavit, is served on the parties, and carries a fee (reg. 19; Kol-Zchut reports a 2026 fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md), ⚠️ manual check). If the file has already moved to the court, file there.

## Worked example

Olga Petrova, a widow from Karmiel who immigrated in 1995, died in May 2026. Her son Andrei, in Karmiel, and her daughter Irina, in Germany, are the only heirs. Andrei finds a handwritten will in her desk leaving the flat to Irina's son, Max, and the rest to the two children equally.

1. **Duty to deliver.** Andrei must deliver the will to the Registrar immediately (s. 75); he cannot "decide" it is invalid.
2. **Which order.** The will disposes of the flat and the rest: a probate order. If it had disposed of the flat only, probate for the flat and succession for the rest (s. 66(ב)).
3. **Who applies.** Any interested person: Max (through his parents if a minor), Irina or Andrei. Andrei, as a child of the deceased, can file online.
4. **Notices.** To every beneficiary (Max, Irina, Andrei) on Form 3, with a copy of the will. The extra family notice on Form 3ב is needed only when no beneficiary is a child, parent or spouse of the deceased; here two beneficiaries are her children, so it is not required. Had Olga left everything to Max alone, Andrei and Irina would have had to receive Form 3ב.
5. **Documents.** The original will to the Registrar within seven days of online filing; the death certificate details; proof of registered-mail notices.
6. **Objection.** Andrei thinks Max's mother pushed for the gift. He must object within the published period, on a statutory ground (s. 30), with an affidavit and the fee; the file then moves to the Family Court in the district.
7. **If no one objects.** The Registrar gives the probate order. If Andrei later finds evidence he could not have found earlier, he may apply under s. 72; if he simply changes his mind, he cannot.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The Petrova case or a real estate in your family (dry run, nothing filed).
- The Registrar's online form open in read-only mode (do not submit), or Form 1/2 from the Succession Regulations.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Decide: succession, probate, or both; Registrar, court or religious court.
2. Decide: online or paper, and why (who you are; whether siblings or more distant relatives are heirs).
3. List the heirs or beneficiaries with ID numbers and addresses; mark minors and anyone under guardianship or an enduring power of attorney that has come into force (reg. 14(א)).
4. List the documents of reg. 14(ב) and tick each.
5. Draft the notice to heirs (Form 3 or 3א) for one heir.
6. Write the deadlines: objection period (at least 14 days from publication), appeal from the Registrar (15 days), original will after online filing (7 days).
7. For one realistic disagreement in your case, decide: objection, appeal or s. 72 request.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Individuals routinely apply to the Registrar without a lawyer, online or on paper, and may object in their own name ([can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Once a file moves to the Family Court it is a claim; self-representation is allowed but the procedure is a court procedure ([module 15](../module-15/lesson-01.md)). Applying for another heir without being one yourself, or for a fee, is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Online: the Registrar's application forms on gov.il with the national ID login; payment online.
- Paper: by email to the Registrar's service centre or registered mail to the district office near the deceased's home (Kol-Zchut, ⚠️ manual check).
- Help: the Registrar's service and information centre \*2416; Moked-Yerushot@justice.gov.il (⚠️ manual check).
- Appeals from the Registrar: the Family Court, within 15 days (reg. 46).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Someone objects and the file moves to the Family Court.
- The will is a copy, damaged, foreign, or in a foreign language that needs notarised translation.
- An heir is a minor, missing, abroad and unreachable, or under guardianship.
- There are assets abroad or the deceased was not domiciled in Israel (module 30).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not sending notices to all heirs | Application not accepted (reg. 14(ב)(4)) | Send by registered mail and upload proof | Send notices before filing |
| Applying for a succession order while a will exists | Order may be cancelled; s. 75 offence | Deliver the will; apply for probate | Check the national register for deposited wills (free) |
| Objecting after the order is given | Objection is too late | Request under s. 72 with new facts | Track publications and the online file |
| Missing the 15-day appeal | Registrar's decision stands | Ask for an extension with reasons | Diary the date of service |
| Filing online when the heirs are siblings or cousins | Rejected or delayed | Re-file on paper | Check the online eligibility rules first |

## Review

- The Registrar gives succession and probate orders in uncontested cases; an objection or other listed grounds move the file to the Family Court (ss. 66, 67א).
- Applications are published for at least two weeks; anyone interested may object until the order is given (s. 67).
- Reg. 14 lists what must be attached; online filing is cheaper and open to close family; the original will follows within seven days.
- Orders bind everyone until amended or cancelled; s. 72 allows correction on new facts, and good-faith buyers are protected (ss. 71–73).
- Appeal from the Registrar to the Family Court within 15 days (s. 151א; reg. 46); religious courts act only with everyone's consent (s. 155).
