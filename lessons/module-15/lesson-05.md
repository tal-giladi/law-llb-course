# 15.5 · Interim relief: temporary injunctions, attachments and security for costs

A judgment that comes after two years is worthless if the defendant has meanwhile emptied the bank account, sold the flat or demolished the wall. Interim relief protects the claim while the case runs. This lesson covers chapter 15 of the 2018 Regulations: the general test for every interim order, the self-undertaking and security, ex parte orders and how to attack them, and the specific orders (temporary attachment, temporary injunction, receiver, seizure of evidence, exit ban). It closes with security for costs, the defendant's protection against a plaintiff who could not pay if it lost.

## Why it matters

Interim relief is often the real battle. A temporary injunction against a building project, or an attachment of a business's bank account, can decide the dispute long before trial, because the side under the order settles. That power is why the Regulations surround it with safeguards: evidence of a prima facie claim, a balance of harms, good faith, an unlimited personal undertaking to compensate, security, short deadlines for a hearing, and automatic expiry if conditions are not met.

The policy tension is sharp. **Effectiveness:** without fast, sometimes secret orders, defendants could defeat any judgment. **Fairness to the respondent:** an order given before the respondent is heard, on one side's affidavit, can ruin a business that turns out to be in the right. The Regulations solve this by making ex parte orders exceptional (except for attachments and evidence seizure, where surprise is the point) and by making the applicant pay for damage if the order falls.

## The law

### Purpose and the general test: regs 94–95

> מטרת הסעד הזמני היא להבטיח זכות לכאורה במהלך ההליך המשפטי ואת קיומו התקין והיעיל של ההליך או את ביצועו הראוי של פסק הדין.

*Gist:* Interim relief protects a prima facie right during the case, the proper conduct of the case, or the effective enforcement of the judgment. ([reg. 94](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

The court may grant it if satisfied, on prima facie sufficient evidence, of a cause of action, of the specific conditions for the order, and that the order is necessary (reg. 95(ב)). It weighs (reg. 95(ד)):

> (1) הנזק שעלול להיגרם למבקש אם לא יינתן הסעד הזמני לעומת הנזק שעלול להיגרם למשיב אם יינתן הסעד הזמני, וכן נזק העלול להיגרם לאדם אחר או לעניין ציבורי;
> (2) אם אין סעד אחר שפגיעתו במשיב קלה יותר, המשיג את התכלית שלשמה נועד הסעד הזמני;
> (3) תום לבם של בעלי הדין… והאם המבקש לא השתהה יתר על המידה…

*Gist:* The balance of harms (including to third parties and the public), proportionality (no less harmful alternative), and good faith, including whether the applicant delayed. ([reg. 95(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

Interim relief may be given before the claim is filed, but the claim must then be filed within seven days or another period the court sets (reg. 95(ג)); otherwise the order lapses (reg. 100(3)). A fee is paid on such a pre-claim request as for the claim itself ([Fees Regulations reg. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_המשפט_(אגרות))).

### Undertaking and security: reg. 96

Every interim order is conditional on the applicant's **self-undertaking** (Form 7), unlimited in amount unless the court orders otherwise, and on **security** to the court's satisfaction, to compensate the respondent for damage if the order lapses or is narrowed. The court may waive security for special reasons, and the order takes effect only when all security is deposited (reg. 96). If no claim for that damage is filed within 90 days after the order lapses, the security is returned (reg. 101).

### Ex parte and inter partes: reg. 97

> (א) …הבקשה למתן סעד זמני תידון במעמד בעלי הדין, ואולם בית המשפט רשאי, מטעמים מיוחדים, לתת סעד זמני ארעי במעמד צד אחד, אם שוכנע, על בסיס ראיות מספקות, כי קיים חשש סביר שהעיכוב שייגרם כתוצאה מקיום הדיון במעמד בעלי הדין או הבאת הבקשה לידיעת המשיב תסכל את מטרת הסעד הזמני או תגרום למבקש נזק חמור.

*Gist:* The rule is a hearing with both sides; an ex parte temporary order needs special reasons and a reasonable fear that notice or delay would defeat the order or cause serious harm. ([reg. 97(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

The reverse rule applies to **temporary attachment and seizure of evidence**: they are heard ex parte unless an inter partes hearing would not defeat them (reg. 97(ג)). After an ex parte order, the applicant must serve the order, the request, the documents and the guarantee by personal delivery promptly, within a few days (reg. 97(ד)), or the order lapses (reg. 100(2)). For an ex parte injunction the court sets an inter partes hearing within a short period (reg. 97(ה)); for an ex parte attachment or seizure, the respondent or holder may apply to cancel within a set period, and the hearing follows promptly (reg. 97(ו)). Recess days count in these periods. The decision is due at the end of the hearing or within a short period (reg. 97(ח)). Periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

A party may ask the court to reconsider an order for changed circumstances, new facts, passage of time, or to vary its scope (reg. 98), and the court may instead bring the trial forward (reg. 99).

### The specific orders

| Order | Rule | Specific condition |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary attachment (עיקול זמני) | 103–108 | In a money claim, reasonable fear that without it enforcement of the judgment will be substantially harder; in a claim for a specific asset, attach that asset; land: note in the Land Registry (103(ג)) |
| Temporary injunction (צו מניעה זמני) | 109–110 | Reasonable fear that without it proper enforcement of the judgment will be substantially harder |
| Temporary receiver (כונס) | 111–122 | Real fear of serious loss of value, concealment or destruction of specific assets, or assets produced by or used in the wrong |
| Seizure of evidence (תפיסת ראיות) | 123 | Real fear that evidence will be hidden, altered or destroyed, substantially harming the case or the truth |
| Exit ban (עיכוב יציאה) | 124–128 | Real fear the respondent will leave for a long time or for good and enforcement will be substantially harder; not against a foreign resident or in claims up to a threshold (except maintenance), save special reasons; for a limited period, renewable |

### The holder: regs 104–105

A bank or other holder of the respondent's assets must answer in writing within a set period whether it holds them and whether it claims rights in them; an admission binds it (reg. 104(א)). If the holder does not admit, the applicant must apply within a short period to confirm the attachment, or it lapses as to those assets (reg. 105(א)). Service on the State goes to the accountant of the ministry holding the asset; on a bank, to its head office (reg. 108).

### Relief at judgment and stay

The court may grant relief to secure enforcement at or right after judgment, orally if asked then (reg. 132). An attachment stays in force after judgment for the winning plaintiff until enforcement (reg. 106), and enforcement continues under the Execution Law (reg. 107; lesson [08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md)). Stay of execution pending appeal is in lesson 15.8 (reg. 145).

### Security for costs: reg. 157 and Companies Law s. 353א

> (א) בית המשפט רשאי, אם נראה לו הדבר, לצוות על תובע לתת ערובה לתשלום הוצאותיו של נתבע.
> (ב) לא ניתנה הערובה בתוך המועד שהורה, יימחק כתב התביעה.

*Gist:* The court may order a plaintiff to give security for the defendant's costs; if it is not given in time, the claim is struck out. ([reg. 157](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

For a limited-liability company the Companies Law sets a presumption: on the defendant's request the court may order security and stay the case until it is given, unless the circumstances do not justify it or the company proves it can pay the costs ([Companies Law s. 353א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החברות)). For an individual plaintiff, security is the exception, because it burdens access to court; foreign residence without assets in Israel is the classic ground. An appellant deposits security under a fixed schedule (reg. 135; lesson 15.8).

### Procedure: regs 49–50

A request for interim relief is one of the motions allowed at any stage (reg. 49(א)(1)) and is exempt from the seven-day prior-notice rule when it is for an ex parte temporary order (reg. 49(ד)). It is in writing with an affidavit (reg. 50(1)); the request may run longer than ordinary motions, and the affidavit too (reg. 50(5); limits in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

## Leading cases

### Key text: chapter 15 (regs 94–128)

Before 2021 the tests for interim relief (prima facie claim, balance of convenience, good faith, clean hands, delay) were mostly case law under the 1984 rules. Reg. 95 now states them, and the old cases are cited for their application. For research, search decisions for "תקנה 95(ד)" with the type of order, and check the date (lesson [02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)).

### The freezing order (צו מרווה) in Israel

The English Court of Appeal created the freezing injunction in *Mareva Compania Naviera v International Bulkcarriers* (1975): a personal order forbidding the defendant to deal with its assets. Israeli courts recognised such orders in the early 1990s and the rules were codified in a 2001 amendment; today a freezing order is given as a temporary injunction under reg. 109. Unlike an attachment, it acts on the person, not on the assets, so it does not prevent another creditor from collecting from those assets. Free summary: [Hebrew Wikipedia, צו מרווה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צו_מרווה) (⚠️ secondary source).

**Why it matters:** when a defendant's assets are spread or abroad, a personal injunction may protect you where an attachment cannot.

## How it works in practice

### Is interim relief worth it?

Ask four questions before you apply:

1. **Can I show a prima facie case on documents now?** Interim hearings are decided on affidavits and short cross-examination.
2. **Is there a real risk to enforcement?** Not "they owe me money" but evidence: the debtor is selling the flat, moving abroad, transferring the business.
3. **Can I afford the undertaking and security?** If the order falls, you pay the respondent's losses.
4. **Did I act quickly?** Delay undermines urgency and good faith (reg. 95(ד)(3)).

### The request

- Heading as for a motion; the relief asked precisely (which account, which asset, which act to prevent).
- The facts in numbered paragraphs, each verified in the affidavit.
- The legal test of reg. 95 and the specific rule, applied point by point.
- If ex parte: why notice would defeat the order (reg. 97(א) or (ג)).
- Attached: the claim (or a commitment to file within seven days), the Form 7 self-undertaking, an offer of security.

### If you are the respondent

Read the order for its date of service and expiry conditions. Check: was it served personally within the period (reg. 97(ד))? Was the claim filed within seven days (reg. 100(3))? Then file your response or cancellation request with your own affidavit, and ask for the security to be increased if your potential loss is large.

## Worked example

Samir Abu Hussein, a car-parts dealer from Nazareth, sold ₪210,000 of stock on credit to "מוסך הגליל בע״מ". The garage stopped paying; its owner told Samir's driver it was "closing and moving the equipment to his brother's place in the north". A friend sends Samir a Yad2 listing for the garage's lift and diagnostic machines.

1. **Court and claim.** Money claim, magistrates' court in Nazareth (reg. 7). He drafts the claim, and with it a request for a temporary attachment of the garage's equipment and bank accounts (reg. 103).
2. **Test.** Prima facie case: invoices, delivery notes signed by the garage, a WhatsApp acknowledging the debt. Risk to enforcement: the sale listing and the owner's words (reg. 103(א)). Balance of harms: an attachment of equipment the garage is selling anyway harms it little; the bank attachment could stop its business, so he asks for it only up to the debt (reg. 95(ד)(2)).
3. **Ex parte.** Attachments are heard ex parte as a rule (reg. 97(ג)). He attaches a Form 7 undertaking and offers a bank guarantee.
4. **Order.** The court attaches the equipment and the accounts up to ₪210,000, conditional on a ₪20,000 guarantee. Samir deposits it, and serves the garage personally within the period (reg. 97(ד)). The banks answer in writing (reg. 104).
5. **The garage's response.** Within the period it applies to cancel (reg. 97(ו)), claiming the stock was defective. At the hearing the court keeps the equipment attachment and cancels the bank attachment, because the garage shows that it needs the account to pay salaries and that the equipment alone covers the debt. The case settles three months later.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A dispute where you fear the other side will make a judgment worthless (or Samir's case).
- Regs 94–110 and 157 open; the [written motion template](../../templates/written-motion.md).
- About an hour.

### Steps

1. **Choose the order (10 min).** From the table, pick the order that protects your claim with the least harm to the respondent. Write the specific condition you must prove.
2. **Evidence (15 min).** List the documents and facts that show a prima facie case and the specific risk. Mark anything that is only rumour.
3. **Balance (10 min).** Write two paragraphs: harm to you without the order; harm to the respondent and others with it. Propose limits (amount, assets, time).
4. **Draft (20 min).** On the written motion template, draft the relief paragraph, the reg. 95 analysis and the ex parte justification (if any). List the attachments: claim, affidavit, Form 7, security offer.
5. **Respondent's view (5 min).** Write the three strongest arguments against your request.

### Template

- [Written motion (בקשה בכתב)](../../templates/written-motion.md), interim-relief variant. The self-undertaking follows Form 7 of the First Schedule to the Regulations (⚠️ download the current form from the Judiciary's site).

### How you know it worked

- You named the specific condition (for example "reasonable fear enforcement will be substantially harder") and the evidence for it.
- You proposed a less harmful alternative if one exists.
- You know when you must serve the order and file the claim.
- You can state what you would owe if the order fell.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know the four parts of the reg. 95 test.
- [ ] I know that attachments are heard ex parte as a rule and injunctions are not.
- [ ] I know the undertaking is unlimited unless the court says otherwise.
- [ ] I know when security for costs is likely to be ordered against a company.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may file and answer requests for interim relief yourself (reg. 170). But interim relief is high-stakes and fast: the undertaking exposes you to damages, and the hearing may involve cross-examination on affidavits within days. Self-represented applicants should consider at least a consultation.

### Where and how

- File with the court that hears (or will hear) the claim, in writing with an affidavit (reg. 50); urgent requests can be filed at the registry.
- Fee before a claim is filed: as for the claim (Fees Regulations reg. 7); periods and page limits: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: Legal Aid in eligible matters; court information centre ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are asked to give a large guarantee or the respondent's potential losses are large.
- You face an attachment of your salary, accounts or home, or an exit ban.
- The order involves entering premises or seizing computers (regs 113–116, 123).
- You are a company, or the respondent is a bank or the State.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not filing the claim within seven days of a pre-claim order | Order lapses (reg. 100(3)) | Apply at once for an extension with reasons | Have the claim ready before applying |
| Ex parte order not served in time | Order lapses (reg. 100(2)) | Ask the court to revive it for special reasons | Arrange personal service the same day |
| Asking for everything (all accounts, all assets) | Court refuses or narrows; costs | Offer a narrower order | Ask for the least harmful order that works (reg. 95(ד)(2)) |
| Months of delay before applying | Urgency and good faith doubted | Explain the delay in the affidavit | Apply as soon as the risk appears |
| Ignoring a holder's non-admission | Attachment lapses for those assets (reg. 105(א)) | Apply to confirm within the period | Diary the holder's answer date |

## Review

- Every interim order needs a prima facie case, the specific condition, necessity, a balance of harms, proportionality and good faith (regs 94–95).
- It is conditional on an unlimited self-undertaking and security; the respondent's damages come from them if it falls (regs 96, 101).
- Injunctions are heard inter partes unless special reasons; attachments and evidence seizure ex parte unless notice would not defeat them; short deadlines protect the respondent (reg. 97).
- Orders lapse if not served, if the claim is not filed in time, or at judgment unless extended (reg. 100).
- Security for costs: discretionary against individuals, close to presumed against limited companies (reg. 157; Companies Law s. 353א).
