# 15.10 · Practice lab: a statement of claim and a defence for a magistrates' court case

In this lab you write both sides of one case: the statement of claim, then the defence, then a short reply. Working both sides is how litigators learn: you see which of your paragraphs the other side can deny, and which denials your pleading forces on them. Use a dispute of your own above the fast-track ceiling, or the supplied case. Nothing is filed; this is a dry run.

## Why it matters

Pleadings fix the case (lesson 15.2). A claim that is complete, structured and documented narrows the defence and invites settlement; a defence that denies in detail and raises every preliminary argument keeps all options open. Writing both sides shows you the weak points before the other side does.

## The law

The rules are those of lessons 15.1–15.3 and 15.9. Keep these open while you write:

- Structure and limits: regs 9–14; attachments: reg. 15; reply: reg. 18.
- Joinder and remedies: regs 24–27; counterclaim and third party: regs 21–23.
- Preliminary arguments: regs 28–29; form: regs 30–32; registry: reg. 33.
- Fees: [Fees Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_המשפט_(אגרות)) reg. 6 and schedule item 1; amounts in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Limitation: [Prescription Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ההתיישנות) ss. 5–9.

> (ג) כתב תביעה וכתב הגנה יכללו שלושה חלקים לפי הסדר המפורט להלן: (1) כותרת; (2) תמצית הטענות; (3) פירוט הטענות.

## Leading cases

Not a case lab. The authority is the text of the Regulations; the doctrine on deemed admission, reply, amendment and abuse is in lesson [15.2](lesson-02.md), on venue and parties in [15.1](lesson-01.md), on limitation in [15.9](lesson-09.md).

## How it works in practice

Real pleadings are written detail first, summary second, heading last; exhibits are numbered as you write. A defendant reads the claim with a highlighter: green for admit, red for deny with its own version, yellow for "not within my knowledge" (used sparingly). Then the defendant decides: preliminary arguments, counterclaim, third party, and only then the denials.

## Worked example

The supplied case for this lab:

**Mazal and Getachew Tadesse**, a couple from Rehovot (he is a software tester, she runs a daycare), ordered a kitchen from **"מטבחי השרון בע״מ"** (registered office in Kfar Saba) on 10 January 2026 for ₪96,000, paying ₪60,000 in advance. The signed quote promised installation by 15 March 2026 and stated "כל סכסוך יתברר בבית המשפט המוסמך במחוז המרכז". Installation began on 2 April 2026 by a subcontractor, **Ilan Biton** (sole trader). The countertop was cut 4 cm short, two cabinet doors did not close, and the sink was installed off-centre so that the dishwasher could not be connected. After four emails and two visits, the company wrote on 20 May 2026: "השלמנו את ההתקנה בהתאם להזמנה. היתרה בסך 36,000 ₪ לתשלום מיידי." The Tadesses had a kitchen expert (an independent carpenter) inspect on 1 June 2026; his opinion lists defects and estimates repair at ₪41,000. They paid another kitchen fitter ₪41,000 to replace the countertop and fix the cabinets, and lived without a working kitchen for nine weeks, spending ₪6,800 on take-away food and laundry. They send a pre-action letter on 15 July 2026; no answer.

Their total claim: ₪41,000 repair + ₪6,800 expenses + ₪15,000 non-pecuniary damage (distress) = ₪62,800 — under the fast-track ceiling. For this lab, to practise the ordinary track, add a claim for a declaration that they owe nothing of the ₪36,000 balance, and plead the value of the subject matter as the total of the money claim and the disputed balance (₪98,800); check reg. 10(9) and reg. 78 yourself and decide which track applies. (If you conclude it is fast track, also prepare the reg. 79 affidavit and document list.)

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- Your own dispute above the fast-track ceiling, or the Tadesse case.
- The [statement of claim template](../../templates/statement-of-claim-magistrates.md) and the [statement of defence template](../../templates/statement-of-defence.md).
- Regs 7, 9–33 open; a word processor with page numbering.
- About two and a half hours.

### Steps

**Part A: the claim (70 min)**

1. **Forum and venue (10 min).** Court (Courts Law s. 51), district (reg. 7, the clause), track (reg. 78). Write two sentences of reasons.
2. **Parties (5 min).** Plaintiffs and defendants with exact names and numbers (reg. 10(2), (5)). Decide whether to sue the subcontractor too (reg. 26(ב)) and why.
3. **Causes and remedies (10 min).** List each cause (breach of contract, with remedies under the Contracts (Remedies for Breach) Law; negligence of the installer) and each remedy with its value (regs 24–25). Check that every remedy appears in the heading.
4. **Detail (25 min).** Numbered paragraphs, one fact each, dated, each with exhibit number. Then a short legal section: which law gives each remedy.
5. **Summary (10 min).** The reg. 11 items only, within the page limit.
6. **Heading (5 min).** Reg. 10 items in order, the fee item and the first half of the fee (Fees Regulations reg. 6), the summons (Form 2).
7. **Exhibits (5 min).** A numbered list; copies only (reg. 70).

**Part B: the defence (50 min)**

8. **Switch sides.** You are now the company's in-house manager preparing the defence (as an exercise only).
9. **Preliminary arguments (10 min).** Venue? Limitation? Arbitration? Wrong defendant? Write each, or write why there is none (regs 13(1), 28–29).
10. **Third party and counterclaim (10 min).** Decide whether to issue a third-party notice against the subcontractor (reg. 22) and whether to counterclaim the ₪36,000 balance (reg. 21). Draft the opening paragraph of each.
11. **Denials (20 min).** Go through the claim paragraph by paragraph: admit, or deny with your version and exhibit (reg. 14(ב)). Put the damages in dispute.
12. **Summary and heading (10 min).** Reg. 13 summary; reg. 12 heading including the last date for the defence (reg. 30).

**Part C: reply and review (30 min)**

13. **Reply (10 min).** Back as plaintiffs: a reply of up to the reg. 18(ב) limit answering only new facts in the defence. No new cause.
14. **Registry check (10 min).** Run both pleadings through the checklist below.
15. **Revise (10 min).** Fix every item that fails.

<details>
<summary>Model points for the Tadesse case</summary>

- **Venue:** the Rehovot magistrates' court is in the Central district; the act or omission occurred in Rehovot and the clause points to the Central district, so venue is proper (reg. 7(א)).
- **Track:** the money claim is under the fast-track ceiling; adding a declaration about the balance raises the "value of the subject matter". Reg. 78(א)(1) looks at the sum claimed or the value of the subject matter; the court can move a case on or off the fast track (reg. 78(ב)–(ג)). If you plead the fast track, attach the Form 5 affidavit and documents (reg. 79). Either answer is acceptable if reasoned.
- **Parties:** sue the company (contract). Suing Ilan Biton in tort is possible (reg. 26(ב)), but the company is likely to bring him in by third-party notice.
- **Defence preliminary arguments:** none strong; limitation is not an issue (2026 events). The company might argue that the distress claim is exaggerated; that is a merits argument for the summary, not a preliminary one.
- **Counterclaim:** the ₪36,000 balance arises from the same contract, so a counterclaim is natural (reg. 21; Courts Law s. 51(א)(4)).
- **Denials:** a good defence admits the order, the price and the advance; denies that the countertop was short with its own measurement record; denies that the delay was its fault (for example, a delay by the plaintiffs in approving the design), each with an exhibit.
- **Reply:** answers the design-approval story with the email approving the design on 20 January 2026; adds nothing new.

</details>

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own case (reg. 170). The company in the supplied case would normally need a lawyer to file its defence (see [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)); Part B is an exercise in thinking as the other side, not drafting for anyone else.

### Where and how

- Filing at the registry of the magistrates' court, or on Net HaMishpat where available (lesson [08.8](../module-08/lesson-08.md)); the registry serves your claim (reg. 160).
- Fee and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side has a lawyer and files a counterclaim or third-party notice you do not understand.
- The claim requires an expert opinion that the other side disputes in detail.
- The value is close to the magistrates' ceiling or involves land rights.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summary over the page limit | Registry refuses (reg. 33) | Cut and refile within the cure period | Write the summary from a finished detail |
| A remedy in the detail but not in the heading | Fee and value wrong; remedy may be lost | Amend the heading | Build the heading from the remedies list |
| Defence that answers only the summary | Detail facts deemed admitted (reg. 14(ב)) | Amend quickly (reg. 46) | Deny the detail paragraph by paragraph |
| Forgetting the counterclaim | Separate claim later; extra fee | File a separate claim and ask to consolidate (reg. 40) | Decide with the defence (reg. 21) |

## Review

- Write the detail first, the summary second, the heading last; number exhibits as you go.
- Read your own claim as the defendant: every paragraph should force an admission or a specific denial.
- A defence starts with preliminary arguments, decides third parties and counterclaims, then denies in detail.
- A reply only answers new facts.
