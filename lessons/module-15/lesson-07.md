# 15.7 · The trial: affidavits, cross-examination and summations

The trial (שמיעת הראיות, the evidence hearing) is where facts are found. In a magistrates' court civil case it is usually short: affidavits replace much of the main testimony, cross-examination is limited in time, and summations may be oral on the day. This lesson takes lesson 12.6 (presenting a small claim) to the regular courts: how the court decides between oral and written testimony, how to write an affidavit that is admissible, how to call witnesses and experts, how cross-examination and objections work, and what happens if someone does not appear. By the end you can prepare your own evidence hearing.

## Why it matters

A good case can be lost at trial by an affidavit full of argument and hearsay, a witness who was never summoned, an expert whose opinion was filed late, or a party who stays home. The Regulations make the trial efficient by fixing these things in advance at the final pre-trial hearing (reg. 63(ד)): who testifies, for how long, and in what form. Whatever was not prepared by then is hard to add.

The doctrinal debate is about **oral versus written testimony**. Affidavits save time and let parties prepare, but they are written by lawyers, and judges cannot see a witness's demeanour while reading. The 2018 Regulations moved the default back towards oral testimony where it helps find the truth (reg. 67(א)), while keeping a preference for affidavits in large district-court cases (reg. 67(ב)). A second principle is **open justice**: hearings are public unless a statutory ground for closing them applies (Courts Law s. 68).

## The law

### Form of evidence: reg. 67

> (א) בית המשפט יחליט אם העדויות הראשיות יישמעו בעל פה או בכתב; בהחלטתו לגבי אופן שמיעת העדויות ישקול בית המשפט, בין השאר, את היקף הראיות בכתב ובעל פה, את מורכבותה של התביעה וטיב הסכסוך בין הצדדים תוך מתן עדיפות לשמיעת עדויות בעל פה, אם יש בכך כדי לסייע לגילוי האמת ולניהול יעיל של הדיון.

*Gist:* The court decides whether main testimony is oral or written, preferring oral testimony where it helps find the truth and run the hearing efficiently. ([reg. 67(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

If written testimony was ordered and a party does not file a witness's affidavit, the court may bar the witness or the fact unless there was good reason (reg. 67(א1)). After the warning, the party who called the witness examines in chief, the other side cross-examines, and re-examination is limited to unclear matters arising from the cross-examination (reg. 67(ג)).

### Affidavits: reg. 178 and Evidence Ordinance s. 15

> (א) תצהיר יהיה ערוך בגוף ראשון, מחולק לסעיפים ומכיל רק עובדות שהמצהיר יכול להעיד מתוך ידיעתו הוא…
> (ב) דין מצהיר בתצהיר חלף עדות ראשית כדין עד במשפט, ואולם התצהיר ייחשב כראיה רק אם המצהיר התייצב לחקירה נגדית.

*Gist:* An affidavit is in the first person, in numbered paragraphs, and contains only facts the deponent knows personally (an affidavit attached to a pleading may also state belief, with its source); an affidavit in lieu of testimony is evidence only if the deponent comes for cross-examination. ([reg. 178](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

If the deponent of an affidavit attached to a pleading does not come for cross-examination, the affidavit is not evidence for the party who filed it, except with leave for special reasons (reg. 178(ג)). An affidavit is valid evidence when the deponent was warned to tell the truth and that he is liable to penalties otherwise, and the warning is confirmed on its face by a judge, a lawyer or another listed person ([Evidence Ordinance s. 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הראיות)).

### Order and continuity: regs 64, 66, 76

The plaintiff's case comes first, then the defence; rebuttal evidence needs leave (reg. 66). Once evidence starts, the hearing should continue on consecutive days, and cross-examination should follow immediately after examination in chief (reg. 64(ב)). The court may split the trial and decide some issues first (reg. 76).

### Objections and the court's powers: regs 68–69

> במקרה של התנגדות לשאלה שהוצגה לעד, יציין המתנגד את נימוקי התנגדותו לאלתר ובעל הדין המבקש להציג את השאלה ישיב מיד להתנגדות; בית המשפט יכריע בעניין ההתנגדות לשאלה ובעניין קבילותה מיד…

*Gist:* State the grounds of an objection at once; the other side answers at once; the court rules immediately unless it postpones. ([reg. 68](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

The court may summon any person to testify, require anyone present to testify, recall a witness, put its own questions and order a document produced (reg. 69).

### Documents, media and remote testimony: regs 70–73

Copies, not originals, are filed unless the court orders otherwise (reg. 70). A party who wants to present electronic or digital evidence gives notice at least three days before the hearing, in the format the Courts Director sets (reg. 71). Testimony by video, in Israel or abroad, is allowed if attending would be very hard for the witness, who consents, the testimony is essential, and the foreign state does not object (reg. 72). Early testimony may be taken where there is a reasonable fear the witness cannot be heard at the usual time (reg. 73).

### Witnesses: regs 83–86

You tell your own listed witnesses the place and time, in good time; a witness who does not come is not, as a rule, a reason to postpone (reg. 83(א)). If you need a court summons, ask within 30 days of the hearing date being set and at least 14 days before the hearing (reg. 83(ב)); you undertake to pay the witness's expenses, and the court may require a deposit (regs 84, 86). A public servant gives a certificate instead of oral testimony where possible (reg. 83(ד)).

### Experts: regs 87–93

- **Medical matters:** the expert opinion is attached to the pleading (reg. 87(א)); the defendant may require the plaintiff to be examined by its expert (87(ב)); the plaintiff answers a defence opinion with a counter-opinion within a set period (87(ג)). Without an opinion you cannot prove a medical matter, save special reasons (reg. 93(א)).
- **Other matters:** the opinion is filed a set period before the evidence hearing, and the other side's within a later period (reg. 87(ה)–(ו)); it follows s. 24 of the Evidence Ordinance and lists its sources (87(ז)). You bring your expert for cross-examination unless the other side waives (87(ח)).
- **Court expert:** the court may appoint one at any time, weighing the value and the dispute (reg. 88); questions of clarification and cross-examination have their own deadlines (reg. 91); the court decides who pays and may require a deposit (reg. 92). If the parties agreed to a court expert, their own expert opinions are, by default, not evidence (reg. 88(ד)).

Periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Summations and non-appearance: regs 74–75

Summations are oral, as soon as possible after the evidence ends, unless the court orders written ones; not filing written summations on time is treated like not appearing (reg. 74). And:

> (1) לא התייצב התובע, ימחק בית המשפט את כתב התביעה או ידחה את התביעה אם מצא טעמים מיוחדים לדחייה;
> (2) לא התייצב הנתבע והתובע התייצב, רשאי התובע להוכיח את תביעתו, ויהיה זכאי לסעד שביקש לפי הראיות שהתקבלו.

*Gist:* If the plaintiff does not come without reasonable excuse, the claim is struck out (or dismissed for special reasons); if the defendant does not come, the plaintiff may prove its claim and get the relief the evidence supports. ([reg. 75](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי))

A decision given in your absence can be challenged under reg. 131 (lesson 15.3). If the judge cannot finish, another judge may continue from the same stage (reg. 77).

### Open court: Courts Law ss. 68 and 70

Courts sit in public (s. 68(א)); a hearing may be closed only on listed grounds, such as protecting a minor, a complainant in a sexual offence, a trade secret, or a witness who would otherwise be deterred (s. 68(ב)). Family matters are heard in camera (s. 68(ה)). No one may photograph in the courtroom without leave, and the court may restrict publication to protect safety or prevent serious harm to privacy (s. 70).

## Leading cases

### פלונית בע״מ נ׳ פלוני (בש״פ 8225/12, 24.2.2013)

The Supreme Court (Vogelman J., Hayut and Amit JJ. concurring) heard a request to ban publication of the identity of a suspect. It held that the principle of open proceedings and the public's right to know are the rule, and yield to dignity, reputation and privacy only in extreme circumstances, under the specific statutory tests of Courts Law s. 70.

**Why it matters:** although a criminal-procedure decision, it states the open-justice principle that governs civil trials too. A party who wants a closed hearing or a publication ban in a civil case must bring itself within a ground in s. 68(ב) or s. 70(ד); embarrassment is not enough. English translation: [Versa / LARC](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/132/).

### Key text: reg. 178

The rules on what an affidavit may contain (own knowledge, first person, no argument) and on its value only if the deponent is cross-examined are the most litigated trial rules in the magistrates' courts. Read reg. 178 with Evidence Ordinance s. 15 before you draft.

## How it works in practice

### Writing an affidavit in lieu of testimony

1. Heading: court, case number, parties, "תצהיר עדות ראשית של [name]".
2. Opening: "אני הח"מ, [name], ת"ז [__], לאחר שהוזהרתי כי עליי לומר את האמת וכי אהיה צפוי לעונשים הקבועים בחוק אם לא אעשה כן, מצהיר בזה בכתב כדלקמן:".
3. Numbered paragraphs, one fact each, chronological, each with the exhibit.
4. Only what you saw, heard or did. "Yossi told me the boiler was broken" is evidence that he said it, not that it was broken.
5. No legal argument, no adjectives, no conclusions ("it is clear that the defendant lied").
6. Closing: "זה שמי, זו חתימתי ותוכן תצהירי אמת", signature, and the lawyer's (or authorised person's) confirmation of the warning.

### Cross-examination in five rules

1. Ask short, closed, leading questions ("You received the invoice on 3 March?").
2. One fact per question.
3. Do not ask why. Do not ask a question you do not know the answer to, unless any answer helps you.
4. Confront with the document: read the line, ask the witness to confirm it.
5. Stop when you have the point; save the argument for summations.

### Summations

For each element of each cause of action: what you had to prove, the evidence (exhibit and transcript page), and why the other side's evidence does not displace it. Then the remedy and costs. Ten minutes oral, or the pages the court allows.

## Worked example

Avital Nachum, a social worker from Kiryat Shmona, sues a removal company for ₪58,000 for furniture broken in a move. Fast track; the court orders affidavits for main testimony.

1. **Affidavits.** Avital's affidavit (14 paragraphs) covers the contract, the packing she watched, the damage she saw on arrival, the photos, the repair quote. Her sister's affidavit covers only what the sister saw at the unloading. Avital removes a paragraph saying the movers "were obviously drunk": neither she nor her sister saw them drink.
2. **Expert.** Her furniture restorer's opinion was attached to the claim (reg. 79(א)(1)); the company's opinion, filed with its defence, says the damage is old. The court appoints no expert; both experts will be cross-examined (reg. 87(ח)).
3. **Witness summons.** The company's former driver, who told Avital "we dropped the cabinet", now works elsewhere. She asks the court to summon him within the reg. 83(ב) period and deposits his expenses (reg. 84).
4. **Trial day.** The company's manager is cross-examined: he confirms the inventory signed at loading lists "ארון – תקין". The driver testifies that the cabinet slipped on the stairs. The company's expert concedes he examined only photos.
5. **Summations.** Oral, ten minutes: contract, damage during the move (inventory, driver, photos), amount (restorer's opinion). Judgment within 14 days (reg. 82).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The facts of a dispute of your own (or Avital's case).
- Reg. 178 and Evidence Ordinance s. 15 open.
- About an hour.

### Steps

1. **Affidavit (25 min).** Write an affidavit in lieu of main testimony of at least ten numbered paragraphs on your own facts, with the opening and closing formulas.
2. **Clean it (10 min).** Mark every sentence that is argument, conclusion or hearsay. Delete or rewrite it.
3. **Cross-examination (15 min).** Write ten closed questions for the other side's main witness, each on one fact, each tied to a document.
4. **Summation outline (10 min).** For one cause of action, list its elements and the evidence for each.

### How you know it worked

- Every paragraph of your affidavit is a fact you know personally.
- Your affidavit includes the warning formula, ready for confirmation before a lawyer or other authorised person.
- Each cross-examination question can be answered yes or no.
- Each element of your cause has at least one piece of evidence.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may testify, examine and cross-examine witnesses and argue your case yourself (reg. 170). Your affidavit must still be confirmed before a lawyer or another person listed in Evidence Ordinance s. 15; a lawyer who only confirms the warning does not represent you. No friend or relative may question witnesses for you (lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md)).

### Where and how

- Hearing in the courtroom of the assigned judge; arrive early with three copies of every document.
- Video testimony requests and digital-evidence notices: regs 71–72; periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: court volunteer desks; court information centre ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The case turns on expert evidence and the other side has an expert you cannot answer.
- You must cross-examine a professional (a doctor, an engineer, an accountant) on technical matters.
- Allegations of fraud or forgery are made by or against you.
- A witness needs special protection or a closed hearing.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affidavit full of argument and hearsay | Paragraphs struck; weight lost | Ask leave to file a corrected affidavit | Facts from own knowledge only (reg. 178(א)) |
| Deponent does not come for cross-examination | Affidavit is not evidence (reg. 178(ב)–(ג)) | Ask leave for special reasons | Confirm attendance; summon if needed |
| Witness not told of the date | Hearing not postponed (reg. 83(א)) | Ask the court to summon for a later date | Notify in writing; request summons in time |
| Non-medical expert opinion filed late | Opinion may be excluded (reg. 87(ה)) | Ask leave with reasons | Diary the period before the hearing |
| Plaintiff does not attend | Claim struck out (reg. 75(1)) | Apply to set aside within 30 days (reg. 131) | Attend, or ask in advance to postpone with documents |

## Review

- The court decides oral or written main testimony, preferring oral where it helps find the truth (reg. 67).
- Affidavits: first person, own knowledge, warning confirmed (Evidence Ordinance s. 15); evidence only if the deponent is cross-examined (reg. 178).
- Witnesses: notify them yourself, or ask for a summons in time and pay expenses (regs 83–86). Experts: strict filing periods and cross-examination (regs 87–93).
- Objections are argued and decided on the spot (reg. 68); non-appearance ends a plaintiff's claim or lets a plaintiff prove it alone (reg. 75).
- Hearings are public unless a ground in Courts Law s. 68 applies.
