# 28.6 · The rehabilitation plan, the trustee and discharge

The opening order is the start. The heart of an individual's insolvency is the year that follows, when a trustee investigates, creditors prove their debts and the court (or the dedicated registrar) fixes an economic rehabilitation order: how much the debtor pays, for how long, which assets are sold, and which debts survive. At the end comes discharge (הפטר). This lesson covers that path, the debtor's protections and obligations along it, and the alternative of a negotiated debt arrangement.

## Why it matters

Discharge is the reason most individuals enter the process, and the law makes it the default after a fixed period rather than a favour granted after years. That default depends on the debtor's conduct: cooperation and honesty keep the period at three years; concealment, abuse or reckless borrowing can extend it or make it open-ended, and fraud can cancel the whole proceeding. For creditors, the same rules decide what they will recover and when they may object. The policy debate is the classic one between the "fresh start" and moral hazard, and the 2018 law resolves it with a structured standard: a living allowance computed by rules, a payment based on earning capacity, and a closed list of non-dischargeable debts.

## The law

### The trustee's examination and debt claims: ss. 130, 144–153, 210–211

The trustee manages the estate, investigates the debtor's finances and the circumstances of the debts (s. 144), reads the debtor's report, may demand information from listed bodies and may question people (ss. 145–152). Creditors file debt claims with the trustee within six months of publication of the order; the trustee may extend the time where the creditor could not file and justice requires it (s. 210), decides each claim, and may with the court's approval reject even a judgment debt obtained by fraud or collusion (s. 211). A small fee applies to a debt claim, except for wages and severance (Fee Regulations, reg. 6(ב)).

> (א) עם סיום בדיקת המצב הכלכלי של היחיד ולא יאוחר מתום תשעה חודשים ממועד מינוי הנאמן, יגיש הנאמן לממונה דוח ובו ממצאיו…

*Gist:* Within nine months of appointment (extendable for complexity or the debtor's non-cooperation) the trustee reports on the debtor's education and profession, the estate and how to realise it, approved claims, how the debts arose, the debtor's conduct, suspicious transactions and any business; debtor and creditors may respond within 30 days (s. 153). ([s. 153](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חדלות_פירעון_ושיקום_כלכלי#סעיף_153))

### The proposal and the order: ss. 154–161

Within 60 days of the report the Commissioner files with the court a proposed rehabilitation plan with its factual basis (s. 154); debtor and creditors respond (s. 155). The court then gives an **economic rehabilitation order** fixing: the payment obligation (amount and period) or immediate discharge; which assets join the estate and how they are realised; any restrictions that continue, where needed to protect creditors; financial-conduct training where it would help; and the debts that discharge will not cover (s. 161). In the low-debt track the dedicated registrar gives the order after a hearing on the trustee's report (s. 203).

### How much: s. 162

> (1) גובה התשלומים ייקבע על בסיס כושר ההשתכרות של היחיד לאחר שהופחתו ממנו דמי המחיה;

*Gist:* The monthly payment is earning capacity (what the debtor can earn from work and other sources, computed by the Minister's rules and adjusted to his circumstances) minus the living allowance (what the debtor and dependants need for basic living in dignity, taking into account the spouse's and children's incomes). The court may instead set a percentage of earning capacity, leaving the debtor more than the allowance, to encourage work, if he has no non-dischargeable penal debt (s. 162(א)(2)). ([ss. 160, 162](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חדלות_פירעון_ושיקום_כלכלי#סעיף_162))

### How long: s. 163

> (א) תקופת התשלומים תהיה שלוש שנים ממועד מתן הצו לשיקום כלכלי.

*Gist:* Three years is the default. It may be shorter for personal circumstances; longer where the debtor acted in bad faith in the proceedings, did not cooperate or breached restrictions, where a debt arose from a large obligation taken when he knew he likely could not pay, from grossly negligent bad-faith management of his affairs, or from criminal compensation, where he made preferences or transfers, or where he had another opening order within seven years; open-ended for serious bad faith; and longer where special economic circumstances make three years unjust to creditors (s. 163(ב)–(ה)). ([s. 163](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חדלות_פירעון_ושיקום_כלכלי#סעיף_163))

### Immediate discharge: s. 167

If earning capacity does not exceed the living allowance, the court imposes no payments and gives discharge at once, unless a ground for extension exists and there are no special circumstances; it may still sweep into the estate assets the debtor acquires for up to three years (s. 167).

### During the payment period: ss. 164–173

New assets (except exempt ones) join the estate, but the income on which payments were based does not (s. 164); the trustee may leave the debtor more for real needs (s. 165); if payments stop, the court may order the payer of the debtor's income to pay the trustee directly (s. 166). The order may be varied for changed circumstances; changes in the payment amount go to the Commissioner (s. 170); a debtor with a longer period may ask after three years to shorten it if he cooperated fully and tried hard to pay (s. 171). Three months before the end the trustee reports on compliance, and the Commissioner may ask the court to extend (ss. 172–173).

### Discharge: ss. 174–176

> (א) בתום תקופת התשלומים… יהיה היחיד, והוא בלבד, פטור מחובות העבר שלא ניתן לפרוע מנכסי קופת הנשייה.

*Gist:* At the end of the period (or at once with immediate discharge) the individual, and only he, is released from past debts not paid from the estate; guarantors are not released. ([s. 174](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חדלות_פירעון_ושיקום_כלכלי#סעיף_174))

Not discharged (s. 175(א)): penal payments (fines), debts created by fraud or arising from theft, sexual offences or serious violence, and maintenance under a judgment. The court may, in exceptional circumstances, discharge interest and penalties on a fine and maintenance arrears (s. 175(ב)), and may exclude criminal compensation from discharge (s. 175(ג)). Discharge may be revoked retroactively for new facts or later non-cooperation (s. 176).

### What stays with the debtor: ss. 217, 228, 229 and the Second Schedule

Outside the estate: food, clothing, beds, household and medical items; religious articles; tools of the trade including a vehicle; a disabled person's aids; pets; a computer, TV, phone and washing machine up to a set value; items of special sentimental value up to a linked cap; and benefits protected by the National Insurance Law (s. 217; Second Schedule). Money in a provident fund may be withdrawn only with the court's approval (s. 228). The home may be sold only with the court's approval after a hearing, if the benefit to creditors outweighs the harm, there is no less harmful way, and the family will have reasonable housing in its area or an alternative arrangement (s. 229).

### Undoing transactions: ss. 219–222

The court may set aside: a **preference** (paying or securing one creditor in the three months before the application, one year for a relative, while insolvent), with exceptions for adequate value, the ordinary course of business, small payments to non-relatives and maintenance (s. 219); a transfer for **no or inadequate value** within two years (four for a relative) while insolvent (s. 220); and any transfer made **to hide assets** within seven years (s. 221). A good-faith purchaser for value is protected (s. 222). Amounts in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### The alternative: a debt arrangement, Part 10 and Amendment No. 9

An individual may propose a debt arrangement to his creditors, with or without an opening order; if approved, an existing opening order is cancelled (ss. 185, 319). Amendment No. 9 (passed 19 January 2026, published 21 January 2026) made permanent a **temporary stay of proceedings to negotiate an arrangement**, first introduced as a COVID-era temporary provision, with a dedicated sign for individuals (ss. 319יז–319כ):

- the individual applies to the court with an outline of the proposed arrangement, the s. 104(ב) report and an undertaking to comply; not if he had such an order in the previous 12 months (s. 319יז);
- objections within 14 days of publication (s. 319יח);
- the court may grant a stay if there is a reasonable chance of approval and no real fear of fraud, asset-stripping or abuse, for up to 90 days with limited extensions (s. 319יט);
- during the stay, past debts are not paid, proceedings are frozen, an exit ban normally applies, and the debtor may not dispose of assets outside ordinary conduct; other legal duties continue (s. 319כ). ([ss. 319יז–319כ](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חדלות_פירעון_ושיקום_כלכלי#סעיף_319יז); [Amendment 9](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lsr_10981603.pdf); periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) The amendment's commencement and transitional rules could not be read reliably from the gazette PDF (⚠️ check before relying on it).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 8990/22 מנורה מבטחים פנסיה וגמל בע״מ נ׳ ינקוביץ ז״ל (26.6.2025)

A debtor in bankruptcy (under the old Ordinance) died before the insolvency court gave any direction about his pension savings. The trustee and the court below treated the severance component in his pension fund as part of the creditors' pool. The Supreme Court (Grosskopf J., with Willner and Stein JJ. concurring on partly different reasoning) allowed the fund's appeal: since the pension money had never been vested in the trustee by the court during the debtor's life, on his death it belonged to the beneficiaries under the pension arrangement, not to the creditors. Why it matters: it shows how strongly Israeli law protects pension savings in insolvency, the same policy behind s. 228 of the new law, and that the timing of a court's vesting decision can decide who gets the money. [Full text (free host)](https://www.prisha.co.il/UserFiles/File/pdf/psakdin/psakdin26062025.pdf).

### Key text: s. 163(ג) — conduct sets the length

The extension grounds are the law's answer to moral hazard. For a debtor, they are a checklist of what not to do; for a creditor, of what to prove when objecting to the Commissioner's proposal.

## How it works in practice

### The timeline (default case)

| Stage | Who | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| Opening order; trustee appointed | Commissioner / registrar | Order within 30 days of a complete application |
| Debt claims | Creditors → trustee | Within six months of publication |
| Findings report | Trustee | Up to nine months from appointment |
| Responses to report | Debtor, creditors | 30 days |
| Commissioner's proposal | Commissioner → court | 60 days from the report |
| Rehabilitation order | Court (registrar in the low-debt track) | After a hearing |
| Payment period | Debtor pays the trustee | Default three years |
| Final report; discharge | Trustee; then automatic discharge and the Commissioner's confirmation | Report three months before the end |

Periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### What the debtor does every month

1. Pays the trustee on time.
2. Reports any change in income, household or address.
3. Keeps payslips and bank statements.
4. Asks before taking credit, travelling or starting a business.
5. Keeps the insolvency status visible when contracting (s. 124).

## Worked example

Continuing Natalia (lesson 28.5). Her trustee's report, eight months after appointment, finds that her business failed after a long road closure and her illness, that she disclosed everything, and that she paid her brother ₪20,000 two months before applying "to return a loan".

1. **The brother.** A payment to a relative within one year before the application while insolvent is a preference (s. 219). The trustee asks the brother to return the ₪20,000 to the estate; he agrees to repay in instalments.
2. **Earning capacity.** She is a trained bookkeeper earning ₪7,400 as a shop assistant. The trustee assesses earning capacity at ₪8,500 under the rules. Living allowance for her and her son, after her son's small income, is set at ₪6,600.
3. **Payment.** ₪8,500 − ₪6,600 = ₪1,900 a month. The court, to encourage her to take bookkeeping work, sets 20% of earning capacity instead, so she keeps more if she earns more (s. 162(א)(2)): about ₪1,700 now.
4. **Period.** No extension ground: the brother's payment was a preference but she disclosed it and cooperated. Three years.
5. **Debts surviving.** None of hers are on the s. 175 list (VAT and National Insurance are ordinary past debts here, though some of them may have priority in the distribution, s. 234).
6. **Discharge.** After 36 payments and the trustee's final report, she is discharged from the balance.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The facts you used in lesson 28.5, or the supplied case: Ahmad Zoabi (lesson 28.5), with these findings: net income ₪6,800, earning capacity ₪7,000, living allowance for six people after his wife's income ₪6,900; he sold his car to his cousin for ₪5,000 eleven months before the application (market value ₪25,000); one debt is a traffic fine of ₪2,400.
- Insolvency Law ss. 162–167, 175, 217, 219–221.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Payment (10 min).** Compute earning capacity minus living allowance. Does s. 167 (immediate discharge) apply?
2. **Conduct (10 min).** Test the car sale under s. 220 (and s. 221). Is it also an extension ground under s. 163(ג)(3)?
3. **Exempt assets (5 min).** What stays with the family under s. 217 and the Second Schedule?
4. **Surviving debts (5 min).** Which debts survive discharge?
5. **Outcome (15 min).** Write a one-page, reasoned proposal as if you were the Commissioner: payment, period, assets, restrictions, training, non-dischargeable debts.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. The debtor files reports, responds to the trustee and the Commissioner's proposal, and may appear in the hearing in his own name. A creditor may file a debt claim and object in his own name. Complex disputes (avoidance claims, home sale, cancellation requests) are where a lawyer, or Legal Aid for eligible debtors, matters most.

### Where and how

- Debt claims: to the trustee, within the period, on the official form, with the fee where it applies.
- The debtor's responses and requests: to the Commissioner (or the registrar in the low-debt track), and to the court for rehabilitation-order matters.
- A temporary stay to negotiate an arrangement: an application to the court (Amendment 9); check the Commissioner's published declaration form.
- Figures: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The trustee wants to undo a transfer to a relative, or to sell your home.
- The Commissioner proposes an extended or open-ended period.
- You want to propose a debt arrangement with secured creditors or while running a business.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing monthly payments | Period extended; possible cancellation (ss. 170(ב), 183) | Pay arrears; ask to vary the amount (s. 170(ג)) | Ask for a realistic amount at the proposal stage |
| Hiding a new job | Discharge revoked or period extended (s. 176) | Report it | Report every change |
| Creditor misses the debt-claim period | No share in the estate | Ask the trustee to extend (s. 210(ג)) | Diary six months from publication |
| Assuming a guarantor is freed by the debtor's discharge | Guarantor still sued | Guarantor's own defences (module 20) | Read s. 174(א): "he alone" |
| Selling a car cheaply to a relative before applying | Set aside (s. 220); longer period (s. 163(ג)(3)) | Disclose and restore | Do not transfer assets before applying |

## Review

- Trustee's report within nine months, Commissioner's proposal within 60 days, then the rehabilitation order.
- Payment = earning capacity − living allowance, for three years by default; immediate discharge when nothing is left over.
- Bad faith, non-cooperation, preferences and transfers lengthen the period or end the proceedings.
- Discharge releases the debtor alone, and not from fines, fraud debts or judgment maintenance.
- A debt arrangement is the negotiated alternative; since Amendment 9 an individual can ask the court for a temporary stay to reach one.
