# 12.2 · Evidence you can collect lawfully

The small claims court is flexible about evidence, but it still decides on proof, and evidence obtained by breaking the law can be excluded and can expose you to a criminal charge or a privacy suit. In this lesson you learn where the legal line runs for recordings, photos and other people's messages, and then you fill the gaps in your facts file with evidence you may lawfully collect and use.

## Why it matters

Most everyday disputes are won by one or two documents: the message where the landlord promised to return the deposit, the photo of the flat on handover day, the technician's report. Many people also reach for a hidden recorder, a camera pointed at the neighbour's door or a screenshot from someone else's phone. Israeli law draws a sharp distinction. Recording a conversation **you take part in** is not secret eavesdropping. Recording a conversation between **other people** without the consent of any of them is a crime punishable by five years' imprisonment, and the recording is inadmissible. Between these poles, the Privacy Law protects people in their private domain, their letters and their private information, and makes material obtained by infringing privacy inadmissible unless the court allows it.

Knowing the line lets you collect strong evidence with confidence and avoid turning a ₪5,000 claim into a criminal file against you.

## The law

### Recording a conversation: Eavesdropping Law ss. 1, 2, 13

> ”האזנה“ – האזנה לשיחת הזולת, קליטה או העתקה של שיחת הזולת, והכל באמצעות מכשיר;
> ”האזנת סתר“ – האזנה ללא הסכמה של אף אחד מבעלי השיחה;

*Gist:* "Eavesdropping" is listening to, capturing or copying **another's** conversation by a device; it becomes **secret** eavesdropping only when **none** of the parties to the conversation consents. A party to a conversation (the speaker or the person it is addressed to) who records it is not eavesdropping on "another's" conversation and, in any case, consents. ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_האזנת_סתר#סעיף_1))

> המאזין האזנת סתר שלא על פי היתר כדין, דינו – מאסר חמש שנים.

*Gist:* Unlawful secret eavesdropping is a crime; so is knowingly using or disclosing its product (s. 2(ב)) and planting a device for it (s. 2(ג)). ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_האזנת_סתר#סעיף_2))

> דברים שנקלטו בדרך של האזנת סתר בניגוד להוראות חוק זה… לא יהיו קבילים כראיה בבית משפט…

*Gist:* What was captured by unlawful secret eavesdropping is inadmissible, with narrow exceptions that do not help a civil plaintiff (consent of a party in some cases, or serious criminal proceedings). ([s. 13(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_האזנת_סתר#סעיף_13))

**In practice:** you may record your own phone call with the landlord or the shop's call centre, with or without telling them, and offer it in court. You may not leave your phone recording in the landlord's office after you leave, or record a conversation between the shop manager and his staff.

### Privacy: Protection of Privacy Law ss. 1, 2, 18, 32

> לא יפגע אדם בפרטיות של זולתו ללא הסכמתו.

*Gist:* No one may infringe another's privacy without consent. ([s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_1))

Section 2 lists what counts as an infringement. The items an evidence collector meets most:

> (3) צילום אדם כשהוא ברשות היחיד;
> (5) העתקת תוכן של מכתב או כתב אחר שלא נועד לפרסום, או שימוש בתכנו, בלי רשות מאת הנמען או הכותב…

*Gist:* Photographing a person while he is in his private domain, and copying or using a letter or other writing (including an electronic message) not meant for publication without the permission of the addressee or the writer, are infringements; so are prohibited eavesdropping (2(2)), stalking and harassment (2(1)), and using private information for a purpose other than the one for which it was given (2(9)). ([s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_2))

Messages the other side sent **to you** are yours to use: you are the addressee. Messages between other people that you obtained from someone else's phone are not.

> חומר שהושג תוך פגיעה בפרטיות יהיה פסול לשמש ראיה בבית משפט, ללא הסכמת הנפגע, זולת אם בית המשפט התיר מטעמים שיירשמו להשתמש בחומר, או אם היו לפוגע, שהיה צד להליך, הגנה או פטור לפי חוק זה.

*Gist:* Material obtained by infringing privacy is inadmissible without the victim's consent, unless the court allows it for recorded reasons or the party who obtained it has a defence under the Law. The exclusion is therefore relative, not absolute. ([s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_32)) The defences in s. 18 include an infringement done in good faith to protect a legitimate personal interest (s. 18(2)(ג)) and a photo taken in the public domain in which the person appears incidentally (s. 18(2)(ה)). Do not plan on a defence: collect evidence that needs none.

### The small claims court's flexibility: Courts Law s. 62(א)

The small claims court may admit evidence that would be inadmissible elsewhere, except privileged material, and is not bound by ordinary procedure. ([s. 62](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט#סעיף_62)) This relaxes formal rules (a printout without a certificate, a written statement instead of a witness), but it does not make a criminal recording or a privacy-infringing photo safe to obtain, and the court may still refuse or discount it.

### Information about you: Privacy Law s. 13

> כל אדם זכאי לעיין בעצמו… במידע האישי שעליו המוחזק במאגר מידע.

*Gist:* You may inspect the personal information about you held in a business's database (call records, account history, sometimes recordings of your calls with its service centre); the manner and fees are set in regulations. ([s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הפרטיות#סעיף_13)) Ask in writing, specify what you want, and diary the follow-up.

## Leading cases

### Key text: the definitions in s. 1 of the Eavesdropping Law

No ruling is needed to decide the everyday question. The definition of "האזנת סתר" ("without the consent of any of the parties") answers it: a participant's recording is outside the offence. University courses on evidence teach this from the text. Where a ruling would matter (a recording you were not part of, a photo taken inside someone's home), you are already in lawyer territory (lesson 12.8).

### Key text: s. 32 of the Privacy Law and its relative exclusion

Section 32 leaves the court a discretion ("זולת אם בית המשפט התיר מטעמים שיירשמו"). Courts weigh the importance of the evidence for finding the truth against the seriousness of the infringement and whether it was itself a crime. A self-represented party should treat any privacy-infringing material as likely to be excluded and as a possible ground for a counterclaim. (⚠️ The leading Supreme Court decisions on s. 32 could not be opened on a free host on 17 October 2026 and are not cited.)

## How it works in practice

### The traffic-light table

| Evidence | Light | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your own contract, receipts, bank and card statements | Green | Yours |
| Messages, emails and letters the other side sent you | Green | You are the addressee (Privacy Law s. 2(5) does not bite) |
| Recording of a call or meeting you take part in | Green | Not "secret eavesdropping": you are a party (Eavesdropping Law s. 1) |
| Photos of your flat, your car, the product, a public street | Green | Your property or public domain; people appearing incidentally (s. 18(2)(ה)) |
| A professional's written quote or assessor's report | Green | Ordered by you |
| A business's CCTV of an incident involving you | Amber | Ask the business in writing at once; footage is often overwritten within days; it may refuse; the court can order it |
| Your home camera covering a shared stairwell | Amber | Limited to your door; do not aim at neighbours' doors or windows |
| Photo of a person inside his own home or garden | Red | Photographing a person in the private domain (s. 2(3)) |
| A recording left running after you left the room | Red | Others' conversation, no party consents: a crime (s. 2) |
| Messages copied from someone else's phone or email | Red | Writings not meant for you (s. 2(5)); possibly computer offences |

### Collecting and preserving

- **Messages.** Use the app's "export chat" function and also take dated screenshots that show the sender's number and the date; print to PDF.
- **Recordings.** Keep the original file unedited, note date, time and participants, and write a transcript of the relevant part (Hebrew). For a small claim filed online, audio and video go on USB drives delivered to the registry (lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)).
- **Photos.** Keep the original files with their metadata; on handover or delivery day photograph wide shots first, then details, and send them to the other side the same day so the date cannot be disputed.
- **Quotes and reports.** A written quote from a licensed professional, with his details, is stronger than your own estimate. For larger damage an assessor's or technician's report.
- **Witnesses.** Ask early, note what they saw, and tell them the hearing date. The court does not summon your witnesses for you; ask for a summons if needed.
- **The business's records.** Write to the company asking for your account history and recordings of your calls with its service centre (Privacy Law s. 13). Write to a business with CCTV the same day an incident happens, asking it to preserve the footage.

### Preparing exhibits

Number them as in your evidence index, one PDF per exhibit, named "נספח 1 – חוזה שכירות". Refer to each by number in the claim. Online filing accepts PDFs up to a total size limit (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); bring the originals and copies to the hearing.

## Worked example

Rami Haddad, a teacher from Nazareth, was promised by phone by a cellular company's sales agent that his new plan would cost ₪59 a month "forever, no increases". After three months the bill rose to ₪89. The company's written terms are vague.

**His evidence plan.**

1. **The sales call.** He did not record it. He writes to the company (Privacy Law s. 13) asking for the recording of the sales call and his account history. The company sends the account history; it says the recording "is not available".
2. **The follow-up call.** Rami calls the service centre and records the call on his phone (green: he is a party). The agent says: "I can see the sales agent promised you 59 shekels, but the system changed." He transcribes those two sentences.
3. **The bills.** He downloads the bills as PDFs (green).
4. **A tempting shortcut.** His cousin works at a branch and offers to forward internal emails about the price change. Rami refuses: those are writings not addressed to him (Privacy Law s. 2(5)), and using them could expose both of them.
5. **Exhibits.** נספח 1 – the plan confirmation SMS; נספח 2 – bills; נספח 3 – account history; נספח 4 – recording (USB) with transcript; נספח 5 – his s. 13 request and the reply.

In lesson 12.4 Rami complains to the regulator; in 12.5 he would sue for the overcharge.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your facts file from lesson 12.1, with the facts marked "no exhibit yet".
- A phone that records calls (check your device's settings and the app's notice), a PDF printer or scanner.
- About 90 minutes, plus waiting time for answers to your written requests.

### Steps

1. **Gap list (10 min).** List every fact in your chronology without an exhibit. For each, write what would prove it.
2. **Traffic light (15 min).** Classify each planned piece of evidence green, amber or red using the table, with the section that decides it. Drop every red item.
3. **Collect the greens (30 min).** Export the chats, download statements, photograph the product or the flat, ask for a written quote.
4. **Written requests (15 min).** Send a short letter or email to the business asking for your personal information or for footage to be preserved (s. 13). Log it in the case log with a follow-up date.
5. **One recorded call (optional, 10 min).** If a key fact is only oral, call the other side, ask a calm factual question, record, and transcribe the answer.
6. **Package (10 min).** Convert every exhibit to a numbered PDF; update the evidence index.

- [Clinic case file](../../templates/clinic-case-file.md), part ד (evidence index) — new in this module.

### How you know it worked

- Every fact in the chronology now points to an exhibit or is honestly marked "oral only".
- No exhibit comes from a conversation you were not part of, a private-domain photo, or someone else's messages.
- Every recording has a transcript and a note of date, time and participants.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know that recording my own call is not secret eavesdropping.
- [ ] I know that recording others' conversation is a crime and inadmissible.
- [ ] I sent a written request for information or footage and logged a follow-up date.
- [ ] My exhibits are numbered PDFs that match the index.

## Without a lawyer

> [!WARNING]
> Secret eavesdropping, planting a recording device and using its product are crimes (Eavesdropping Law s. 2). If you already made a recording of a conversation you were not part of, do not use or share it; take advice before doing anything with it.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Collecting your own documents, recording conversations you take part in and asking businesses for your information need no lawyer. Asking another person to collect evidence for you does not change who is responsible for how it was obtained.

### Where and how

- Business records about you: a written request under Privacy Law s. 13 to the company's privacy or customer-service address.
- Evidence formats for small claims: lesson [08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md); size limits in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Privacy questions: the Privacy Protection Authority at the Ministry of Justice publishes guidance (gov.il, ⚠️ manual check).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your key evidence is a recording you were not part of, a photo inside someone's home, or messages from someone else's device.
- The other side threatens a privacy or defamation suit over evidence or posts.
- The business refuses to preserve footage and the incident involved injury.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leaving the phone recording in the room after you leave | Crime (s. 2); recording inadmissible (s. 13) | Do not use it; take advice | Record only while you are in the conversation |
| Using screenshots from a friend's phone of the landlord's messages to him | Privacy infringement (s. 2(5)); likely excluded (s. 32) | Ask the friend to testify about what he received | Use only messages sent to you, or witnesses |
| Filming the neighbour's balcony to prove noise | Photo in the private domain (s. 2(3)); counterclaim risk | Record sound levels from your own flat; keep a log | Film your own space only |
| Editing a recording to the "good part" | Credibility destroyed | Submit the full original with a transcript of the relevant part | Never edit originals |
| Asking for CCTV a month later | Footage overwritten | Rely on witnesses and other evidence | Write on the same day |

## Review

- Recording a conversation you take part in is not secret eavesdropping; recording others' conversation without any party's consent is a crime, and the recording is inadmissible (Eavesdropping Law ss. 1, 2, 13).
- Photos in someone's private domain and copying writings not addressed to you infringe privacy; such material is inadmissible unless the court allows it (Privacy Law ss. 2, 32).
- Small claims relaxes the form of evidence (Courts Law s. 62) but not the legality of how you got it.
- You may ask a business for your own information (Privacy Law s. 13); ask early for footage.
- Package every exhibit as a numbered PDF; transcribe every recording.
