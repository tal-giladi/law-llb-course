# 14.4 · Fair procedure: the right to be heard, reasons and conflict of interest

By the end of this lesson you will be able to insist on being heard before an authority decides against you, force a written answer with reasons within the statutory period, and recognise when the official deciding your case should not be deciding it. Procedural defects are often easier to prove than bad discretion, and fixing them gives you a second chance on the merits.

## Why it matters

Lesson [02.7](../module-02/lesson-07.md) used the Decisions and Reasons Law to write a formal letter. This lesson goes to the doctrine behind it: the rules of natural justice (כללי הצדק הטבעי) and their statutory supplement. Israeli administrative law has three procedural pillars:

- **The right to be heard** (זכות הטיעון) before a decision that harms a person's interests.
- **The duty to decide and give reasons** within a set time.
- **Freedom from bias and conflict of interest** (ניגוד עניינים, דעה קדומה): no one decides a matter in which they have a personal stake, or that they have already prejudged.

Courts treat these as conditions of a lawful decision. A decision made without hearing you is usually sent back to be made again, properly. For a self-represented person, a request to be heard is also the cheapest and fastest remedy: officials who meet you often change their minds.

## The law

### Who is bound

> בחוק זה – ”עובד הציבור“ – עובד מדינה, עובד רשות מקומית וכן כל רשות שהוענקה לה סמכות על פי דין.

*Gist:* The Law binds State and local employees and any body with statutory power. ([Decisions and Reasons Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_(החלטות_והנמקות)))

### The duty to decide and answer

> (א) נתבקש עובד הציבור, בכתב, להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין, יחליט בבקשה וישיב למבקש בכתב בהקדם, אך לא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום קבלת הבקשה.

*Gist:* A written request to use a power must be decided and answered in writing promptly, within the set period ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Exceptions (s. 2(ב)): the matter needs a statutory investigation, another law sets another period, further examination is needed, or other arrangements set a later date; in those cases the official must tell you in writing, within the same period, which exception applies and, for some, the new date (s. 2(ג)). A request sent to the wrong official must be passed on or you must be told who is competent; the period then runs from receipt by the right official (s. 2ב).

### Reasons for a refusal, and exemptions

> עובד הציבור שנתבקש כאמור בסעיף 2(א) וסירב לבקשה, יודיע למבקש בכתב את נימוקי סירובו.

*Gist:* A refusal must state its reasons in writing (s. 2א). Section 3 exempts: a law allowing a decision without reasons; State security or foreign relations; a refused appointment to a post; harm to a third party's rights; professional secrets. If reasons are withheld, the official must say which exemption applies, and you may appeal within 30 days to the responsible minister (s. 4). Where a decision is subject to an objection or appeal, the official must tell you of the right, how to use it and the deadline (s. 5).

### What happens if the official does not comply

> (א) החלטה או פעולה של עובד הציבור לא יהיו פסולות בשל כך בלבד שהוא לא מילא אחרי הוראות הסעיפים 2א, 2ב, 4 או 5; ואולם בכל הליך לפני ועדת ערר או ועדת ערעור או לפני בית המשפט, על עובד הציבור הראיה כי החלטה או פעולה שלא ניתנה לגביהן תשובה או הודעה כאמור, נעשו כדין.
> (ב) לא השיב עובד הציבור או לא הודיע תוך שלושת החדשים כאמור בסעיף 2(א) או (ג), רואים בכך, לענין כל דין, החלטה לסרב לבקשה, ללא מתן נימוקים.

*Gist:* A missing reason does not by itself void the decision, but in any appeal or court proceeding the official must prove the decision was lawful: the presumption of regularity is reversed. Silence for three months counts as a refusal without reasons, which you can then challenge. (s. 6) Note: the Law does not apply to certain firearms decisions and to most decisions under the Entry into Israel Law (s. 9).

### The right to be heard: judge-made

No general statute grants a right to be heard before an administrative decision; it comes from the common law as adopted by the Supreme Court (Berman, below). Many statutes add specific hearings (for example, before dismissal of a public employee or revocation of a licence). Lesson [07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md) recapped Wael: there is no general right to be heard before regulations of general application.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 3/58 ברמן נ׳ שר הפנים (30.10.1958)

The Minister of the Interior moved a neighbourhood from Tel Aviv to Givatayim after a commission of inquiry, and abruptly refused to meet residents who opposed the change. The Court held that, under a long-established common-law rule, a citizen must be given a fair hearing when their person, property or status may be affected, even when the decision is not judicial in nature; the Minister, though acting in good faith, had fallen short. It added that the commission did not have to seek out the other side on its own initiative: those who did not come forward had themselves to blame.

Why it matters: it established the right to be heard as a general rule of Israeli administrative law, and its second point is a practical warning: if you know a process is under way, ask to be heard. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/93)

### Key text: conflict of interest and prejudgment

The rule that no one may decide a matter in which they have a personal or institutional interest, or which they have already decided in their mind, is judge-made and applies to every official, committee member and minister. A member of a planning or tender committee who is related to an applicant must not take part; a minister whose personal interest is affected must not decide. The rule was at the centre of the May 2025 ruling on the dismissal of the head of the Shin Bet, described in lesson [06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md) (that ruling's text was not available to this course from a free source; treat the description as reported). Supporters of strict conflict rules see them as basic integrity; critics argue that applying them to a Prime Minister's choice of senior officials prevents an elected government from governing.

## How it works in practice

### Asking to be heard

Ask in writing, as soon as you learn a decision affecting you is being considered: "I ask to be heard before any decision, in writing and in person, and to see the material the decision will rely on." The right to be heard includes knowing the main facts and allegations against you; ask for them. If the authority refuses, record the refusal: it is your first ground.

### Using the 45-day clock

1. Send your request in writing to the competent official, with proof of delivery.
2. Diary day 45 from receipt. If there is no answer and no s. 2(ג) notice, send a short reminder citing s. 2(א).
3. After three months of silence you have a deemed refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)): you may object, appeal or petition, and the authority will carry the burden of showing legality.
4. If you get a refusal without reasons and no s. 3 exemption is stated, ask for reasons and note s. 6(א).

### Conflict of interest

Ask the authority who will decide, and who sits on the committee. If a member has a link to the other side, raise it in writing before the decision. Raising it only after you lose looks tactical and may be held too late.

## Worked example

Avi Mizrahi, a reservist and kiosk owner in Kiryat Shmona, holds a municipal permit to place tables on the pavement. In June he receives a letter: "Following an inspection, your permit is revoked from 1 July." There was no warning. He also learns that the deputy mayor who chairs the permits committee owns the café across the road.

1. **Hearing.** Revocation of an existing permit harms his property and occupation: Berman applies. He writes at once: he asks to be heard, to see the inspection report, and for the revocation to be suspended until then.
2. **Reasons.** He asks for the reasons and the legal source of the revocation (lesson 14.1) under s. 2א; the letter gave none.
3. **Conflict.** In the same letter he asks that the deputy mayor not take part in any decision on his permit, stating the reason.
4. **Clock.** He diaries 45 days for the answer and the petition period (lesson 14.9) from the date he received the revocation letter, not from the end of the correspondence.
5. **Result.** The municipality holds a hearing with another committee member; the inspection report shows tables blocking a wheelchair route. They agree on fewer tables and a marked passage. The procedural request led to a better decision on the merits.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A pending or recent decision affecting you, or Avi's case.
- The [Decisions and Reasons Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_(החלטות_והנמקות)).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Map the procedure (10 min).** Who decides? Is there a hearing in the governing statute? Was one held? Did you see the material?
2. **Compute the clock (10 min).** Date of your request's receipt; day 45; three months; any s. 2(ג) notice received.
3. **Check reasons (10 min).** Did you get reasons? If not, is a s. 3 exemption stated? Is there a right of appeal you were told about (s. 5)?
4. **Conflict check (5 min).** List the decision-makers and any link to the other side.
5. **Draft the request (15 min).** One page: request to be heard, to see the material, for reasons, and for recusal if needed.

### Template

[Letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md), which already contains the Decisions and Reasons paragraphs.

### How you know it worked

- Your letter names s. 2(א), s. 2א and, if relevant, s. 6.
- You asked to see the material, not only to "be heard".
- You raised any conflict before the decision.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can explain Berman's two lessons.
- [ ] I know the periods in s. 2 and s. 6(ב) and where they are recorded.
- [ ] I know the five s. 3 exemptions and the s. 4 appeal.
- [ ] I know that a missing reason shifts the burden rather than voiding the decision.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Requests to be heard, for reasons and for recusal are letters you write in your own name. At the hearing itself you may come alone; whether you may bring a lawyer depends on the statute and the authority's rules (Bar Association Law s. 22 gives a right to be represented by a lawyer before public bodies).

### Where and how

- Send to the deciding official and the authority's legal adviser, by email and registered mail.
- Periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) (45 days; three months' silence; 30-day s. 4 appeal).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The hearing is a disciplinary or dismissal hearing with serious consequences for your job or licence.
- The authority wants to rely on confidential material you cannot see.
- The conflict of interest involves senior officials and you need evidence to prove it.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting to be invited to a hearing | Court may say you had yourself to blame (Berman) | Ask in writing at once | Ask whenever you learn a process has started |
| Treating the 45-day period as a deadline for you | You lose time while the petition period runs | Count petition periods from the decision | Diary both clocks separately |
| Arguing the decision is void because no reasons were given | Rejected: s. 6(א) shifts the burden but does not void | Argue the burden shift and the merits | Quote s. 6 accurately |
| Raising conflict of interest after losing | Seen as tactical; possible waiver | Explain when you learned of it | Ask who decides at the start |
| Asking for a hearing without asking for the material | An empty hearing | Ask for the report and allegations | Use the template paragraph |

## Review

- The right to be heard is a general judge-made rule for decisions affecting a person's interests (Berman); there is no general right to be heard before regulations (Wael).
- The Decisions and Reasons Law requires a written decision within the set period and reasons for refusals, with five exemptions and a 30-day appeal to the minister.
- Missing reasons do not void a decision but shift the burden of proving legality to the authority; three months' silence is a refusal without reasons.
- Officials with a personal interest or a closed mind must not decide; raise it early.
- Procedural requests are cheap, fast and often change the decision itself.
