# 14.6 · Tenders, administrative contracts and administrative promises

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell when a public body must run a tender and what a fair tender requires, use the losing bidder's right to see the winning bid and the committee's file, and decide whether an official's promise to you is legally binding. These are the points where administrative law meets contract law (module 3): the State buys, sells and promises like a private person, but it remains a public trustee.

## Why it matters

Public bodies spend a large share of the economy through contracts: construction, cleaning, software, land, licences. The Mandatory Tenders Law 1992 requires most of these contracts to go through a public tender that gives everyone an equal chance. For a small business, a tender is a market; for a citizen, it is a guarantee that public money and public land are not handed out by connections.

Administrative contracts and promises raise a deeper question taught in every administrative-law course: when the State acts like a private party, is it bound by private law (the contract), by public law (equality, reasonableness, the duty to use discretion), or both? Israeli law answers "both" (the dual-norm doctrine), and the tensions show in three places: the tender, the contract once signed, and the promise an official made to you.

## The law

### The duty to tender

> (א) המדינה, כל תאגיד ממשלתי, תאגיד מקומי, מועצה דתית, קופת חולים ומוסד להשכלה גבוהה לא יתקשרו בחוזה לביצוע עיסקה בטובין או במקרקעין, או לביצוע עבודה, או לרכישת שירותים, אלא על־פי מכרז פומבי הנותן לכל אדם הזדמנות שווה להשתתף בו.

*Gist:* The State, government corporations, local-authority corporations, religious councils, health funds and State-funded universities may contract for goods, land, works or services only by a public tender giving everyone an equal opportunity. ([Mandatory Tenders Law s. 2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חובת_המכרזים)) Municipalities themselves tender under their own rules, including the Municipalities (Tenders) Regulations 1987.

> (ב) לא יפלה עורך המכרז בין מציעים מחמת מוגבלות, מין, נטיה מינית, מעמד אישי, גיל, הורות, גזע, דת, לאומיות, ארץ מוצא, השקפה או חברות במפלגה [...]

*Gist:* No discrimination between bidders on listed grounds; a condition irrelevant to the subject counts as discrimination, but a distinction required by the tender's nature does not (s. 2(ב)–(ג)). A threshold condition must be required by the tender's nature (s. 2א(א)), and stricter conditions on listed subjects must be reasoned in the tender documents (s. 2א(ב)).

### Exemptions

The Minister of Finance sets exemptions by regulation, including urgent contracts to prevent real harm and "special and rare circumstances" (s. 4(ב)). The Mandatory Tenders Regulations list them (reg. 3): small contracts up to a set value, urgent contracts, contracts whose tendering would harm security or foreign relations, follow-on contracts under conditions, and more. Check the value threshold in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Choosing the winner and seeing the file

> (ב) ועדת המכרזים תבחר את ההצעה הזולה או היקרה ביותר, לפי הענין, זולת אם החליטה שלא לעשות כן בנסיבות מיוחדות ומטעמים מיוחדים שיירשמו ולאחר שנתנה לבעל ההצעה הזולה או היקרה ביותר, לפי הענין, הזדמנות להביא טענותיו בפניה. [...]

*Gist:* Unless the tender sets other published criteria, the committee picks the best-priced bid, and may depart from it only for special recorded reasons after hearing that bidder. ([Mandatory Tenders Regulations reg. 21(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_חובת_המכרזים))

> (ה)(1) המשתתף יהיה רשאי בתוך 30 ימים ממועד מסירת ההודעה לעיין בפרוטוקול ועדת המכרזים, בהתכתבויותיה עם המציעים, בחוות דעת מקצועיות שהוכנו לבקשתה, בעמדת היועץ המשפטי בוועדה ובהצעת הזוכה במכרז, ולקבל עותק ממסמכים אלה, למעט [...]

*Gist:* Every bidder may, within the set period after notice of the result, inspect and copy the committee's minutes, correspondence, expert opinions, legal adviser's position and the winning bid, except trade secrets, security matters and internal legal advice. (reg. 21(ה))

For municipalities: no negotiation with a bidder before the winner is chosen (Municipalities (Tenders) Regulations reg. 18א), and the committee recommends the cheapest fair bid, recording reasons for any other choice (reg. 22(ב)–(ג)). ([Municipalities (Tenders) Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_העיריות_(מכרזים)))

### Where to sue

Tender matters of these bodies, and of local authorities, are First Schedule item 5 of the Administrative Affairs Courts Law: an administrative petition. A claim for damages arising from a tender is an administrative claim (Third Schedule item 1). ([Administrative Affairs Courts Law, schedules](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 316/63 גזית נ׳ רשות הנמלים (23.1.1964)

A port authority tendered for a warehouse, then negotiated a lower price with a bidder who was not the cheapest but was already building a wharf on the site, because of an urgency only that bidder knew about. The lowest bidder petitioned. The Court held that a public tender serves two aims, the best price for the public and fair competition on equal terms; that a contract may not be awarded on grounds undisclosed in the tender; and that a bid must be accepted or rejected as it stands, without negotiating a reduction with one bidder. Saving public money in good faith does not justify breaking equality.

Why it matters: it states the core of tender law sixty years before the current statute, and its rule against negotiation with one bidder is now written into the municipal regulations (reg. 18א). [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/166)

### בג״ץ 164/97 קונטרם בע״מ נ׳ משרד האוצר (4.2.1998)

A company obtained a customs warehouse licence without disclosing that its right to the land was disputed; when the authority learned of the dispute it refused to renew. All three justices upheld the refusal, each differently: Justice Zamir held that citizens owe the government a reciprocal duty of fairness, including disclosure of material facts; President Barak held that any duty of the individual is specific to the matter and arises because proper decisions need the material facts; Justice Cheshin held that the individual has no duty to volunteer information, the authority should ask, but the company did not meet a condition of the regulations.

Why it matters: when you deal with an authority (a tender, a licence, a grant), you may lose your remedy if you hid material facts. The three opinions are a classic university debate on the relationship between citizen and State. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/115)

### בג״ץ 135/75 סאי־טקס קורפוריישן בע״מ נ׳ שר המסחר והתעשייה, פ״ד ל(1) 673

Exporters were promised a special loan per dollar of exports, convertible into a grant. After a devaluation increased their profits, the Government stopped paying the promised aid. The Court, for the first time, treated the promise itself as a ground for ordering the authority to perform it. As summarised in the HUJI article listed in the sources, the elements are: a promise given by an official authorised to give it, within the authority's power, which the authority is able to fulfil, and with no legal justification for departing from it; a change of circumstances that makes the public interest require a change is such a justification, after weighing the promisee's reliance.

Why it matters: a written assurance from the right official can bind the authority; an oral remark from a clerk usually cannot. (The ruling's full text was not available from a free source; citation and summary are from the HUJI article.) [Stein, "Administrative promise", Mishpatim 14 (1985)](https://lawjournal.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/2018-02/הבטחה%20מינהלית.pdf)

## How it works in practice

### Checking a tender you lost

1. **Was a tender required?** Is the body in s. 2? Is the contract goods, land, works or services? Was an exemption invoked, and which?
2. **Were the conditions relevant?** Threshold conditions that fit one bidder only, or that demand experience with this very authority, need reasons (s. 2א).
3. **Was the process equal?** Same information to all, no late changes, no negotiation with one bidder, no undisclosed criteria (Gazit).
4. **Was the choice reasoned?** If not the best price, are special reasons recorded and was the best-price bidder heard (reg. 21(ב))?
5. **Inspect the file.** Use reg. 21(ה) within the period. Read the minutes and the winning bid before you petition.
6. **Act fast.** Tender petitions are urgent: once the contract is signed and performed, courts rarely cancel it and may award damages instead (administrative claim).

### Administrative promises

| Question | Binding if | Not binding if |
|---|---|---|
| Who promised? | An official authorised to decide the matter | A clerk with no power over it |
| Was it within power? | The authority could lawfully do it | It would require acting beyond power or breaking a tender |
| Was it definite? | Clear, specific, meant to be relied on | A general statement of policy or intention |
| Can it be kept? | Performance is still possible | Circumstances make it impossible |
| Is there a justification to depart? | None | Public interest after a real change, weighed against your reliance |

Ask for promises in writing, from the official with power, and keep the email. If an authority departs from a promise, ask it to explain the change of circumstances and how it weighed your reliance.

## Worked example

Sarit Levi owns a two-person cleaning company in Ashdod. A government hospital (a body covered by s. 2) tenders for cleaning services. Threshold condition: "at least five years' experience cleaning hospitals of this hospital's group". Sarit has eight years' experience cleaning clinics and an old-age home, but none in that group. The tender is won by the incumbent.

1. **Tender required:** yes; no exemption.
2. **Condition:** a requirement of experience with this very body or its group must be reasoned in the documents (s. 2א(ב)); the documents give no reason. Experience in comparable facilities would serve the purpose (lesson 14.3: less harmful means). It looks tailored to the incumbent.
3. **Timing:** Sarit should have challenged the condition before the bids were due; she wrote to the tender committee then, asking to change it, and kept the answer (a refusal). This protects her against a delay argument.
4. **After the result:** within the reg. 21(ה) period she inspects the minutes and the winning bid. The minutes show the committee knew only the incumbent met the condition.
5. **Route:** administrative petition (First Schedule item 5) with a request for an interim order before the contract starts. Given the urgency and the incumbent's lawyer, she considers getting a lawyer for this step.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A current tender from the Government tenders site (mr.gov.il) or your municipality, or Sarit's case.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Coverage (5 min).** Who issues it? Under which law (s. 2 or municipal)?
2. **Conditions (15 min).** List every threshold condition. For each, is it required by the tender's nature? Does any need reasons under s. 2א(ב)?
3. **Criteria (10 min).** How will the winner be chosen? Price only, or a weighted score? Are the weights published?
4. **Inspection plan (5 min).** Write the request you would send under reg. 21(ה) if you lost: the documents and the date.
5. **Promise check (10 min).** Recall a promise an authority made you (a refund, a permit, a date). Run it through the promise table.

- Your inspection request lists the documents in reg. 21(ה).

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. An individual bidder (a sole trader) may write to the committee, inspect the file and petition in their own name (lesson 14.9). A company bidder generally needs a lawyer to appear in court (see [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md); the corporate rule is unverified in a free source). A citizen who is not a bidder may still use FOI to see tender documents (lesson 14.7).

### Where and how

- Tender documents: the Government procurement site (mr.gov.il) and municipal websites.
- Inspection: written request to the tender committee secretary within the reg. 21(ה) period ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Petition: Administrative Affairs Court, First Schedule item 5; damages: administrative claim.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The contract is about to be signed and you need an interim order.
- The winning bidder is joined as a respondent with its own lawyers.
- You are a company, or the sums are large.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Challenging a threshold condition only after losing | Delay argument; "you accepted the terms" | Explain when you learned of the defect | Write to the committee before bids are due |
| Petitioning without inspecting the file | Weak facts; surprises at the hearing | Inspect, then amend | Use reg. 21(ה) first |
| Waiting until the contract is performed | Court refuses to cancel; damages only | Ask for an interim order early | Treat tenders as urgent |
| Relying on a clerk's oral promise | Not binding | Ask the competent official to confirm in writing | Get promises in writing from the right person |
| Hiding a material fact from the authority | Loss of remedy (Conterm) | Disclose and explain | Disclose disputes affecting the condition |

## Review

- Covered public bodies must contract by public tender with equal opportunity (Mandatory Tenders Law s. 2); municipalities follow their own tender rules.
- Conditions must be relevant; the best bid wins unless special reasons are recorded; no negotiation with one bidder (Gazit; reg. 21; municipal reg. 18א).
- Losing bidders may inspect the committee's file within the set period (reg. 21(ה)).
- An authorised, definite promise within power binds the authority unless a change of circumstances justifies departing from it (Sai-Tex).
- Citizens dealing with authorities may also owe a duty of disclosure (Conterm).
