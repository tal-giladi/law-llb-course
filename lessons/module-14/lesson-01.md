# 14.1 · Authority and the principle of legality

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find the legal source of any power a public authority uses against you, test whether the decision falls inside it, and write the paragraph of a letter or petition that says "you had no power to do this". Lack of authority is the first and strongest ground of administrative review: if it succeeds, the court does not need to weigh anything.

## Why it matters

Module 6 taught the constitutional frame (lessons [06.2](../module-06/lesson-02.md) and [06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md)), and lesson [07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md) showed how to check whether a regulation or by-law is valid. This module, which follows the Israeli administrative-law course (TAU 1411-9224, HUJI 62819), turns to the individual decision: the licence refused, the fine imposed, the benefit cut, the information withheld. Every such decision is the use of a power, and every power must come from law.

The principle of legality (עקרון החוקיות המינהלית) has two sides. An individual may do anything the law does not forbid; an authority may do only what the law permits. Israeli courts have applied this since the first year of the State (Bejerano, 1949, recapped below), and it is the one ground of review that no side of the 2023–2026 dispute proposed to remove. In practice it is also the ground most often missed by self-represented people, who argue that a decision is unfair before checking whether the official had any power to make it.

## The law

### Who is an authority, and what is a decision

> ”רשות“ – רשות מרשויות המדינה, רשות מקומית, וכן גופים ואנשים אחרים הממלאים תפקידים ציבוריים על פי דין;
> ”החלטה של רשות“ – החלטה של רשות במילוי תפקיד ציבורי על פי דין, לרבות העדר החלטה וכן מעשה או מחדל.

*Gist:* An "authority" includes State bodies, local authorities and anyone else who performs a public function under law; a "decision" includes failure to decide, an act or an omission. ([Administrative Affairs Courts Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

### Rights may be limited only by or under statute

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש או לפי חוק כאמור מכוח הסמכה מפורשת בו.

> כל רשות מרשויות השלטון חייבת לכבד את הזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה.

*Gist:* An official who limits a protected right needs a statute that itself limits it, or an express authorisation in a statute; and every authority is bound to respect these rights. ([Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty ss. 8, 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו)) The proportionality part of s. 8 is lesson 14.3; here the point is the source.

### The Government's residual power

> הממשלה מוסמכת לעשות בשם המדינה, בכפוף לכל דין, כל פעולה שעשייתה אינה מוטלת בדין על רשות אחרת.

*Gist:* The Government may act for the State in anything not assigned by law to another authority, subject to every law. ([Basic Law: The Government s. 32](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה)) Courts read this narrowly: it supports contracts, grants and policy, but not limiting a right, which needs express authorisation (s. 8 above).

### Ancillary powers

> (ב) הסמכה לעשות דבר או לכפות עשייתו – משמעה גם מתן סמכויות עזר הדרושות לכך במידה המתקבלת על הדעת.

*Gist:* A power to do or enforce something carries the ancillary powers reasonably needed for it, and (s. 17(א)) the power to set working procedures. ([Interpretation Law s. 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות)) Authorities often lean on this section; it does not create a new power to burden people.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 1/49 בז׳רנו נ׳ שר המשטרה (1949)

Recap from lesson 07.4. The traffic department barred petition writers from acting as agents for vehicle owners, with no statute behind the ban. The Court held that public officials may not stop a person from pursuing a lawful occupation without express or implied legal authority. Why it matters here: it is the root of the rule that the authority, not you, must show the source of its power. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/86)

### עע״מ 3782/12 מפקד מחוז תל אביב־יפו במשטרת ישראל נ׳ איגוד האינטרנט הישראלי (24.3.2013)

The police ordered Internet access providers to block gambling websites, relying on the Penal Law power of a district commander to close an illegal gambling "place". The Israel Internet Association petitioned the Administrative Affairs Court as a public petitioner and won; the State appealed. The Supreme Court (Vogelman J., President Grunis concurring) assumed that a website could be a "place" under a purposive reading, but held that a power to close a place does not include a power to order private third parties to carry out the closure. Absent an explicit statutory source, an authority may not compel a private body to act for it, and there is an interpretive presumption against handing enforcement powers to those outside the enforcement system. The appeal was dismissed.

Why it matters: purposive interpretation can stretch the object of a power, but not the people it binds. When an authority tells a private company (your bank, your landlord, your Internet provider) to act against you, ask which statute empowers it to give that order. [English translation and abstract](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/381)

### בג״ץ 2109/20 בן מאיר נ׳ ראש הממשלה (26.4.2020)

In March 2020 the Government authorised the Israel Security Agency, by a Government decision under a general provision of the ISA Law, to track the phones of coronavirus patients. The Court (President Hayut, Deputy President Melcer and Sohlberg J.) held that the decision was a primary arrangement in substance, infringing privacy and extending a security service into civilian matters. Urgency at the outbreak made the general authorisation sufficient for a short time, but any further use required primary legislation by the Knesset, meeting the limitation clause; and, by majority, journalists could be tracked only with consent.

Why it matters: it shows the primary-arrangements doctrine (lesson [06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md)) at work on an executive decision, and how the required level of explicitness rises with the seriousness of the harm. Supporters of the ruling see it as protecting the Knesset's role; critics argue the Court set the timetable for legislation itself. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/89)

## How it works in practice

### The authority check, in order

1. **Who decided?** Name the official and the body (minister, director-general, municipal engineer, committee). A decision by the wrong official is a lack of authority even if the right official could have made it (delegation is lesson 14.5).
2. **Under which provision?** Ask the authority in writing to state the section it relied on. A public official must answer a written request and give reasons for a refusal (Decisions and Reasons Law, lesson 14.4), and the section is part of the reasons.
3. **Does the provision cover this subject and this person?** Read its words and purpose. Note the Internet Association distinction: object of the power versus who may be ordered to act.
4. **Does it limit a right?** If yes, the authorisation must be express (s. 8). A general "the minister may do anything necessary" will not do for a serious infringement.
5. **Is it a primary arrangement?** A broad policy that divides society or creates a new regime needs the Knesset (Rubinstein, lesson 06.6; Ben Meir above).
6. **Is it a dual-nature body?** A government company, a public hospital or a body with a statutory monopoly may be bound by some public-law duties even when it acts by contract (tenders, lesson 14.6).

### What the court does when authority is missing

Lack of authority is a fundamental defect, but Israeli law treats voidness as relative: the court decides the remedy by the circumstances (for example, whether people relied on the decision, and whether the challenge is direct or raised in a different proceeding; see Wael in lesson 07.4). Raise the point early, in the prior demand, not only in court.

## Worked example

Yosef Ben-Haim, a taxi owner in Bnei Brak, receives a letter from the municipality's parking department: his vehicle will be "flagged" and the municipality has "instructed" the private towing company it contracts with to tow it on sight until he pays an old arnona debt.

1. **Who decided?** The parking department director.
2. **Source?** Yosef writes asking for the provision relied on. The answer cites the municipality's general collection powers.
3. **Analysis.** Arnona collection has its own statutory tools (the subject of module 27). Towing a vehicle to force payment of an unrelated tax debt is a coercive act that limits property and occupation; under s. 8 it needs express authorisation. Like the police in the Internet Association case, the municipality is also ordering a private company to act against him; the general collection provision says nothing about that.
4. **Letter.** Yosef's prior demand (template below) has an "authority" paragraph: the decision lacks a statutory source, the cited provision does not authorise towing for tax debt or instructing a private contractor, and he asks that the instruction be withdrawn within seven days.
5. **Back-up.** If the municipality insists, the matter is a decision of a local authority on arnona (First Schedule items 1 and 8): an administrative petition (lesson 14.11).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One decision of a public authority that affected you or your family (or Yosef's case above).
- Wikisource or the Knesset database to read the empowering law.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Name the decision-maker (5 min).** Write the official's title and the body.
2. **Find the source (15 min).** Search the decision letter for a section number; if none, search the law that governs the subject. Copy the empowering words exactly.
3. **Test it (15 min).** Answer the six questions under "The authority check". For each "no", write one sentence why.
4. **Write the paragraph (10 min).** Draft three to five sentences in Hebrew: the decision, the provision the authority relied on (or that none was given), why it does not cover the act, and the request.

### Template

[Letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md): use its request-for-reasons paragraph to ask for the source of power, then add your authority paragraph.

### How you know it worked

- You quoted the empowering provision, not a summary of it.
- Your paragraph says which element is missing (subject, person, express authorisation, or Knesset decision).
- A reader can see the difference between "no power" and "power used badly".

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for the letter: writing to an authority about your own matter needs no lawyer. Yes for a petition in your own name: the Administrative Affairs Court's procedure regulations apply chapter 20 of the Civil Procedure Regulations, including reg. 170 (a party may act "בעצמו או בידי עורך דין"), and the petition form asks for a lawyer's details only "אם הוא מיוצג" (lesson 14.9). Writing such letters for others as an occupation is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Send the demand to the official and the authority's legal adviser (every ministry and municipality has one).
- Petition deadlines and fees: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md) (Legal Aid in some matters, university clinics, NGOs).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The act is imminent and irreversible (demolition, closure, seizure) and you need an interim order today.
- The authority answers with a long legal opinion on its power and you cannot tell which provision it relies on.
- The decision is part of a national scheme (a Government decision), where the petition will go to the High Court of Justice.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing only that the decision is unfair | The court weighs discretion and defers to the authority | Add the authority ground first | Run the six-question check before writing |
| Accepting "general powers" as an answer | A burden without an express source goes unchallenged | Ask for the exact section | Quote s. 8 and s. 17 in your letter |
| Confusing an individual's freedom with an authority's power | You look for a law that forbids the act instead of one that permits it | Reframe: "show me the power" | Remember Bejerano |
| Raising lack of authority only at the hearing | The authority may say you waived it; delay | Put it in the prior demand | Write the authority paragraph first |
| Ignoring the private contractor | The order to the third party stays in force | Name the contractor as a respondent | Ask who carries out the decision |

## Review

- An individual may do what is not forbidden; an authority may do only what the law permits (Bejerano).
- Limiting a right needs a statute or an express authorisation in one (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8); every authority must respect rights (s. 11).
- Residual (Basic Law: The Government s. 32) and ancillary (Interpretation Law s. 17) powers do not create a power to burden people.
- A power over an object does not include a power to order private third parties to act (AAA 3782/12); serious, novel arrangements need the Knesset (HCJ 2109/20).
- Lack of authority is the first ground to check, and the first paragraph of any demand.
