# 14.5 · The duty to decide, delegation and administrative evidence

By the end of this lesson you will be able to challenge three common defects that are neither "no power" nor "bad judgment": an authority that does not decide at all or binds itself not to decide; an official who decided without having been properly given the power; and a decision built on facts nobody checked. Each has its own test, and each is easy to miss.

## Why it matters

A power is also a duty. An authority given discretion must use it: it may not ignore your request, decide by a rigid rule that leaves no room for your case, or promise someone else in advance never to change its mind. Equally, the power belongs to the official the statute names; when someone else uses it, the question is whether the law allowed the transfer. And every decision must rest on a factual basis that a reasonable authority would rely on (administrative evidence, ראיה מינהלית), even though the authority is not a court and is not bound by the rules of evidence.

These doctrines are where many ordinary disputes are won: the municipality that "has a policy" of never granting exceptions, the clerk who signs a decision only the director may make, the benefit cut because of an unverified anonymous report.

## The law

### The duty to decide

The Decisions and Reasons Law requires an official asked in writing to use a power to "decide on the request" (יחליט בבקשה) within the set period, and treats three months' silence as a refusal (ss. 2, 6(ב); lesson 14.4). The Administrative Affairs Courts Law defines a reviewable "decision" to include "העדר החלטה וכן מעשה או מחדל" (s. 2; lesson 14.1). Failure to decide is itself reviewable.

### Delegation

> (ב) סמכות הנתונה לאחד השרים על פי חוק או שהועברה לו על פי סעיף 31(ב), למעט סמכות להתקין תקנות, רשאי השר לאצול, כולה, מקצתה או בסייגים, לעובד ציבור.

*Gist:* A minister may delegate a statutory power, wholly, partly or with conditions, to a public servant, except the power to make regulations; Government powers may be delegated to a minister (s. 33(א)) and onward only if the Government allows (s. 33(ג)). ([Basic Law: The Government s. 33](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_הממשלה))

> שר הממונה על ביצועו של חוק רשאי ליטול לעצמו כל סמכות, למעט סמכות בעלת אופי שיפוטי, הנתונה על פי אותו חוק לעובד המדינה, אם אין כוונה אחרת משתמעת מן החוק [...]

*Gist:* The minister responsible for a law may take over a power that the law gives to a State employee, except a judicial-type power and unless the law implies otherwise. (s. 34)

### Powers that come with a power

> הסמכה לעשות מינוי – משמעה גם הסמכה להתלות את תקפו, לבטלו, לפטר את מי שנתמנה או להשעותו מתפקידו.

> הסמכה להתקין תקנות או ליתן הוראת מינהל – משמעה גם הסמכה לתקנן, לשנותן, להתלותן או לבטלן בדרך שהותקנו התקנות או ניתנה ההוראה.

*Gist:* The power to appoint includes the power to dismiss or suspend; the power to make a rule includes the power to amend or revoke it in the same way. ([Interpretation Law ss. 14, 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות)) The "same way" matters: a rule made by a committee cannot be changed by a clerk.

### Administrative evidence

No statute defines it. The rule is judge-made: an authority may rely on any material that a reasonable person would regard as having probative value (reports, complaints, documents, even hearsay), provided it checks the material and gives the person a chance to respond (lesson 14.4). The weightier the harm, the stronger the evidence required. In court, the Administrative Affairs Court decides petitions on affidavits and may allow cross-examination (procedure regs, reg. 15; lesson 14.9).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 4921/13 אומ״ץ נ׳ רוכברגר (14.10.2013)

Two mayors were indicted, one for bribery, one for fraud and breach of trust. One city council voted not to remove its mayor; the other took the question off its agenda. By a 6–1 majority the Court held that the councils had a duty under the Local Authorities (Election of Mayor) Law to convene and decide on removal; that the failure to convene was itself a decision to refuse; that the indictments were weighty administrative evidence of conduct unbecoming the office; and that, with elections approaching and both mayors running again, refusing to remove them was extremely unreasonable. It ordered their removal. President Grunis dissented: with elections less than two months away, the Court should leave the matter to the voters.

Why it matters: it joins the three themes of this lesson: an omission is a decision, an authority must use its power, and an indictment (not a conviction) can be the factual basis. The dissent states the strongest argument for restraint when voters are about to decide. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/293)

### בג״ץ 4374/15 התנועה למען איכות השלטון בישראל נ׳ ראש הממשלה (27.3.2016)

The Government's natural-gas outline included a "stability clause": for ten years the Government would not change taxation, competition rules or export caps, and would oppose legislation that did. A five-justice panel held, by majority (Sohlberg J. dissenting), that the clause breached the basic rule against fettering discretion: an authority given a power has a duty to exercise it and may not decide not to decide, still less bind future governments and, through coalition discipline, the Knesset. The majority suspended the ruling for a year to allow a fix; Hayut J. would have struck only the clause.

Why it matters: an authority's commitment to a private party (a contract, an outline, a promise) cannot remove its future discretion unless a statute allows it. You will meet this again with administrative promises in lesson 14.6. [English summary](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/272)

## How it works in practice

### Failure to decide

- Keep proof of your written request and its receipt.
- After the Decisions and Reasons period, send one reminder; after three months, treat it as a refusal (s. 6(ב)).
- In a petition, the remedy is an order to decide within a set time, not an order to decide in your favour.

### Rigid policies

Authorities may adopt policies and guidelines to ensure equality, and they must publish guidelines that matter to the public (FOI Law s. 6, lesson 14.7). But a policy must leave room for exceptions. Ask: "Did you consider whether my case justifies an exception to the policy?" A refusal that only quotes the policy invites the fettering argument.

### Who signed?

1. Find the statute that names the power-holder (minister, director, committee).
2. If someone else signed, ask for the delegation instrument. Delegations of ministers' powers are often published in Reshumot (Yalkut HaPirsumim); search it.
3. Check that the delegation covers the power, the area and the date, and that the power was delegable (regulations are not, s. 33(ב)).

### Factual basis

Ask for the documents the decision relied on (lesson 14.4; FOI, lesson 14.7). Check: was each fact verified? Were you allowed to answer it? Is the evidence proportionate to the harm?

## Worked example

Fatma Abu Ghanem, a resident of Rahat, receives a letter from a regional office of a ministry: her small-business grant is cancelled and must be repaid because "information was received that the business is not active". The letter is signed by a regional coordinator.

1. **Who may decide?** The grant rules (published as a ministry procedure) give the power to cancel to the head of the grants unit. Fatma asks for the delegation to the regional coordinator.
2. **Evidence.** She asks what "information" was received. It is an anonymous phone call. She has invoices, a lease and photographs showing the business is active.
3. **Hearing.** No one asked her before cancelling (lesson 14.4).
4. **Letter.** She writes: the decision was signed by an official without power (or show the delegation); it rests on an unverified anonymous report that is not adequate administrative evidence for a decision requiring repayment; she was not heard; she asks that the decision be cancelled and, if the ministry wishes to reconsider, that she be heard first.
5. **Outcome.** The unit head withdraws the decision; the ministry may start again properly, but now with her evidence on file.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A decision against you, or Fatma's case.
- The statute or procedure that names who decides.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Power-holder (10 min).** Write who the law says decides, and who actually signed.
2. **Delegation (10 min).** If they differ, search Reshumot or ask the authority for the delegation; check scope and date.
3. **Discretion used? (10 min).** Did the decision consider your case, or only quote a rule? Did the authority promise someone else something that blocks your request?
4. **Evidence (10 min).** List each fact relied on, its source, whether it was checked and whether you could respond.
5. **Paragraph (5 min).** Draft one sentence for each defect found.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Requests for the delegation instrument, for the factual basis, and to decide within the statutory period are ordinary letters. A petition for an order to decide is one of the simplest petitions to bring yourself (lesson 14.11).

### Where and how

- Reshumot (Yalkut HaPirsumim) is searchable online for delegations of ministers' powers (⚠️ manual check: gov.il blocks automated access).
- Periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The authority demands repayment of large sums or threatens criminal proceedings over the same facts.
- The decision rests on intelligence or confidential material.
- The fettering question involves a contract with third parties whose rights may be affected.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asking the court to grant what the authority never decided | The court orders a decision, not a result | Ask for an order to decide within a set time | Frame the remedy correctly |
| Assuming any official may sign | You miss a lack-of-authority ground | Ask for the delegation | Compare the statute and the signature |
| Attacking a policy as such | Courts uphold policies that promote equality | Attack the refusal to consider an exception | Ask whether your case was considered |
| Ignoring the evidence behind a decision | Unverified facts stand | Ask for the material and answer it | List every fact and its source |
| Waiting years after silence | Delay defeats the petition | Treat three months' silence as a refusal | Diary s. 6(ב) |

## Review

- A power is also a duty: failure to decide is reviewable, and an authority may not fetter its discretion (Gas Outline).
- Omission can be a decision, and an indictment can be weighty administrative evidence (Ometz).
- Delegation must have a source: ministers may delegate to public servants, not the power to make regulations (Basic Law: The Government s. 33).
- The power to appoint includes dismissal; the power to make a rule includes amending it in the same way (Interpretation Law ss. 14–15).
- Administrative evidence must have real probative value, proportionate to the harm, and you must be able to answer it.
