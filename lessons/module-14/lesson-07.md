# 14.7 · Freedom of information

By the end of this lesson you will be able to get information from almost any public body in Israel, read a refusal and tell whether it is lawful, and answer it: by narrowing the request, asking for partial disclosure, appealing a fee, or petitioning. Freedom of information is the tool that makes every other lesson in this module work, because you cannot challenge a decision whose basis you cannot see.

## Why it matters

Lesson [02.7](../module-02/lesson-07.md) showed how to add an FOI request to a formal letter. This lesson teaches the Freedom of Information Law 1998 as a subject: who must disclose, what may be withheld, how courts balance the exemptions, and what it costs. Since 1998 the starting point has been reversed: public information belongs to the public, and the authority must justify withholding it. In practice, FOI is how journalists, NGOs and individuals have obtained minutes, contracts, data on enforcement, and the criteria behind decisions about them.

For a self-represented person FOI has two further advantages: you need not give a reason (s. 7(א)), and information about yourself is largely free (fees regs reg. 6(א)).

## The law

### The right and who must disclose

> לכל אזרח ישראלי או תושב הזכות לקבל מידע מרשות ציבורית בהתאם להוראות חוק זה.

*Gist:* Every citizen or resident (including an Israeli company) may obtain information from a public authority (s. 1); a non-resident may obtain information about their own rights in Israel (s. 12). "Information" is anything held in writing, recording, film, photo or computer form (s. 2). Public authorities (s. 2) include ministries and their units, the President's office, the Knesset, the State Comptroller (not audit and complaint material), courts and tribunals (not the content of proceedings), local authorities, municipal corporations, statutory corporations, government companies (unless exempted), sports associations, State-funded universities (as to their finances) and bodies designated by order (such as health funds and religious councils). ([FOI Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע))

Section 14 excludes whole bodies (intelligence services, the Shin Bet, the Mossad, investigation material of any investigating body, medical quasi-judicial bodies as to their proceedings, and others) and information transferred to the State Archives.

### Procedure and periods

> (א) בקשה לקבלת מידע תוגש בכתב לממונה או למי שהוסמך לכך על ידו; אין המבקש חייב לציין את הטעם לבקשתו.

*Gist:* Apply in writing to the FOI officer; no reason needed. The authority must decide without delay and within the set period, extendable once with a written reasoned notice and again, for volume or complexity, by the head of the authority with reasons; delivery after a decision to disclose has its own limit (s. 7(ב)–(ד); all periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). Information is given as held; the authority need not process it for you (s. 7(ה)). A refusal, in whole or in part, must give reasons and tell you of your right to petition (s. 7(ו)).

### When an authority may refuse (s. 8)

Unreasonable allocation of resources; information more than seven years old that is very hard to locate; information that cannot be found or is not held; information already published (the authority must say where); information created by another authority (it must refer you there).

### Exemptions (s. 9)

> (א) רשות ציבורית לא תמסור מידע שהוא אחד מאלה: (1) מידע אשר בגילויו יש חשש לפגיעה בבטחון המדינה, ביחסי החוץ שלה, בבטחון הציבור או בבטחונו או בשלומו של אדם; [...] (3) מידע שגילויו מהווה פגיעה בפרטיות [...] (4) מידע אשר אין לגלותו על פי כל דין.

> (ב) רשות ציבורית אינה חייבת למסור מידע שהוא אחד מאלה: [...] (4) מידע בדבר דיונים פנימיים, תרשומות של התייעצויות פנימיות [...] וכן חוות דעת, טיוטה, עצה או המלצה, שניתנו לצורך קבלת החלטה [...] (6) מידע שהוא סוד מסחרי או סוד מקצועי [...]

*Gist:* Section 9(א) lists information that must not be disclosed (security, foreign relations, public safety, privacy, any law forbidding disclosure); s. 9(ב) lists information the authority need not disclose (disruption of functioning, policy in formation, negotiations, internal deliberations and opinions, internal management, trade secrets, information given in confidence, enforcement methods, discipline of employees, privacy of the deceased, academic freedom). Environmental emissions and noise, odour and radiation measurements cannot be withheld as trade secrets.

### Considerations, partial disclosure, third parties

When considering a refusal, the authority must weigh your interest (if you stated it) and the public interest in health, safety and the environment (s. 10). If exempt parts can be removed without unreasonable effort, it must disclose the rest, noting the omissions (s. 11). If information concerns a third party who may be harmed, the authority notifies them; they have a set period to object, which does not count against your periods, and disclosure waits until their time to petition has passed (s. 13).

### Court and the public-interest override

> (ד) על אף הוראות סעיף 9, רשאי בית המשפט להורות על מתן מידע מבוקש, כולו או חלקו ובתנאים שיקבע, אם לדעתו הענין הציבורי בגילוי המידע, עדיף וגובר על הטעם לדחיית הבקשה, ובלבד שגילוי המידע אינו אסור על פי דין.

*Gist:* Petitions against FOI decisions go to the Administrative Affairs Court (s. 17(א); First Schedule item 2), which may examine the information itself and may order disclosure despite s. 9 where the public interest prevails, unless another law forbids disclosure. If a privilege certificate under the Evidence Ordinance s. 44 was issued, the petition goes to the Supreme Court.

### Fees

The regulations set an application fee, a handling fee per hour from the fourth hour, production fees, and a cap above which the officer may demand a deposit (regs 1–4). Exemptions (reg. 6): information about yourself and your rights (no application fee, and four handling hours free); registered non-profits with a proper-management certificate and academic researchers; recipients of listed benefits such as income support. No production fee below a small amount (reg. 6(ד)). An authority that missed the statutory periods may not charge handling or production fees (reg. 6א). A fee decision can be appealed to the magistrates' court within a set period (reg. 8; Law s. 18(ה)). All amounts (updated each October) and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). ([Fees Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_חופש_המידע_(אגרות)))

## Leading cases

### עע״מ 9135/03 המועצה להשכלה גבוהה נ׳ הוצאת עיתון הארץ (19.1.2006)

Haaretz, and separately an organisation refused a licence for a college, asked for minutes of the Council for Higher Education's meetings. The Council relied on the internal-deliberations exemption, s. 9(ב)(4). The Supreme Court held that s. 9(ב) does not prohibit disclosure; it frees the authority from a duty, so the authority must still exercise discretion, weigh the "chilling effect" on frank discussion against the public and private interest in disclosure, and do so case by case. A blanket refusal was unreasonable, and the lower court's disclosure orders stood.

Why it matters: when an authority says "internal deliberations", ask it to show how it weighed your interest and the public's. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/116)

### עע״מ 2975/15 הוצאת עיתון הארץ בע״מ נ׳ משרד החוץ (6.6.2016)

Haaretz asked for the guest list of a Passover Seder at the Israeli ambassador's residence in Washington. The Ministry claimed the foreign-relations exemption (s. 9(א)(1)), citing a general chilling effect on diplomatic meetings. By majority (Barak-Erez and Baron JJ., President Naor dissenting), the Court held that the exemption requires a real, concrete concern of harm, weighed by its severity and likelihood; a general argument that would create "an enclave entirely concealed from public view" is not enough, especially where guests had publicised their attendance.

Why it matters: even mandatory exemptions in s. 9(א) need evidence of a real risk in the specific case. The dissent shows the weight courts still give to the executive's assessment in foreign affairs. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/176)

### עע״מ 3908/11 מדינת ישראל – הנהלת בתי המשפט נ׳ דה מרקר (22.9.2014)

Recap from lesson [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md): an expanded bench held that the public interest in data on pending cases by judge outweighed the claimed harm to the courts' functioning (s. 9(ב)(1)). It shows that the courts' own administration is a public authority. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/371)

## How it works in practice

### Writing a request that succeeds

- **Be specific:** documents, dates, units ("the minutes of the permits committee meetings of 1 January to 30 June 2026 concerning..."). Vague requests invite s. 8(1) refusals.
- **Ask for the format:** electronic copies, which also reduce production fees (reg. 3א).
- **State your interest if it helps:** not required, but s. 10 makes it a consideration.
- **Say if it is about you:** fee exemption, reg. 6(א); if what you receive about yourself is wrong, you may ask for correction (s. 16, applying the Privacy Protection Law).
- **Ask in advance for partial disclosure:** "If any part is exempt, please disclose the rest under s. 11."
- **Keep proof of submission and payment:** the periods run from the later of receipt and payment of the application fee (reg. 9(א)).

Most ministries and many authorities accept requests through an online form on the Government FOI unit's site, with payment online (⚠️ manual check: gov.il blocks automated access).

### Reading the answer

| Answer | What to do |
|---|---|
| No answer within the period | Send a reminder; note that late authorities may not charge handling fees (reg. 6א); consider a petition |
| Extension | Check it is in writing, reasoned, and within the limits |
| "Unreasonable resources" (s. 8(1)) | Narrow the request (shorter period, fewer units) and resubmit |
| "Not held" (s. 8(3)) | Ask which searches were made; ask who holds it (s. 8(5)) |
| s. 9(ב) exemption | Ask how your interest and the public interest were weighed (CHE v. Haaretz); ask for partial disclosure |
| s. 9(א) exemption | Ask for the concrete risk (Haaretz v. MFA); ask for redacted disclosure |
| Fee estimate above the cap | Narrow the request, or appeal the fee to the magistrates' court within the period (reg. 8) |

## Worked example

Daniel Cohen, a parent in Modi'in, wants to know why the municipality closed his children's school bus route. He sends an FOI request to the municipality's FOI officer for: the decision and its date; the transport department's professional opinion; the data on the number of pupils on the route in 2025–2026; and the minutes of the meeting where the decision was made.

1. **Fee.** Not information about himself; he pays the application fee online and keeps the receipt.
2. **Answer at day 28.** The municipality sends the decision and the pupil data, but refuses the professional opinion and minutes as internal deliberations (s. 9(ב)(4)).
3. **Reply.** He writes back: s. 9(ב) is discretionary; the authority must weigh the parents' interest and the public interest in road safety for children (s. 10); a blanket refusal is unreasonable (CHE v. Haaretz); at minimum, disclose the factual parts of the opinion and the minutes, with deliberations redacted (s. 11).
4. **Result.** The municipality releases the opinion with names redacted. It shows the route was closed because of a contractor dispute, not because of low numbers. Daniel uses this in his objection (lesson 14.10).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A question about a decision affecting you or your community, or Daniel's case.
- The FOI officer's contact details (on the authority's website or the Government FOI unit's list).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Coverage (5 min).** Is the body a public authority under s. 2? Is it excluded by s. 14?
2. **Draft the request (20 min).** Use the [FOI request template](../../templates/foi-request.md): specific items, dates, format, partial-disclosure request, fee status.
3. **Fee (5 min).** Work out the application fee and whether an exemption applies, from [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
4. **Plan the clock (5 min).** Write the decision date, the latest extension dates and the delivery limit.
5. **Pre-write your reply (10 min).** For the two exemptions most likely to be raised, write one paragraph each in answer.

### Template

[FOI request (בקשה לקבלת מידע)](../../templates/foi-request.md).

### How you know it worked

- Your request lists concrete documents, not "all information".
- You know which periods and fees apply and from when.
- You have a prepared answer to the likely exemption.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. FOI requests are designed for the public and are a recommended real-world action in this course: file one in your own name about a matter that concerns you. A fee appeal in the magistrates' court and an FOI petition in the Administrative Affairs Court may also be brought in your own name (lesson 14.9).

### Where and how

- Request: to the authority's FOI officer, by its online form or email.
- Fees, periods, appeal period: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Petition: Administrative Affairs Court, First Schedule item 2, within the petition period from the refusal (lesson 14.9).
- Free help: some NGOs specialise in FOI; see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A third party objects and joins the petition with its own lawyers.
- The authority claims security or a privilege certificate (petition goes to the Supreme Court).
- The information is needed urgently for another proceeding with its own deadline.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Send me all information about X" | s. 8(1) refusal or a huge fee estimate | Narrow and resubmit | List documents, dates and units |
| Asking the authority to create a summary | Refused: no duty to process (s. 7(ה)) | Ask for the existing documents or data | Ask for what exists |
| Accepting a blanket s. 9(ב) refusal | Information lost | Ask for the weighing and partial disclosure | Pre-write your reply |
| Missing the petition period after a refusal | Petition late | Ask to extend with reasons | Diary the period from the refusal date |
| Paying handling fees after a late answer | Unnecessary cost | Cite reg. 6א | Keep proof of the request date |

## Review

- Every citizen and resident may obtain information from a public authority without giving reasons; periods and fees are fixed by the Law and the regulations.
- Section 8 allows refusals for practical reasons; s. 9(א) forbids disclosure, s. 9(ב) permits refusal only after weighing.
- Authorities must disclose the non-exempt part (s. 11) and protect third parties (s. 13).
- Courts require concrete reasons for exemptions (CHE v. Haaretz; Haaretz v. MFA) and may override s. 9 for the public interest (s. 17(ד)).
- Information about yourself is largely fee-free; late authorities may not charge handling fees; fee decisions can be appealed to the magistrates' court.
