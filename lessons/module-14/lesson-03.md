# 14.3 · Proportionality in administrative decisions

By the end of this lesson you will be able to test a single decision of an official (a closure order, a permit condition, a restriction on movement or business) for proportionality, separate the parts where a court will defer to the authority from the parts where it will weigh the harm itself, and propose a realistic less harmful alternative. Lesson [06.3](../module-06/lesson-03.md) taught the tests for statutes and lab [06.7](../module-06/lesson-07.md) applied them to a law; this lesson applies them to the everyday decisions of the administration.

## Why it matters

Proportionality began in Israeli law as an administrative ground and only later became the core of constitutional review. Today it is used for any decision that limits a right: a municipality closing a business, a ministry setting a licence condition, the police restricting a demonstration, a military commander seizing land. For a self-represented person it is often the most persuasive ground, because it does not say the authority had a bad motive or no power. It says: you can reach your goal with less harm to me.

Proportionality also meets reasonableness. Some justices see proportionality as the more structured tool that could replace "balancing" reasonableness (lesson 14.2); others see the two as overlapping. For a petitioner the practical point is to argue it whenever a right is limited.

## The law

### The source of the duty

> כל רשות מרשויות השלטון חייבת לכבד את הזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה.

*Gist:* Every authority must respect the rights in the Basic Law. ([Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו)) Read with the limitation clause in s. 8 ("במידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש"), this means that an administrative decision that limits a right must be proportionate, in addition to having a statutory source (lesson 14.1).

### The tests, as the Court states them for administrative decisions

There is no statute spelling out the subtests. In Beit Sourik (below), the Court summarised them for an administrative authority: the means must lead rationally to the purpose; they must harm the individual to the least extent; and the harm must stand in proper proportion to the benefit. These are the same three subtests as for statutes (lesson 06.3), applied to the decision in front of you rather than to a law. Use the [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 2056/04 מועצת הכפר בית סוריק נ׳ ממשלת ישראל (2004)

Palestinian villages challenged orders seizing land for about 40 km of the separation barrier north-west of Jerusalem. The Court held that the military commander may build a barrier for security reasons but not for political ones, and found the route security-based. On the first two subtests it gave special weight to the commander's professional military view when experts disagreed. But it held that the balance between security benefit and harm to residents (35,000 people, separated from much of their farmland) is a legal question on which the Court is expert, and that for most of the route the harm was disproportionate in the narrow sense; the orders for those sections were annulled for a new route to be planned.

Why it matters: it is the leading statement that courts defer on professional means but decide the balance themselves. Supporters of the ruling say it applied the law of occupation and Israeli administrative law even in wartime; critics say the Court second-guessed security decisions and delayed a barrier built to stop attacks on civilians. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/85)

### בג״ץ 953/01 סולודקין נ׳ עיריית בית שמש (14.6.2004)

A Knesset law empowers local authorities to restrict the sale of pork by by-law. Sellers and consumers challenged by-laws in several towns. The Court held that the power must be used by balancing the religious and national sensibilities of those who object against the freedom of occupation and lifestyle of those who sell or eat pork, in light of the local character of each neighbourhood: a total ban in a mixed city may be disproportionate, while a ban in a neighbourhood that is almost wholly religious may not. It sent the matter back to the councils to decide again by these criteria.

Why it matters: proportionality is applied area by area and person by person; the remedy is usually reconsideration, not a court-made rule. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/357)

### בג״ץ 2435/20 לוינטל נ׳ ראש הממשלה (7.4.2020)

Bnei Brak residents challenged the declaration of the city as a "restricted zone" in April 2020. The Court (Amit, Baron, Elron JJ.) found authority under the emergency regulations, a proper purpose and a rational connection, and held that less harmful means had already been tried nationally without success; on the narrow-sense balance it relied on the health professionals. The petition was dismissed, with the Court stressing that the declaration was temporary and must be re-examined.

Why it matters: in professional, urgent matters the margin of deference is wide, but the authority still has to show that milder steps were tried or considered and that the measure is limited in time. Compare Beit Sourik, where the Court did its own balancing. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/244)

## How it works in practice

### Which part will the court decide?

| Question | Who has the expertise | Your strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Is the purpose proper? | Court | Show the stated purpose is not the real one, or not one the law allows |
| Does the means advance it? | Mostly the authority | Point to evidence that it does not (data, experts, the authority's own reports) |
| Is there a milder means? | Shared | Name a specific, workable alternative the authority can adopt |
| Is the harm proportionate to the benefit? | Court | Give concrete facts about your harm; ask for mitigation (time limits, exceptions, compensation) |

### Writing the alternative

The weakest proportionality argument is "there must be a better way". The strongest is a concrete measure: a daytime-only closure instead of a full closure, a warning before a fine, an individual exception procedure, a shorter period with review, an access gate opened at fixed hours. If the authority already uses that alternative elsewhere, say so: it proves feasibility.

### Remedies

Courts rarely substitute their own measure. Expect an order to reconsider, to add an exception or a mitigating step, or to limit the period.

## Worked example

Nadia Haddad runs a small falafel stand in Haifa's lower city. After neighbours complain about smell, the municipality orders the stand closed for 60 days under its business-licensing powers.

1. **Authority.** Business licensing gives power to impose conditions and, in some cases, close a business (lesson 14.1). Assume it exists.
2. **Purpose.** Preventing nuisance to neighbours: proper.
3. **Rational connection.** Closure stops the smell: yes.
4. **Least harmful means.** Nadia offers to install an extractor hood with a filter within 14 days, as other stands on the street did. That achieves the same purpose with much less harm. The municipality required hoods in two similar cases (she obtains the decisions by FOI request).
5. **Narrow sense.** Sixty days without income may close her business permanently; the benefit over a 14-day fix is small.
6. **Ask.** Suspend the closure on condition the hood is installed within 14 days, with an inspection.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A decision that limits something you do (business, movement, property, expression), or Nadia's case.
- The [proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Purpose (5 min).** Write the authority's stated purpose and, if you suspect another, the real one.
2. **Three subtests (20 min).** Fill in the worksheet's part C for the decision: authority's case, your case, conclusion.
3. **Alternative (15 min).** Write two concrete less harmful measures, with evidence each would work (the authority's practice elsewhere, other cities, the authority's own data).
4. **Paragraph (10 min).** Draft a short proportionality paragraph for your demand, ending in a specific request.

### Template

[Proportionality worksheet](../../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md); [letter to a public authority](../../templates/letter-to-authority.md) for the demand.

### How you know it worked

- Each subtest has the authority's best case written before yours.
- Your alternative is specific enough for an official to adopt tomorrow.
- Your request matches what a court would order (reconsideration, condition, limit).

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. A proportionality argument in a letter or objection is the most effective thing a self-represented person can write, because it gives the official a way to change the decision without admitting fault. A petition in your own name is possible (lesson 14.9).

### Where and how

- First, the statutory objection or appeal if the law gives one (business licensing, planning, arnona: lesson 14.10); otherwise a prior demand.
- Then, for local and listed matters, the Administrative Affairs Court; deadlines and fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need an urgent interim order to stop a closure, seizure or demolition.
- The authority relies on expert evidence (health, security, engineering) that you need an expert to answer.
- The decision is a national emergency measure, where petitions go to the HCJ and move very fast.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arguing the authority's experts are wrong without evidence | The court defers on professional means | Bring your own data or narrow the claim to the balance | Use the "who decides" table |
| Proposing an alternative that does not achieve the purpose | Fails the least-harmful-means test | Offer a measure that achieves the same goal | Ask: would this stop the nuisance as well? |
| Skipping the narrow-sense test | You lose your strongest ground in Beit Sourik-type cases | Add concrete facts about harm | Quantify income, time, people affected |
| Asking the court to set the measure | Refused | Ask for reconsideration with your alternative | Phrase the remedy as the court does |
| Ignoring the time element | Long or open-ended measures pass unnoticed | Ask for a time limit and review | Always check duration |

## Review

- Every authority must respect rights (Basic Law s. 11), so an administrative decision limiting a right must be proportionate.
- The subtests are rational connection, least harmful means and proportionality in the narrow sense.
- Courts defer on professional means but decide the balance between benefit and harm (Beit Sourik).
- Proportionality is applied to local and individual circumstances, and the usual remedy is reconsideration (Solodkin).
- In urgent professional matters deference is wide, but milder means and time limits still matter (Loewenthal).
