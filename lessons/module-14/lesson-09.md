# 14.9 · Administrative adjudication: the Administrative Affairs Courts Law, threshold doctrines and remedies

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a dispute with an authority to court correctly: choose the forum, survive the threshold objections the authority will raise, follow each procedural step of the Administrative Affairs Court, and ask for a remedy the court can grant. Lesson [08.5](../module-08/lesson-05.md) gave the procedural map and the time limits; lesson [06.8](../module-06/lesson-08.md) did the same for the HCJ. This lesson goes to the doctrines and the step-by-step procedure at university depth.

## Why it matters

Most administrative petitions are lost at the threshold, not on the merits: filed late, against the wrong body, without a prior demand, while a statutory appeal was available, or without joining the person who would be harmed. The Administrative Affairs Courts Law 2000 was designed, in its own words, to move administrative matters "gradually" from the HCJ to district-court judges "according to special rules of procedure" (s. 1), and those rules are strict. The court also has a powerful early filter: it may strike out or dismiss a petition on its face, before any answer (reg. 7(א)(2)), and may then charge costs to the State Treasury (reg. 41(ב)).

## The law

### Structure

- **Court and judges.** A district court sits as Administrative Affairs Court, with judges designated for four-year terms (s. 3); usually one judge, three if so decided (s. 4); the district registrar also acts there, with appeal from the registrar to a judge (s. 10; reg. 31 period).
- **Jurisdiction (s. 5).** Petitions against decisions of authorities on matters in the First Schedule (except where the main remedy concerns regulations); appeals in the Second Schedule; claims in the Third Schedule; and matters assigned by other laws. The Jerusalem court also hears listed matters from Judea and Samaria (s. 5א; Fourth and Fifth Schedules).
- **Grounds and remedies (s. 8).** Those of the HCJ, with necessary changes.
- **Interim orders (s. 9)** at any stage.
- **Transfer to the HCJ (s. 6).** If a petition raises a matter of special importance, sensitivity or urgency, the court may transfer it, at a party's or the Attorney General's request or on its own initiative. A petitioner asking for transfer must do so with the petition (reg. 21(א)).
- **Appeals (ss. 11–12).** From a judgment in a petition or claim: as of right; from a judgment in an administrative appeal and listed interim decisions: by leave. Periods: reg. 33 ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

### The schedules, in practice

The First Schedule lists the subjects: for example arnona (item 1), FOI (item 2), education decisions below ministerial level (item 3), religious councils (item 4), tenders (item 5), non-profits (item 6), business licensing (item 7), local authorities (item 8), planning and building (item 10), population, entry and foreign workers (item 12), public housing (item 13), transport licensing (item 14). Each item has exceptions, often for decisions of the Government or a minister, which stay in the HCJ. The Second Schedule lists statutory appeals (for example, arnona appeals-committee decisions and planning appeals); the Third, damages claims arising from tenders and class actions against authorities. ([Schedules](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים))

### Self-representation: settled by the regulations

> הוראות פרק כ׳ לתקנות סדר הדין האזרחי, למעט תקנות 176(ב), 177(5) ו־(6), 178 ו־179, יחולו בבית המשפט, בשינויים המחויבים, לפי העניין.

*Gist:* Chapter 20 of the Civil Procedure Regulations 2018 applies in the Administrative Affairs Court (reg. 44א). Chapter 20 opens with reg. 170:

> כל פעולה בבית משפט הנדרשת מבעל דין או המותרת לו לפי דין, רשאי הוא לעשותה בעצמו או בידי עורך דין שמינה לכך כדין, והוא כשאין הוראה מפורשת אחרת בכל דין.

*Gist:* A party may take any step "himself or through a lawyer". The petition form confirms it: the petitioner's details, and "אם הוא מיוצג – שם עורך דינו" (reg. 5(ב)(1)). So an individual may petition, appeal and appear in their own name. ([procedure regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין)); [Civil Procedure Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_סדר_הדין_האזרחי)) A corporation is a different question (see "Without a lawyer").

### The procedure, step by step

1. **Venue (reg. 2):** the district where the decision was made; for land, where the land is; no venue: Jerusalem.
2. **Period (regs 3–4):** without delay and within the default period from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge; extension possible; dismissal for delay even within the period.
3. **Petition (reg. 5):** Form 1 with the items in reg. 5(ב), including the schedule item and the enabling provision, the prior demand or the reason for its absence, the fee item; verified by an affidavit in Form 2 that separates personal knowledge from belief; copies of the decision and documents attached.
4. **Respondents (reg. 6):** the authority, any other authority concerned, and anyone who may be harmed if the petition succeeds. Missing a necessary respondent can lead to striking out.
5. **Service (regs 7(ב), 35):** the petitioner serves the petition on the respondents within a few days of filing and files proof of service; until then, the petition is not put before the judge.
6. **First look (reg. 7):** the court may order a preliminary response, strike out or dismiss on the face of the petition, order amendments, set a preliminary hearing, or order an answer.
7. **Interim and temporary orders (regs 9–9א):** a separate application verified by affidavit; an interim order only after the respondent could respond; a temporary order ex parte if delay may cause irreparable or serious harm, with a quick hearing; both may be conditioned on security. Serve on the day of filing (reg. 35(ב)).
8. **Answer (reg. 10):** within the set period if ordered; a respondent that does not answer may argue only with leave.
9. **Further particulars (reg. 11):** within short periods after the answer, each side may demand information or documents; FOI exemptions and privileges apply (reg. 13).
10. **Skeleton arguments (reg. 14):** shortly before the hearing, with copies of unpublished rulings relied on.
11. **Evidence (reg. 15):** by affidavits; cross-examination only with leave, requested in advance.
12. **Hearing and judgment (regs 16–17):** the petitioner argues first; the court may dismiss, grant in whole or part, or return the matter to the authority with directions. Non-appearance (reg. 18) can end the petition, with a short period to apply to set aside.
13. **Costs (reg. 41):** to the other side, and in a petition dismissed on its face, also to the State Treasury.

The Civil Procedure Regulations otherwise do not apply to petitions, except where the court finds a gap and applies them with reasons (reg. 20). Administrative claims, by contrast, follow civil procedure (reg. 29), and a petition may be converted into a claim with the petitioner's consent (reg. 30).

## Leading cases

### Threshold: standing, AAA 3782/12 (24.3.2013)

Recap from lesson 14.1. The Israel Internet Association was not itself ordered to block anything, and the access providers did not petition. The Supreme Court (Vogelman J.) held that it had standing as a public petitioner: it sought to promote the public interest of Internet users rather than its own, the question was a fundamental first attempt to define a police power, and it had presented a sufficient factual basis. Why it matters: public-petitioner standing, developed in the HCJ (Ressler, lesson 06.6), applies in the Administrative Affairs Court too; but an individual should still show a personal interest (reg. 5(ב)(4)). [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/381)

### Threshold: delay, בג״ץ 9029/16 אבירם נ׳ שרת המשפטים (1.2.2017)

Recap from lessons 06.8 and 08.5. The three-part test (subjective delay, objective delay harming others' reliance, and the weight of the rule-of-law interest) applies under reg. 4. Why it matters here: in the Administrative Affairs Court the default period is short, and courts apply delay strictly, especially in tenders and planning where third parties rely on the decision. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/70)

### Key text: the threshold doctrines as a checklist

The remaining doctrines are judge-made and recur in almost every ruling on a petition:

- **Exhaustion and alternative remedy.** If a statute provides an objection, appeal or appeals committee, use it first (lesson 14.10); a petition will usually be dismissed. If a civil claim gives full relief, the court may send you there.
- **Prior demand.** Built into reg. 5(ב)(6).
- **Ripeness.** The court decides concrete disputes; a draft, a recommendation or a decision not yet applied to you may be premature (in 5658/23, Justice Elron would have dismissed on ripeness, lesson 14.2).
- **Clean hands and full disclosure.** A petitioner who hid material facts from the authority or the court may lose a remedy even on a good claim (Conterm, lesson 14.6).
- **Proper respondents.** Reg. 6; join the person who benefits from the decision (the winning bidder, the permit holder).

## How it works in practice

### Choosing the forum

| Situation | Forum |
|---|---|
| Decision of a municipality, ministry unit or committee on a First Schedule subject | Administrative Affairs Court, petition |
| Decision of a tribunal or appeals committee listed in the Second Schedule | Administrative Affairs Court, administrative appeal (reg. 23 default period unless the law sets one) |
| Damages from a tender; class action against an authority under the Class Actions Law | Administrative claim (Third Schedule) |
| Government or ministerial decision excluded from the schedule item, national policy, Knesset | HCJ (lesson 06.8) |
| Statutory objection or appeal exists (arnona director, planning objection, NII) | That route first (lesson 14.10) |

### Remedies you can realistically get

- **Return to the authority** to decide again properly (the most common).
- **Annulment** of the decision, with or without suspension.
- **Order to decide** within a set time (for omissions).
- **Order to act** (mandamus), mainly where the law leaves no discretion.
- **Interim relief** to keep things as they are until judgment.
- **Damages** only through an administrative claim or a civil claim, not in the petition itself (reg. 30).

### Fees

The petition fee and the possibility of a fee exemption: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). State the fee item or the exemption regulation in the petition (reg. 5(ב)(8)).

## Worked example

Leah Goldberg, an immigrant from Ukraine living in Netanya, applied to the Population Authority's local office to register her son's change of surname. After five months, and two reminders, nothing has happened.

1. **Forum.** Population administration decisions under the Names Law are First Schedule item 12 (excluding Government decisions and matters for the appeals tribunal). Venue (reg. 2) is where the decision was made: the Netanya office, so the Central District court sitting in administrative matters.
2. **Ripeness.** There is no decision, but an omission is a decision (Administrative Affairs Courts Law s. 2), and over three months' silence is a deemed refusal (Decisions and Reasons Law s. 6(ב); s. 9 excludes only Entry into Israel Law decisions).
3. **Period.** From when did she "know" of the refusal? The deemed refusal arose at three months; she files within the default period from that date and explains the reminders.
4. **Prior demand.** Her reminders are the prior demand; she attaches them (reg. 5(ב)(6)).
5. **Respondents.** The Population and Immigration Authority (reg. 6).
6. **Remedy.** An order to decide within 30 days.
7. **Self-representation.** She files in her own name: reg. 44א and reg. 170. She serves the petition on the respondent and files proof of service (regs 7(ב), 35).
8. **Likely result.** Authorities often decide soon after a petition is filed; the court may then strike out the petition as moot, possibly with costs in her favour.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A dispute with an authority, or Leah's case.
- The schedules and the regulations (links above).
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Forum (10 min).** Find the schedule item, its exceptions, and the venue under reg. 2. If none fits, write why the HCJ is the forum.
2. **Threshold (15 min).** For each doctrine (period, delay, prior demand, exhaustion, ripeness, clean hands, respondents, standing), write one line: risk low, medium or high, and what you would do.
3. **Timeline (10 min).** List the procedural steps 1–13 above with the dates or periods that would apply.
4. **Remedy (10 min).** Write the remedy paragraph: main remedy and an alternative (for example, "to annul, and alternatively to return to the respondent to decide within 30 days").
5. **Representation (5 min).** Note that you file in your own name, and what you would do if you needed an interim order.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for an individual: reg. 44א of the procedure regulations applies chapter 20 of the Civil Procedure Regulations 2018, including reg. 170 ("בעצמו או בידי עורך דין"), and reg. 5(ב)(1) asks for a lawyer's details only if the petitioner is represented. You may not be represented by a non-lawyer friend or relative (Bar Association Law s. 20; the no-lay-friend rule in the civil courts). A company or other corporation generally needs a lawyer to appear (the known case-law rule; not confirmed in a free source).

### Where and how

- The district court for the right district, sitting in administrative matters (reg. 2); filing at the registry or online where available to unrepresented parties (⚠️ manual check; lesson [08.8](../module-08/lesson-08.md)).
- Fee, fee exemption, periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: Legal Aid in some administrative matters, university clinics, NGOs; see [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need a temporary or interim order (demolition, deportation, closure, tender about to be signed).
- The authority files a long answer with affidavits you need to cross-examine.
- A third party with lawyers is joined as a respondent and the stakes are high.
- You are acting for a company.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not serving the petition or not filing proof of service | The petition is not put before the judge; delay | Serve and file proof at once | Serve within days of filing (reg. 35) |
| Leaving out the person who benefits from the decision | Struck out or ordered to join; delay | Ask to join the respondent | Apply reg. 6 at drafting |
| Filing a petition when an appeals committee exists | Dismissed for exhaustion | Use the statutory route if still in time | Check the governing law first |
| Asking for damages in the petition | Refused; damages need a claim | Ask to convert (reg. 30) or file a claim | Ask for administrative remedies only |
| Missing the hearing | Petition dismissed (reg. 18) | Apply to set aside within the short period | Diary every date; ask to postpone in writing |
| Weak petition dismissed on its face | Costs to the Treasury (reg. 41(ב)) | Withdraw early if the authority fixes the issue | Run the threshold checklist before filing |

## Review

- The Administrative Affairs Court hears listed petitions, appeals and claims with the HCJ's grounds and remedies; important cases can be transferred to the HCJ (ss. 5, 6, 8).
- Petitions fail most often at the threshold: delay, no prior demand, an alternative remedy, missing respondents, unclean hands or prematurity.
- The petitioner files, serves and proves service; the court can strike out on the face of the petition, order a response or answer, and decide on affidavits.
- Individuals may petition in their own name: reg. 44א applies reg. 170 of the Civil Procedure Regulations.
- Realistic remedies are return to the authority, annulment, an order to decide and interim relief; damages need an administrative or civil claim.
