# 14.10 · Practice lab: an FOI request and an objection to a decision

In this lab you file two documents in your own life: a freedom-of-information request that you actually send, and an objection (or request for reconsideration) to an administrative decision that affects you, which you send if it is low-risk and in time, or keep as a dry run. Together they are the first two steps of almost every dispute with an authority: find out what the authority knows, then ask it to change its mind.

## Why it matters

Most administrative disputes end at the authority's door, not in court, if the first letter is good. A precise FOI request gets you the decision's real basis; an objection built on that basis gives the official legal reasons to change the decision without losing face. Both are things anyone may do, they cost little or nothing, and they preserve your position if you later petition (lesson 14.11), because a court will ask what you asked the authority and when.

## The law

> (א) בקשה לקבלת מידע תוגש בכתב לממונה או למי שהוסמך לכך על ידו; אין המבקש חייב לציין את הטעם לבקשתו.

*Gist:* An FOI request goes in writing to the FOI officer, with no reason required ([FOI Law s. 7(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חופש_המידע); periods and fees in lesson 14.7).

> היתה החלטה של עובד הציבור נתונה לערר או לערעור על פי חיקוק, יודיע עובד הציבור בכתב לאדם הזכאי להגיש את הערר או את הערעור, על זכות הערר או הערעור ועל דרכי הגשתם ומועדיהם במידה שנקבעו בחיקוק.

*Gist:* If a decision is subject to an objection or appeal, the official must tell you in writing of the right, how to use it and the deadline ([Decisions and Reasons Law s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_(החלטות_והנמקות))). Look for that paragraph at the bottom of the decision.

> (א) מי שחוייב בתשלום ארנונה כללית רשאי תוך תשעים ימים מיום קבלת הודעת התשלום להשיג עליה לפני מנהל הארנונה על יסוד טענה מטענות אלה: [...]

*Gist:* The arnona objection is limited to listed grounds (wrong zone; wrong type, size or use of the property; not the holder; certain business-control claims); other claims of illegality, and discount requests, go by request and then petition ([Arnona Appeals Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הרשויות_המקומיות_(ערר_על_קביעת_ארנונה_כללית)); periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). This is a model for every statutory objection: read which grounds it covers.

## How it works in practice

### Which route for the objection?

| The decision letter says | Your route |
|---|---|
| "You may object / appeal within X days to Y" | That statutory route, within X days |
| Nothing about objection, and the governing law has none | Request for reconsideration to the decision-maker, framed as a prior demand |
| Silence for three months on your request | Treat as refusal (Decisions and Reasons s. 6(ב)); request for reconsideration, then petition |
| The problem is delay or run-around, not a decision | Ombudsman complaint (lesson 14.8), plus a reminder letter |

### Timing

Diary the objection deadline first. An FOI request can run in parallel, but do not wait for its answer if the objection deadline comes first: file the objection on what you have, and say that you have asked for information and will supplement.

## Worked example

Hila and Tomer Avraham, a young couple in Be'er Sheva, received a decision from the municipality's welfare department refusing a rent-assistance supplement for a family in crisis, because "the family does not meet the criteria". No criteria were attached, no reasons given, no hearing held, and no objection route stated.

1. **FOI request (sent the same week).** To the municipality's FOI officer: the written criteria for the supplement in force in 2026; the decision document in their file and the professional opinion it relied on (information about themselves: fee-exempt, reg. 6(א)); the number of families approved and refused in 2025–2026 (data as held).
2. **Route for the objection.** No statutory route is stated (s. 5), so a request for reconsideration to the department head, copied to the city's legal adviser, which also serves as a prior demand.
3. **Grounds.** Procedure: no reasons (s. 2א) and no hearing (Berman). Discretion: unpublished criteria cannot be applied fairly (Open House). Evidence: they attach rent contract, income documents and a social worker's letter.
4. **Remedy.** Reconsideration within 30 days after a hearing, and the criteria in writing.
5. **Outcome.** The department sends the criteria and invites them to a meeting; the supplement is approved for six months.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- A real decision of a public body that affected you or your household in the last few months (a fine, a refusal, a benefit, a permit, an arnona bill), or Hila and Tomer's case as a dry run.
- The decision letter and your correspondence.
- The [FOI request template](../../templates/foi-request.md) and the [objection template](../../templates/objection-to-administrative-decision.md).
- About 150 minutes over two sessions.

### Steps

**Part A: the FOI request (60 min)**

1. **Choose the information (10 min).** What do you need to know to challenge the decision? Typically: the criteria or procedure, the documents in your file, the professional opinion, and aggregate data on similar decisions.
2. **Find the officer (10 min).** The authority's website or the Government FOI unit's list; note the online form if there is one.
3. **Draft (25 min).** Fill in the FOI template: specific items, dates, electronic format, partial-disclosure request, fee status.
4. **Fee and clock (5 min).** Pay or claim the exemption; diary the decision date and extension limits from [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
5. **Send (10 min).** Submit; save the confirmation, the reference number and the payment receipt.

**Part B: the objection (90 min)**

6. **Route and deadline (15 min).** Read the decision for a s. 5 notice and the governing law for a statutory objection. Write the route and the last day. If the deadline is less than a week away, file a short objection now and supplement later.
7. **Facts (15 min).** A dated timeline of five to ten lines.
8. **Grounds (30 min).** One paragraph for each ground that fits, in this order: authority (14.1), considerations and equality (14.2), proportionality with a concrete alternative (14.3), procedure (14.4), evidence and delegation (14.5). Two strong grounds beat six weak ones.
9. **Remedy (10 min).** What exactly you ask for and by when; ask to be heard before any new decision.
10. **Attachments and review (15 min).** List attachments; read the draft aloud; remove anything personal or political that is not a legal argument.
11. **Decide: send or keep (5 min).** Send it if it is your own matter, in time and low-risk. If the stakes are high (for example, your residence status or a large sum), use it as the draft you take to a lawyer or a clinic.

### How you know it worked

- You have an FOI reference number and a diary entry for the answer.
- Your objection was sent (or is ready) before the deadline, on the right route.
- Each ground cites a provision or a case, and the remedy is something the official can do.
- Someone reading only your objection could tell what you want and why.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Both documents are designed to be filed by the public in their own name. Do not file for a friend or relative as a service (Bar Association Law s. 20); you may help them write their own.

### Where and how

- FOI: the authority's FOI officer; fees and periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Objection: as stated in the decision or the governing law; otherwise to the decision-maker and the legal adviser by email and registered mail.
- Free help: [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md) (Kol-Zchut for the specific benefit or service, ⚠️ manual check; Legal Aid; clinics).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The decision affects your liberty, residence status, home or a large sum.
- The authority answers with threats of enforcement or criminal proceedings.
- You cannot meet the deadline and need an extension argument.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the FOI answer before objecting | Objection deadline missed | Ask for an extension with reasons; consider a petition | File the objection first, supplement later |
| Using an objection route for a ground it does not cover | Director or committee lacks power to decide it | Raise that ground by request and petition | Read the grounds in the governing law |
| Long, angry objection | Official stops reading; credibility lost | Rewrite: facts, grounds, remedy | Two strong grounds, one page if possible |
| No remedy stated | Nothing to grant | Add a concrete request and date | Use the template's remedy section |
| No proof of sending | Dispute over dates | Resend with proof | Registered mail or saved online confirmation |

## Review

- Start every dispute with a precise FOI request and an objection on the right route, in time.
- The decision letter must tell you of objection rights (s. 5); statutory objections cover only listed grounds.
- Do not let the FOI request delay a statutory deadline.
- Build the objection from this module's grounds, with evidence and a remedy the official can grant.
- Keep a complete file: it becomes the petition's appendix if you need lesson 14.11.
