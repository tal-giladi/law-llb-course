# 13.7 · Possession, neighbours and easements; self-help and protection of possession

By the end of this lesson you will be able to protect your possession of land and goods, know the narrow window in which you may act yourself, handle the classic neighbour problems (a wall on the boundary, roots and branches, a building that crosses the line) and recognise an easement. You will analyse a neighbour dispute of your own and write the letter that comes before any claim.

## Why it matters

Most land disputes between private people are not about who owns the land but about **who holds it and how it is used**: a neighbour who moved a fence half a metre, a squatter in an empty flat, a path the village used for decades, a tree whose roots crack your floor. The Land Law protects possession as such, even against the owner, because it wants disputes decided in court, not by force. It allows self-help only in a tight frame. Knowing the frame keeps you out of trouble (forcible self-help outside it can be a tort and an offence) and tells you which court and remedy to use.

## The law

### Possession protected as such

> בעל מקרקעין ומי שזכאי להחזיק בהם זכאי לדרוש מסירת המקרקעין ממי שמחזיק בהם שלא כדין.

*Gist:* The owner and whoever is entitled to possess may demand delivery of the land from anyone holding it unlawfully. [Land Law s. 16](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_16)

> המחזיק במקרקעין זכאי לדרוש מכל מי שאין לו זכות לכך שיימנע מכל מעשה שיש בו משום הפרעה לשימוש במקרקעין ושיסלק כל דבר שיש בו משום הפרעה כזאת.

*Gist:* The possessor (even without ownership) may demand that anyone without a right stop interfering with his use and remove the interference. [Land Law s. 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_17)

A "possessor" includes one who holds through someone else, such as a landlord through a tenant (s. 15). The same rules protect movables (Movable Property Law s. 8).

### Self-help: a narrow window

> (א) המחזיק במקרקעין כדין רשאי להשתמש בכוח במידה סבירה כדי למנוע הסגת גבולו או שלילת שליטתו בהם שלא כדין.
> (ב) תפס אדם את המקרקעין שלא כדין רשאי המחזיק בהם כדין, תוך שלושים ימים מיום התפיסה, להשתמש בכוח במידה סבירה כדי להוציאו מהם.

*Gist:* A lawful possessor may use reasonable force to prevent a trespass, and to remove someone who seized the land, but only within a fixed short period from the seizure. [Land Law s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_18)

> מי שמוציא מקרקעין מידי המחזיק שלא כאמור בסעיף 18(ב) חייב להחזירם למחזיק; אולם אין בהוראה זו כדי לגרוע מסמכות בית המשפט לדון בזכויות שני הצדדים בעת ובעונה אחת [...]

*Gist:* Whoever takes land from the possessor outside s. 18(ב) must give it back, even if he is the owner; the court may decide both sides' rights together and regulate possession meanwhile. [Land Law s. 19](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_19) Damages and other remedies remain (s. 20).

So: an owner who finds a squatter after the period has passed, or a landlord who changes the locks on a tenant, must go to court; if he takes the land himself, he will be ordered to give it back first and argue ownership second.

### Building on another's land

If someone builds or plants on another's land without right, the owner chooses to keep the fixtures (paying the lower of the investment or the value) or to demand removal and restoration (s. 21); if he does not choose within six months of a written demand, he is treated as keeping them (s. 22). On **unsettled** land a good-faith builder who believed he was the owner and invested more than the land's value may buy the land (s. 23). The court may award reasonable payment for use and set payment terms (ss. 24–25).

### Neighbours: walls, fruit and roots

Walls, fences and trees on the boundary are presumed co-owned; each neighbour may use them and shares upkeep (s. 49). Fruit that falls on the neighbour's side is his (s. 50).

> עצים או צמחים אחרים הגדלים סמוך למיצר וענפיהם או שורשיהם מתפשטים לתוך מקרקעין שכנים, אין [...] השכן רשאי לדרוש סילוק הענפים או השורשים, אלא אם יש בהם כדי להזיק למקרקעין או להפריע לו במידה בלתי סבירה בהנאה מן המקרקעין; דרש השכן מבעל הצמחים לסלק ענפים או שרשים כאלה ולא סולקו תוך זמן סביר, רשאי הוא לסלקם בעצמו על חשבון בעל הצמחים.

*Gist:* You may demand removal of a neighbour's branches or roots only if they damage your land or unreasonably disturb you; if he does not remove them within a reasonable time after your demand, you may do it yourself at his cost. [Land Law s. 51](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_51) Tree-felling rules under the Forest Ordinance and municipal permits still apply (⚠️ check before cutting a protected tree).

### Easements

An easement is a burden on land for a benefit without possession (s. 5): a right of way, a right to run pipes, a duty not to build above a height. It may benefit land, a person, a class or the public (s. 92), may require use or abstention (s. 93) and may cover a defined part of a parcel (s. 93(ג)).

> (א) מי שהשתמש בזכות הראויה להוות זיקת הנאה במשך תקופה של שלושים שנים רצופות רכש את הזיקה והוא זכאי לדרוש רישומה.
> (ב) הודעה בכתב של בעל המקרקעין למי שמשתמש בזכות כאמור, לרבות הודעה לציבור, שהוא מתנגד לשימוש בזכות, מפסיקה את התקופה האמורה בסעיף קטן (א).

*Gist:* Thirty continuous years of use acquire an easement that can be registered; a written objection by the owner, including to the public, interrupts the period. [Land Law s. 94](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_94) The court may cancel or vary an easement for non-use or changed circumstances, with compensation (s. 96).

### The limit again: s. 14

Ownership does not justify causing harm or discomfort to others (s. 14); nuisance and the other torts are in [lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md).

## Leading cases

### Key text: s. 19 as "possession first, title later"

The case law reads ss. 18–19 as a rule of public order: the court restores the possessor who was ousted by force outside s. 18(ב) without first deciding who owns the land, so that self-help does not pay. The owner's title is decided afterwards or in the same case (s. 19, second part). The same idea explains why a claim for possession or against interference is heard in the **magistrates' court whatever the value** (Courts Law s. 51(א)(3)), while ownership disputes go to the district court. We could not verify a free full text of a leading ruling for this lesson; a Bar-exam answer rests on the sections themselves.

### Key text: easements and planning

Many "rights of way" in Israeli villages and old neighbourhoods rest on decades of use, not on registration. Section 94 lets the user ask for registration after 30 years, and s. 94(ב) gives the owner an easy way to stop the clock: a written notice. In urban areas access is often solved instead by planning (a road in an approved plan) or by co-owners' agreements; check the plan before litigating.

## How it works in practice

> [!WARNING]
> Using force against people can be a criminal offence and a tort, even when you have the better right. Section 18 permits only reasonable force, only by a lawful possessor, and to remove a trespasser only within the period in s. 18(ב). Outside that, call the police if there is violence and go to court.

### Remedies map

| Problem | Remedy | Forum |
|---|---|---|
| Squatter or ex-tenant in your flat | Claim for possession (s. 16); interim order | Magistrates' court (s. 51(א)(3)) |
| Neighbour blocks your access path | Order against interference (s. 17), easement if 30 years (s. 94) | Magistrates' court; district court for registration of the easement as a right in land |
| Neighbour's fence moved onto your land | Possession and removal; survey (מדידה) by a licensed surveyor | Magistrates' court |
| Roots cracking your floor | Written demand, then self-removal at his cost (s. 51); damages | Small claims for damages within the ceiling ([lesson 08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)) |
| Someone built on your land | Choose under s. 21 within six months of his demand | Court |

## Worked example

Natan Weiss and Samira Khatib are neighbours in a moshav near Hadera. For 34 years residents walked across the edge of Natan's plot to reach the orchards. In June 2026 Natan fences the edge. Samira cuts the fence the same night. Separately, roots from Samira's ficus have lifted Natan's paving.

1. **The path.** Use for 34 continuous years, by "a class of people" or the public, may have created an easement (ss. 92, 94), unless Natan or his predecessors gave written notice of objection. Samira and the other residents can ask the court to declare and register it.
2. **Cutting the fence.** Natan was in possession of his fenced plot. Samira did not act to prevent a trespass on her own land; she acted on his. Section 18 does not cover her; she may owe damages, and Natan may ask the court for an order against interference (s. 17) while the easement question is decided.
3. **The roots.** They damage Natan's paving: he may demand removal (s. 51). If Samira does not act within a reasonable time, he may remove them himself at her cost, without harming the tree more than necessary.
4. **Advice.** Both should stop acting alone. Natan sends a written demand about the roots; the residents' committee asks Natan in writing to restore access pending a decision; if not, a claim for a declaration of easement.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A neighbour or possession problem of your own (a fence, parking, a tree, noise, a path), or the Weiss–Khatib case.
- A site plan or GovMap printout; photographs with dates.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Facts (10 min).** Who holds what? Since when? What changed and on what date? Attach photos.
2. **Classify (10 min).** Possession (ss. 16–19), interference (s. 17), building on land (ss. 21–26), boundary fixture (s. 49), tree (s. 51), easement (ss. 92–96), or nuisance (s. 14; tort).
3. **Self-help check (5 min).** Is self-help allowed at all? If so, within what period, and what is "reasonable force"? If not, write "court only".
4. **Letter (15 min).** In Hebrew, a short, polite demand: the facts, the section, what you ask (remove the roots, restore access, stop parking on your plot), a reasonable deadline, and that you will otherwise apply to the court. Use the [pre-action letter template](../../templates/pre-action-letter.md).
5. **Forum (5 min).** Name the court and the remedy if the letter fails.

### Template

- [Pre-action letter (מכתב התראה לפני הגשת תביעה)](../../templates/pre-action-letter.md), adapted to a neighbour.

### How you know it worked

- The problem is classified under one or two sections.
- Your letter asks for a specific act by a date and cites the section.
- You know whether you may act yourself, and if so how far.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own land: possession, interference and damages claims may be filed in your own name in the magistrates' court or, for damages within the ceiling, in small claims (reg. 170; [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Representing a neighbour or the village committee for pay is reserved to lawyers (s. 20).

### Where and how

- Magistrates' court in the area of the land; urgent interim orders can be requested with the claim ([lesson 08.2](../module-08/lesson-02.md)).
- A licensed surveyor's measurement is the key evidence in boundary disputes.
- Free help: community mediation centres run by many municipalities (⚠️ check locally); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Someone has occupied your property and refuses to leave, especially with threats.
- The dispute involves registering or cancelling an easement, or ownership of a strip of land.
- A building crosses the boundary (ss. 21–23 choices have deadlines and big money).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Changing the locks on a tenant or relative | Ordered to restore possession first (s. 19); damages | Restore access and sue for possession | Go to court for eviction |
| Removing a squatter long after he entered | Unlawful self-help; return order | Restore and file a possession claim | Act within s. 18(ב) or through the court |
| Cutting a neighbour's roots without a demand | Damages for the tree | Settle | Demand in writing first (s. 51) |
| Letting decades of use run without objection | An easement by long use (s. 94) | Send written notice now | Object in writing early |
| Ignoring a builder's written demand to choose | Treated as keeping the building and paying (s. 22) | Get advice at once | Diary six months from the demand |

## Review

- The owner and the person entitled to possess may demand the land back (s. 16); a possessor may stop interference (s. 17).
- Self-help: reasonable force to prevent a trespass, and to remove a trespasser only within the s. 18(ב) period; otherwise the taker must give the land back first (s. 19).
- Building on another's land gives the owner a choice (ss. 21–22); good-faith builders on unsettled land may buy (s. 23).
- Boundary walls are co-owned (s. 49); roots and branches may be removed after a demand if they harm (s. 51).
- Easements may be created by agreement or 30 years of use, interrupted by written objection (ss. 92–96).
