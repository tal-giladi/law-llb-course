# 13.3 · Transactions in land: obligation and transaction, the writing requirement, competing transactions (s. 9)

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say exactly what a buyer of land holds at each stage of a deal, decide who wins when an owner sells the same flat twice, and explain why the Supreme Court made the first buyer's failure to register a caveat part of the answer. You will solve three priority problems of the kind the Bar exam sets.

## Why it matters

An Israeli apartment deal stretches over months or years: memorandum, contract, payments, possession, tax clearances, and only then registration. During that time the buyer is exposed. The seller may sell again, take a mortgage, be sued by creditors who attach the flat, or go bankrupt. Section 9 of the Land Law, one short sentence, decides most of these fights, and the courts have built on it a body of doctrine that is a fixture of property exams and of real litigation. Knowing it tells a buyer what to do on the day he signs; knowing its limits tells a second buyer when he is at risk.

## The law

### Two stages

> עסקה במקרקעין היא הקניָה של בעלות או של זכות אחרת במקרקעין לפי רצון המקנה, למעט הורשה על פי צוואה.

*Gist:* A land transaction is a voluntary grant of ownership or another right in land; inheritance by will is excluded. [Land Law s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_6)

> (א) עסקה במקרקעין טעונה רישום; העסקה נגמרת ברישום [...]
> (ב) עסקה שלא נגמרה ברישום רואים אותה כהתחייבות לעשות עסקה.

*Gist:* The transaction completes only on registration; until then it is an undertaking to make the transaction. [Land Law s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_7)

> התחייבות לעשות עסקה במקרקעין טעונה מסמך בכתב.

*Gist:* The undertaking must be in a written document. [Land Law s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_8) What the document must contain, and when the courts relax the rule, is in [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md); preliminary agreements and זיכרון דברים in [lesson 03.2](../module-03/lesson-02.md).

Short leases are the exception: a lease of up to five years needs neither registration nor writing (s. 79(א)), unless an option or a deferred start takes it past five years (s. 79(ב)).

### Competing transactions

> התחייב אדם לעשות עסקה במקרקעין ולפני שנגמרה העסקה ברישום חזר והתחייב כלפי אדם אחר לעסקה נוגדת, זכותו של בעל העסקה הראשונה עדיפה, אך אם השני פעל בתום־לב ובתמורה והעסקה לטובתו נרשמה בעודו בתום־לב – זכותו עדיפה.

*Gist:* The first in time wins, unless the second acted in good faith, for value, and registered while still in good faith. [Land Law s. 9](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_9)

Read it as a test with four questions:

1. Are there **two undertakings by the same person** about the same land, the second made before the first was registered?
2. Do they **conflict** (both cannot be performed in full)? A sale and a later mortgage conflict only partly; a sale and a later sale conflict fully.
3. Did the second party act **in good faith and for value**? Good faith means not knowing, and not deliberately shutting his eyes to, the first deal. A gift is not "for value".
4. Was the second transaction **registered while he was still in good faith**? Learning of the first deal between signing and registration defeats him.

If any answer for the second party is "no", the first wins. The movables rule is parallel but uses delivery instead of registration (Movable Property Law s. 12; lesson 13.8).

### Good faith reaches property law

> הוראות חוק זה יחולו, ככל שהדבר מתאים לענין ובשינויים המחוייבים, גם על פעולות משפטיות שאינן בבחינת חוזה ועל חיובים שאינם נובעים מחוזה.

*Gist:* The general part of the Contracts Law, including the duty to act in good faith (s. 39), applies to legal acts and obligations beyond contracts. [Contracts (General Part) Law s. 61(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_החוזים_%28חלק_כללי%29#סעיף_61) This is the bridge the courts used to bring good faith into s. 9 contests ([lesson 03.4](../module-03/lesson-04.md)).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 2643/97 גנז נ׳ בריטיש וקולוניאל חברה בע״מ (2003)

The first buyer of a property did not register a caveat for many years. A second buyer, in good faith and for value, bought from the same seller but had not completed registration. On a literal reading of s. 9 the first buyer wins, because the second never registered. The Court held that the first buyer's long failure to register a caveat, when it was easy to do and he knew the risk, can be a breach of the good-faith duty owed to the world of potential buyers, and that in such a case the second buyer may take priority even without registration.

Why it matters: Ganz turned the caveat from an option into, in practice, a duty. A first buyer who sits on his rights cannot rely on "first in time". Critics (including some justices) argued that this rewrites the clear words of s. 9 and weakens certainty; supporters answered that it gives effect to the purpose of the register. ⚠️ Free text: search the case number on [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il); we could not open it on 17 October 2026 (site unreachable).

### ע״א 189/95 בנק אוצר החייל בע״מ נ׳ אהרונוב (1999)

Buyers of a flat paid the price and took possession but never registered a caveat. A creditor of the seller then attached the flat. Section 9 does not mention attachments, because an attachment is not an undertaking to make a transaction. The Court held that, as a rule, a buyer who paid and whose contract preceded the attachment has a right that prevails over the later attaching creditor, since the creditor takes the debtor's property "as it is", subject to the debtor's existing obligations; the buyer's conduct, including not registering a caveat, remains relevant to good faith.

Why it matters: Aharonov recognised the unregistered buyer as holding a quasi-property ("equitable") right, despite s. 161. It is the starting point for every buyer-versus-creditor dispute. ⚠️ Free text: search the case number on [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il) (unreachable on 17 October 2026).

## How it works in practice

### The buyer's timeline and exposure

| Stage | What the buyer holds | Main risks | Protection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memorandum or contract signed | Undertaking (s. 7(ב)), if s. 8 is met | Second sale, mortgage, attachment, bankruptcy | Caveat at once (13.4) |
| Payments made, possession taken | Undertaking plus strong equities (Aharonov) | Same, but stronger position | Caveat; pay into trust for the last part |
| Tax clearances and deed signed | Undertaking; deed ready | Last-minute attachment | Up-to-date extract the day the deed is filed |
| Registration | Ownership (s. 7(א)) | Only fraud or court order | Keep the signed extract |

### Priority checklist for a dispute

1. Write the dates of each undertaking, payment, possession, caveat and registration on one line.
2. Identify the "first" and the "second" party.
3. Apply the s. 9 test to the second party.
4. Ask whether the first party's conduct (no caveat, delay, allowing the seller to appear as free owner) raises a Ganz argument.
5. For a creditor or a receiver, use the Aharonov line rather than s. 9.

## Worked example

On 1 March 2026 Hila and Omer Ben-David sign a contract to buy a flat in Afula from Nadav Levi and pay 20%. Their lawyer is abroad and no caveat is registered. On 10 May Levi signs a second contract with Wassim Haddad for the same flat at a higher price. Haddad orders an extract on 9 May (clean), pays 90% and registers a caveat on 12 May. On 20 June the Ben-Davids learn of the second sale.

1. **Two conflicting undertakings by the same person before registration?** Yes: two full sales.
2. **Second party in good faith and for value?** Haddad checked the extract, paid most of the price and had no notice of the first sale: good faith and value.
3. **Registered?** Haddad registered a caveat, not the transfer. On the words of s. 9 he has not "registered the transaction", so the Ben-Davids, first in time, would win.
4. **Ganz.** The Ben-Davids left the flat without a caveat for over two months while the seller appeared free. Haddad will argue that this is a breach of good faith that shifts priority to him even before registration. The court will weigh the length and reasons of the delay (a short delay with an explanation weighs less than years of neglect) and Haddad's conduct.
5. **Outcome and advice.** Likely contested; the Ben-Davids should immediately register a caveat (it will rank after Haddad's), ask the court for a temporary injunction against registering the transfer, and claim against Levi. Haddad should not complete registration without legal advice once he knows of the first sale, because good faith must exist "while registering".

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The s. 9 checklist above.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

Solve each scenario in five lines: the parties, the dates, the rule (s. 9, Ganz or Aharonov), the likely result, and the first practical step for the losing party.

1. **Gift then sale.** Malka promises in writing to give her Ramat Gan flat to her nephew. A month later she sells it to a stranger, who pays, knows nothing of the promise and registers the transfer.
2. **Sale then mortgage.** Sami sells his Nazareth flat to Rana, who pays half and registers a caveat. Sami then takes a bank loan secured by a mortgage on the flat; the bank does not check the extract.
3. **Sale then attachment.** Efrat buys a flat in Be'er Sheva, pays in full, moves in, but never registers a caveat. Two years later the seller's creditor attaches the flat.
4. Then write the Ben-David case from the creditor's angle: what if Levi's creditor, not Haddad, had attached the flat on 10 May?

None.

### How you know it worked

- Each scenario names the right rule: s. 9 for two undertakings, Aharonov for an attachment.
- You tested good faith, value and registration separately for the second party.
- You identified when the first party's own delay matters.

1. The stranger wins: second transaction in good faith, for value, registered while in good faith. The nephew's promise was also a gift promise, revocable until reliance (Gift Law s. 5); he has at most a claim against Malka.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may analyse your own deal, order extracts, and sign a caveat request before the registrar (lesson 13.4). Buying or selling land in practice needs a lawyer: deeds are signed before a lawyer or the registrar and online filing is for lawyers. Representing others before the registry is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(2)).

### Where and how

- Priority disputes go to court: the district court for ownership disputes in land; a temporary injunction against registration can be sought there. Magistrates' courts hear possession and use, not ownership (Courts Law s. 51(א)(3), [lesson 01.4](../module-01/lesson-04.md)).
- An attachment by the Execution Office: a request to the execution registrar ([lesson 08.7](../module-08/lesson-07.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You discover a second contract, a mortgage or an attachment on a flat you paid for.
- You are a second buyer and learn of an earlier deal before registration.
- Bankruptcy, insolvency or a receiver is involved.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| No caveat after signing | Priority lost to a later buyer or creditor (Ganz, Aharonov) | Register now; seek an injunction | Caveat the day you sign |
| Paying most of the price before checking the extract | Paying for a flat already sold or mortgaged | Stop payment; claim against the seller | Fresh extract the day you pay |
| Completing registration after learning of the first deal | Not in good faith "while registering" (s. 9) | Get advice before registering | Re-check for notice until registration |
| Treating a gift promise as a "transaction for value" | The donee cannot win under s. 9 | Rely on the Gift Law instead | Know the value requirement |
| Using s. 9 against an attaching creditor | Wrong framework | Argue the Aharonov line | Classify the competing party first |

## Review

- A land transaction completes on registration; before that the buyer has a written undertaking (ss. 7–8).
- Under s. 9 the first undertaking prevails unless the second party acted in good faith, for value, and registered while still in good faith.
- Through s. 61(ב) and s. 39, the first buyer's own conduct (no caveat) can cost him priority (Ganz, ⚠️ text to confirm).
- Attaching creditors are outside s. 9; the Aharonov line usually prefers a buyer who paid before the attachment.
- The practical rule: check the extract before paying and register a caveat on signing.
