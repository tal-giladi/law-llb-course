# 13.2 · Land registration: the Land Registry, Israel Land Authority leases and the registration of rights

By the end of this lesson you will know how Israel records rights in land, what the register proves and when you can rely on it, why most Israeli homes stand on land the state owns, and where to look for the rights in a particular flat. You will find the block and parcel of a property you know and identify which register holds its rights.

## Why it matters

In Israel "the tabu" (from the Ottoman *tapu*) is the public register of rights in land, kept by the Land Registration and Settlement of Rights Division of the Ministry of Justice in regional offices (לשכות רישום מקרקעין). Registration is not a formality: a land transaction is completed only when it is registered (s. 7, [lesson 03.3](../module-03/lesson-03.md)), and for settled land the register is conclusive. But many flats are not (yet) registered in the owner's name at all. Their rights are recorded only at the Israel Land Authority (רמ״י) or at the developer or a housing company. A buyer who checks only the tabu, or only the developer's papers, can miss a mortgage, an attachment or a seller who is not the owner.

## The law

### The registers are public

> הפנקסים המתנהלים בלשכה יהיו פתוחים לעיון הציבור, וכל אדם רשאי לעיין בהם ולקבל העתקים מן הרשום בהם.

*Gist:* Anyone may inspect the registers and get copies; no reason or connection to the property is needed. [Land Law s. 124](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_124)

The regulations add the procedure: a request naming the property and the fee (regs 91–92), and an extract that may be issued electronically, signed with a secure electronic signature.

> התקבל נסח מקוון החתום בחתימה אלקטרונית מאושרת, לא יראו פלט נייר שלו כהעתק נאמן של הנסח; פלט נייר כאמור של נסח לא יהיה קביל בכל הליך שיפוטי להוכחת זכויות במקרקעין.

*Gist:* A printout of an online extract is not a true copy and is not admissible in court to prove rights; keep the signed electronic file. [Management and Registration Regulations reg. 92(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_המקרקעין_(ניהול_ורישום))

The **documents behind** a registration (the deed, the old contract) are not open to all: you must show a direct connection and that inspection will not harm anyone (reg. 93(ב)).

### What the register proves

> (א) רישום בפנקסים לגבי מקרקעין מוסדרים יהווה ראיה חותכת לתכנו [...]
> (ב) רישום בפנקסים לגבי מקרקעין לא־מוסדרים יהווה ראיה לכאורה לתכנו.

*Gist:* For land registered after settlement of title the register is conclusive evidence; for unsettled land it is only prima facie evidence that can be rebutted. [Land Law s. 125](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_125)

> מי שרכש זכות במקרקעין מוסדרים בתמורה ובהסתמך בתום־לב על הרישום, יהא כוחה של זכותו יפה אף אם הרישום לא היה נכון.

*Gist:* A person who acquires a right in settled land for value, relying in good faith on the register, keeps it even if the register was wrong. [Land Law s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_10)

Section 10 is the "mirror" principle of a title register: the buyer may trust what he sees. It cuts against the true owner whose land was registered to a forger, which is why courts read "good faith" strictly (a buyer who ignored warning signs, paid in cash to a stranger or did not see the property is not in good faith).

### Who runs the registry

The Minister of Justice appoints a **registrar** for each office (s. 116), **superintendents of land registration** (מפקחים), qualified to be magistrates' court judges, who also decide condominium disputes (s. 117; lesson 13.6), and a **registration commissioner** (s. 118). A person harmed by a decision of a registrar or superintendent (except a superintendent's ruling in a condominium dispute) may file an administrative appeal (ערר) to the commissioner (s. 121), and from him appeal to the district court (s. 122); the periods are in regs 74 and 76 (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). A registration already made cannot be challenged by ערר; you need a court remedy (s. 121(ג)).

### How a transaction is registered

The parties sign a deed (שטר עסקה) **before the registrar** at the office (reg. 12) or **before a lawyer**, who verifies the signatures and confirms in writing that he checked the deed and documents (reg. 14). In practice almost all deeds go through lawyers, and the online filing system is built for them (see [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Deeds also need tax clearances (purchase and betterment tax, municipal certificate), covered in year 3 (module 25).

### Israel Land Authority land

> מקרקעי ישראל, והם המקרקעין בישראל של המדינה, של רשות הפיתוח או של הקרן הקיימת לישראל, הבעלות בהם לא תועבר, אם במכר ואם בדרך אחרת.

*Gist:* Land of the State, the Development Authority and the Jewish National Fund may not be transferred in ownership, except as a statute allows. [Basic Law: Israel Lands s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_מקרקעי_ישראל#סעיף_1)

About nine-tenths of the land in Israel is "Israel lands". The Israel Land Authority manages it (ILA Law s. 2(א)); its tasks include allocating land and **promoting the registration of rights in the land registers** (s. 2א(4)). Instead of selling, the ILA grants long leases (חכירה לדורות, s. 3 of the Land Law), typically for decades and renewable; the lessee is treated as the "owner of the flat" for condominium purposes (s. 52). The 2009 reform allows transfer of ownership of urban residential land (Israel Lands Law s. 2(7)), and s. 4יז of the ILA Law sets the procedure: for a lessee under a **capitalised lease** for housing, the ILA sends a registered-mail notice of its intention to transfer ownership, free of charge for flats in apartment buildings; if the lessee consents or does not object within the stated period, the ILA itself asks the registry to register ownership in the lessee's name.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 6698/95 קעדאן נ׳ מינהל מקרקעי ישראל (8.3.2000)

The State, through the Israel Lands Administration, allocated land to the Jewish Agency, which set up the community of Katzir through a cooperative society that admitted only Jews. The Ka'adan family, Arab citizens, were refused a plot. The Court held that equality binds every act of a government authority, including the allocation of state land, that the policy was unlawful discrimination on the basis of nationality, and that acting through the Jewish Agency could not legitimise it. Justice Kedmi would have limited the ruling to a forward-looking declaration.

Why it matters: the ILA is not a private landowner. Its allocations, prices and conditions are administrative acts, reviewable for equality and reasonableness (module 14). The case remains contested: supporters see it as the core of civic equality in land; critics argued that it left too little room for the Zionist settlement purposes of the national institutions, and the Knesset later enacted admissions committees for small communities. Read it in English at [Cardozo ISCP](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/218/).

### Key text: s. 10 and the forged-deed problem

The Supreme Court's case law on s. 10 deals with forged sales: an impostor "sells" a flat registered to someone abroad, the buyer registers and resells. The Law protects the good-faith purchaser of settled land; the courts ask whether the buyer checked identity, saw the property and paid through traceable channels. We could not verify a free full text of a leading s. 10 ruling, so read the section with s. 9 (lesson 13.3) and the good-faith cases there.

## How it works in practice

### Three places where rights may be recorded

| Situation | Where the rights are | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Flat registered as a sub-parcel in a condominium, or a house on its own parcel | Land Registry | נסח רישום (online or at the office) |
| Flat on Israel Land Authority land where the lease is not yet registered in the registry | ILA records | ILA "rights confirmation" (אישור זכויות) (⚠️ manual check of the current online service) |
| New building not yet registered as a condominium | The developer's records, sometimes a housing company; caveats may sit on the whole parcel | Developer's confirmation; the parcel's נסח with caveats for each buyer |

### Ordering an extract

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

1. Find the block, parcel and sub-parcel: from your purchase contract, the municipal tax (arnona) notice, or the government map GovMap (enter the address, turn on the parcels layer).
2. Order online on the Ministry of Justice service ([gov.il, ⚠️ manual check](https://www.gov.il/he/service/land_registration_extract)), paying by card, or ask at any Land Registry office.
3. Choose the kind: a current extract (נסח רגיל), a concentrated extract of a whole condominium (נסח מרוכז) or a historical extract (shows deleted entries).
4. Save the signed PDF. The fee for an electronic extract is much lower than for a paper one; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Worked example

Yosef Tesfaye from Netanya wants to buy a flat in an old public-housing building in Ashdod. The seller, Galina Petrov, shows an Amidar purchase contract from 2003 and her own sale contract. Yosef orders a נסח for the parcel and finds the building is registered as a condominium, but sub-parcel 7 (the flat) is registered in the name of "מדינת ישראל" with a lease to Amidar.

1. **Where are Galina's rights?** Not in the registry under her name. They are recorded at Amidar, or at the ILA if the lease was transferred to her there.
2. **What does the register prove?** Settled land: conclusive (s. 125(א)). It proves the State owns and Amidar leases. It does not prove Galina's right; Yosef must get confirmation from the body that holds the record.
3. **Can Yosef rely on s. 10?** Only for what is registered. Galina is not registered, so s. 10 does not protect him against a defect in her chain of rights.
4. **Next steps.** Ask Amidar or the ILA for a written confirmation of Galina's rights and of any mortgage or attachment recorded there; make the contract conditional on registration of Galina's rights or on a direct transfer; and register a caveat where it can be registered (lesson 13.4). Use a lawyer for the deal.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An address you know well: your home, a parent's home or a flat you might buy.
- GovMap (free) and the Ministry of Justice extract service, or the Ashdod case.
- 45 minutes; an extract costs a fee if you order one.

### Steps

1. Find the block (גוש) and parcel (חלקה) on GovMap; for a flat, find the sub-parcel (תת־חלקה) from the contract or the arnona notice.
2. Decide whether the land is likely Israel Land Authority land (ask: who sold the first owner the land? Is there an ILA file number?).
3. Order a current extract for the parcel (optional; real, low-risk, in your own name).
4. Write down: the land office (לשכה), whether the land is settled, the registered owners and their shares, the type of right (ownership or lease), and the lessor if a lease.
5. Decide where the rest of the rights might be recorded (ILA, developer, housing company).
6. Write three lines: what the register proves here (s. 125), and whether a good-faith buyer could rely on s. 10.

None. The full extract-reading checklist comes in lesson 13.9: [apartment purchase check (draft)](../../templates/apartment-purchase-check.md).

### How you know it worked

- You have the block and parcel and know which office holds them.
- You can say whether the land is settled and what that means for s. 125.
- You named the register that holds the current owner's right.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. Anyone may order an extract and inspect the registers (s. 124). A private person may sign a deed or a caveat request **before the registrar** instead of a lawyer (regs 12, 21), but online filing is for lawyers, and representing another person before the Land Registry is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(2) names the land registry office). See [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- Extracts: the Ministry of Justice online service or any Land Registry office; fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- ILA rights: the ILA's online services and regional offices (⚠️ manual check).
- Appeals against a registrar's decision: ערר to the registration commissioner within the regulation period, then the district court ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut guides on the tabu and on ILA land; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The seller's rights are not registered in his name in the register you checked.
- The extract shows a mortgage, an attachment, a caveat in favour of someone else, or a note about inheritance or a receiver.
- You suspect forgery or an impostor seller (for example, a registered owner who lives abroad and is "represented").

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checking only the developer's or seller's papers | Missing a mortgage or a prior buyer | Order the parcel extract and ILA confirmation | Check every register that may hold rights |
| Relying on a printout of an online extract in court | Not admissible (reg. 92(ג)) | Submit the signed electronic file or a certified extract | Keep the signed PDF |
| Assuming s. 10 protects you on unsettled land | The register is only prima facie there | Investigate the chain of title | Check whether the land is settled |
| Assuming ILA land cannot be sold | Losing a good purchase | The lease is transferable, subject to ILA rules | Ask for the lease terms and ILA confirmation |
| Missing the ערר period against a registrar's refusal | Decision becomes final | Ask the commissioner to extend (reg. 74(ב)) | Diary the date the decision reached you |

## Review

- The registers are public: anyone may inspect them and get an extract (s. 124); the documents behind them need a direct connection (reg. 93).
- For settled land the register is conclusive (s. 125(א)) and a good-faith buyer for value is protected even if it was wrong (s. 10).
- Registrars, superintendents and the commissioner run the system; ערר to the commissioner, then appeal to the district court.
- Most Israeli land is Israel lands, held by long leases; s. 4יז of the ILA Law lets capitalised residential lessees receive ownership.
- Rights may be recorded at the registry, at the ILA, or at a developer or housing company: check them all.
