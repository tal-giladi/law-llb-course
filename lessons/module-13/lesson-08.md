# 13.8 · Movables: good-faith acquisition, lost property and pledges

By the end of this lesson you will be able to decide who owns a car, a phone or a machine when the seller was not the owner or sold it twice, what to do with something you find, and how a lender's security over movables works and how to spot it before you buy. These are frequent small-claims and Bar-exam questions, and they protect you in every second-hand purchase.

## Why it matters

The rule for land is "check the register". For movables there is usually no register, so the law must choose between an owner who lost his property (it was stolen, sold twice, pledged) and a buyer who paid in good faith. Israeli law chooses differently in different situations: the first buyer in a double sale unless the second took delivery in good faith; the buyer from a dealer in the ordinary course (market overt); the original owner against a buyer from a private thief. Pledges add another layer: a bank's registered pledge may follow the car into your hands.

## The law

### Ownership passes on delivery

> הבעלות בממכר עוברת לקונה במסירתן, אם לא הסכימו הצדדים על מועד אחר או על דרך אחרת להעברת הבעלות.

*Gist:* Ownership of goods passes on delivery unless the parties agreed otherwise. [Sale Law s. 33](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_33)

### Double sale of a movable

> התחייב אדם להקנות לחברו בעלות או זכות אחרת במיטלטלין, ולפני שמסר לו את המיטלטלין או הקנה לו את הזכות חזר והתחייב עם אדם אחר בהקניה נוגדת, זכותו של הראשון עדיפה, אך אם השני התקשר בעסקה וקיבל את המיטלטלין או את הזכות בתום לב ובתמורה – זכותו עדיפה.

*Gist:* First in time wins, unless the second contracted and **received the goods** in good faith and for value. [Movable Property Law s. 12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המיטלטלין#סעיף_12) Compare Land Law s. 9 (lesson 13.3): delivery here plays the role of registration.

### Buying from a dealer: market overt

> נמכר נכס נד על ידי מי שעוסק במכירת נכסים מסוגו של הממכר והמכירה היתה במהלך הרגיל של עסקיו, עוברת הבעלות לקונה נקיה מכל שעבוד, עיקול וזכות אחרת בממכר אף אם המוכר לא היה בעל הממכר או לא היה זכאי להעבירו כאמור, ובלבד שהקונה קנה וקיבל אותו לחזקתו בתום־לב.

*Gist:* A buyer who buys movables from a dealer in goods of that kind, in the ordinary course of his business, takes clean ownership even if the dealer was not the owner, provided the buyer bought and took possession in good faith. [Sale Law s. 34](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_34)

Four conditions, each a frequent exam point: (1) a movable; (2) the seller **deals** in goods of that kind (a phone shop selling a phone, not a dentist selling his car); (3) **the ordinary course** of his business (not a sale at night from a van at half price); (4) the buyer **bought and took possession in good faith**. If all are met, the original owner loses the goods and keeps a claim against the dealer and the thief. A sale by a court, an Execution Office or another authority also passes clean title, except rights that survive under the sale terms (s. 34א); the charge then moves to the proceeds (s. 34ב).

### Lost property

> (א) המוצא אבידה ונוטלה [...] חייב להשיבה לבעלה או להודיע עליה בהקדם למשטרה, זולת אם בנסיבות הענין יש להניח שבעל האבידה התייאש ממנה מחמת מיעוט שוויה.

*Gist:* A finder who takes lost property must return it to the owner or report it to the police promptly, unless its value is so small that the owner can be assumed to have given up. [Return of Lost Property Law s. 2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_השבת_אבידה#סעיף_2)

If the finder complied and the owner is not found within a fixed period, the finder becomes the owner (s. 4(א)); a finder who did not comply loses the find to the State (s. 4(ב)). The former owner may redeem it within a year for its value (s. 5). Property found on someone else's premises (a shop, a bus) is reported to the premises' owner (s. 3); items left in hotels or hospitals are treated as lost after a period (s. 7). Failing to comply is an offence (s. 9). The periods are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

Ownerless property may be acquired by taking it with intent to own (Movable Property Law s. 3); abandonment ends ownership (s. 7). Lost property is not abandoned.

### Pledges in brief

> (א) מישכון הוא שעבוד נכס כערובה לחיוב; הוא מזכה את הנושה להיפרע מן המשכון אם לא סולק החיוב.

*Gist:* A pledge charges an asset as security for a debt and lets the creditor be paid from it on default. [Pledges Law s. 1(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המשכון#סעיף_1) It applies to any transaction meant to secure a debt, whatever it is called (s. 2(ב)), and is created by agreement (s. 3).

Against **other creditors** the pledge is effective (s. 4): for movables, by **deposit** with the creditor (a pawnshop) or by **registration** under the regulations (the Registrar of Pledges at the Companies Authority, or the Companies Registrar for company charges), and in any case against a creditor who knew or should have known of it. A pledgee in good faith who received goods by deposit or registration is protected even if the pledgor was not the owner, if the goods came to him with the owner's consent (s. 5). Later pledges rank after earlier ones unless agreed otherwise (s. 6). A person who pledges his asset for another's debt is treated like a guarantor (s. 12). The debtor may redeem by paying (s. 13). Realisation is by court order, or for deposited or registered pledges by the Execution Office (s. 17), in the manner of an execution sale (s. 18). For land the mortgage follows the Land Law ss. 85–90.

### The lien

A lien (עיכבון) is a right under law to keep movables as security until a debt is paid, such as a garage holding a repaired car (Movable Property Law s. 11). It ends if the creditor gives the goods up voluntarily (s. 11(ד)).

## Leading cases

### ע״א 2833/04 רגיס בע״מ נ׳ טרבלסי

A company bought heavy engineering equipment and agreed to a charge in favour of the seller; the seller failed to register the charge with the Companies Registrar within the statutory period. When the company went into liquidation, the court considered whether a late registration, allowed by the Registrar, could defeat the liquidator. The case shows the central point of the Pledges Law: an unregistered charge is good between the parties but weak against other creditors and in insolvency. ⚠️ We read only the abstract; read the details at [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/regis-ltd-v-trabelsi) before relying on the outcome.

Why it matters: registration is to pledges what the caveat is to land.

### Key text: market overt and stolen cars

Section 34 covers cars, but a buyer who does not check the vehicle's licence, the ownership history and whether a pledge is noted may fail the good-faith condition. Courts also ask whether the seller really deals in cars and whether the price and place of sale were normal. There is no single free leading ruling we could verify; apply the four conditions to the facts.

## How it works in practice

### Before buying a second-hand car or equipment

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

1. **Who is the owner?** Compare the vehicle licence (רישיון רכב) with the seller's ID. A dealer should be a registered business.
2. **Pledges.** Ask the seller for a pledges extract (the Registrar of Pledges' online search for a car by licence number, for a fee, ⚠️ manual check of the current service), and look for a lien note on the vehicle licence.
3. **Price and place.** A price far below market or a sale outside the dealer's premises weakens good faith (s. 34).
4. **Pay traceably.** Bank transfer to the registered owner's account; transfer ownership at the post office or the licensing office on the same day.
5. **Keep the evidence.** The ad, messages, receipt, transfer confirmation.

### If you find something

Return it to the owner if you can identify him (an ID card, a phone you can call from), or report it to the police promptly; diary the date. If it is found in a shop or on a bus, hand it to the premises' owner (s. 3).

## Worked example

Daniel Ohana, a student in Jerusalem, buys a used laptop for ₪3,200 from "Tech-Bazaar", a shop on Jaffa Road that sells new and refurbished computers. He gets a receipt and takes the laptop. A month later Rivka Stern sees it at the university, recognises a scratch, and shows a police report: it was stolen from her flat. She demands it back.

1. **Movable?** Yes.
2. **Dealer in goods of that kind?** The shop sells computers: yes.
3. **Ordinary course?** A sale at the shop, with a receipt, at a plausible second-hand price: yes.
4. **Bought and took possession in good faith?** Nothing suggested theft. Yes.
5. **Result.** Under s. 34 Daniel owns the laptop free of Rivka's right. Rivka's claims are against the thief and against the shop (possibly in tort for buying stolen goods carelessly).
6. **Change one fact.** If Daniel had bought it from a stranger in a car park for ₪800, the seller is not a dealer and the circumstances negate good faith: Rivka wins (nemo dat: no one gives what he does not have, subject only to the statutory exceptions).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A planned or recent second-hand purchase (a car, a bicycle, a phone, equipment), or the Ohana case.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Classify the seller (5 min).** Owner, dealer, private non-owner, authority (execution sale)?
2. **Apply the rule (10 min).** s. 12 (double sale), s. 34 (dealer), s. 34א (authority), or nemo dat. Write each condition and whether it is met.
3. **Pledge check (10 min).** For a car or equipment: plan (or do) the pledges search; write what it would show and what you would do if a pledge appears (payoff letter from the lender, payment to the lender directly).
4. **Lost-item rule (5 min).** Write what you would do if you found a wallet with an ID, a phone without ID, and a ₪10 note on the street.
5. **Purchase checklist (10 min).** Write your own five-line checklist for the next purchase.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for most of it: a claim for the return of goods or their value can be filed in your own name, in small claims for money within the ceiling ([lesson 08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)) or in the magistrates' court for possession of movables. Pledge and charge disputes in insolvency need a lawyer.

### Where and how

- Lost property: the police station; theft: a police complaint ([lesson 05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)).
- Pledges: the Registrar of Pledges' search service (⚠️ manual check) and, for companies, the Companies Registrar.
- Fees and the small-claims ceiling: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The police seize goods you bought as stolen and you rely on s. 34.
- A lender realises a pledge on an asset you bought.
- The seller is in liquidation or bankruptcy.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying from a private stranger at a bargain price | The true owner takes the goods back | Claim against the seller | Buy from the owner or a real dealer |
| Not checking pledges on a car | The lender realises the pledge | Pay off the lender; claim against the seller | Search the pledges register first |
| Keeping a found phone without reporting | No ownership; an offence (s. 9) | Report now | Report or return promptly |
| Paying a second buyer's price before delivery | Losing to a first buyer if no delivery (s. 12) | Take delivery or cancel | Pay against delivery |
| A garage releasing the car before payment | Lien lost (s. 11(ד)) | Sue for the debt | Keep the car until paid, or take security |

## Review

- Ownership of goods passes on delivery unless agreed otherwise (Sale Law s. 33).
- Double sale of movables: first wins unless the second received the goods in good faith and for value (s. 12).
- Market overt: a good-faith buyer who buys and takes possession from a dealer in the ordinary course gets clean title (Sale Law s. 34); otherwise no one gives what he does not have.
- A finder must return or report; after the period the complying finder becomes owner (Return of Lost Property Law ss. 2–5).
- A pledge binds other creditors through deposit or registration (Pledges Law s. 4); check the register before buying.
