# 13.5 · Co-ownership and dissolution of joint ownership

By the end of this lesson you will be able to work out the rights and duties of people who own land together, decide what a co-owner may do alone and what needs a majority or everyone, predict how a court will end the co-ownership, and write a proposal to your co-owners. These are the most common property disputes in Israeli families: siblings who inherit a flat, divorcing spouses, partners in a plot.

## Why it matters

Joint ownership arises constantly: two spouses register a flat in both names, parents leave a house to four children, partners buy a plot. It works while everyone agrees and becomes painful when one wants to sell, one lives in the property for free, or one builds on it. The Land Law gives a complete set of default rules, a near-absolute right for each owner to end the co-ownership, and a short list of protections for a family home. A co-owner who knows them can usually settle without a court; one who does not may pay rent he did not expect, or watch the family home sold at an execution auction.

## The law

### Undivided shares

> מקרקעין שהם של כמה בעלים, בעלותו של כל אחד מהם לפי חלקו מתפשטת בכל אתר ואתר שבמקרקעין ואין לשום שותף חלק מסויים בהם.

*Gist:* Each co-owner's share extends over every point of the land; no co-owner owns a particular part. [Land Law s. 27](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_27) Shares are presumed equal (s. 28).

### Default rules, unless the co-owners agree otherwise

A co-ownership agreement on management and use can be registered and then binds later co-owners and everyone else (s. 29(א)); absent one, ss. 30–36 apply (s. 29(ב)).

> (א) בעלי רוב החלקים במקרקעין המשותפים רשאים לקבוע כל דבר הנוגע לניהול הרגיל של המקרקעין ולשימוש הרגיל בהם. [...]
> (ג) דבר החורג מניהול או משימוש רגילים טעון הסכמת כל השותפים.

*Gist:* The majority of shares decides ordinary management and use; anything beyond the ordinary needs unanimity; a co-owner who feels wronged may ask the court for directions. [Land Law s. 30](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_30)

Each co-owner may alone make reasonable use that does not prevent others' equal use, take urgent unforeseen steps to maintain the property, and act to prevent damage and protect ownership, notifying the others (s. 31). Expenses are shared by shares, with a right of recovery (s. 32).

> שותף שהשתמש במקרקעין משותפים חייב ליתר השותפים, לפי חלקיהם במקרקעין, שכר ראוי בעד השימוש.

*Gist:* A co-owner who used the property owes the others a reasonable payment for the use, by their shares. [Land Law s. 33](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_33)

Each co-owner may sell or mortgage his share without consent (s. 34(א)); a clause restricting this binds for no more than five years (s. 34(ב)). Fruits are shared by shares (s. 35).

### The right to dissolve

> (א) כל שותף במקרקעין משותפים זכאי בכל עת לדרוש פירוק השיתוף.

*Gist:* Every co-owner may demand dissolution at any time; a clause excluding dissolution for more than three years may be overridden by the court after three years if just. [Land Law s. 37](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_37)

Dissolution is by agreement; an agreed division in kind needs the superintendent's approval that it complies with planning law (s. 38(א)). Without agreement the court orders it under ss. 39–43:

- **Division in kind** for divisible land, with equalising payments if needed and easements between the new parcels (s. 39).
- **Sale and division of proceeds** if the land cannot be divided, or division would cause significant loss; the sale is by the execution-sale method unless the court finds a better one (s. 40).
- **Special arrangements:** a co-owner who owns adjoining land, groups of co-owners keeping their shares together, or some co-owners keeping the property and paying out the others (s. 41).
- **Conversion to a condominium** where the property is mainly a building fit to be registered as one, after the superintendent's opinion (s. 42).
- The court considers the co-owners' wishes, including a request by some to stay together (s. 43), and may give interim orders on use until dissolution (s. 44).

### The family home

> החליט בית המשפט לפי סעיף 40 על פירוק השיתוף במקרקעין משותפים שהם דירה של בני זוג המשמשת להם למגורים, בדרך של מכירה, לא יורה על ביצועה והמכירה תעוכב, כל עוד לא נוכח בית המשפט כי לילדי בני הזוג הקטינים ולבן הזוג המחזיק בהם, יחדיו, נמצא הסדר מגורים אחר המתאים לצרכיהם [...]

*Gist:* A sale of the spouses' home is delayed until the court is satisfied that the minor children and the parent who has them have suitable alternative housing. [Land Law s. 40א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_40א) The same rule applies to rights in a home that are not registered (Movable Property Law s. 10א).

A co-owner who builds on the common land without right lets any other co-owner demand dissolution despite any restriction (s. 46), and the dissolution then credits or charges him for the building (ss. 47–48). Movables follow parallel rules (Movable Property Law ss. 9–10).

## Leading cases

### דנ״א 1558/94 נפיסי נ׳ נפיסי (25.10.1996)

A couple married in Iran in 1944. The husband bought a shop in Tel Aviv in 1979, registered in his name, and after immigrating in 1983 deposited $320,000 in accounts in his name. A nine-justice further hearing applied the case-law presumption of community of assets (הלכת השיתוף) to the money, which was deposited after they settled in Israel, so the wife co-owned it, but not to the shop, where the husband's rights had vested before and a waiver required clear conduct. Justices Mazza and Tal dissented.

Why it matters: co-ownership of a family asset may exist even when only one spouse is registered. Between spouses married before 1974 the presumption of community still governs; for later marriages the Spouses (Property Relations) Law's balance of resources applies (module 18), and the courts have also recognised specific co-ownership of the family home in some cases. Read it in English at [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/nafisi-v-nafisi).

### Key text: dissolution as a near-absolute right

The case law treats s. 37(א) as giving each co-owner a right that the court will not refuse except in exceptional cases, such as a demand made in bad faith (Contracts Law s. 39 through s. 61(ב)) or the s. 40א protection. The debate is about *how*, not *whether*: whether to divide in kind, which co-owner may buy out the others, and whether the court should order a sale between the co-owners themselves (an internal auction) instead of an open sale. We could not verify a free full text of a leading ruling on this; rely on ss. 37–43 and the family-court practice in module 18.

## How it works in practice

### Which court

| Co-owners | Forum | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Spouses or family members (including siblings in many cases) | Family Affairs Court | Family Court Law; Courts Law s. 51(א)(5) |
| Unrelated co-owners | Magistrates' court (division of land is within its jurisdiction whatever the value) | Courts Law s. 51(א)(3) |
| Agreed division in kind | Superintendent's approval, then registration | s. 38(א) |

### Before going to court

1. Get an extract: the registered shares, mortgages and attachments.
2. Agree on a valuer, or get one each.
3. Offer a buy-out: one co-owner buys the others' shares at the agreed value (a sale between co-owners still needs a deed and taxes).
4. If you agree on a sale, sign a short agreement: price floor, agent, who lives there until sale, how proceeds and expenses are split, and reasonable payment for use (s. 33).

## Worked example

Three siblings, Fatma, Omar and Leila Daher, inherited their father's two-storey house in Shfar'am in equal shares. Omar has lived on the ground floor for six years without paying anything; Fatma lives abroad; Leila wants to sell. Omar refuses and has added a room on the roof without asking.

1. **Use and payment.** Omar may make reasonable use (s. 31(א)(1)), but he has had exclusive use. He owes Fatma and Leila reasonable payment for use by their shares (s. 33), subject to limitation (Prescription Law, [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) and to any agreement or waiver they can prove.
2. **The roof room.** Building is beyond ordinary management (s. 30(ג)), so it needed everyone's consent. Under s. 46 Leila may demand dissolution even if the siblings had agreed not to; the room will be credited to Omar only to the extent it raised the price (s. 48).
3. **Method.** Two floors with separate entrances may be fit for a condominium (s. 42): Omar could take the ground floor and pay equalising sums, or the house is sold (s. 40). The court considers the wish of some to stay together (s. 43).
4. **Forum.** Siblings in an inheritance dispute: often the family court; Leila checks before filing.
5. **Next step.** Leila writes a proposal (below): buy-out by Omar at a valuer's price within 90 days, or an agreed sale; payment for use from now on.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A shared property in your family or among friends (a flat, a plot, a car, a holiday home), or the Daher case.
- An extract, if it is land (lesson 13.2).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Map the shares (10 min).** Who owns what share? Is there a registered co-ownership agreement (s. 29)? Any mortgage or attachment on one share?
2. **Classify the current dispute (10 min).** Ordinary management (majority), beyond ordinary (unanimity), a single co-owner's urgent act, expenses, payment for use, or dissolution.
3. **Predict the method (10 min).** Divisible in kind? Fit for a condominium? Family home with minor children (s. 40א)?
4. **Write a proposal (15 min).** In Hebrew, one page, to your co-owners: the shares; what you propose (management rule, buy-out, agreed sale, or a co-ownership agreement); the valuation method; payment for use; a date for an answer; and that otherwise you will seek dissolution in the competent court. Use the structure of the [letter to a company template](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md) adapted to a private co-owner.

### Template

None specific; adapt the [demand letter](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md) structure.

### How you know it worked

- Your map shows shares, encumbrances and any agreement.
- You classified the dispute under the right section.
- Your proposal offers a concrete method and a valuation, not only a demand.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may negotiate and write to your co-owners, and you may file a dissolution claim in your own name: anyone may act for himself in the magistrates' and family courts (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170; [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)). Executing the dissolution (a sale, a deed, registration) involves lawyers and the registry.

### Where and how

- Magistrates' court (unrelated co-owners) or Family Affairs Court (family members); in the family court a request to settle a dispute (בקשה ליישוב סכסוך) usually comes first (module 18).
- Fees: as for a civil claim in that court ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: court information centres; Legal Aid for eligible people ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The property is a family home with minor children, or the co-ownership is part of a divorce.
- A co-owner has built, mortgaged his share or is in execution proceedings.
- The court has ordered a sale and a receiver (כונס נכסים) is appointed.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living in a shared home "for free" for years | A claim for reasonable payment for use (s. 33) | Agree a payment or a set-off now | Put use arrangements in writing |
| Building without all co-owners' consent | Dissolution despite any agreement (s. 46); no credit beyond the added value | Settle; get the others' written consent | Ask first; it is beyond ordinary management |
| Assuming a co-owner cannot sell his share | A stranger becomes your co-owner | Offer to buy; register a right of first refusal (s. 99) | Agree a first-refusal clause and register it |
| Refusing every offer and waiting for the court | Execution-style sale at a lower price | Agree on a sale method and price floor | Negotiate a buy-out early |
| Ignoring s. 40א in a divorce | A sale order that cannot be carried out | Arrange alternative housing | Plan housing for the children first |

## Review

- Co-owners hold undivided shares over the whole property, presumed equal (ss. 27–28).
- Ordinary matters need a majority, others unanimity; each owner may use reasonably, act urgently and must share expenses; exclusive use carries reasonable payment (ss. 30–33).
- Each co-owner may dissolve at any time (s. 37); courts prefer division in kind, then sale, with special methods and conversion to a condominium (ss. 39–43).
- The sale of a spouses' home is delayed until the children have suitable housing (s. 40א).
- Family co-ownership can exist without registration (Nafisi); family disputes go to the family court.
