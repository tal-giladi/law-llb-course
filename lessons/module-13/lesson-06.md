# 13.6 · Condominiums (בית משותף): bylaws, common property and the superintendent

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read a condominium's registration and bylaws, tell which parts of the building are yours alone, which are attached to your flat and which belong to everyone, work out what you must pay and which majority a decision needs, and decide where a dispute with a neighbour or the house committee goes. Most Israelis live in such buildings; most building disputes are decided under this chapter.

## Why it matters

A flat in an apartment building is a separate unit of ownership only because Chapter 6 of the Land Law says so. With it comes a share of the land, the roof, the stairwell and the shelter, and a set of rules: who pays for the lift, who may close a balcony, whether the parking spaces belong to particular flats, who may build on the roof. The Law also created a specialist tribunal: the **superintendent of land registration** (המפקח על רישום מקרקעין), with the powers of a magistrates' court judge, who decides most condominium disputes, often faster and with more flexible procedure than a court. Self-represented flat owners and house committees appear there routinely.

## The law

### What a condominium is and what the owner owns

> ”בית משותף“ – בית שיש בו שתי דירות או יותר והוא נרשם בפנקס הבתים המשותפים;

*Gist:* A condominium is a building with two or more flats **registered** in the condominium register. [Land Law s. 52](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_52)

> ”רכוש משותף“ – כל חלקי הבית המשותף חוץ מן החלקים הרשומים כדירות, ולרבות הקרקע, הגגות, הקירות החיצוניים, המסד, חדרי המדרגות, מעליות, מקלטים [...]

*Gist:* Everything not registered as a flat is common property, including the land, roofs, outer walls, foundations, stairwells, lifts, shelters and shared installations, even inside a flat. [s. 52](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_52)

Each flat is a separate object of ownership (s. 54), and an undivided share in the common property is attached to it; the share cannot be dealt with separately from the flat (s. 55(א)–(ב)). The share is by floor area unless the bylaws say otherwise (s. 57). The bylaws may **attach** a specific part of the common property (a roof, a garden, a parking space) to a flat, except stairwells, the lift, shelters and installations serving everyone; an attached part is treated as part of that flat (s. 55(ג)).

A building with two or more flats that is **not registered** as a condominium is still governed by most of these rules (ss. 77א–77ב), and the superintendent decides its disputes too.

### Bylaws

> בעלי הדירות רשאים לערוך תקנון ולשנות את הוראותיו ברוב של בעלי הדירות ששני שלישים של הרכוש המשותף צמודים לדירותיהם, אולם [...] אין לקבוע הצמדה של חלק מסויים מהרכוש המשותף לדירה פלונית אלא בהסכמת כל בעלי הדירות.

*Gist:* Owners holding two-thirds of the common property may adopt and change bylaws, but new rights, duties or payments of a kind the Law does not set need the affected owners' consent, and a new attachment needs all owners. [Land Law s. 62(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_62) Registered bylaws bind later owners (s. 62(ג)). Where no bylaws were registered, or they are silent, the **model bylaws** in the Schedule apply (s. 64).

### Expenses

> בעל דירה חייב להשתתף בהוצאות הדרושות להחזקתו התקינה ולניהולו של הרכוש המשותף ולהבטחת השירותים המחוייבים על פי דין או המקובלים על פי הנוהג, לפי יחס שטח רצפת דירתו אל שטח הרצפה של כל הדירות שבבית המשותף, זולת אם נקבע בתקנון שיעור השתתפות אחר;

*Gist:* Each owner pays for proper maintenance and management of the common property by the ratio of his floor area to the total, unless the bylaws set another ratio; "proper maintenance" means keeping the building as it was when completed, plus improvements agreed by the owners. [Land Law s. 58(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_58) In a building with several wings, expenses can be separated per wing (s. 59).

### Management

Every condominium has a representative committee (נציגות, s. 65) acting as the owners' agent for maintenance and management, able to contract and to sue in their name (s. 69). If none functions, the superintendent may appoint one, usually after trying to convene a general meeting (s. 67), and may convene a meeting that was not held (s. 70). Owners' decisions recorded in the decisions book bind present and future owners (s. 71(א)); a professional management company (מתחזק) may be engaged (s. 71(ב)).

### Changes to common property

Beyond s. 62, s. 71ב allows owners of **three-quarters of the flats** holding **two-thirds** of the common property to attach parts of it to a flat for an extension permitted under a valid plan; for building a protected space (ממ״ד) the majority is **60% of the flats**. An owner who claims substantial harm may sue the superintendent within a short period from the decision (s. 71ג(ב); see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). The Earthquake Strengthening Law 2008 (TAMA 38) and the Urban Renewal Law set their own majorities (year 3, module 27).

### The superintendent's jurisdiction

> (א) סכסוך בין בעלי דירות בבית משותף בדבר זכויותיהם או חובותיהם לפי התקנון או לפי סעיפים 58, 59, או 59א עד 59ג ו־59ה עד 59י [...] יכריע בו המפקח.
> (ב) סכסוך בין בעלי דירות בבית משותף בדבר הסגת גבול מצד בעל דירה לגבי דירה אחרת או לגבי הרכוש המשותף, רשאי התובע להביאו, לפי בחירתו, לפני בית משפט מוסמך או לפני המפקח.

*Gist:* Disputes about rights and duties under the bylaws and the expense sections go to the superintendent; disputes about trespass by an owner on another flat or on the common property may go to the superintendent or to a court, at the claimant's choice. [Land Law s. 72](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_72)

Any owner, another party to the dispute, or the representative committee may sue (s. 73). The superintendent has a magistrates' judge's powers (s. 74), may depart from the rules of evidence and procedure with recorded reasons (s. 75(א)), gives written reasoned decisions (s. 75(ג)) that are enforced like a magistrates' court judgment (s. 76), and is appealed to the district court; a trespass case under s. 72(ב) is heard on appeal by three judges (s. 77). The appeal period is in reg. 83 (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

## Leading cases

### רע״א 6339/97 רוקר נ׳ סלומון (23.12.1999)

Facts and outcome in [lesson 13.1](lesson-01.md). For condominium law the majority's rules are the core: a change in the common property requires the prior consent of the general meeting under the bylaws; building on common property without consent infringes the other owners' rights and permanently deprives them of use; a building permit from the planning committee does not authorise that infringement. Justice Englard's dissent (damages instead of demolition where enforcing the right would be an abuse) shows the limits debate.

Why it matters: in every "my neighbour closed the balcony / built on the roof / took the yard" dispute, the first question is consent under the bylaws and the Law, not the building permit. Planning and property are separate tracks. Read the English excerpts at [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/roker-v-solomon).

### Key text: the model bylaws (התקנון המצוי)

The Schedule to the Land Law is the default constitution of most Israeli buildings: the general meeting, the committee and its election, the treasurer's accounts (s. 16 of the model bylaws, also binding a professional manager under s. 71(ד)), and the rules on changes to the common property (s. 12 of the model bylaws). Read it once in full in the Schedule at [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין); it takes about 20 minutes and answers most practical questions.

## How it works in practice

### Reading a condominium file

1. Order a **concentrated extract** (נסח מרוכז) of the whole building: every sub-parcel, its owner, its share in the common property and its attachments (fee: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
2. Ask for the **registration order and the registered bylaws** from the registry (the condominium file, תיק בית משותף), and the **plan** (תשריט) showing each flat and attachment.
3. Compare with reality: is the roof attached to the top flat? Are the parking spaces attached or common? Has anyone built where the plan shows common property?

### Which forum for which dispute

| Dispute | Forum |
|---|---|
| A neighbour does not pay the house committee | Superintendent (s. 72(א), expenses under s. 58) |
| The committee spent money on something not "proper maintenance" | Superintendent (s. 58, bylaws) |
| A neighbour closed part of the common stairwell or took the roof | Superintendent or a court, at your choice (s. 72(ב)) |
| Water leaking from the neighbour's flat into yours | Not between owners about the bylaws: a court (tort, [lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)); a leak from common pipes is the committee's responsibility and may go to the superintendent |
| Owners' decision to attach the roof for extensions (s. 71ב) | Superintendent, within the s. 71ג period |
| Owners have an arbitration agreement | Arbitration remains possible (s. 72(ד)) |

## Worked example

In a 12-flat building in Holon, the committee decides at a general meeting (8 of 12 flats present) to replace the lift for ₪240,000, split equally among the flats. Ruth Ganor, who lives on the ground floor and has a 60 m² flat, refuses to pay her ₪20,000: "I never use the lift, and the old one worked." The building has registered bylaws that say nothing about the lift.

1. **What is the expense?** Replacing a working lift is maintenance only if the old one could no longer be kept in proper condition; otherwise it is an improvement, which binds only with the owners' consent (s. 58(א) definition).
2. **What is the ratio?** With no bylaw on the point, by floor area (s. 58(א)), not equally. If the total floor area is 1,080 m², Ruth's share is 60/1,080, about ₪13,300.
3. **Not using the lift?** The model bylaws and the Law tie expenses to floor area, not to use; ground-floor owners generally share lift costs unless the bylaws exempt them.
4. **Forum.** The committee may sue Ruth before the superintendent (ss. 72(א), 73(2)); Ruth may sue to challenge the split.
5. **Result.** If the committee proves the lift needed replacement (a safety report), Ruth pays by floor area. If it was an upgrade, the committee needs consent under the bylaws. The committee should correct the split to floor area before suing.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The building you live in (registered or not), or the Holon case.
- The concentrated extract, the registration order and bylaws, and the plan, if you can get them; otherwise the model bylaws.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Status (5 min).** Is your building registered as a condominium? (The extract shows sub-parcels if it is.)
2. **Your unit (10 min).** Your sub-parcel, floor area, share in the common property, and attachments.
3. **Common property (10 min).** List the roof, yard, parking, storage, shelter: for each, common or attached to which flat? Compare with what you see.
4. **Bylaws (10 min).** Registered or model? What do they say on expenses, committee elections and changes to the common property?
5. **Your share (5 min).** Compute your share of last year's maintenance budget by floor area, and compare with what you pay.
6. **One dispute (5 min).** Pick a real or likely dispute in your building and name the forum and the section.

### Template

None. Lesson 13.10 drafts a superintendent claim.

### How you know it worked

- You know which parts of the building are yours, attached, or common.
- You can quote the bylaw or section that governs expenses in your building.
- You computed your share and named the right forum for one dispute.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know that a new attachment needs all owners' consent (s. 62(א)).
- [ ] I can compute an expense share by floor area (s. 58).
- [ ] I can say which disputes go only to the superintendent and which may go to court.
- [ ] I know that a building permit does not authorise use of common property (Roker).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, as a flat owner. Any owner, party or the representative committee may bring a dispute to the superintendent (s. 73); acting for yourself is allowed, and the procedure applies the civil procedure rules with adaptations (regs 77–78). Representing another owner as an occupation or for pay is reserved to lawyers, since the superintendent has quasi-judicial authority (Bar Association Law s. 20(1)). Whether a committee member who is not a lawyer may appear for the committee: Unverified (⚠️ check with the superintendent's office).

### Where and how

- The superintendent's regional office for the land office where the building is or will be registered (reg. 79), using the claim form; see the [Ministry of Justice service page](https://www.gov.il/he/service/condominiums-supervisor-claims) (⚠️ manual check).
- Fee: as for a civil claim in the magistrates' court (Land Fees Regulations, Schedule; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut's page on claims to the superintendent (⚠️ manual check), court information centres ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The dispute is about building rights, TAMA 38 or urban renewal (big money, majorities and appraisals).
- A neighbour has built on common property and you want demolition: interim relief is urgent (Roker).
- The other side is a developer or a professional management company with a lawyer.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Splitting expenses equally by habit | Owners can challenge the split | Recalculate by floor area or the bylaws | Read s. 58 and the bylaws |
| Relying on a building permit to build on the roof | Demolition order (Roker) | Get the owners' consent under the bylaws | Consent first, permit second |
| Suing a neighbour over fees in small claims | Likely dismissal or transfer: s. 72(א) gives the superintendent the dispute | File with the superintendent | Check s. 72 before filing |
| Assuming the yard is "yours" because you use it | No right; others may use it | Check attachments in the extract and plan | Read the concentrated extract |
| Missing the period to challenge an extension decision | Decision stands | Ask the superintendent to extend for special reasons (s. 71ג(ב)) | Diary the date the decision reached you |

## Review

- A condominium is a registered building with flats as separate units; everything else is common property, with an undivided share attached to each flat (ss. 52–57).
- Bylaws are changed by two-thirds; new attachments need everyone; the model bylaws fill gaps (ss. 62–64).
- Expenses follow floor area unless the bylaws say otherwise, for proper maintenance only (s. 58).
- The committee manages and may sue; the superintendent can appoint one and convene meetings (ss. 65–71).
- The superintendent decides bylaw and expense disputes; trespass disputes may go to him or a court; his decisions are enforced like judgments and appealed to the district court (ss. 72–77).
