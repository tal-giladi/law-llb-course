# 13.1 · Property rights: ownership and its limits under the Land Law 1969 and the Movable Property Law 1971

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say what makes a right a property right in Israeli law, list the rights the Land Law and the Movable Property Law recognise, decide what passes with a flat when it is sold, and test an owner's conduct against the limits the law puts on ownership. You will then map the property rights in your own household.

## Why it matters

Most of the money a private person ever has is in property: a flat, a car, savings, a share in a family plot. Property law decides three things that contract law cannot. First, **who wins against third parties**: a buyer who has only a contract can lose to a bank, a creditor or a second buyer; a buyer who has a property right usually does not. Second, **what exactly you own**: the air above the roof, the solar heater, the garden behind the building. Third, **what you may do with it**: an owner may sell, lease and mortgage, but may not use ownership to harm a neighbour.

Property is also one of the subjects of the Israel Bar exam. The questions there are rarely about definitions; they are about priority (who comes first), about which right a person holds, and about the remedies of an owner or a possessor. This module builds that skill step by step, and the practice labs apply it to the two places where private people meet property law most: buying a flat and living in a condominium.

## The law

### Ownership of land

> הבעלות במקרקעין היא הזכות להחזיק במקרקעין, להשתמש בהם ולעשות בהם כל דבר וכל עסקה בכפוף להגבלות לפי דין או לפי הסכם.

*Gist:* Ownership is the right to possess, use and deal with land in any way, subject to limits set by law or by agreement. [Land Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_2)

The Law then defines the "lesser" rights an owner can carve out of ownership: lease (s. 3), mortgage (s. 4) and easement (s. 5). Chapter 7 adds the right of first refusal (זכות קדימה, ss. 99–106).

> שכירות לתקופה שלמעלה מחמש שנים תיקרא ”חכירה“; שכירות לתקופה שלמעלה מעשרים וחמש שנים תיקרא ”חכירה לדורות“.

*Gist:* A lease longer than five years is a חכירה; longer than 25 years, a "lease for generations", the form in which most Israel Land Authority land is held (lesson 13.2). [Land Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_3)

### What "land" includes: depth, height and fixtures

> ”מקרקעין“ – קרקע, כל הבנוי והנטוע עליה וכל דבר אחר המחובר אליה חיבור של קבע, זולת מחוברים הניתנים להפרדה;

*Gist:* Land is the ground plus everything built, planted or permanently attached to it, except attached things that can be separated. [Land Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_1)

> הבעלות בקרקע חלה גם על הבנוי והנטוע עליה ועל כל דבר אחר המחובר אליה חיבור של קבע, זולת מחוברים הניתנים להפרדה, ואין נפקא מינה אם המחוברים נבנו, ניטעו או חוברו בידי בעל המקרקעין או בידי אדם אחר.

*Gist:* Whoever owns the ground owns what is permanently attached to it, whoever attached it. [Land Law s. 12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_12)

Ownership extends down into the depth and up into the airspace (s. 11), subject to the laws on water, oil and minerals and to air traffic. Since amendment 33 (2018) a **three-dimensional parcel** (חלקה תלת־ממדית), such as an underground car park under a public road, can be registered as a separate unit of ownership (ss. 14א–14ח). A transaction in land covers the whole parcel with its fixtures; there is no transaction in a defined part of a parcel "unless the law says otherwise" (s. 13). The big exceptions are flats in a condominium (s. 54, lesson 13.6), leases of a defined part (s. 78) and easements over a part (s. 93(ג)).

### The limit inside ownership

> בעלות וזכויות אחרות במקרקעין, אין בהן כשלעצמן כדי להצדיק עשיית דבר הגורם נזק או אי נוחות לאחר.

*Gist:* Owning land does not in itself justify doing something that causes harm or discomfort to another. [Land Law s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_14)

### A closed list of property rights in land

> מתחילת חוק זה אין זכות במקרקעין אלא לפי חוק.

*Gist:* Since 1970 there is no right in land except one created by statute: the Ottoman and English categories, including English "equitable" rights, were abolished. [Land Law s. 161](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המקרקעין#סעיף_161)

### Movables

> הבעלות במיטלטלין היא הזכות להחזיק ולהשתמש בהם ולעשות בהם כל דבר וכל עסקה, בכפוף להגבלות לפי דין או לפי הסכם.

*Gist:* Ownership of movables mirrors ownership of land. [Movable Property Law s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המיטלטלין#סעיף_2)

Movables are "tangible property other than land" (s. 1), but s. 13(א) applies the Law, with adaptations, to **rights** too: a bank deposit, a debt, a share. A person acquires ownerless movables by taking them with intent to own (s. 3) and ownership ends when an owner gives up control intending to abandon (s. 7). Mixing and processing are covered in ss. 4–6, possession and its protection by reference to the Land Law (s. 8), co-ownership in ss. 9–10 and the lien (עיכבון) in s. 11; lesson 13.8 covers the rest.

### Property as a constitutional right

> אין פוגעים בקנינו של אדם.

*Gist:* A person's property may not be harmed; any harm must meet the limitation clause. [Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו#סעיף_3) The constitutional side (expropriation, planning, taxes) is in [lesson 06.4](../module-06/lesson-04.md).

## Leading cases

### רע״א 6339/97 רוקר נ׳ סלומון (23.12.1999)

A four-flat condominium in Haifa. The Rokers got a building permit and, during a two-month window with no injunction, built an extension into the common property over their neighbours' objection. A seven-justice panel denied their appeal and left in place the order to demolish and restore. The majority held that any change in the common property needs the owners' prior consent and that a building permit does not authorise infringing other owners' property rights. Justice Englard, dissenting, would have refused demolition and awarded damages: he relied on s. 14 of the Land Law and the Jewish-law principle כופין על מידת סדום ("one is compelled not to act like Sodom") to argue that insisting on demolition was an abuse of the property right.

Why it matters: the case is the leading Israeli debate on whether an owner's right can be cut down to damages when enforcing it causes the other side great loss and the owner little gain. The majority's answer, for a deliberate infringer, was no. Read the dissent and the reply in English at [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/roker-v-solomon); the full Hebrew ruling: search the case number on [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il).

### Key text: the theories behind ownership

University courses open with the justifications of property: Locke's labour theory, the utilitarian or economic account (property rights solve the "tragedy of the commons" and lower transaction costs; see Coase in [lesson 11.1](../module-11/lesson-01.md)), Hegel and Radin's personhood theory (a home is part of the self, a rental unit is fungible), and Hohfeld's analysis of property as a bundle of claims, liberties, powers and immunities. Israeli law reflects several of them at once: s. 2 lists a bundle of powers; s. 40א of the Land Law (lesson 13.5) delays the sale of a family home to protect children, a personhood idea; and s. 161 keeps the list of rights closed (numerus clausus), an economic idea, because buyers can only check a register if the possible rights are few and known.

## How it works in practice

### Property right or contract right?

| You have | Kind of right | Good against | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registered ownership of a flat | Property | Everyone | Your name on the נסח טאבו |
| Signed contract to buy a flat, not yet registered | Personal, protected by a caveat (lesson 13.4) | The seller; others only through s. 9 and the caveat | Before the transfer is registered |
| A residential lease of three years | Property-like (s. 3), no registration needed (s. 79) | The landlord, and generally a later buyer who takes the flat subject to it | Module 20 covers the Rental and Borrowing Law |
| A car registered in your name | Property in a movable | Everyone, subject to good-faith purchase rules (lesson 13.8) | |
| Money in a bank account | A right (debt) against the bank, treated as property for many purposes (s. 13) | The bank | |

### Fixtures when you buy or sell

Ask two questions: is the thing **permanently attached** (חיבור של קבע), and can it be **separated without real damage** (ניתן להפרדה)? Built-in kitchen cabinets, the solar water heater on the roof and an installed air conditioner are usually attached; whether they are "separable" is a question of fact. A sale contract should list them, because s. 12 is only a default.

## Worked example

Avraham and Shoshana Mizrahi from Kiryat Gat sell their ground-floor flat to Dina Abu-Hussein of Rahat. Two days before the handover the Mizrahis remove the kitchen cabinets, the split air conditioners, a lemon tree planted in the private garden and the external awning. The contract says "the flat as is, including fixtures" (כולל מחוברים).

1. **What is "land" here?** The cabinets are screwed to the walls and fitted to the plumbing; the tree is planted; the awning is bolted to the outer wall. All are attached. The question is whether they are "separable" (s. 1). Air conditioners can be removed with little damage: arguably separable movables. Cabinets fitted to the kitchen and a rooted tree are hard to separate without damage: part of the land.
2. **What did the contract add?** "Including fixtures" covers attached things; whether it covers the air conditioners depends on interpretation ([lesson 03.7](../module-03/lesson-07.md)): price, the listing photos and pre-contract messages matter.
3. **The garden.** If the garden is attached to the flat in the registered condominium bylaws (s. 55(ג)), it passes with the flat. If it is common property the Mizrahis only used, Dina gets no exclusive right to it (lesson 13.6).
4. **Result.** Dina can demand the return of the cabinets and the value of the tree as a breach of the sale contract, and can argue that the air conditioners were included by the contract's wording and photos. She writes a notice of breach ([template](../../templates/notice-of-breach-and-cancellation.md)) and withholds the part of the price held in trust for handover, if the contract allows.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A list of the main assets in your household, or the Mizrahi case.
- The Land Law ss. 1–14 and 161 and the Movable Property Law ss. 1–13 (links above).
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. List ten assets or rights you or your household hold: the flat (owned or rented), a parking space, a storage room, a car, a bank deposit, a pension fund, a share in an inherited plot, furniture, a lent item, a debt someone owes you.
2. For each, write: land, movable or right (s. 13 of the Movable Property Law).
3. Write which right you hold: ownership, lease (and its length: under 5 years, 5–25, over 25), mortgage, easement, co-ownership, or only a contract right.
4. Mark whether the right is good against everyone or only against one person, and what would protect it better (registration, a caveat, a written agreement).
5. For your home, list five items that are attached and decide, for each, whether it would pass on a sale under s. 12.
6. Write one paragraph: which of your rights is weakest against third parties, and why.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- Each of the ten items has a category and a right with a section number.
- You found at least one right that is only contractual and named how to strengthen it.
- Your fixtures list applies both tests (attached; separable).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Classifying your own rights and reading your own documents is something anyone may do. Drafting a property document for someone else, or advising others on it as an occupation, is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(3)–(4)).

### Where and how

The ideas show up whenever you deal with the Land Registry (lessons 13.2, 13.4, 13.9), a condominium (13.6, 13.10), a neighbour (13.7) or a second-hand purchase (13.8). Free full texts: Wikisource and the Knesset legislation database ([lesson 02.1](../module-02/lesson-01.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are about to sign anything that transfers, leases for more than five years, or mortgages land.
- A seller, a relative or a builder claims a right in "your" land that you did not know about.
- An item worth a lot (a building extension, a pool, a solar installation) is in dispute on a sale.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating a signed purchase contract as ownership | Losing to a creditor or second buyer | Register a caveat at once (13.4) | Caveat on signing; transfer as soon as possible |
| Assuming "the flat" includes the garden or roof | Paying for a right you do not get | Check the condominium bylaws and the נסח | Read the attachments (הצמדות) before signing |
| Removing fitted items before handover | Breach of contract, damages | Return the items or agree a price | List every fixture in the contract |
| Relying on an "equitable" or customary right in land | No such right since 1970 (s. 161) | Find a statutory basis (contract, s. 9, caveat) | Ask which statute creates the right |
| Treating ownership as a licence to disturb neighbours | Injunction and damages (s. 14; nuisance, [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)) | Stop and settle | Check neighbours' rights before building or using |

## Review

- A property right binds everyone; a contract right binds the other party only. Priority disputes turn on that difference.
- The Land Law recognises ownership, lease, mortgage, easement and the right of first refusal; since 1970 there is no right in land except under statute (s. 161).
- Land includes what is permanently attached, unless it can be separated (ss. 1, 12); a transaction covers the whole parcel unless the law allows a part (s. 13).
- Ownership is limited by law, by agreement and by s. 14; Roker shows the debate over cutting an owner's remedy down to damages.
- Property is a constitutional right (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 3).
