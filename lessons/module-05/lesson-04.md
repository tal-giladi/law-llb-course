# 05.4 · Defences: self-defence, necessity, duress, mistake, de minimis

By the end of this lesson you will be able to test a claimed defence element by element, explain why the law forgives some harmful acts, and judge, as a victim who fought back or as a complainant facing a "he attacked me first" story, how the defences are likely to be applied.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson explains the defences in the Penal Law so that you understand court decisions and can describe events accurately as a victim. It does not tell anyone how to use force or how to defend themselves in a criminal case. If you used force and the police want to question you, consult a defence lawyer before giving a statement.

In Israel the defences are statutory "exemptions from criminal liability" (סייגים לאחריות פלילית), set out in chapter ה1 of the Penal Law since 1994. They are where criminal law meets ethics: may you kill to save your life, steal to feed your child, break a law because you were threatened? They are also very practical. Many assault complaints are met with a counter-complaint ("he hit me first"), and the police then decide whom to charge. A victim who knows the elements of self-defence describes the events in the right order and with the right detail: who started, what danger there was, what was done, when it stopped.

## The law

### Self-defence

> לא יישא אדם באחריות פלילית למעשה שהיה דרוש באופן מיידי כדי להדוף תקיפה שלא כדין שנשקפה ממנה סכנה מוחשית של פגיעה בחייו, בחירותו, בגופו או ברכושו, שלו או של זולתו; ואולם, אין אדם פועל תוך הגנה עצמית מקום שהביא בהתנהגותו הפסולה לתקיפה תוך שהוא צופה מראש את אפשרות התפתחות הדברים.

*Gist:* No liability for an act that was **immediately necessary** to repel an **unlawful attack** posing a **real danger** to life, liberty, body or property, one's own or another's; but not for one who brought on the attack by wrongful conduct while foreseeing how things might develop. [Penal Law s. 34י](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34י)

The courts break this into conditions: an unlawful attack; a real (not imagined) danger; immediacy (the response is in time, not revenge after the danger passed); necessity (no less harmful way); proportionality (s. 34טז below); and the person did not bring on the attack.

### Home, business and fenced farm

> (א) לא יישא אדם באחריות פלילית למעשה שהיה דרוש באופן מיידי כדי להדוף מי שמתפרץ או נכנס לבית המגורים, בית העסק או המשק החקלאי המגודר, שלו או של זולתו, בכוונה לבצע עבירה, או מי שמנסה להתפרץ או להיכנס כאמור.
> (ב) הוראת סעיף קטן (א) לא תחול אם – (1) המעשה היה לא סביר בעליל, בנסיבות העניין, לשם הדיפת המתפרץ או הנכנס; [...]

*Gist:* Against a person breaking into a home, business or fenced farm to commit an offence, the defence is lost only if the response was **manifestly** unreasonable. [s. 34י1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34י1) This section was added in 2008 after public debate about farmers' self-defence against intruders; supporters saw it as restoring security at home, critics warned it lowers the value placed on an intruder's life.

### Necessity and duress

> לא יישא אדם באחריות פלילית למעשה שהיה דרוש באופן מיידי להצלת חייו, חירותו, גופו או רכושו, שלו או של זולתו, מסכנה מוחשית של פגיעה חמורה הנובעת ממצב דברים נתון בשעת המעשה, ולא היתה לו דרך אחרת אלא לעשותו.

*Gist:* Necessity covers an act immediately needed to save from a real danger of serious harm arising from the situation, where there was no other way. [s. 34יא](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34יא) Duress covers an act a person was **ordered** to do under a threat of serious harm (s. 34יב). Neither applies to a person whose duty is to face the danger (s. 34טו), nor to one who culpably put himself in the situation (s. 34יד).

> הוראות סעיפים 34י, 34יא ו־34יב לא יחולו כאשר המעשה לא היה סביר בנסיבות הענין לשם מניעת הפגיעה.

*Gist:* Self-defence, necessity and duress all fail when the act was not reasonable in the circumstances to prevent the harm. [s. 34טז](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34טז)

### Mistake

> (א) העושה מעשה בדמותו מצב דברים שאינו קיים, לא יישא באחריות פלילית אלא במידה שהיה נושא בה אילו היה המצב לאמיתו כפי שדימה אותו.

*Gist:* A person is judged on the facts as he honestly imagined them; for negligence offences the mistake must also be reasonable. [s. 34יח](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34יח) This is how "putative self-defence" works: someone who honestly believed he was being attacked.

> לענין האחריות הפלילית אין נפקה מינה אם האדם דימה שמעשהו אינו אסור, עקב טעות בדבר קיומו של איסור פלילי או בדבר הבנתו את האיסור, זולת אם הטעות היתה בלתי נמנעת באורח סביר.

*Gist:* Not knowing the law is no defence, unless the mistake was reasonably unavoidable. [s. 34יט](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34יט)

### De minimis, age and capacity

> לא יישא אדם באחריות פלילית למעשה, אם, לאור טיבו של המעשה, נסיבותיו, תוצאותיו והאינטרס הציבורי, המעשה הוא קל ערך.

*Gist:* A trivial act, judged by its nature, circumstances, results and the public interest, carries no criminal liability. [s. 34יז](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34יז)

Other exemptions: age under 12 (s. 34ו), lack of control over bodily movement (s. 34ז), insanity (s. 34ח), involuntary intoxication (s. 34ט), and justification: acting under law, a lawful order, emergency treatment, consensual medical treatment, and sport within its rules (s. 34יג). A reasonable doubt about any of them is enough (s. 34כב(ב)).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 5100/94 הוועד הציבורי נגד עינויים בישראל נ׳ ממשלת ישראל (1999)

A nine-justice panel held that the General Security Service had no statutory authority to use physical interrogation methods such as shaking, the "shabach" position and sleep deprivation for interrogation purposes. The State had relied on the necessity defence. The court held that necessity is a defence that an individual may raise after the fact if charged; it cannot serve as a source of advance authority for the State to adopt such methods as policy. Any such power would need express legislation.

Why it matters: it draws the line between a **defence** (forgiving an individual's act in an emergency) and a **power** (permission given in advance). That distinction runs through all the exemptions. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/303) (the site's short abstract is inaccurate; read the opinion or the summary in Tbeish below).

### בג״ץ 9018/17 טביש נ׳ היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (2018)

A detainee asked the High Court to order a criminal investigation of his interrogators. The court upheld the Attorney General's decision not to open one, finding reasonable the conclusion that the interrogators could rely on necessity because of a concrete, highly probable threat to life, no alternative, and proportionate means. It restated that no general authority can be founded on the necessity defence alone, and that prosecutors may take a defence into account when deciding whether to prosecute.

Why it matters: it shows how defences work **before trial**: the prosecution may close a file because a defence would clearly succeed. The ruling is contested: human-rights organisations argue it effectively allows ex ante approval of harsh methods; the State and the majority view it as applying the 1999 framework to a ticking-bomb case. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/380)

### ע״פ 746/14 ימר נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2016)

The appellant strangled and struck to death a man who had sexually assaulted him twice in the preceding weeks, after the man took him to a dark car park, demanded money and threatened to beat him. The Supreme Court rejected self-defence and putative self-defence: the immediacy and necessity conditions were not met, and his conduct after the man fell excluded an honest belief in danger. It accepted **cumulative provocation**, acquitted him of murder, convicted him of manslaughter (under the law before the 2019 reform), and reduced the sentence from 20 to 12 years. It kept the objective "ordinary person" test for provocation rather than a "reasonable victim of a sexual offence" test.

Why it matters: it shows the strict conditions of self-defence, and how the law mitigates rather than excuses when a person who was abused kills; the 2019 reform now gives this situation its own reduced offence (s. 301ב, lesson 05.5). [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/400)

## How it works in practice

### Who has to prove what

The prosecution must prove the offence; a defence applies if the evidence raises a reasonable doubt about it (s. 34כב(ב)), and the law presumes there was no defence unless the evidence suggests one (s. 34ה). In practice the defence is raised in the suspect's first statement or at trial, and the prosecutor weighs it before charging.

### De minimis in practice

Prosecutors close files for "lack of public interest" or treat minor matters with conditional arrangements; courts use s. 34יז rarely and only for truly trivial acts. A victim cannot expect a prosecution over a single shove that caused no harm, but repeated "minor" acts together are not trivial.

### Your own reaction as a victim

If you pushed or hit back, write it in your statement honestly and in sequence: what the other person did, what danger you felt, what you did, and when you stopped. Leaving it out and having it emerge from a video damages your credibility more than the act itself.

## Worked example

Shlomi Peretz, 41, a moshav farmer near Kiryat Shmona, wakes at 03:00 to noise in his fenced chicken coop. He sees a stranger loading crates into a van. He shouts; the stranger runs at him with a crowbar. Shlomi strikes him with a shovel, and the stranger falls and flees. The next day the stranger complains that Shlomi attacked him.

1. **Which defence?** Self-defence (s. 34י): an unlawful attack with a crowbar, real danger to body, immediate response. Also s. 34י1: an intruder in a fenced farm with intent to steal.
2. **Reasonableness.** One blow with a shovel against a crowbar charge is likely reasonable (s. 34טז), and under s. 34י1 only a manifestly unreasonable response loses the defence.
3. **What would change it?** If Shlomi had continued hitting the man on the ground, or chased him and struck him after he fled, immediacy and necessity would fail, as in Yamar.
4. **His complaint.** Shlomi files his own complaint first (theft, s. 384; trespass to commit an offence, s. 447; threat with a weapon), describing the sequence and stating that he struck once to stop the attack. He gives the police his security camera footage.
5. **Likely outcome.** The counter-complaint is likely closed; Shlomi should still consult a lawyer if he is summoned for questioning as a suspect.

**Scenario A.** Noa, a nurse in Afula, is followed home at night by a man who grabs her bag strap and pulls. She sprays pepper spray in his face; he lets go and falls. She then kicks him twice while he is on the ground and runs.

**Scenario B.** Ibrahim drives his pregnant wife, in labour, to hospital at 140 km/h on an empty road at night because the ambulance is 40 minutes away.

1. For each scenario, name the defence that fits best.
2. List its conditions and test each against the facts.
3. Mark any part of the conduct that falls outside the defence and say why.
4. Write the paragraph Noa should include in her complaint about the robbery attempt, describing her own acts accurately.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. As a victim you can and should describe your own reactions accurately in your complaint. Relying on a defence when you are a suspect is a matter for a defence lawyer.

### Where and how

- If you used force, keep all evidence (videos, medical records of **your** injuries, messages showing earlier threats).
- If a counter-complaint is filed against you, you may be summoned: ask for time to consult a lawyer; the Public Defender may represent you if you are charged and eligible ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are summoned for questioning after you used force.
- Someone was seriously injured or died.
- You acted under a threat (duress) and fear the person who threatened you.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hiding your own use of force | Credibility lost when video appears | Give a supplementary statement through a lawyer | Describe everything, in sequence |
| Continuing after the danger ended | Self-defence fails; you may be charged | Get a defence lawyer at once | Stop when the danger stops |
| "I didn't know it was illegal" | No defence (s. 34יט) | Argue only a reasonably unavoidable mistake | Check the law before acting |
| Treating necessity as permission | Liability; the defence is judged after the fact (PCATI) | Get legal advice | Look for lawful alternatives first |

## Review

- Defences are statutory exemptions; a reasonable doubt about one is enough (s. 34כב(ב)).
- Self-defence needs an unlawful attack, real danger, immediacy, necessity and reasonableness, and fails for one who provoked the attack; home, business and fenced-farm intrusions get a wider rule (s. 34י1).
- Necessity answers a danger from a situation; duress answers a threat ordering an act; both are limited by duty and prior fault.
- Mistake of fact is judged on the facts as honestly believed; mistake of law rarely helps.
- PCATI: a defence is not a power; Tbeish: prosecutors may weigh defences before charging; Yamar: no self-defence once the danger has passed, though provocation may mitigate.
