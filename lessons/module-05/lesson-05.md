# 05.5 · Core offences: against the person, property and public order

By the end of this lesson you will be able to match a set of facts to the right offence among the ones that ordinary people meet most often (violence, threats, theft, fraud, damage, trespass and public disorder), read their elements and maximum penalties, and check whether the offence is one that a private person may prosecute alone.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson describes offences so that victims can name what happened to them and understand charges. It contains no guidance on committing or concealing any act. If you are in danger now, call the police (100) or, for violence at home, the police and the welfare services; this lesson is not an emergency guide.

The special part of the Penal Law has hundreds of offences; a handful make up most police files. Choosing the right one matters: it decides the class of the offence (lesson 05.1), the limitation period, whether the police must investigate, which victims' rights apply (some apply only to "sexual or violent offences" listed in the Crime Victims' Rights Law), and whether you may file a private criminal complaint if the State does not act (lesson 05.8).

The homicide chapter was rewritten in 2019, which replaced the old murder and manslaughter scheme with a graded structure. University courses spend much time on it because it shows how the general part (mental element, defences, provocation) is built into specific offences.

## The law

### Homicide after the 2019 reform

> (א) הגורם בכוונה או באדישות למותו של אדם, דינו – מאסר עולם.

*Gist:* Murder is causing death with intent **or indifference**; the penalty is up to life imprisonment. [Penal Law s. 300](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_300)

Murder in aggravating circumstances (s. 301א) carries mandatory life imprisonment and lists circumstances such as planning, killing to enable another offence, killing a witness, racist motive, killing a spouse after systematic abuse, special cruelty, a helpless victim or a child under 14, and terrorism; the court may still convict of basic murder if special circumstances show the act does not reflect especially severe culpability (s. 301א(ב)). [s. 301א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_301א)

Killing in circumstances of reduced responsibility (s. 301ב) carries 15 or 20 years: after severe and prolonged abuse of the killer or a family member by the victim; immediately after provocation that substantially impaired self-control; with severely limited mental capacity; or slightly exceeding self-defence, necessity or duress. Killing by rashness (s. 301ג) carries 12 years, and causing death by negligence (s. 304) three years.

A 2026 amendment added s. 301א(ג), allowing the death penalty or life imprisonment for an intentional killing in a terrorist act aimed at denying the existence of the State of Israel. Supporters argue it expresses the gravity of terror murder and deters; opponents, including legal scholars, argue it is unconstitutional, conflicts with Israel's international commitments and will not deter. Its status before the courts should be checked (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) and the Knesset database).

### Bodily harm and assault

> המכה אדם, נוגע בו, דוחפו או מפעיל על גופו כוח בדרך אחרת, במישרין או בעקיפין, בלא הסכמתו או בהסכמתו שהושגה בתרמית – הרי זו תקיפה; [...]

*Gist:* Assault is any application of force to another's body without consent (or with consent obtained by fraud), including heat, light, electricity, gas or smell. [s. 378](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_378)

| Offence | Section | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Assault | 379 | 2 years |
| Assault causing actual bodily harm | 380 | 3 years |
| Wounding | 334 | 3 years |
| Grievous harm | 333 | 7 years |
| Harm with aggravated intent | 329 | 20 years |
| Assault of a public servant or service provider at work | 382א | 3 years (5 in listed cases) |
| Assault of a family member | 382(ב)–(ג) | double the basic penalty |

### Threats

> המאיים על אדם בכל דרך שהיא בפגיעה שלא כדין בגופו, בחירותו, בנכסיו, בשמו הטוב או בפרנסתו, שלו או של אדם אחר, בכוונה להפחיד את האדם או להקניטו, דינו – מאסר שלוש שנים.

*Gist:* Threatening unlawful harm to a person's body, liberty, property, good name or livelihood (or another's), with intent to frighten or harass. [s. 192](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_192)

### Property

> (א) אדם גונב דבר אם הוא – (1) נוטל ונושא דבר הניתן להיגנב, בלי הסכמת הבעל, במרמה ובלי תביעת זכות בתום לב, כשהוא מתכוון בשעת הנטילה לשלול את הדבר מבעלו שלילת קבע; [...]

*Gist:* Theft is taking and carrying away a thing without the owner's consent, fraudulently and without a good-faith claim of right, intending to deprive the owner permanently; it also covers misappropriation by one who held the thing lawfully. [s. 383](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_383) Penalty: three years (s. 384), more in special circumstances (s. 384א), by an employee (s. 391) or for vehicles (s. 413ב, seven years).

- **Fraud:** "המקבל דבר במרמה, דינו – מאסר שלוש שנים", five years in aggravating circumstances. [s. 415](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_415)
- **Malicious damage:** destroying or damaging property wilfully and unlawfully, three years. [s. 452](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_452)
- **Entering a dwelling or place of worship to steal or commit a felony:** five years; breaking in, seven. [s. 406](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_406)
- **Trespass to commit an offence or to frighten, insult or harass the occupier:** two years; four if armed. [s. 447](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_447)

### Public order

Riot and insult in a public place (s. 194, three months), disorderly conduct in a public place likely to cause a breach of the peace (s. 216, six months), forcible entry onto land (s. 189) and offensive trades causing great noise or smells (s. 223). [s. 194](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_194)

### Which of these can a private person prosecute?

> עבירות לפי סעיפים 189, 190, 192, 194, 196, 223, 334, 336 רישה, 379, 380, 447 זולת אם נעברה העבירה כשהעבריין נושא נשק חם או קר, 452, 494 ו־496 לחוק העונשין [...] ולמעט עבירות כאמור, שנעברו כלפי בן משפחה [...]

*Gist:* The Second Schedule allows a private criminal complaint for, among others, threats, wounding, assault and assault causing harm, trespass (unarmed), malicious damage and public-place insult, except where committed against a family member; it also lists offences under the Protection of Privacy Law, copyright, trademarks, nuisance and others. [CPL Second Schedule](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#תוספת_2) Theft and fraud are **not** on the list.

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 10828/03 נג׳אר נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2005)

The appellant stabbed his unmarried sister to death because, he said, she intended to travel alone to Egypt and had questioned his paternity of his children, and he asked the court to take into account his community's view of family honour so as to reduce the offence from murder to manslaughter (provocation). The Supreme Court (Justices Rubinstein, Naor and Adiel) dismissed the appeal: "family honour" is not a recognised ground to mitigate a killing, and the victim's dignity and the sanctity of life take precedence.

Why it matters: it addresses whether the objective test of provocation should reflect a defendant's culture. The court refused to let a norm that devalues women's lives lower criminal responsibility. Under the 2019 reform, provocation mitigates only when it substantially impaired self-control **and** the difficulty mitigates culpability in all the circumstances (s. 301ב(ב)(1)), which keeps this evaluative check. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/280)

### Yamar (lesson 05.4)

The same provocation doctrine, applied with the opposite result: prolonged sexual abuse of the defendant by the victim supported "cumulative provocation". Read the two cases together to see where the line falls. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/400)

## How it works in practice

### Choosing between neighbouring offences

| Facts | Offence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A slap, no injury | Assault (s. 379) | Force without consent, no harm |
| A slap that bruises | Assault causing harm (s. 380) | "חבלה" includes passing pain or injury (s. 34כד) |
| A cut from a broken bottle | Wounding (s. 334) or more | Skin broken |
| "I'll break your legs" by text | Threats (s. 192) | Unlawful harm to body, intent to frighten |
| Shouted insults in a mall | Insult in a public place (s. 194(ב)) | Only if likely to provoke a breach of the peace |
| Seller takes payment and never meant to deliver | Fraud (s. 415) | Deceit at the start |
| Seller meant to deliver but went bankrupt | Civil breach | No deceit, no offence |
| Ex-partner keys your car | Malicious damage (s. 452) | Wilful and unlawful damage |

### Victims' rights depend on the offence

The Crime Victims' Rights Law gives some rights to every victim and others only to victims of offences in its First Schedule ("sexual or violent offences") or the most serious ones. Naming the offence correctly in your complaint helps you claim them (lesson 05.7).

## Worked example

Daniel Levi, 29, a student in Jerusalem, has a neighbour, Moshe, with whom he has argued about parking. One evening Moshe blocks his car, shouts "next time I'll smash your face" in front of other residents, and kicks Daniel's car door, leaving a dent. Daniel photographs the dent and records part of the shouting.

1. **Threats (s. 192):** threatening unlawful harm to Daniel's body with apparent intent to frighten; the recording and witnesses support it. Misdemeanour.
2. **Malicious damage (s. 452):** kicking the door and denting it, wilfully and unlawfully. Misdemeanour.
3. **Insult in a public place (s. 194(ב)):** probably not; the building's car park may be a public place, but the threat is the stronger charge.
4. **Second Schedule check:** ss. 192 and 452 are both listed, and Moshe is not a family member. If the police close the file, Daniel can appeal (lesson 05.8) or file a private criminal complaint.
5. **Civil route in parallel:** a small claim for the repair cost (module 8).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Three incidents from your life, your family's or detailed news reports, or the list below.
- The Penal Law and the CPL Second Schedule on Wikisource.
- 45 minutes.

Incidents: (a) a co-worker takes ₪500 from the shared till and admits it; (b) someone sells you concert tickets online, takes payment and blocks you; (c) a man spits at a bus driver after an argument; (d) an ex-partner shares an intimate photo of you in a WhatsApp group.

### Steps

1. Name the most likely offence for each, with section and maximum penalty.
2. Name one neighbouring offence and say why it fits less well.
3. Classify each (felony, misdemeanour, contravention).
4. Check the Second Schedule: may you file a private criminal complaint? Mark exceptions (family member, weapon).
5. For (d), check the Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law and the Protection of Privacy Law and record which law the offence is in.

### Template

None. The table you build goes into the project.

### How you know it worked

- Each incident has an offence with a section, a class and a Second Schedule answer.
- You distinguished fraud from breach of contract and assault from assault causing harm.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Naming the offence is part of your own complaint as a victim (lesson 05.7); the police and prosecution decide the final charge.

### Where and how

- Emergencies: police 100. Non-urgent complaints: any police station; some offences online (lesson 05.7).
- For intimate images shared without consent, also ask the platform to remove them and contact a victims' support organisation; Legal Aid covers some victims (lesson 05.7).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The offence is serious violence, sexual violence, domestic violence or a death: ask for a victims' lawyer (Legal Aid covers some of these without a means test).
- The incident is part of a wider dispute (business partner, family property) where criminal and civil claims interact.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calling every insult a threat | Complaint closed: insult is not unlawful harm | Report the specific threatened harm, if any | Ask: what harm did he say he would do? |
| No medical record of a bruise | Assault causing harm cannot be proved | Get a doctor's report now; photos with dates | See a doctor the same day |
| Calling a failed business "fraud" | Closed as civil | File a civil claim | Look for deceit at the start |
| Assuming any offence can be a private complaint | Private complaint dismissed | Check the Second Schedule | Read it before planning |

## Review

- After 2019: murder (intent or indifference), aggravated murder (mandatory life), reduced-responsibility killing, reckless homicide and negligent homicide.
- Assault ladder: assault (379), with harm (380), wounding (334), grievous harm (333), aggravated intent (329); family and public-service victims carry higher penalties.
- Theft needs taking without consent and intent to deprive permanently; fraud needs deceit; failure to pay later is usually civil.
- The Second Schedule lists the offences a private person may prosecute (threats, assault, wounding, trespass, malicious damage, insult and others, not against family members).
- Najar: family honour does not mitigate; provocation is judged by an objective, evaluative test.
