# 05.7 · Practice lab: filing a police complaint as a victim and using the Crime Victims' Rights Law

In this lab you prepare a police complaint the way a careful victim does: facts in order, every participant named, evidence listed and secured, the offence identified, and a written request for the rights the Crime Victims' Rights Law gives you. You finish with a complaint ready to file (or a dry-run file for the supplied case) and a follow-up record.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lab is for **victims** of crime. If you are in danger now, call the police on 100. If you are a suspect, or you fear a counter-complaint, do not use this lab: consult a defence lawyer. Never file a complaint you know to be false: a false report is itself an offence.

A complaint is the start of almost every criminal case in Israel, and its first version is often decisive. Investigators work from what you said on day one; contradictions with later statements are used by the defence. A complaint that names the offence, the participants and the evidence, and asks for footage to be secured at once, is more likely to be investigated and less likely to be closed for "insufficient evidence".

Since 2001 the victim is also recognised as a person with rights in the process, not only a witness. Most rights must be **asked for**: the police and prosecution inform you of the stages only if you request it (s. 8(ג)). A victim who knows this asks on day one.

## The law

### Anyone may complain; the police must investigate felonies

> כל אדם רשאי להגיש תלונה למשטרה על שבוצעה עבירה.

*Gist:* Anyone may file a complaint with the police about an offence. [CPL s. 58](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_58)

> נודע למשטרה על ביצוע עבירה, אם על פי תלונה ואם בכל דרך אחרת, תפתח בחקירה; אולם בעבירה שאינה פשע רשאי קצין משטרה בדרגת פקד ומעלה להורות שלא לחקור אם היה סבור שנסיבות העניין בכללותן אינן מתאימות לפתיחה בחקירה או אם היתה רשות אחרת מוסמכת על פי דין לחקור בעבירה.

*Gist:* Once the police learn of an offence they must investigate; only for non-felonies may an officer of the rank of pakad (inspector) or above decide not to, because the circumstances as a whole do not suit an investigation or another authority is competent. [CPL s. 59](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_59) For sexual or violent misdemeanours against a partner, the victim's request not to investigate cannot by itself be the only reason, and special approval is needed (s. 59א).

> (א)(1) על החלטה שלא לחקור תימסר למתלונן הודעה בכתב בציון עילת סגירת התיק.

*Gist:* A decision not to investigate must be sent to the complainant in writing, **with the reason**. [CPL s. 63](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_63) That notice starts the appeal period (lesson 05.8).

### The victims' rights framework

> חוק זה מטרתו לקבוע את זכויותיו של נפגע עבירה ולהגן על כבודו כאדם, בלי לפגוע בזכויותיהם על פי דין של חשודים, נאשמים ונידונים.

*Gist:* The law sets victims' rights and protects their dignity, without harming the legal rights of suspects, accused and convicted persons. [Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_נפגעי_עבירה#סעיף_1)

A "victim" is a person directly harmed by an offence, or a family member of a person whose death it caused (s. 2); the law covers felonies and misdemeanours investigated by the police and prosecuted by a prosecutor, but not most traffic offences. The rights are given "with consideration for the victim and his needs, respecting his dignity and privacy, and within a reasonable time" (s. 3).

### The rights you will claim in this lab

> (ג) נוסף על הוראות סעיף קטן (ב) – (1) נפגע עבירה שביקש זאת זכאי לכך שהגופים המפורטים בתוספת השניה יידעו אותו על שלבים בהליך הפלילי בקשר לעבירה שממנה נפגע, כמפורט באותה תוספת; [...]

*Gist:* A victim who asks is entitled to be told of the main stages: transfer to the prosecutor, a decision not to investigate or not to prosecute (with the right to appeal), release from arrest, indictment, hearing dates, verdict, sentence and appeal. [s. 8(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_נפגעי_עבירה#סעיף_8) Victims of serious sexual or violent offences may also ask for further details, including about plea bargains (s. 8(ג)(2), s. 17).

| Right | Section | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Protection from the suspect, and information on protection options | 6 | Every victim |
| Personal details (address, workplace, phone) withheld from the defence | 7 | Serious sexual or violent offences; others at the prosecutor's discretion |
| Information on rights, the process and stages | 8 | Every victim (stages on request) |
| A copy of the indictment | 9 | Every victim, on request |
| Information on support services | 11 | Every victim |
| An accompanying person at your questioning | 14 | Sexual or violent offences |
| Choosing the sex of the investigator | 14א | Sexual offences |
| A view on plea bargains or closure arrangements | 17 | Serious sexual or violent offences, on notice |
| A written victim statement before sentencing | 18 | Every victim |

Breach of a right does not in itself invalidate the criminal proceeding or give a damages claim against the authority, but can lead to disciplinary action (s. 21). The police appoint officers responsible for victims' rights (s. 24), and the prosecution has victims' assistance units (s. 23).

### Free legal help for some victims

The Legal Aid Law's schedule gives free representation, without a means test, to victims of listed sexual offences in exercising their rights in the criminal process (from the investigation stage under a temporary order from 30 April 2025), and to family members of homicide victims. [Legal Aid Law, schedule](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסיוע_המשפטי)

## Leading cases

This lab applies statutes rather than case law. One ruling frames how the police and prosecutor see your complaint:

- **Adalah v. Attorney General, HCJ 3292/07 (2011):** a demand for a criminal investigation must rest on a suitable prima facie foundation; opening an investigation is not automatic whenever the outcome is grave. A complaint that sets out concrete facts and evidence meets this test; one that relies on outrage alone does not. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/24)

## How it works in practice

### Where and how to file

- **In person** at any police station, usually as an oral statement typed by an officer, which you read and sign. Bring your written complaint (the template) and copies of evidence; ask that it be attached to the file.
- **Online** for some offence types through the police's digital services (check the current list on the police website; anything urgent goes to 100).
- **Emergencies:** call 100.
- Ask for a **confirmation of the complaint** with the file number (פל״א).

### Before you go

1. Write the facts in time order, short lines, with exact words in quotation marks.
2. Separate what you saw from what you heard from others.
3. List every participant and their role (lesson 05.3).
4. List evidence and who holds it; ask in writing for **footage to be secured** at once (shops, buses, municipal cameras).
5. Get medical treatment and a report on the same day if you were hurt.
6. Identify the likely offence and its class (lessons 05.1, 05.5).

### After filing

- Diary: the date of filing, the file number, the officer's name.
- If you hear nothing for some weeks, write to the station's officer responsible for victims' rights asking for the stage of the case (s. 8(ב)).
- A notice that the police decided **not to investigate** or the prosecutor decided **not to prosecute** opens the appeal route in lesson 05.8. Keep the envelope or email showing the date you received it.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- An offence you actually suffered and want to report, **or** the supplied case (dry run; do not file anything for the supplied case).
- The [police complaint and victims' rights request](../../templates/police-complaint-victim.md) template.
- Your elements table from lesson 05.2 and your offence table from lesson 05.5.
- 2.5 hours: 40 minutes for facts and participants, 30 for evidence, 30 for the complaint, 20 for the rights request, 30 for review and the follow-up plan.

**Supplied case.** Sapir Ohana, 31, a nurse from Be'er Ya'akov, has received over three weeks 14 WhatsApp messages from a former colleague, Eli, after she refused to go out with him. They include "את תצטערי על זה" and, on 9 October 2026, "אני יודע איפה את חונה, הצמיגים שלך לא יחזיקו הרבה". On 12 October she found two tyres slashed in the hospital car park. The hospital has cameras over the car park. She has screenshots, the tyre-shop invoice (₪1,180) and a colleague who heard Eli say he would "teach her a lesson".

### Steps

1. **Facts.** Write a dated, numbered chronology. Quote the exact words of each message that matters.
2. **Offences.** Identify the offences and their class: threats (s. 192); malicious damage (s. 452); consider the Prevention of Sexual Harassment Law (repeated unwanted propositions after refusal) and stalking offences, and record which law each is in.
3. **Participants.** Who did what? If the camera may show another person, say so.
4. **Evidence.** List each item, who holds it and whether it is at risk (camera footage); draft a one-line request to secure footage.
5. **Fill part A** of the template (the complaint).
6. **Fill part B** (rights). Decide which rights apply: are threats and malicious damage "sexual or violent offences" under the First Schedule? (Threats, s. 192, is listed; malicious damage is not.) Is a sexual-harassment offence listed?
7. **Safety.** Decide whether to ask for her address to be withheld (s. 7), and whether to ask the police about protection options (s. 6).
8. **Follow-up plan.** Dates to check the file, the officer to write to, what to do on receiving a closure notice (lesson 05.8).

### How you know it worked

- A stranger can read your complaint in two minutes and know what happened, when, who did it and what evidence exists.
- Every right you requested is one that applies to the offence.
- Your follow-up plan has dates.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Any person may complain to the police (CPL s. 58), and the rights in the Crime Victims' Rights Law belong to the victim personally. You need no lawyer to file a complaint or to ask for your rights.

### Where and how

- Any police station, or online for some offences; 100 for emergencies.
- The station's officer responsible for victims' rights (s. 24); the prosecution's victims' assistance unit (s. 23).
- Free help: victims of sexual offences and families of homicide victims through Legal Aid ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)); [Kol-Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il) (search "נפגעי עבירה"); support organisations listed by the police and the Ministry of Justice.
- Domestic violence: you may also ask the Family Affairs Court or magistrates' court for a protection order under the Prevention of Family Violence Law 1991.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The offence is sexual, involves serious violence or a death (free Legal Aid may be available).
- You fear for your safety and need a protection order or restraining conditions quickly.
- The suspect has filed a counter-complaint against you.
- The offender is a public official or police officer: complaints against police officers go to the Police Investigations Department in the Ministry of Justice, not to the police.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Telling the story with no dates or exact words | Weak complaint, contradictions later | Give a supplementary statement with a chronology | Write the chronology before you go |
| Not asking to be informed (s. 8(ג)) | You never hear that the file was closed; appeal deadline missed | Write to the victims' rights officer now | Fill part B on day one |
| Waiting a week to ask for footage | Footage overwritten | Ask the camera owner directly too | Ask in writing on day one |
| Exaggerating to "make it serious" | Credibility destroyed; possible false-report offence | Correct it promptly in writing | Report only what you know |
| Complaining about a police officer at the police station | Wrong body | Complain to the Police Investigations Department | Check who investigates police |

## Review

- Anyone may complain (s. 58); the police must investigate felonies and may decline only non-felonies, by an authorised officer, with written reasons to you (ss. 59, 63).
- The Crime Victims' Rights Law gives every victim information, a copy of the indictment and a victim statement; serious sexual or violent offences add protection of details, a voice on plea bargains and more.
- Most rights must be requested; ask in writing on day one and keep the file number.
- A strong complaint is a dated chronology with participants, evidence and a request to secure at-risk evidence.
- Free legal help exists for victims of sexual offences and families of homicide victims.
