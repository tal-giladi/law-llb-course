# 05.3 · Attempt, accomplices and inchoate offences

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell when an unfinished crime is already punishable, sort the people involved in a crime into the roles the Penal Law recognises, and describe each of them accurately in a complaint, so that a lookout, a driver or the person who sent the attacker is not left out.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson explains how the law extends liability to attempts, helpers and plans, so that victims can describe what happened and understand charges. It is not a guide to anyone's defence. A person who may be involved in an offence should consult a defence lawyer.

Many crimes against ordinary people are unfinished or committed by groups: a burglar caught at the door, a fraud ring with a caller, a courier and a bank-account holder, a fight in which one person holds the victim while another hits him. If you complain only about "the one who hit me", the others may never be investigated. If you assume that nothing happened because the burglar fled before taking anything, you may not complain at all, though attempted burglary is a crime.

Inchoate offences (attempt, inducement, conspiracy) also raise a classic theoretical debate: why punish someone who has not yet harmed anyone? The answers (dangerousness, culpable intent, the need for police to intervene in time) shape where the line falls between thought, preparation and attempt.

## The law

### Attempt

> אדם מנסה לעבור עבירה אם, במטרה לבצעה, עשה מעשה שאין בו הכנה בלבד והעבירה לא הושלמה.

*Gist:* An attempt needs the **purpose** of committing the offence and an act that goes beyond mere preparation, while the offence is not completed. [Penal Law s. 25](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_25)

> לענין נסיון, אין נפקה מינה אם עשיית העבירה לא היתה אפשרית מחמת מצב דברים שהמנסה לא היה מודע לו או טעה לגביו.

*Gist:* An impossible attempt is still an attempt: picking an empty pocket counts. [s. 26](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_26)

Mandatory and minimum penalties do not apply to attempts (s. 27), but otherwise an attempt carries the penalty of the completed offence. A person who, of his own free will and out of remorse, stops or substantially helps to prevent the result is exempt from liability for the attempt, though not for any completed offence on the way (s. 28). [s. 28](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_28)

### Principals

> (ב) המשתתפים בביצוע עבירה תוך עשיית מעשים לביצועה, הם מבצעים בצוותא, ואין נפקה מינה אם כל המעשים נעשו ביחד, או אם נעשו מקצתם בידי אחד ומקצתם בידי אחר.

*Gist:* People who take part in committing an offence by doing acts towards it are joint principals, even if each did only part of the acts. [s. 29(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_29) A person who uses another as a tool (a child, a person without intent, a person acting under duress or mistake) is a perpetrator by means of another (s. 29(ג)).

### Inducers and accessories

> המביא אחר לידי עשיית עבירה בשכנוע, בעידוד, בדרישה, בהפצרה או בכל דרך שיש בה משום הפעלת לחץ, הוא משדל לדבר עבירה.

*Gist:* An inducer is one who brings another to commit an offence by persuasion, encouragement, demand or pressure. [s. 30](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_30)

> מי אשר, לפני עשיית העבירה או בשעת עשייתה, עשה מעשה כדי לאפשר את הביצוע, להקל עליו או לאבטח אותו, או למנוע את תפיסת המבצע, גילוי העבירה או שללה [...] הוא מסייע.

*Gist:* An accessory acts before or during the offence to enable, ease or secure it, or to prevent the offender's capture or the discovery of the offence or its loot. [s. 31](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_31) The accessory's penalty is half the maximum for the offence, with special rules for life and mandatory sentences (s. 32). Help given only **after** the offence is a separate offence (for example, being an accessory after the fact, s. 260), not s. 31.

Attempt, inducement, attempted inducement and aiding are **not punishable for contraventions** (s. 34ג). If one participant commits a different or additional offence that a reasonable person could have foreseen, the others may be liable for it too, at a reduced mental level (s. 34א).

### Conspiracy and the duty to prevent a felony

> (א) הקושר קשר עם אדם לעשות פשע או עוון [...] דינו – (1) אם העבירה היא פשע – מאסר שבע שנים או העונש שנקבע לאותה עבירה, הכל לפי העונש הקל יותר; [...]

*Gist:* Agreeing with another to commit a felony or misdemeanour is itself an offence; the agreement completes it, and liability for the target offence follows only if the conspirator was a party to it under the complicity rules (s. 499(ב)). [s. 499](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_499)

Knowing that someone plans a felony and not taking all reasonable means to prevent it is an offence (s. 262, lesson 05.2).

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 4596/05 רוזנשטיין נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2005)

The United States asked to extradite Ze'ev Rosenstein for conspiracy to import drugs into the US and distribute them there. The Supreme Court upheld the declaration that he was extraditable. It found that the conspiracy was completed in Israel, while its intended effects and its "centre of gravity" lay in the United States, so both countries had jurisdiction, and the place where the appellant physically acted was of little importance.

Why it matters: it illustrates that conspiracy is an offence complete at the agreement, before any drug moves, and that an inchoate offence can create jurisdiction in more than one country. For a victim of an organised fraud run from abroad, this is why the Israeli police may still act. [English translation (abstract)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/319)

### ע״פ 3417/99 הר־שפי נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2001)

Covered in lesson 05.2: a conviction for failing to prevent a felony (s. 262). Here it matters for a different reason: s. 262 punishes a person who is **not** a participant at all, neither principal nor accessory, for staying silent with knowledge of a planned felony. It marks the outer edge of liability around an offence. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/184)

### The line between joint principal and accessory

Israeli case law separates joint principals from accessories mainly by the participant's control over the offence and his place in the plan: a joint principal belongs to the "inner circle" that shares the decision and the execution, while an accessory stays in the "outer circle", helping someone else's offence. The distinction halves the maximum penalty (s. 32). Criminal-law textbooks discuss it at length; the statutory basis is ss. 29 and 31 above.

## How it works in practice

### From preparation to attempt

There is no formula. Courts look at how close the act was to completion and whether it unambiguously shows the purpose. Buying gloves is preparation; breaking the lock of a flat's door is an attempted break-in; arriving at the meeting with a forged cheque in hand and presenting it is an attempted fraud.

### Naming every participant in a complaint

| Role | What to tell the police |
|---|---|
| Principal | Who did the act; description, name if known |
| Joint principal | Who acted together; who held, who hit, who took |
| Inducer | Who sent or pressured the doer (messages, payments) |
| Accessory | Lookout, driver, the person who lent a bank account or a SIM card |
| Conspiracy | Evidence of a plan before the act (chats, calls) |

### Withdrawal does not undo harm

If a thief stopped halfway out of remorse, he is exempt only for the attempt; damage to your door is still malicious damage (s. 452), a completed offence (s. 28).

## Worked example

Yulia Morozova, 58, from Ashdod, receives a call from a man claiming to be from her bank's fraud department. He tells her to transfer ₪20,000 to a "safe account". She becomes suspicious and calls her bank branch before transferring. The bank says the call was fraud. The next day her neighbour's son admits he gave his bank account details to "a friend" for ₪500, knowing it would be used "to move money".

1. **Was there an offence even though she lost nothing?** Yes: an attempt to obtain by deceit (ss. 25, 415). The caller acted with purpose and went beyond preparation (he made the call and the demand). Her suspicion made it fail, which does not matter.
2. **Is the impossibility relevant?** No: even if her account had been empty, s. 26 makes it an attempt.
3. **The neighbour's son:** he gave his account to enable the transfer, knowing its purpose. That is aiding (s. 31), and possibly also an offence under the money-laundering law; his role is described separately in the complaint.
4. **The "friend":** if he recruited account holders and passed them to the callers, he may be a joint principal or an accessory, depending on his place in the scheme; a conspiracy charge is also possible (s. 499).
5. **Complaint:** Yulia writes one complaint naming the caller (phone number and time of call), the account details she was given, and the neighbour's son as a possible accessory, attaching the call log and the bank's confirmation.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The supplied case, or a group incident you know of (from your life or a detailed news report).
- 40 minutes.

**Supplied case.** At 02:00 in Beersheba, three young men approach a parked car. One (A) watches the street, one (B) breaks the window, and one (C), who planned it, waits in a car with the engine running. A neighbour shouts, and they run before taking anything. Earlier that evening, C texted A and B: "Tonight, the white Mazda, I'll drive."

### Steps

1. Decide whether the conduct went beyond preparation and name the offence attempted (vehicle offences, ss. 413ב–413ו; check the current text).
2. Name any completed offence (the broken window).
3. Classify A, B and C, with the section for each, and explain the classification in one sentence.
4. Decide whether conspiracy applies and what evidence shows it.
5. Write the "participants" paragraph of a complaint by the car owner.

### Template

None. The participants paragraph goes into the complaint template in lesson 05.7.

### How you know it worked

- You found both an attempt and at least one completed offence.
- Each participant has a role, a section and a reason.

Breaking the window of a specific car at night, with a lookout and a getaway driver ready, is well beyond preparation: an attempted car theft (s. 25 with s. 413ב) or at least an attempt to steal from a car (s. 413ד); breaking into a car is itself a completed offence (s. 413ו), and the broken window is also malicious damage (s. 452). ## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Describing participants accurately is part of your own complaint (lesson 05.7); you never decide who is charged, the prosecution does.

### Where and how

- Fraud by phone or online: complain to the police, and tell your bank at once so it can try to stop the transfer.
- Group violence: list each person's acts separately and give the police any video.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You were asked to "lend" your bank account, SIM card or car to someone and you now fear it was used for a crime: you may be a suspect; get a defence lawyer.
- The fraud is large and involves companies or people abroad: a lawyer can also pursue civil recovery.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not complaining because "nothing was taken" | An attempt goes uninvestigated | Complain, describing the attempt | Remember s. 25 and s. 26 |
| Naming only the person who hit you | Helpers and planners are never questioned | Add a supplementary statement naming each role | Use the participants table |
| Treating help after the crime as aiding | Wrong legal framing in your letters | Describe the facts; let the prosecutor choose the section | Remember s. 31 covers help before or during the act |
| Deleting the chat that showed the plan | Conspiracy cannot be proved | Ask the police to recover it from the other side's phone | Preserve everything; screenshot with dates |

## Review

- Attempt = purpose + an act beyond preparation + no completion (s. 25); impossibility does not matter (s. 26); voluntary withdrawal exempts only from the attempt (s. 28).
- Joint principals share the execution (s. 29); inducers bring another to offend (s. 30); accessories help before or during (s. 31) and face half the maximum (s. 32).
- None of these extensions apply to contraventions (s. 34ג).
- Conspiracy is complete at the agreement (s. 499; Rosenstein); failing to prevent a known planned felony is a separate offence (s. 262; Har-Shefi).
- A complaint should name every participant and the evidence of each one's role.
