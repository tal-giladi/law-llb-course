# 05.1 · Principles: legality, culpability and the general part of the Penal Law 1977

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read any Israeli offence through the principles that frame all of criminal law: no offence without a law, no punishment without fault, doubt works for the accused, and an unclear criminal provision is read in the way most lenient to the person charged. You will also classify offences by seriousness, which decides how long the State has to prosecute, whether attempt and complicity are punishable, and which procedure applies.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This module deals with crime, police and prosecution. It teaches the law and how a **victim** uses it. If you are a suspect, or you have been summoned for questioning, do not rely on this course: ask to consult a lawyer before you answer, and see lesson 01.6 on the Public Defender.

Criminal law is the sharpest tool the State has: it can take liberty, reputation and livelihood. That is why it is bound by principles that civil law does not need. A contract can be enforced by analogy, by good faith or by an implied term; a person cannot be convicted by analogy. A tort plaintiff wins on the balance of probabilities; the prosecution must prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt.

For a victim the principles matter just as much. They explain why the police say "this is not a criminal offence, it is a civil matter", why a file is closed for "insufficient evidence" even when you are sure of what happened, and why an offence committed long ago may no longer be prosecutable. Knowing the principles lets you write a complaint that names a real offence and survives the prosecutor's first reading.

Israel's general part was rewritten in 1994 (Amendment 39 to the Penal Law), which replaced the Mandate-era Criminal Code Ordinance 1936 approach with a modern, codified general part: definitions of the mental element, attempt, complicity and defences. Most university courses (TAU 1411-9109, HUJI 62114) spend half the semester on this general part, and the Bar exam tests it.

## The law

### No offence and no punishment without law

> אין עבירה ואין עונש עליה אלא אם כן נקבעו בחוק או על־פיו.

*Gist:* An act is an offence, and can be punished, only if a law or a regulation made under a law says so. [Penal Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_1)

Section 2 limits offences created by regulations: imprisonment set in a regulation may not exceed six months, and regulations creating offences need approval by a Knesset committee (except local by-laws). [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_2)

### No retroactivity; the lenient law applies

> (א) חיקוק היוצר עבירה לא יחול על מעשה שנעשה לפני יום פרסומו כדין או יום תחילתו, לפי המאוחר.

*Gist:* A new offence does not apply to acts done before it was published or came into force, whichever is later; the same goes for a heavier penalty (s. 3(ב)). [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_3)

> (א) נעברה עבירה ובטרם ניתן פסק־דין חלוט לגביה, חל שינוי בנוגע להגדרתה או לאחריות לה, או בנוגע לעונש שנקבע לה, יחול על הענין החיקוק המקל עם העושה [...]

*Gist:* If the law changes after the act but before a final judgment, the version more lenient to the accused applies; if the offence is repealed altogether, liability ends (s. 4). [s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_5)

### Strict (lenient) construction

> ניתן דין לפירושים סבירים אחדים לפי תכליתו, יוכרע הענין לפי הפירוש המקל ביותר עם מי שאמור לשאת באחריות פלילית לפי אותו דין.

*Gist:* Interpret a criminal provision by its purpose first; only if several reasonable readings remain does the one most lenient to the accused win. [s. 34כא](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34כא)

### Fault and doubt

> אדם מבצע עבירה רק אם עשאה במחשבה פלילית, זולת אם – (1) נקבע בהגדרת העבירה כי רשלנות היא היסוד הנפשי הדרוש לשם התהוותה; או (2) העבירה היא מסוג העבירות של אחריות קפידה.

*Gist:* The default mental element is criminal intent (awareness); negligence or strict liability apply only where the offence says so. [s. 19](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_19)

> (א) לא יישא אדם באחריות פלילית לעבירה אלא אם כן היא הוכחה מעבר לספק סביר.
> (ב) התעורר ספק סביר שמא קיים סייג לאחריות פלילית, והספק לא הוסר, יחול הסייג.

*Gist:* Guilt must be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and a reasonable doubt about a defence is enough for the defence to apply. [s. 34כב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34כב)

### Three classes of offence

> (1) "פשע" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש חמור ממאסר לתקופה של שלוש שנים;
> (2) "עוון" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש מאסר לתקופה העולה על שלושה חודשים ושאינה עולה על שלוש שנים; [...]
> (3) "חטא" – עבירה שנקבע לה עונש מאסר לתקופה שאינה עולה על שלושה חודשים [...]

*Gist:* Offences are felonies (maximum above three years), misdemeanours (above three months up to three years) or contraventions (up to three months), classified by the **maximum** penalty in the law, not the sentence actually given. [s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_24)

The general part applies to offences in other laws too, unless they say otherwise (s. 34כג). [s. 34כג](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_34כג)

## Leading cases

### דנ״פ 8613/96 מדינת ישראל נ׳ ג׳בארין (2000)

A journalist was convicted of publishing words of praise or encouragement for acts of violence under s. 4(א) of the Prevention of Terrorism Ordinance 1948. In a further hearing, the majority (Justice Or) read the provision narrowly: it covers praise of acts of violence **of a terrorist organisation**, and the article did not concern such acts, so the conviction was set aside. Three justices (Vice-President Levin, Justices Kedmi and Mazza) would have upheld the conviction on a wider reading.

Why it matters: it shows the tension at the heart of criminal interpretation. Purposive interpretation (lesson 02.4) applies to criminal statutes too, but when a speech offence can be read widely or narrowly, both freedom of expression and s. 34כא push toward the narrower reading. The split also shows that "the purpose" is itself contested: the dissent saw the purpose as fighting any terror-like violence. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/363)

### The theory behind the principles

Israeli textbooks (and the explanatory notes to the 1994 general part) present legality as serving three values: fair warning to citizens, separation of powers (the Knesset, not the courts, decides what is criminal), and limiting State power. The culpability principle reflects the idea that punishment presupposes a free and blameworthy choice; that is why strict liability (s. 22) is the exception, and why even then a person who acted without intent or negligence and did everything possible to prevent the offence is not liable, and no one may be imprisoned without proof of intent or negligence (s. 22(ב)–(ג)). The text is in the Penal Law itself: [s. 22](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_22).

## How it works in practice

### Reading an offence: the five questions

1. **Where is it?** The Penal Law, or another statute (traffic, planning, privacy, consumer)? The general part applies to both (s. 34כג).
2. **What is the current text?** Use the Wikisource consolidation and check its date line against the Knesset database.
3. **What is the maximum penalty?** That decides the class (s. 24).
4. **What is the mental element?** Intent by default (s. 19); negligence or strict liability only if stated.
5. **When was the act?** Check whether the provision was in force then (s. 3) and whether a later, lenient change applies (s. 5).

### What the classification changes

| Felony (פשע) | Misdemeanour (עוון) | Contravention (חטא) |
|---|---|---|
| Longest limitation periods; attempt and complicity punishable | Attempt and complicity punishable | Attempt, inducement and aiding are **not** punishable (s. 34ג) |
| Police must open an investigation when they learn of it (CPL s. 59) | A police officer may decide not to investigate in listed circumstances (CPL s. 59) | Same as misdemeanour |

Limitation periods for prosecution are set by Criminal Procedure Law s. 9 (with no limitation for murder) and are listed in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). The clock can restart with investigative steps, so a victim should never assume that an old file is dead without asking.

### Where the principles meet a victim's complaint

- The police can only investigate an **offence**. A complaint that describes only a broken promise or an unpaid debt will be closed as "not a criminal matter" (אין עבירה פלילית), and the route is civil (modules 3 and 8).
- The prosecutor must believe guilt can be proved beyond reasonable doubt. "I believe him, but the evidence is thin" ends in closure for insufficient evidence (lesson 05.8).

## Worked example

Tamar Asefa, 34, from Kiryat Gat, receives a WhatsApp message in March 2026 from a former business partner: "I'll make sure everyone in the community knows what you did, and you'll lose the shop." She wants to know whether this is a crime and what kind.

1. **Find the candidate offence.** Threats, Penal Law s. 192: threatening a person with unlawful harm to body, liberty, property, **good name or livelihood**, with intent to frighten or harass.
2. **Check the text and date.** The section has been in force for decades; no retroactivity problem.
3. **Classify it.** Maximum three years' imprisonment: not "more than three years", so it is a **misdemeanour**, not a felony.
4. **Mental element.** Intent to frighten or harass, a purpose requirement; the context (a business dispute) matters.
5. **Interpretation.** Is threatening to tell the truth about someone an "unlawful" harm to their good name? Courts read "unlawful" carefully; telling the community true facts may not be unlawful, while spreading lies would be. The doubt is resolved by purpose first, and only then leniently (s. 34כא).
6. **Practical result.** Tamar can file a police complaint. Because threats is a misdemeanour, the police may decide not to investigate in suitable cases (CPL s. 59), and threats against a non-family member is one of the offences in the Second Schedule for which a private criminal complaint is possible (lesson 05.8).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Three incidents: one from your life or news you know well, and two from the list below.
- The Penal Law on Wikisource and a notebook or spreadsheet.
- 45 minutes.

Incidents to choose from: (a) a neighbour scratches your car with a key; (b) a customer shouts insults at a cashier in a supermarket; (c) a landlord keeps your deposit; (d) someone posts your private photos without consent.

### Steps

1. For each incident, write the facts in three sentences.
2. Search the Penal Law for a matching offence (use the table of contents and your browser's find). For (d), check whether the Protection of Privacy Law fits better, and note that its offences are in another statute.
3. Copy the section number and its maximum penalty; note the date line of the page you used.
4. Classify each offence under s. 24.
5. Write one sentence on the mental element (intent, negligence or strict liability).
6. Mark any incident that is not a crime at all (c is usually civil) and name the civil route.

### Template

None. Lesson 05.7 gives the complaint template that will use this analysis.

### How you know it worked

- Every incident has a section number, a maximum penalty and a class, or a reasoned "not a crime".
- You can say which incident cannot be attempted or aided (contraventions, s. 34ג).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Reading statutes and classifying an offence is something anyone may do for themselves; it is the first step of every complaint (lesson 05.7) and private criminal complaint (lesson 05.8).

### Where and how

- Text: the Penal Law and the Criminal Procedure Law on Wikisource; the Knesset National Legislation Database for the official version.
- Limitation periods and other deadlines: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Plain-language guides: [Kol-Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il) (search "נפגעי עבירה").

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are, or may become, a **suspect** in any matter: get a defence lawyer or ask for the Public Defender.
- The question is whether an old act is still prosecutable and the file had investigative steps you do not know about.
- The conduct may be both a crime and a large civil claim (fraud, embezzlement by a partner).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classifying by the sentence actually given | Wrong limitation period and wrong conclusions about attempt | Reclassify by the maximum in the section | Always read the penalty line of the section |
| Treating a breach of contract as fraud | Complaint closed as "not a criminal matter" | Separate the civil claim; complain only if there was deceit at the start | Ask: was there a lie when the deal was made? |
| Quoting an outdated version of an offence | The prosecutor dismisses the complaint's analysis | Check the date line and the Knesset database | Record the version and date you used |
| Assuming any doubt means acquittal | Unrealistic expectations | Distinguish reasonable doubt from any doubt | Read s. 34כב with its "reasonable" |

## Review

- No offence or punishment without law (s. 1), no retroactivity (s. 3), the lenient change applies (s. 5), and an unclear provision is read leniently only after purposive interpretation (s. 34כא).
- The default fault requirement is awareness (s. 19); negligence and strict liability are exceptions.
- Guilt must be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and a reasonable doubt about a defence is enough (s. 34כב).
- Offences are felonies, misdemeanours or contraventions by maximum penalty (s. 24); the class affects limitation, attempt and complicity, and police discretion to investigate.
- Jabarin shows how purpose, free speech and leniency interact, and that judges disagree.
