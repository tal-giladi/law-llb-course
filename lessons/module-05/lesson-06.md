# 05.6 · Sentencing: structuring discretion (Amendment 113)

By the end of this lesson you will be able to follow a sentencing decision step by step as Israeli courts must write it since 2012, tell which facts raise or lower the range and which only move the sentence within it, and prepare the one document in which a victim speaks directly to the sentencing court: the victim statement.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson explains sentencing law for understanding and for victims who want to take part in the process. It is not advice to a defendant on a plea or sentence; a defendant needs a defence lawyer.

Before 2012 Israeli law set only maximum penalties, and judges balanced retribution, deterrence and rehabilitation as they saw fit. Critics pointed to inconsistent sentences for similar crimes. Amendment 113 to the Penal Law (2012) did not introduce sentencing guidelines with numbers, as in the United States; instead it "structured" discretion: it named a leading principle, ordered the steps and required reasons for each. Judges now must set an **appropriate range** (מתחם העונש ההולם) and justify where the defendant falls within it.

For victims, sentencing is where their harm is formally weighed. The prosecutor brings the victim statement to the court; the court may order the offender to pay the victim compensation; and in serious sexual and violent cases the victim may voice a view on a plea bargain before it is signed.

## The law

### The guiding principle

> העיקרון המנחה בענישה הוא קיומו של יחס הולם בין חומרת מעשה העבירה בנסיבותיו ומידת אשמו של הנאשם ובין סוג ומידת העונש המוטל עליו [...]

*Gist:* The guiding principle is proportionality between the gravity of the offence in its circumstances, together with the defendant's culpability, and the type and severity of the sentence. [Penal Law s. 40ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_40ב)

### Two stages: the range, then the sentence

> (א) בית המשפט יקבע מתחם עונש הולם למעשה העבירה שביצע הנאשם בהתאם לעיקרון המנחה, ולשם כך יתחשב בערך החברתי שנפגע מביצוע העבירה, במידת הפגיעה בו, במדיניות הענישה הנהוגה ובנסיבות הקשורות בביצוע העבירה כאמור בסעיף 40ט.
> (ב) בתוך מתחם העונש ההולם יגזור בית המשפט את העונש המתאים לנאשם, בהתחשב בנסיבות שאינן קשורות בביצוע העבירה כאמור בסעיף 40יא, ואולם בית המשפט רשאי לחרוג ממתחם העונש ההולם בשל שיקולי שיקום או הגנה על שלום הציבור [...]

*Gist:* First the court sets a range for the offence, considering the social value harmed, the degree of harm, the usual sentencing policy and the circumstances of the offence; then it chooses the sentence within the range based on circumstances unrelated to the offence; it may leave the range only for rehabilitation or public safety. [s. 40ג](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_40ג)

### Circumstances of the offence (they shape the range)

Section 40ט lists, among others: planning; the defendant's relative part; the harm expected and caused; the reasons for the offence; capacity to understand; provocation by the victim; mental distress from abuse by the victim; closeness to a defence; and, as aggravating factors, cruelty or abuse of the victim and abuse of power or of the relationship with the victim. [s. 40ט](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_40ט)

### Circumstances unrelated to the offence (they place the sentence within the range)

> (4) נטילת האחריות של הנאשם על מעשיו, וחזרתו למוטב או מאמציו לחזור למוטב;
> (5) מאמצי הנאשם לתיקון תוצאות העבירה ולפיצוי על הנזק שנגרם בשלה;
> (6) שיתוף הפעולה של הנאשם עם רשויות אכיפת החוק; ואולם כפירה באשמה וניהול משפט על ידי הנאשם לא ייזקפו לחובתו;

*Gist:* Within the range the court may weigh, among others, the effect of punishment on the defendant and family, acceptance of responsibility, efforts to repair and compensate, cooperation (though denying guilt and going to trial may not count against him), positive conduct, hard life circumstances, conduct of the authorities, passage of time and criminal record. [s. 40יא](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_40יא)

### Departing from the range

Rehabilitation (s. 40ד) allows going below the range where the defendant has been rehabilitated or has a real chance of it, but for especially grave offences only in special and exceptional circumstances. Protection of the public (s. 40ה) allows going above it, but not substantially, and only with a significant criminal record or a professional opinion. Individual and general deterrence may be weighed only **within** the range (ss. 40ו–40ז). Several offences forming one "event" get one range; several events get a range each (s. 40יג). Every step must be reasoned (s. 40יד). [s. 40יד](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_40יד)

### The victim's place

> (א) נפגע עבירה זכאי למסור הצהרה בכתב לגוף החוקר או לתובע, על כל פגיעה ונזק שנגרמו לו בשל העבירה, לרבות נזק גוף, נזק נפשי או נזק לרכוש; מסר הנפגע הצהרה כאמור, זכאי הוא שהתובע יביא את הצהרתו לפני בית המשפט בדיון בענין גזר דינו של הנאשם [...]

*Gist:* Every victim may give the police or prosecutor a written statement of the harm caused, and the prosecutor must bring it before the court at sentencing. [Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_נפגעי_עבירה#סעיף_18)

> (א) הורשע אדם, רשאי בית המשפט לחייבו, בשל כל אחת מן העבירות שהורשע בהן, לשלם לאדם שניזוק על ידי העבירה סכום שלא יעלה על [...] לפיצוי הנזק או הסבל שנגרם לו.

*Gist:* On conviction the court may order the offender to pay the person harmed compensation for damage or suffering, up to a statutory ceiling per offence (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); it is collected like a fine. [Penal Law s. 77](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_77)

Victims of serious sexual or violent offences are entitled, on request, to be told about a plea bargain being negotiated and to express their view to the prosecutor before it is decided (Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 17), and the court must check that this right was respected (s. 17(ד)).

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 8641/12 סעד נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2013)

The leading early decision on Amendment 113. The Supreme Court set out the order of the sentencing steps: first decide whether several offences form one event or several; then set an appropriate range for each event using the circumstances of the offence; then place the sentence within the range using the unrelated circumstances; then consider departing for rehabilitation or public safety. It also stressed that firearm violence calls for strict sentences. Later judgments cite it for this structure. No free full text was found for this course; search the case number at the Supreme Court's decisions site.

Why it matters: the order is not cosmetic. A judge who mixes rehabilitation into setting the range, or uses deterrence to go above it, errs in law and can be corrected on appeal.

### דנ״פ 1187/03 מדינת ישראל נ׳ פרץ (2005)

A nine-justice panel considered what the prosecution should do on appeal when a trial court rejected a plea bargain and imposed a harsher sentence. The court held that, as a rule, the prosecution should defend the plea bargain on appeal, departing from it only in exceptional cases where the public interest overrides the considerations that support keeping the State's word.

Why it matters: plea bargains decide most Israeli criminal cases. The ruling protects the reliability of the State's promises to defendants; it is also why victims' rights to be heard come **before** the bargain is made (s. 17), not after. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/367)

## How it works in practice

### Reading a sentencing decision

Look for these headings or paragraphs in order: the facts and conviction; the victim statement (if any); the probation officer's report (תסקיר שירות המבחן); the arguments for sentence; **the appropriate range** with reasons; **the sentence within the range**; departure (if any); the operative part (imprisonment, community-service imprisonment, suspended sentence, fine, **compensation to the victim**).

### The victim statement in practice

- You give it to the investigating officer or the prosecutor, in writing, at any stage before sentencing; ask the victims' rights officer at the station or the prosecution's victims' assistance unit how to submit it.
- It should describe the harm: physical, emotional, financial, effects on work, family and daily life, with documents attached.
- It is not a place to argue guilt or to demand a specific sentence; it informs the court. The defence will see it.

### Compensation under s. 77 and civil claims

Compensation in the criminal case is quick but capped and rough; a civil claim (tort, module 4) can be filed for full damages. For appeal purposes the s. 77 order is treated like a civil judgment of the same court (s. 78); tell the civil court about any compensation already ordered so that the same loss is not claimed twice.

## Worked example

Hila Ben-David, 45, a teacher from Netivot, was attacked in a car park by a man who snatched her bag and pushed her to the ground; she broke a wrist and has been afraid to go out alone since. He was charged with robbery and pleaded guilty under a plea bargain.

1. **Hila's rights.** Robbery is a serious violent offence; she should have been told of the plea bargain and allowed to express her view to the prosecutor (s. 17). If she was not, she can write to the prosecutor and the court will ask about it (s. 17(ד)).
2. **Her statement (s. 18).** She describes the fracture and surgery, eight weeks off work, the loss of income, physiotherapy costs, the fear, sleep problems and her daughter now escorting her; she attaches medical and salary records.
3. **The court's steps.** Range: robbery with injury to a vulnerable target in a public place, low planning: a range set by sentencing policy for similar cases. Within the range: the defendant's guilty plea and acceptance of responsibility, no compensation paid yet, a prior record. Departure: none unless a strong rehabilitation report.
4. **Compensation.** The prosecutor asks for compensation under s. 77, using Hila's documents.
5. **After sentencing.** If the compensation does not cover her losses, she can sue in tort for the rest.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- An incident in which you were harmed by a crime, or the supplied case.
- 50 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Mahmoud Aziz, 60, from Umm al-Fahm, owns a small grocery. A youth broke in at night, stole the cash drawer (₪2,300) and damaged the door (₪4,000 repair). The shop was closed for two days. Mahmoud now sleeps badly and installed cameras for ₪6,000. The youth, 19, has no record and has started a rehabilitation programme.

### Steps

1. List the circumstances of the offence (s. 40ט) and those unrelated to it (s. 40יא) in two columns.
2. Mark which ones the victim can document, and how.
3. Write a one-page victim statement in Hebrew: who you are, what happened (two lines), physical, emotional and financial harm with numbers, effect on daily life, attachments.
4. Add a short paragraph asking the prosecutor to request compensation under s. 77, listing the amounts.
5. Note whether a rehabilitation departure (s. 40ד) is likely and how the statement can still be useful if it happens.

### How you know it worked

- Your statement describes harm with documents, not opinions about guilt or the sentence length.
- Every sum is supported by an attachment.
- You can explain to someone else which facts affect the range and which only the position within it.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own victim statement and requests to the prosecutor about your rights: the Crime Victims' Rights Law gives these rights to the victim personally. You are not a party to the criminal case and do not argue the sentence in court; the prosecutor represents the public.

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- Submit the statement to the investigating officer or the prosecutor handling the file; keep proof of delivery.
- Ask the prosecution's victims' assistance unit (Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 23) and the police officer responsible for victims' rights (s. 24) for information about the stage of the case.
- Victims of sexual offences, and family members of homicide victims, can get free Legal Aid representation for their rights in the criminal process (Legal Aid Law, schedule; see lesson 05.7).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The offence was sexual or a homicide (free Legal Aid may be available).
- You want to challenge a plea bargain you were not told about.
- Your losses are large and you plan a civil claim alongside the criminal case.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement argues guilt or demands "maximum punishment" | Less weight; may help the defence | Submit a revised, factual statement | Describe harm only |
| No documents for financial loss | Low or no compensation | Send receipts to the prosecutor before sentencing | Keep every receipt from day one |
| Waiting until the sentencing hearing | Statement not before the court | Ask the prosecutor at once | Submit early and update |
| Expecting s. 77 to cover everything | Unpaid losses | File a civil claim for the rest | Plan both routes from the start |

## Review

- Amendment 113 makes proportionality the guiding principle and structures sentencing into a range (offence circumstances) and a position within it (other circumstances).
- Departures: below the range for rehabilitation, above it modestly for public safety; deterrence only within the range; reasons for every step.
- Sa'ad set the order of steps; Peretz protects plea bargains on appeal.
- Victims speak through a written statement (s. 18), can be heard on plea bargains in serious cases (s. 17) and can receive compensation (s. 77).
- A good victim statement is factual, documented and about harm.
