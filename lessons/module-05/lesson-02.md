# 05.2 · The elements of an offence: conduct, circumstances, result, mental element

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take any offence apart into the pieces the prosecution must prove, name the mental state each piece requires, and turn a messy story into an elements table: the tool prosecutors, judges and good complainants all use.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson analyses offences so that you can understand charges and write an accurate complaint as a victim. It is not a guide for a suspect. If you may be suspected of an offence, consult a defence lawyer before speaking to the police.

Every conviction is a match between evidence and elements. If one element is missing, the charge fails, however bad the conduct looks. A victim who understands elements writes a complaint that gives the police what they must prove (the date, the act, the injury, the words used, what the offender knew) instead of a page of anger. A student who understands elements can answer most criminal-law exam questions, which are built as "find the missing element".

## The law

### The factual element

> (א) "פרט", לענין עבירה – המעשה בהתאם להגדרתה, וכן נסיבה או תוצאה שנגרמה על ידי המעשה, מקום שהן נמנות עם הגדרת אותה עבירה.
> (ב) "מעשה" – לרבות מחדל, אם לא נאמר אחרת.
> (ג) "מחדל" – הימנעות מעשייה שהיא חובה לפי כל דין או חוזה.

*Gist:* An offence is made of "particulars": the conduct, plus any circumstances and results in its definition; conduct includes an omission, but only a failure to do what law or contract requires. [Penal Law s. 18](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_18)

### Criminal intent: awareness, plus a state of mind about results

> (א) מחשבה פלילית – מודעות לטיב המעשה, לקיום הנסיבות ולאפשרות הגרימה לתוצאות המעשה, הנמנים עם פרטי העבירה, ולענין התוצאות גם אחת מאלה:
> (1) כוונה – במטרה לגרום לאותן תוצאות;
> (2) פזיזות שבאחת מאלה: (א) אדישות – בשוויון נפש לאפשרות גרימת התוצאות האמורות; (ב) קלות דעת – בנטילת סיכון בלתי סביר לאפשרות גרימת התוצאות האמורות, מתוך תקווה להצליח למנען.

*Gist:* Criminal intent means awareness of the conduct, the circumstances and the possible result; for results it also requires intent, indifference or rashness. [s. 20(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_20)

> (ב) לענין כוונה, ראייה מראש את התרחשות התוצאות, כאפשרות קרובה לוודאי, כמוה כמטרה לגרמן.
> (ג) לענין סעיף זה – (1) רואים אדם שחשד בדבר טיב ההתנהגות או בדבר אפשרות קיום הנסיבות כמי שהיה מודע להם, אם נמנע מלבררם; [...]

*Gist:* Foreseeing a result as almost certain counts as intending it (the foresight rule); suspecting a fact and choosing not to check counts as knowing it (wilful blindness). Section 20(ג)(2) adds that hitting a different person or property than intended does not matter (transferred intent). [s. 20(ב)–(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_20)

### Negligence and strict liability

> (א) רשלנות – אי מודעות לטיב המעשה, לקיום הנסיבות או לאפשרות הגרימה לתוצאות המעשה, הנמנים עם פרטי העבירה, כשאדם מן היישוב יכול היה, בנסיבות הענין, להיות מודע לאותו פרט [...]
> (ב) רשלנות יכול שתיקבע כיסוד נפשי מספיק רק לעבירה שאיננה מסוג פשע.

*Gist:* Negligence is failing to be aware of what a reasonable person would have been aware of, with an unreasonable risk; it can be the mental element only of a non-felony. [s. 21](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_21) Strict liability needs an express statement, allows a no-fault defence, and never allows imprisonment without proof of intent or negligence. [s. 22](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_22)

### Causation

Section 309 lists situations where a person is treated as causing death even if they were not the immediate or only cause: for example, a wound later mistreated in good faith by a doctor, or a victim who dies escaping violence in a natural way. [s. 309](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_309) Beyond that, courts apply factual causation ("but for") and legal causation (foreseeability), the same pair you met in tort law (lesson 04.3).

### An omission offence with a knowledge element

> מי שידע כי פלוני זומם לעשות מעשה פשע ולא נקט כל האמצעים הסבירים למנוע את עשייתו או את השלמתו, דינו – מאסר שנתיים.

*Gist:* A person who **knew** that someone planned a felony and did not take all reasonable means to prevent it commits an offence. [s. 262](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין#סעיף_262)

## Leading cases

### ע״פ 3417/99 הר־שפי נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2001)

Margalit Har-Shefi knew that Yigal Amir was planning to murder Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and did not take reasonable means to prevent it. She was convicted in the magistrates' court of failing to prevent a felony (s. 262); the conviction was upheld on appeal, and the Supreme Court dismissed her further appeal on the conviction unanimously (the sentence appeal was dismissed by majority, Justice Turkel dissenting).

Why it matters: it is the leading Israeli example of liability for an **omission**. Israel has no general duty to rescue, so an omission is a crime only where the law imposes a duty (s. 18(ג)); s. 262 is such a duty, and its element is **knowledge** of the plan. The case also shows how courts weigh a defendant's claim that she did not take the other person's talk seriously against the evidence of what she actually knew. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/184)

### Theory: the structure the 1994 reform built

The general part follows a "particulars" model: the mental element is checked **separately for each particular** of the factual element. For conduct and circumstances, awareness is enough; for results, the law adds a scale (intent, indifference, rashness, negligence). This is why one event can lead to different offences: killing with indifference is murder after the 2019 reform (s. 300), rashness is reckless homicide (s. 301ג), negligence is causing death by negligence (s. 304) (lesson 05.5).

## How it works in practice

### The elements table

Prosecutors check each element against each piece of evidence. You can do the same:

| Element | What the law requires | Evidence | Gap? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conduct | e.g. "המכה אדם, נוגע בו, דוחפו" (s. 378) | Your statement; video; witness | — |
| Circumstance | "בלא הסכמתו" | Your statement | — |
| Result (if any) | "חבלה של ממש" (s. 380) | Medical record, photos | Photos not dated |
| Mental element | Awareness of the conduct and circumstances | Words he said; messages before and after | — |

### What a victim should bring for each element

- **Conduct:** exact words or acts, the date, time and place, and who saw it.
- **Circumstances:** the facts that make it criminal (no consent; you are a public servant on duty; it was in your home).
- **Result:** medical documents (go to a doctor or emergency room on the day), photos with dates, repair invoices.
- **Mental element:** what shows the offender knew or intended: earlier threats, messages, the use of a weapon, repetition.

## Worked example

Anwar Khoury, a bus driver from Shefa-'Amr, is attacked by a passenger at a stop in Haifa after he refuses to let the passenger board without paying. The passenger pushes him, Anwar falls, and his wrist is fractured. The passenger shouts "I'll teach you" while pushing.

1. **Candidate offences:** assault causing actual bodily harm (s. 380); assault of a person who gives a service to the public on behalf of a body providing a public service, where the assault is connected to his job (s. 382א(א)), which fits a bus driver refusing an unpaid fare; grievous harm (s. 333) is less likely.
2. **Factual element:** conduct (a push, s. 378), circumstance (no consent), result (a fracture is at least "actual bodily harm").
3. **Mental element:** awareness of the push and of the lack of consent; for the injury, a result, the general rule of s. 20 asks at least whether he was aware that harm was possible and was indifferent or rash. Exactly how much awareness of the result s. 380 requires is debated in the literature; in a shove at a bus door the point is rarely decisive.
4. **Causation:** the push caused the fall, which caused the fracture: factual and legal causation are clear.
5. **Evidence plan:** the bus camera footage (ask the company to preserve it at once), witness passengers, the emergency room report on the same day, and the passenger's words.
6. **Gap:** the passenger's identity. The bus's electronic ticketing system and the footage may identify him; Anwar gives the police the time, line number and stop.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One incident in which you, or someone in a published news report, were harmed by another person, or the supplied case below.
- The Penal Law on Wikisource.
- 45 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Rivka Stern, 72, from Bnei Brak, gave her neighbour's son ₪3,000 in cash after he told her that her grandson had been arrested and needed bail "tonight". There was no arrest. He has stopped answering her calls.

### Steps

1. Write the facts in date order, in short numbered lines.
2. Choose the most likely offence (for the supplied case: obtaining by deceit, s. 415) and copy its text.
3. Split it into conduct, circumstances, result.
4. For each, write the mental state required (s. 20 or s. 21).
5. Fill in the elements table with the evidence you have and the evidence you could still get.
6. Mark any gap and write how a complaint could close it (for example, bank or phone records the police can request).

### Template

None. Your elements table becomes the "facts and evidence" part of the complaint template in lesson 05.7.

### How you know it worked

- Every element of the chosen offence appears in your table with at least one piece of evidence or a marked gap.
- You named the mental state for each element and did not confuse motive with intent.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Building an elements table is preparation that any victim may do for their own complaint; it is not legal advice to anyone else.

### Where and how

- Use the table when you file a police complaint (lesson 05.7) or prepare a private criminal complaint (lesson 05.8).
- Evidence held by others (bus cameras, bank records, phone records) is obtained by the police; ask them in writing to secure it quickly, because many systems overwrite footage within days.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The incident is serious (sexual offences, serious violence, family violence): a victims' lawyer, free in some cases through Legal Aid (lesson 05.7), can protect your interests.
- You might also have done something that could be seen as an offence (for example, you hit back).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Describing motive ("he hates me") instead of intent | No evidence of the mental element | Add words and acts that show what he knew and wanted | Ask: what did he say or do that shows awareness? |
| No proof of the result | Assault causing harm reduced to simple assault | Get a medical report as soon as possible | See a doctor on the day of the injury |
| Assuming an omission is always criminal | Complaint closed | Find the specific duty (law or contract) | Look for the duty first |
| Waiting before asking to secure footage | Footage overwritten | Ask the operator and the police in writing at once | Act on day one |

## Review

- Every offence has a factual element (conduct, circumstances, result) and a mental element checked for each part (s. 18, s. 20).
- Criminal intent is awareness, plus intent, indifference or rashness for results; foresight of near-certainty counts as intent; wilful blindness counts as knowledge.
- Negligence is the exception, and never for felonies (s. 21); strict liability is rarer still and limited by s. 22.
- Omissions are criminal only where a duty exists; Har-Shefi shows a statutory duty to prevent a felony with a knowledge element.
- An elements table connects law to evidence and is the backbone of a good complaint.
