# 34.4 · Environmental data: the pollutant release register and other public sources

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find out, from free public sources and without asking anyone, which plants near you report emissions, of what, into which medium and in what quantity, and to read those records with the care their limits demand. Data first, complaint second: a complaint that cites the plant's own reported emissions is harder to dismiss than one that says "it smells".

## Why it matters

Since 2012 every large plant in Israel must report its releases and waste transfers each year, and the Ministry must publish them online, free, in a form you can download and map. Few residents use it. Journalists, municipalities and NGOs do, and so do plants' lawyers. The register will not tell you whether a plant breaks the law (most of its data is self-reported annual totals), but it tells you what to ask, whom to ask, and what the authority already knows.

## The law

### What plants report

> בעל מפעל ימסור לרשם, אחת לשנה, לא יאוחר מיום 31 במרס של אותה שנה, דיווח על המפעל [...] לגבי שנת הכספים שקדמה למועד הדיווח

*Gist:* Each year, by 31 March, the owner of a covered plant reports for the previous year: each listed pollutant released to each medium at or above the threshold, transfers in wastewater, the existence of releases below the threshold, what part came from malfunctions, waste transferred and to whom, water and energy use, the calculation method, and the plant's details and location; the report is verified by a declaration of the owner or, for a company, the CEO. [PRTR Law s. 3(ב)–(ג)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הסביבה_(פליטות_והעברות_לסביבה_–_חובות_דיווח_ומרשם))

### What the public sees

> המרשם ורשימת המצאי יועמדו לעיון הציבור, בלא תשלום, בין השאר באמצעות מערכת מידע גאוגרפי (GIS), באתר האינטרנט של המשרד, ויעודכנו לכל הפחות אחת לשנה, לא יאוחר מיום 1 בספטמבר [...]

*Gist:* The register and the inventory are published free on the Ministry's website, including on a map, updated at least once a year by 1 September, in a way that allows saving, retrieval and output. [s. 12(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הסביבה_(פליטות_והעברות_לסביבה_–_חובות_דיווח_ומרשם))

What is withheld (s. 12(ב)–(ג)): details of the waste handler that received waste (except foreign handlers of hazardous waste), the plant's water and energy consumption, data a senior security official certifies as harmful to State security, and, temporarily, data the registrar reasonably suspects is wrong until checked. Where data is withheld, the registrar must publish a notice with reasons in its place (s. 12(ד)), and if a plant failed to report, a notice saying so after giving it a chance to respond (s. 12(ו)).

The register also includes efficiency ratings where required (s. 10); the registrar prepares an inventory of releases from all sources every seven years (s. 11) and reports to the Knesset committee every year (s. 13). Financial sanctions imposed for reporting breaches are published with the facts, the amount and, for a company, its name (s. 27).

### Other duties to publish

- Air-quality data and forecasts, regularly and free (Clean Air Law s. 8).
- Emission-permit applications, draft permits and comments (s. 21).
- Administrative orders (s. 46א) and financial sanctions with the offender's name (s. 60).
- Environmental information held by any public authority (FOI Law s. 6א and the 2009 regulations): [lesson 34.5](lesson-05.md).

## Leading cases

### Key text: PRTR Law s. 12(ב)(2) and Clean Air Law s. 21(ד)(2)(ג)

No free ruling on the register was found. The key texts are the two limits. The register hides water and energy consumption, which plants regard as commercially sensitive; but the Clean Air Law says that the identity of an emission source and the types, quantities, concentrations and rates of pollutants are never a trade secret. Why it matters: when a plant or an authority refuses emission data citing commercial confidentiality, you quote s. 21(ד)(2)(ג) and FOI s. 6א (lesson 34.5); when you ask for water consumption from the register, expect a lawful refusal and ask the Water Authority instead.

## How it works in practice

### The sources

| Permits, orders, sanctions | Draft and final emission permits; orders; sanctions | Ministry site (⚠️ manual check) |
| Business licence file | Conditions set by the Ministry and the municipality | Municipal licensing unit; FOI |

### Reading the dataset

The multi-year dataset has, among others, these columns (checked 18.10.2026): reporting year (ShnatDivuach), plant name (ShemAtarSvivatiMenifa), town (YeshuvAtarSvivatiMenifa), local authority and district, activity and sector, medium (SugPlita: air, water, sea, soil, wastewater), pollutant in Hebrew (MezahemBeplitaHeb), quantity (KamutPlita, with a tons column), calculation method (ShahamPlita), waste transferred, and a link to the plant page. Its latest rows are for 2025, reported in 2026 (the s. 3 deadline is 31 March; publication by 1 September).

Read with care:

- **Below threshold** entries show that a pollutant is released but not how much (s. 3(ב)(3)).
- **Units**: check kilograms against tons before comparing.
- **Method**: measured, calculated or estimated (s. 3(ב)(7)); estimates can be revised.
- **Malfunction** releases are reported separately (s. 3(ב)(4)); these are often what residents smell.
- **Annual totals** do not show peaks. For a night-time smell, the station readings and the plant's own continuous monitoring (ask by FOI) are more telling.

### Query without a spreadsheet

The dataset has a public API. This query returns up to 100 rows mentioning a town (replace the town name; the browser encodes Hebrew):

## Worked example

Salma Alatawna lives in Segev Shalom, a Bedouin town near the Ramat Hovav industrial council. Residents report a smell at night.

1. **Plants.** She queries the dataset for רמת חובב and filters the 2025 rows; she lists every plant reporting releases to air.
2. **Pollutants.** For each, she notes the pollutants above the threshold and those marked "below threshold", the method, and any malfunction releases.
3. **Trend.** She compares 2021 to 2025 for the three largest air emitters.
4. **Monitoring.** She checks the nearest monitoring station's night readings for the dates in the residents' log.
5. **Gaps.** One plant reported a malfunction release in 2025 but the dataset does not say when. That is her FOI question (lesson 34.5): the malfunction reports to the Ministry, and the Ministry's inspection reports for those dates.
6. **Use.** Her one-page data sheet goes with the residents' complaint (lesson 34.8).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A town, neighbourhood or industrial zone (yours, or the supplied one: Nesher and the Haifa Bay).
- A browser; a spreadsheet program is optional; 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Open the dataset page on data.gov.il, or run the API query above with your town.
2. List the plants and their sectors for the latest year.
3. For the top three by air releases, write: pollutant, quantity and unit, method, malfunction flag.
4. Compare with an earlier year.
5. Open the nearest air-monitoring station on air.sviva.gov.il and note what it measures.
6. Write a one-page data sheet: the plants, the main pollutants, what the data does not say, and the three questions you would ask the Ministry.

### Template

None; the questions feed the data request in [lesson 34.8](lesson-08.md) ([FOI request](../../templates/foi-request.md)).

### How you know it worked

- Your sheet names plants, pollutants, media and quantities with units and years.
- It lists at least one gap that only the Ministry or the plant can fill.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know the reporting deadline (31 March) and the publication deadline (1 September).
- [ ] I know what the register may withhold and that it must say so.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Reading public data needs no standing and no fee. Asking for more (lesson 34.5) is an FOI request in your own name.

### Where and how

- data.gov.il (no login); the Ministry's PRTR pages and map (⚠️ manual check); air.sviva.gov.il.
- PRTR questions to the registrar: the 2024 annual report gives the register's contact mailbox (mirsham at sviva.gov.il; ⚠️ manual check that it is current).
- Free help: environmental NGOs and university clinics that work with the data; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You plan to publish conclusions that a named plant is breaking the law; check the facts and the wording (defamation risk).
- You want to use the data to support a damages claim for illness (expert causation evidence).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading kilograms as tons | A 1,000-fold error; credibility lost | Recalculate and correct publicly | Check the unit column every time |
| Treating "below threshold" as no emission | Missing a relevant pollutant | Ask for the actual quantity by FOI | Read s. 3(ב)(3) |
| Claiming the register proves a breach | Authority dismisses the complaint | Present data as context; ask for permit limits | Pair data with the permit conditions |
| Comparing years after a method change | False trend | Check the method column | Note methods in your sheet |
| Expecting water use data | Lawful refusal (s. 12(ב)(2)) | Ask the Water Authority | Know the exclusions |

## Review

- Covered plants report each year by 31 March, verified by a declaration; the register is published free, on a map, by 1 September.
- Some data is withheld by law (waste handlers, water and energy use, certified security data), and the registrar must say so.
- The multi-year dataset on data.gov.il can be searched by town; read units, methods, thresholds and malfunction flags carefully.
- Air-quality data, draft permits, orders and sanctions are also published by law.
