# 34.7 · Climate law: the current state of legislation and litigation

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain, accurately and without taking sides, where Israeli climate law stands in October 2026: there is no climate statute in force, a government Climate Bill was prepared for its final readings in December 2024 and has not been reported enacted, and a petition asking the High Court to require a stricter emissions target is pending. You will also be able to take part in the debate yourself, through the Knesset rather than only the courts.

## Why it matters

Climate policy is where environmental law meets the hardest questions of public law: who sets long-term targets, whether targets bind, and whether courts may enforce them. The Israeli answers are being written now, in a bill and a petition. A learner who can state each side's best argument and the current facts, with sources, can take part in a committee debate, comment on a draft, or follow the petition intelligently, which is more than most commentators do.

## The law

### What is in force

No Israeli statute sets a binding greenhouse-gas target. Emissions targets have been set by Government resolutions; in the climate petition the State described its target as a 27% reduction by 2030 relative to 2015 (reported by Calcalist, 15.9.2024). Existing statutes reach greenhouse gases indirectly: the Clean Air Law's purpose clause adopts precaution and names future generations (s. 1); the PRTR register includes carbon dioxide releases reported by plants (lesson 34.4); planning and business-licensing decisions may weigh climate considerations.

> [...] בהתאם לעקרון הזהירות המונעת [...] למען הציבור ולמען הדורות הבאים ובהתחשב בצורכיהם.

*Gist:* The Clean Air Law's purpose includes acting on the precautionary principle for the public and future generations. [Clean Air Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אוויר_נקי)

### The Climate Bill

The government Climate Bill passed its first reading and was prepared for the second and third readings by the Knesset Interior and Environmental Protection Committee, which approved a text on 3 December 2024 ([committee text](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/Committees/25_cs_bg_5226748.pdf)). As reported:

- **Target:** a 27% reduction by 2030 relative to 2015 (lowered in committee from the Government's 30%), with a long-term net-zero aim for 2050 (Calcalist 3.12.2024; ⚠️ check the final text).
- **Flexibility:** the Government may postpone implementing a plan once, by up to three years, with reasons; changes to plans or targets need committee approval ([ynet report of the committee vote](https://www.ynet.co.il/environment-science/article/s1cnj6hxkx); ⚠️ check).
- **Bindingness:** Justice Ministry representatives told the committee that the targets would not bind the Government in a way that could found a petition (ynet; ⚠️ check against the text).
- **Next step then:** the Finance Ministry opposed the text, and a vote in the Ministerial Committee for Legislation was expected before the plenum (Calcalist 3.12.2024).

As of 18 October 2026 this course found no report that the bill was enacted, and Wikisource has no "Climate Law" (⚠️ check the Knesset legislation database before saying it is still a bill).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 7063/23 מגמה ירוקה ואח׳ נ׳ ממשלת ישראל ואח׳ (pending)

Filed in September 2023 by the organisation Megama Yeruka with young petitioners and their families, represented by Tel Aviv University's environmental justice clinic. The petitioners ask for an order requiring the Government to set a target of a 43% reduction by 2030 relative to 2019, as they read Israel's commitments and the science. The State asks for dismissal at the threshold: courts should not review the professional and economic balance behind a target; the international agreements do not require a specific figure; the operative target is 27% (the State also acknowledged that existing measures would yield only about 12% by 2030). At a hearing reported in February 2026 (Barak-Erez, Willner and Ronen JJ.), the Court asked the State to state its "bottom line" for targets and the status of the Climate Bill by a set date (Maariv, 25.2.2026; other reports give a later date, ⚠️). No final decision was found as of 18 October 2026. Sources: [filing PDF](https://img.haarets.co.il/bs/00000191-f4a7-d1c1-adbf-f5b7f2aa0000/55/8e/48b1cd144777a7549e8cd4914f5a/1.pdf), [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/hynblueta), [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/opinions/article-1288981).

**The strongest argument for the petitioners:** climate harm threatens the rights to life, body and dignity (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty ss. 2, 4), the Government has itself adopted targets and international commitments, and a court reviewing whether the Government acts reasonably and proportionately on its own commitments is applying ordinary administrative law, not making policy.

**The strongest argument for the State:** setting a national target allocates costs across the whole economy and involves foreign-policy and budget choices that belong to the elected Government and the Knesset; there is no statute that sets a duty, and the bill before the Knesset shows that the legislature is the forum; a court-imposed number would lack a democratic and professional basis. For the general debate on judicial review, see [lesson 06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md).

### Comparative: ICJ Advisory Opinion, Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (23.7.2025)

The International Court of Justice delivered an advisory opinion on States' obligations regarding climate change at the request of the UN General Assembly. Advisory opinions do not bind a State the way a judgment does; parties in national cases cite it as an authoritative statement of international law, and States differ on its weight. Why it matters: both sides of HCJ 7063/23 can be expected to address international law, so a learner should know the opinion exists and read the summary. [ICJ case page](https://www.icj-cij.org/case/187)

### Writing a neutral, useful submission

State one question (for example: should targets be binding?), the current text, your position, the strongest counter-argument and your answer to it, and one concrete wording change. Cite sources. Committees read short, specific submissions.

## Worked example

Two students from Jerusalem, Noa Friedman (who supports binding targets) and Elchanan Biton (who thinks targets should be set by Government decision and reviewed by the Knesset), each write a one-page submission to the Interior and Environmental Protection Committee on the postponement clause.

1. **Facts.** Both cite the committee text of 3.12.2024 and the Calcalist report of the 27% target and the three-year postponement.
2. **Noa:** a single postponement with reasons and committee approval keeps flexibility; she proposes that a postponement also require publishing the expected emissions effect.
3. **Elchanan:** targets set in statute invite litigation over economic policy; he proposes that the statute keep reporting duties and parliamentary review but state that targets are policy goals.
4. **Counter-arguments.** Each answers the other's best point: Noa addresses cost and democratic choice; Elchanan addresses accountability if targets are missed.
5. **Result.** Two short, sourced submissions that a committee legal adviser can use, from opposite positions.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The sources linked above; the [letter to a Knesset committee](../../templates/letter-to-knesset-committee.md) template; 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Check the bill's status on the Knesset legislation database and note the date you checked.
2. Choose one question: bindingness of targets, the postponement clause, local-authority funding, or the role of courts.
3. Write your position in three sentences with one source each.
4. Write the strongest opposite argument in two sentences, then your answer.
5. Propose one wording change.
6. Put it into the template as a one-page submission. Do not send it unless you choose to, in your own name.

[Letter to a Knesset committee](../../templates/letter-to-knesset-committee.md).

### How you know it worked

- A reader cannot tell your politics from your summary of the facts; only from your position paragraph.
- Every factual statement has a source and a date.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Writing to a Knesset committee, commenting on drafts and requesting local plans are open to anyone in his own name. Joining the petition or filing as amicus is for organisations, usually with counsel ([lesson 33.5](../module-33/lesson-05.md)).

### Where and how

- The Knesset website: the committee's page, its email for public submissions, and the bill's page (⚠️ manual check of the current addresses).
- Government public consultations: the government consultation portal on gov.il (⚠️ manual check).
- Free help: environmental NGOs, policy institutes on all sides, university clinics.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- An organisation you belong to wants to join the petition or apply as amicus.
- You plan to publish accusations against named officials or companies.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saying "Israel has a climate law" | Factual error | Correct: a bill, not a law (as of the date checked) | Check the Knesset database and date it |
| Citing a 2024 article as the current status | Out-of-date claim | Add the date and recheck | Always write "as of" |
| One-sided summary of the petition | Submission dismissed as advocacy | Add the other side's best argument | Steelman first, then argue |
| Treating the ICJ opinion as binding on Israeli courts | Overstated argument | Describe it as advisory | Check its legal effect |

## Review

- No Israeli statute sets a binding greenhouse-gas target; targets come from Government resolutions.
- A government Climate Bill was approved in committee for final readings on 3.12.2024 (27% by 2030, a postponement option, disputed bindingness); no enactment was found as of 18.10.2026.
- HCJ 7063/23 asks for a 43% target; the State argues non-justiciability; the Court asked the State for its bottom line and the bill's status; it is pending.
- Citizens act through Knesset submissions, consultations, FOI and planning objections; organisations through petitions and amicus briefs.
