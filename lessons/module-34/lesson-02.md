# 34.2 · Pollution and nuisance: air, noise and odour

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell whether a noise, smell or smoke problem breaks the law, which standard applies (a fixed hour, a measured decibel limit, or the open "strong or unreasonable" test), and how to record it so that an inspector or a judge will act on it. This is the statutory layer; the tort of nuisance you studied in [lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md) runs alongside it.

## Why it matters

Noise and smell are the most common environmental complaints in Israel and the ones most often lost for lack of proof. The statutes help the complainant: a breach of a regulation is presumed to disturb, the person in control of a place is treated as having caused what happens there, and a breach is also a private nuisance. But the complainant still has to show what happened, when, and how often. A good log wins more cases than a good argument.

## The law

### The open standard and its helpers

> בכל הליך משפטי לפי חוק זה – (1) מפגע שנקבע כחזק או כבלתי סביר בתקנות לפי סעיף 5, חזקה שהוא עשוי לגרום הפרעה לאדם המצוי בקרבת מקום, כל עוד לא הוכח היפוכו;

*Gist:* If regulations define a noise or smell as strong or unreasonable, it is presumed to disturb people nearby unless the other side proves otherwise; compliance with a personal order under s. 8 is a defence. [Nuisances Law s. 10](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים)

> רואים אדם כאילו גרם לאותו מעשה גם אם הוא נעשה במקום הנתון לשליטתו או לפיקוחו, או על ידי מי שנתון לשליטתו או לפיקוחו, והוא לא נקט כל האמצעים הסבירים למניעתו;

*Gist:* The owner or operator of a place "causes" what happens there unless he proves he took all reasonable measures. [s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים)

> לענין פקודת הנזיקין האזרחיים, 1944, דין כל הפרה של הוראת חוק זה כדין מטרד היחיד.

*Gist:* Every breach of the Law is also a private nuisance in tort. [s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_מפגעים) The Law does not apply to noise or smell that stays inside the private domain of the person causing it (s. 17(א)), and offences under it are strict liability (s. 11(ה)).

### Quiet hours: no measurement needed

> לא ישיר אדם, לא יצעק ולא יפעיל כלי נגינה, מקלט רדיו או טלוויזיה ולא יקים רעש באמצעות פטיפון, רמקול, מגביר קול [...] בין השעות 14.00 ל־16.00 ובין השעות 23.00 ל־07.00 למחרת, באזור מגורים [...]

*Gist:* In a residential area, music, shouting and amplified sound outdoors, in partly open places or in a residential building are banned 14:00–16:00 and 23:00–07:00, except on listed festive nights (Independence Day, Purim, Lag Ba'Omer, Mimouna, Jerusalem Day, the civil New Year's Eve, and the eve and end of a day of rest until midnight). [Noise Prevention Regs 1992, reg. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_למניעת_מפגעים_(מניעת_רעש))

Repair, renovation and building work in a residential building is banned 20:00–07:00 on weekdays and from 17:00 on the eve of a day of rest to 07:00 after it (reg. 4). Car horns and silencers are in reg. 2.

### Measured limits

The Unreasonable Noise Regulations 1990 define "day" as 06:00–22:00 and "night" as 22:01–05:59, and set decibel limits by type of building (hospitals and schools, residential, mixed, residential in industrial zones, industrial) and duration, measured as an equivalent level with corrections for background noise and +5 dB for tonal or impulsive noise (regs 1–3 and First Schedule, [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_למניעת_מפגעים_(רעש_בלתי_סביר))). Only a calibrated meter used as the regulations require counts; a phone app is a log, not a measurement.

### Air pollution

> לא יגרום אדם לזיהום אוויר חזק או בלתי סביר.

*Gist:* The general ban; exceeding the ambient values set under s. 6 or emitting in breach of the Law is unreasonable by definition. [Clean Air Law s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אוויר_נקי)

Large stationary sources need an emission permit (s. 17); the application, its attachments and the draft permit are published and "כל אדם" may comment within 45 days (s. 21(ה)). The Ministry must publish air-quality data and forecasts regularly and free of charge, and warn the public of exceptional pollution (s. 8). Breach of the Law is a civil wrong (s. 70), and an environmental organisation listed under the Civil Claims Law may sue, with the consent of an injured person if the claim is for his damage (s. 71).

### Orders

An authorised official who finds unlawful strong or unreasonable noise or smell may order the person causing it, or the owner of the property, to stop it and restore the previous state, before any indictment; the order can be challenged in the competent court, which does not suspend it unless the court says so (Nuisances Law ss. 11ב, 11ב1). A police officer who sees a noise offence disturbing the peace may, after warning and identifying himself, enter even a home without a warrant to stop it (s. 11א1).

## Leading cases

### Key text: the noise regulations as a two-tier system

There is no free full-text Supreme Court ruling on these regulations that this course could verify. The key text is the pair of regulations themselves: reg. 3 of the 1992 Regulations is a per se rule (no measurement, only the hour and the place), while the 1990 Regulations require a measurement and give the s. 10 presumption. Choose the tier that your evidence can meet. For the common-law test of nuisance (the character of the place, coming to the nuisance) see the rulings briefed in [lesson 04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md).

### Key text: Clean Air Law s. 21 and public comment

Section 21 lets anyone comment on a draft emission permit and requires the commissioner to consider the comments before issuing it, and it limits the "trade secret" exemption: the identity of the source and the types, quantities and concentrations of pollutants are never a trade secret (s. 21(ד)(2)(ג)). Why it matters: it is the citizen's way into permit conditions before the plant is licensed, and its data limits also govern what you can demand later (lesson 34.5).

## How it works in practice

### Choosing the standard

| Situation | Standard | Evidence that works |
|---|---|---|
| Party music at 01:00 | Reg. 3 (1992): banned hour | Time-stamped video with audio; police call log |
| Renovation drilling at 21:00 on a weekday | Reg. 4 (1992) | Video; neighbours' statements |
| Air-conditioner compressor humming all night | 1990 limits; Nuisances Law s. 2 | Inspector's measurement; your log |
| Bakery or restaurant smell | Nuisances Law s. 3; business-licence conditions | Log; inspector's visit report; the Ministry's odour procedure |
| Smoke from a chimney | Clean Air Law s. 3; permit or licence conditions | Photos; monitoring data; PRTR (34.4) |

### The evidence log

For two weeks: date, start and end time, what you heard or smelled, intensity (1–5), where in your home, who else noticed, what you did (closed windows, called the hotline: reference number). Add photos or short videos with the time visible. Ask neighbours for short signed statements. Ask the authority for its inspection report (lesson 34.5).

### Who to call

At night, for a party, the police (100) or the municipal hotline. For a business, the municipal licensing or environmental unit (and the business-licence file). For a plant, the Ministry district office or the town association; for an acute event, *6911 (⚠️ manual check). Air-quality data: [air.sviva.gov.il](https://air.sviva.gov.il).

## Worked example

Rabbi Menachem Weiss runs a small yeshiva in Bnei Brak. A new events hall opened across the street; on weekdays music from its open courtyard is loud until 00:30. A neighbour, Dina Levi, has a newborn.

1. **Standard.** The courtyard is "under the open sky" in a residential area: amplified music after 23:00 breaches reg. 3 regardless of decibels. The festive-night exceptions do not cover ordinary weekdays.
2. **Causation.** The hall's operator controls the place: under s. 14 he is treated as the cause unless he proves all reasonable measures.
3. **Licence.** An events hall needs a business licence; its conditions often require a noise limiter (the Law makes tampering with a noise-limiting device an offence, s. 11(ב2)). The licence file is a public authority record (lesson 34.5).
4. **Action.** Both log the nights for two weeks, call the municipal hotline each night and keep reference numbers, and write to the licensing department asking for enforcement and for the licence conditions (lab, lesson 34.8).
5. **Next tier.** If the municipality does nothing, a private complaint is available to a person whose private domain is affected, after notice to the Minister (lesson 34.6).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A noise, smell or smoke problem of your own, or the supplied one: Avi Dahan of Ashkelon hears a rooftop cooling unit of a supermarket every night from 22:30 to 06:00.
- The regulations linked above; your phone; 60 minutes now and 5 minutes a day for two weeks.

### Steps

1. Classify the source: private activity, business, or plant; indoors, outdoors, or rooftop.
2. Pick the standard from the table. If it is a quiet-hour rule, write the regulation and hour; if it needs a measurement, write that you need an inspector.
3. Set up the log (a sheet with the columns listed above).
4. Find the business licence or permit: the municipality's licensing unit, or the Ministry's published emission permits.
5. Write a one-paragraph statement of facts you could attach to a complaint.

### Template

None in this lesson; the complaint is drafted in lesson 34.8 ([environmental complaint and notice](../../templates/environmental-complaint-and-notice.md)).

### How you know it worked

- Your log has at least 10 dated entries with times and a reference number for every call.
- You can state the exact regulation breached or explain why a measurement is needed.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for the log, complaints and data requests, and for commenting on a draft emission permit (Clean Air s. 21). A private complaint or a civil injunction (lesson 34.6) can also be brought in your own name.

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- Municipal hotline and environmental or licensing unit; police for night noise.
- Ministry district office; town association; *6911 for acute events (⚠️ manual check).
- Comments on draft emission permits: on the Ministry's site within 45 days of the notice (s. 21(ה)) (⚠️ manual check of the page: gov.il blocks bots).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut (noise, air pollution pages, ⚠️ manual check); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side sends you a lawyer's letter about defamation after you posted about the problem.
- You want compensation for illness, or the dispute is with your own landlord or neighbour in a shared building and touches property rights.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on a phone decibel app | Rejected as a measurement | Ask the authority to measure; keep the app readings as a log | Use quiet-hour rules where they fit |
| Complaining about noise inside the neighbour's own flat that you cannot hear | No breach (s. 17) | Describe how it reaches your home | Log where in your home you hear it |
| No reference numbers for calls | Authority says it never heard of it | Ask for the call log by FOI | Note every number |
| Calling it "noise" when it is a festive night listed in reg. 3(ב) | No breach | Wait; log ordinary nights | Check the list before complaining |
| Missing the 45-day comment window on a draft permit | Comments not considered | Ask to be heard anyway; object at the next renewal | Follow the Ministry's notices for your area |

## Review

- The Nuisances Law bans strong or unreasonable noise and smell; regulations define some cases, which are then presumed to disturb (s. 10).
- The 1992 Regulations set quiet hours without measurement; the 1990 Regulations set measured limits by building type and time.
- The person who controls a place is treated as causing what happens there (s. 14), and every breach is a private nuisance (s. 13).
- The Clean Air Law adds emission permits with public comment, public air data, and a civil wrong.
