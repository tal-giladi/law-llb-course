# 33.8 · Practice lab: an objection to a settlement

In this lab you will turn the reading from lesson 33.7 into a document a judge can use: a reasoned objection, in Hebrew, that tells the court what is wrong with the settlement, why it matters under the Law, and what change would cure it, with a short affidavit. You will write it for the Lime settlement as a dry run, or for a live notice that concerns you, in which case you may file it in your own name. A good objection is short, specific and constructive; it is read by the judge, the parties, the examiner and often the Attorney General's representative.

## Why it matters

Objections are one of the few checks on a settlement negotiated by people whose interests are not identical to the class's. Courts must address every objection in their reasoned decision (s. 19(ג)(2)(ב)), and an objection that succeeds in whole or part may earn the objector an award (s. 18(ד1)). Badly drafted objections, angry, general or late, are dismissed in a sentence and can make the settlement look better by comparison.

## The law

No new law. The objection rests on s. 18(ד) (who, when, what), s. 18(ד1) (no withdrawal or benefit without approval; possible award), s. 18(ה) (the parties respond), s. 19 (the court's test, the examiner, the reasons, the power to impose conditions) and ss. 20, 22–23 (remedies and fees) ([lessons 33.3](lesson-03.md) and [33.4](lesson-04.md)). Affidavits are made before a lawyer or another person authorised under the Evidence Ordinance s. 15. Procedure not covered by the Law or the 2010 Regulations follows the Civil Procedure Regulations (reg. 19(א)).

## Leading cases

None new; cite sections, not cases, unless you have read the ruling. Reichert and Schwartzman (lesson 33.4) are the authorities on fees; quote them only as described in the government's 2024 explanatory notes, or after you have read them.

## How it works in practice

- **Length.** Aim for three to six pages plus the affidavit; the Law sets no length for objections, but short ones are read closely.
- **Structure.** Request, standing, the settlement in brief, reasons (one heading per point), proposed changes, declarations, affidavit. The [template](../../templates/class-action-objection-and-opt-out.md), part B, follows this order.
- **Tone.** Respectful and factual. Do not accuse counsel of bad faith unless you can prove it; say what the settlement does, and why the Law requires more.
- **After filing.** The parties may respond within the time the court sets (s. 18(ה)); the examiner receives the objections (s. 19(ב)(3)); the court may hold a hearing. You may not withdraw or accept anything in return without approval (s. 18(ד1)). If the court accepts your points, it may impose them as conditions (s. 19(ד)(1)) and may award you an award for the benefit you brought.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your notes from lesson 33.7 (the Lime notice), or a live settlement notice that concerns you and its full settlement from the registry.
- The [exit and objection template](../../templates/class-action-objection-and-opt-out.md), part B and part C.
- A word processor with Hebrew; 150 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Choose two to four points (20 min).** From your s. 19(ג)(2) table in lesson 33.7, keep only points that (a) rest on a fact you can show, (b) tie to a section of the Law, (c) have a cure the court can impose. Typical strong points: members who get nothing but release; release or class wider than the application (s. 18(ז)(1)); public relief where payment is practical (s. 20(ג)(1)); fee on face value of credits (s. 23(ב)); no examiner (s. 19(ב)(1)).
2. **Write the request (10 min).** Primary and alternative: do not approve as is; alternatively, approve subject to listed changes (s. 19(ד)(1)); fix award and fee under ss. 22–23.
3. **Standing and timing (10 min).** One paragraph: why you are a member, when the notice was published, that you are within the period (or ask for leave to file late, with reasons).
4. **Summarise the settlement (15 min).** Quote the class, the benefit, the future change, the release and the recommendation. Neutral, short.
5. **Write the reasons (45 min).** One heading per point; under each: the term, the problem, the section, the fact, and the effect on members. Use numbers.
6. **Propose changes (20 min).** One cure per point, specific enough to write into an order.
7. **Declarations and affidavit (15 min).** No benefit received; copies sent; the affidavit verifies only facts you know personally.
8. **Check against part C (15 min).** Then read the whole objection aloud and cut anything that is not a fact, a section or a request.

### Template

[Exit and objection template](../../templates/class-action-objection-and-opt-out.md), part B (objection) and part C (checklist).

### How you know it worked

- Every reason heading names a section and every reason has a number or a quoted term.
- Each reason has a matching proposed change.
- The objection fits in six pages and the affidavit verifies only your own facts.
- Someone who has not read the settlement understands, from your section C, what it does.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. A class member may object in his own name (s. 18(ד)); the Law does not require a lawyer. Objecting on behalf of other members or as a service to others is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- File in the case file at the court named in the notice, through Net HaMishpat or at the court registry, within 45 days of publication ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)); attach the affidavit and exhibits.
- Send copies to all counsel named in the notice; sending a copy to the District Attorney's office (civil) for the court's area, the AG's address for service (reg. 12(ה)), is optional but useful.
- Ask the registry whether a fee applies to your filing ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Free help: court information centres; the Israel Consumer Council; university legal clinics.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The parties respond with a request for costs against you.
- The court approves despite your objection and you want to appeal.
- The settlement releases a large personal claim of yours, so that leaving and suing may be better than objecting.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A general complaint ("the settlement is unfair") | Dismissed in a sentence | Supplement with specific points before the hearing | One section, one fact, one cure per point |
| Personal attacks on counsel | Credibility lost | Withdraw the remark (with leave) | Criticise terms, not people |
| Asking only to reject | The court approves anyway | Offer conditions under s. 19(ד)(1) | Always propose changes |
| Affidavit verifying legal argument | Defective affidavit | Limit it to facts you know | Verify facts only |
| Accepting the company's offer to withdraw | Unlawful without approval (s. 18(ד1)) | Disclose to the court | Refuse, or ask the court |

## Review

- A good objection is short, specific and constructive: standing, summary, reasons tied to sections and facts, proposed changes, declarations, affidavit.
- Attack the fee recommendation separately; tie fees to the benefit actually delivered.
- The court must address your objection; it may impose your changes and award you for the benefit you brought; you may not withdraw or accept a benefit without approval.
