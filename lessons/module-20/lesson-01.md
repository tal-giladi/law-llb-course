# 20.1 · Sale of goods: conformity, inspection and notice

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a faulty delivery, from a sofa to a pallet of tiles, and say exactly which duty the seller broke, whether the buyer still may rely on it after the time that passed, and which remedy to claim. You will write the notice that keeps those rights alive, which is the step most buyers get wrong.

## Why it matters

Lesson [03.10](../module-03/lesson-10.md) showed the notice of non-conformity as one paragraph in a breach letter. Here it is the centre of the lesson, because in the Sale Law 1968 the buyer's rights depend less on how bad the defect is than on what the buyer did after delivery. A buyer who did not inspect, or who notified late, may lose every remedy for a defect that was plainly the seller's fault (s. 14(ב)). A buyer who can show the seller knew of the defect is freed from those rules and from any contrary clause (s. 16). Between those poles lie the hidden defect (s. 15), the two-year and four-year cut-offs for movables, and the special remedy of price reduction (s. 28). The same template recurs in the Sale (Apartments) Law (lesson 20.2), in rentals (s. 6 of the Rental and Borrowing Law, lesson 20.3) and in contracts for work (lesson 20.4), so this lesson is the grammar for the whole module.

## The law

### Scope: what is a sale, and when the law applies

> מכר הוא הקניית נכס תמורת מחיר.

*Gist:* A sale is the transfer of property for a price. [Sale Law s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_1)

> חוזה להספקת נכס שיש להפיקו או לייצרו, רואים אותו כחוזה מכר, זולת אם המזמין קיבל עליו לתת את עיקר החמרים הדרושים להפקת הנכס או לייצורו.

*Gist:* A contract to supply a thing yet to be made is a sale, unless the customer supplies most of the materials, in which case it is a contract for work. [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_2); the mirror rule is [Contract for Services Law s. 8(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חוזה_קבלנות#סעיף_8) (lesson 20.4).

> הוראות חוק זה יחולו כשאין בדין אחר הוראות מיוחדות לענין הנדון, ובאין כוונה אחרת משתמעת מן ההסכם בין הצדדים.

*Gist:* The Sale Law is a default code: a special statute or the parties' agreement displaces it. [s. 4(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_4) It applies to movables and, with the necessary changes, to land and rights (s. 4(א)); for new apartments the Sale (Apartments) Law is the special statute.

### Conformity

> המוכר לא קיים את חיוביו, אם מסר –
> (1) רק חלק מהממכר או כמות גדולה או קטנה מן המוסכם;
> (2) נכס שונה או נכס מסוג או תיאור שונה מן המוסכם;
> (3) נכס שאין בו האיכות או התכונות הדרושות לשימושו הרגיל או המסחרי או למטרה מיוחדת המשתמעת מן ההסכם;
> (4) נכס שמבחינת סוגו, תיאורו, איכותו או תכונותיו אינו מתאים לדגם או לדוגמה שהוצגו לקונה [...];
> (5) נכס שאינו מתאים מבחינה אחרת למה שהוסכם בין הצדדים.

*Gist:* Delivering the wrong quantity, the wrong thing, a thing unfit for its ordinary use or for a special purpose implied in the deal, a thing unlike the sample, or anything else that differs from the agreement is a breach. [s. 11](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_11)

Head (3) is the workhorse: fitness for ordinary use needs no promise at all, and fitness for a special purpose binds the seller once that purpose is "implied in the agreement", typically because the buyer told the seller what the goods were for. Head (5) is a residual catch-all that makes any agreed specification part of conformity.

> אין הקונה זכאי להסתמך על אי־התאמת הממכר [...] אם ידע עליה בעת גמירת החוזה.

*Gist:* A buyer who knew of the non-conformity when contracting cannot rely on it. [s. 12](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_12) Note: actual knowledge, not "should have known".

### Inspection and notice

> על הקונה לבדוק את הממכר מיד לאחר קבלתו.

*Gist:* The buyer must inspect the goods immediately after receiving them; if the goods are shipped, on arrival at the destination. [s. 13(א)–(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_13)

> (א) על הקונה להודיע למוכר על אי־התאמה מיד לאחר מועד הבדיקה [...] או מיד לאחר שגילה אותה, הכל לפי המוקדם יותר.
> (ב) לא הודיע הקונה על אי־ההתאמה כאמור בסעיף קטן (א), אין הוא זכאי להסתמך עליה.

*Gist:* Notice must be given immediately after the time for inspection or after discovery, whichever is earlier; without it the buyer may not rely on the non-conformity at all. [s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_14)

> לא היתה אי־ההתאמה ניתנת לגילוי בבדיקה סבירה, זכאי הקונה להסתמך עליה על אף האמור בסעיף 14, ובלבד שנתן למוכר הודעה עליה מיד לאחר שגילה אותה; אולם במכירת נכס נד אין הקונה זכאי לחזור בו מן החוזה אם נתן הודעה כאמור לאחר שעברו שנתיים ממסירת הממכר, ואין הוא זכאי ליתר התרופות בשל הפרת חוזה אם נתן את ההודעה לאחר שעברו ארבע שנים ממסירת הממכר.

*Gist:* A hidden defect may be notified on discovery, but for movables cancellation is lost if notice comes after a set period from delivery, and all other remedies after a longer one. [s. 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_15) The periods are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

> היתה אי־ההתאמה נובעת מעובדות שהמוכר ידע או היה עליו לדעת עליהן בעת גמירת החוזה ולא גילה אותן לקונה, זכאי הקונה להסתמך עליה על אף האמור בסעיפים 14 ו־15 או בכל הסכם, ובלבד שנתן למוכר הודעה עליה מיד לאחר שגילה אותה.

*Gist:* If the seller knew or should have known of the facts behind the defect and did not disclose them, the inspection rules, the cut-offs and any contrary clause fall away; only prompt notice on discovery remains. [s. 16](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_16)

> (א) קונה המודיע על אי־התאמה יפרט אותה פירוט סביר ויתן למוכר הזדמנות נאותה לבדוק את הממכר.
> (ב) ניתנה הודעת אי־התאמה בדרך המקובלת בנסיבות הענין, זכאי הקונה להסתמך עליה אף אם אחרה להגיע לתעודתה או לא הגיעה אליה כלל.

*Gist:* The notice must describe the defect reasonably and let the seller inspect; a notice sent in the customary way counts even if it arrives late or never. [s. 17](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_17)

### Title, risk, remedies

> המוכר חייב למסור את הממכר כשהוא נקי מכל שעבוד, עיקול וזכות אחרת של צד שלישי.

*Gist:* The seller must deliver free of any charge, attachment or third-party right. [s. 18(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_18) Risk passes when the buyer receives the goods (s. 22); ownership passes on delivery unless agreed otherwise (s. 33); market overt (s. 34) was covered in [lesson 13.8](../module-13/lesson-08.md).

> הפר המוכר חיוב כאמור בסעיף 11 ולא תיקן את הדבר תוך זמן סביר אחרי שהקונה נתן לו הודעה על כך, ואין הקונה רוצה לתבוע את ביצוע החוזה או לחזור בו ממנו, זכאי הוא לנכות מן המחיר את הסכום שבו פחת שווי הממכר עקב אי־ההתאמה לעומת שוויו לפי החוזה.

*Gist:* If the seller does not cure within a reasonable time after notice, the buyer who keeps the goods may deduct from the price the loss of value caused by the non-conformity. [s. 28](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_המכר#סעיף_28) The Sale Law's remedies are added to the Remedies Law (s. 27): enforcement (repair or replacement), cancellation for a fundamental breach or after an extension, and damages ([lesson 03.8](../module-03/lesson-08.md)). The parties may also set off debts arising from the same sale (s. 32) and hold a lien (s. 31).

### The consumer layer

For consumer purchases the Consumer Protection Law adds a warranty regime: [s. 18א](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הצרכן#סעיף_18א) empowers the minister to require free repair or replacement during a set period, warranty certificates and stickers, and caps on technician waiting times with fixed compensation. These rights are added to the Sale Law, not substituted for it: a consumer may still rely on s. 11 against the retailer.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 3912/90 Eximin S.A. נ׳ טקסטיל והנעלה איטל סטייל פרארי בע״מ, פ״ד מז(4) 64 (1993)

An importer bought 3,000 pairs of boots from an Israeli manufacturer for a US customer; US customs detained them because the design infringed a registered US trade mark. Both parties knew a trade mark might exist and neither checked. The majority (Shamgar P., Maltz J.) held that both acted without the good faith the contract required, and divided the loss between them; Goldberg J., dissenting, would have put the whole loss on the importer, who knew the destination market. Why it matters: a "defect in title" or non-conformity in a sale is not always all-or-nothing; through s. 6 of the Sale Law and s. 39 of the Contracts Law the court can allocate responsibility when both sides ignored a known risk. [Cardozo ISCP summary and translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/153/)

### ע״א 7833/06 Pamesa Ceramica נ׳ ישראל מנדלסון הנדסה ואספקה טכנית בע״מ (2009)

Spanish floor tiles were imported, sold to a contractor and laid in a Kiryat Motzkin building, where they failed. The importer, sued by the contractor, sent a third-party notice to the Spanish manufacturer, who argued that it had not been notified of the defect within the two-year period of the Sale (International Sale of Goods) Law 1971. The Supreme Court allowed the manufacturer's appeal: general awareness of "problems" with a product is not knowledge of the specific defect, so the seller-knowledge exception did not apply, and the contract claim was barred; a negligence claim in tort remained open, but without the strict contractual liability, and negligence was not proved. Why it matters: it shows the cost of missing a notice deadline, the narrow reading of the "seller knew" exception, and that switching to tort means having to prove fault. The same logic guides lawyers reading s. 16 of the domestic Sale Law. [Cardozo ISCP](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/295/)

## How it works in practice

### The five questions for any faulty delivery

| Question | Rule | What decides it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Is it a non-conformity, or a defect in title? | s. 11 / s. 18 | Contract, sample, ordinary use, purpose told to the seller |
| 2. Did the buyer know at contracting? | s. 12 | Actual knowledge only |
| 3. Was it discoverable by reasonable inspection on receipt? | ss. 13–14 vs s. 15 | Type of goods, buyer's expertise, packaging |
| 4. Was notice prompt and specific? | ss. 14, 15, 17 | Dates; text of the notice; how sent |
| 5. Did the seller know or should have known? | s. 16 | Prior complaints, internal tests, obvious manufacturing faults |

### What "מיד" means in practice

The law sets no number of days. Courts read "immediately" in context: hours for perishables, days for ordinary goods, longer when inspection needs an expert or installation. Waiting weeks without reason is risky. Notify in writing, describe the defect, attach photographs and offer access to inspect (s. 17(א)). Email is a customary way today; registered mail or a courier with proof of delivery remains the safest record (s. 17(ב)).

### Choosing the remedy

- **Repair or replacement** (enforcement) when you still want the goods and the seller can fix them.
- **Price reduction** (s. 28) when you keep the goods: it needs no proof of fundamental breach, only that the seller did not cure within a reasonable time after notice.
- **Cancellation** when the breach is fundamental, or after an unanswered extension (Remedies Law ss. 6–7); return the goods (s. 9).
- **Damages** for consequential loss foreseeable at contracting (Remedies Law s. 10), in addition to any of the above.

## Worked example

Salim Abu-Rabia runs a catering business in Rahat. On 1 March he buys 200 folding chairs from a Holon wholesaler for ₪16,000, for outdoor events, and tells the salesperson so. The chairs arrive on 3 March in sealed cartons; Salim stores them and opens them on 24 March for a wedding. Thirty chairs have visibly cracked legs. In August, after five months of use, the welds of another forty chairs fail under normal load; a metalworker says the weld is too thin for outdoor use. Salim then finds an online forum where other buyers complained to the wholesaler about the same welds in January.

1. **Classification.** Cracked legs: unfit for ordinary use (s. 11(3)). Thin welds: unfit for ordinary use and for the outdoor purpose Salim disclosed (s. 11(3), special purpose implied in the agreement).
2. **Cracked legs, inspection and notice.** Visible on opening; inspection was due "immediately" after 3 March (s. 13(א)). Salim inspected three weeks late. If he notifies now, the wholesaler will argue s. 14(ב): no timely notice, no reliance. Salim's argument: sealed cartons for event furniture are commonly opened at first use; weak, but he should notify at once and keep trying.
3. **Welds, hidden defect.** Not discoverable by a reasonable look (s. 15). Notice "immediately after discovery" in August preserves his rights; it is within both cut-offs for movables (see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
4. **Seller knowledge.** The January complaints suggest the wholesaler knew or should have known of the weak welds before the sale (s. 16). If proved, that also rescues the cracked legs if they share the cause, and overrides a "no returns after 7 days" clause on the invoice.
5. **Remedies.** For the seventy chairs: replacement or repair (enforcement), or price reduction under s. 28 if he keeps them, plus damages for the event where chairs failed (foreseeable). Cancelling the whole sale is harder: 130 chairs are fine; partial cancellation of the defective units is the realistic route.
6. **Forum.** Salim's business cannot use small claims as a company; if he trades as an individual (עוסק מורשה), he may sue in small claims within the ceiling ([lesson 08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- One recent purchase that went wrong (yours), or the supplied case below; the invoice or order confirmation; photographs.
- The [notice of breach and cancellation template](../../templates/notice-of-breach-and-cancellation.md), Part A.
- 45 minutes.

**Supplied case.** Dana Vaknin of Kiryat Gat bought a fridge from an appliance chain on 10 September; it was delivered on 14 September. On 20 September she noticed the freezer does not get below −8 °C. The technician sent on 27 September said "within norms". On 15 October the food spoiled.

### Steps

1. Write the timeline: contract, delivery, inspection, discovery, any contact with the seller, today's date.
2. Classify the defect under s. 11 (which head and why) or s. 18.
3. Decide whether the defect was discoverable on reasonable inspection (s. 13) or hidden (s. 15), and whether s. 16 can be argued.
4. Decide which remedy you want first and which you keep in reserve.
5. Draft the notice in Hebrew on Part A of the template: the defect in reasonable detail, the date of discovery, a request to inspect and repair or replace within a stated reasonable time, and a reservation of price reduction, cancellation and damages.
6. Choose how you will send it and how you will prove it was sent (s. 17(ב)).

### Template

[Notice of breach and cancellation](../../templates/notice-of-breach-and-cancellation.md), Part A (notice and extension); for a consumer purchase add a reference to the warranty certificate under Consumer Protection Law s. 18א.

### How you know it worked

- The notice names the defect, the date you discovered it and the head of s. 11.
- It offers the seller inspection and a reasonable time to cure (s. 17(א), s. 28).
- You can show a sending record.
- You did not waive cancellation by saying "I will keep the fridge whatever happens".

### Self-check

- [ ] I can say the difference between s. 14 and s. 15.
- [ ] I know why s. 16 also overrides a "no returns" clause.
- [ ] I know what s. 28 needs before I can deduct from the price.
- [ ] I know that knowing of the defect at contracting (s. 12) bars reliance.

<details><summary>Reference answer: Dana's case</summary>

The fridge is unfit for ordinary use (s. 11(3)). The defect was discoverable within days of normal use, and Dana noticed it on 20 September, six days after delivery; she should treat that as discovery and notify in writing now, referring to the 27 September visit as an informal notice the seller already received. Her letter: defect described with temperatures, the technician visit, the spoiled food on 15 October; a demand to replace within 7 days; reservation of cancellation (a fridge that does not freeze is arguably a fundamental breach) and damages for the food. As a consumer she also relies on the warranty under s. 18א and its regulations. She sends it by email and by registered mail.

</details>

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Any buyer may write the notice, negotiate and sue in his own name. An individual may sue in the small claims court up to the ceiling; a company may be sued there but cannot sue there (Courts Law s. 60; [lesson 08.3](../module-08/lesson-03.md)). Writing notices for other people as a service is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- First the written notice; then, for a consumer, a complaint to the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority if useful (it does not stop limitation; [lesson 07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)).
- Claim: small claims court for individuals within the ceiling, on the [small claim template](../../templates/small-claim-contract-consumer.md); otherwise the magistrates' court ([lesson 15.2](../module-15/lesson-02.md)). Fees and ceilings: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: the Judiciary's information centre, Kol-Zchut consumer pages, the Israel Consumer Council.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The goods are expensive equipment and the loss of business is large.
- The seller is abroad (international sale rules and jurisdiction, as in Pamesa).
- The seller claims your notice was late and the sum is large enough to litigate the point.
- There is a chain of sellers and manufacturers and you need third-party notices.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not opening cartons until first use | Visible defects lost under s. 14(ב) | Notify at once; argue s. 16 if the seller knew | Inspect and photograph on delivery |
| Complaining only by phone | No proof of notice; dispute over the date | Send a written notice now that refers to the call | Always confirm in writing (s. 17) |
| Vague notice ("it doesn't work well") | Seller argues no reasonable particulars (s. 17(א)) | Send a detailed second notice | Describe the defect, date, photos |
| Deducting from the price before giving notice | You are in breach of payment | Pay or put the sum in escrow; give notice | Notice, reasonable time, then s. 28 |
| Relying on a "no returns" clause as final | Rights abandoned needlessly | Check s. 16 and consumer rules | Read the clause against ss. 11–16 |

## Review

- Section 11 defines conformity broadly; section 18 adds freedom from third-party rights.
- Inspect immediately (s. 13) and notify immediately (s. 14); a late notice can cost every remedy.
- Hidden defects may be notified on discovery (s. 15), but movables carry cut-offs from delivery.
- Seller knowledge or concealment (s. 16) overrides the inspection rules and any contrary clause; Pamesa shows it means knowledge of the specific defect.
- Price reduction (s. 28) is the buyer's tool when keeping the goods; the Remedies Law supplies the rest.
