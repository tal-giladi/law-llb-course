# 20.8 · Unjust enrichment

By the end of this lesson you will be able to recognise when the law orders someone to give back a benefit even though there was no contract and no tort: money paid by mistake, work done under a contract that fell away, someone else's property used for profit, a stranger who saved your flat from a flood. You will know the six sections of the Unjust Enrichment Law 1979 and the debates the Supreme Court built on them.

## Why it matters

Contracts and torts do not cover every unfair transfer. A bank transfer to the wrong account, payment under a contract later cancelled or void, improvements made to a rented flat, a competitor exploiting your unprotected idea: in each, the question is not "who broke a promise" or "who was at fault" but "who holds a benefit that belongs to someone else". Israeli law answers in one short statute whose generality is both its strength and its danger. Courts use it to fill gaps, and also to decide how far it may override the limits deliberately set by other laws, such as intellectual property. On the practical side, restitution of a mistaken payment is among the most common small claims, and the bank will not reverse a transfer without the recipient's consent.

## The law

### The duty to restore

> (א) מי שקיבל שלא על פי זכות שבדין נכס, שירות או טובת הנאה אחרת (להלן – הזוכה) שבאו לו מאדם אחר (להלן – המזכה), חייב להשיב למזכה את הזכיה, ואם השבה בעין בלתי אפשרית או בלתי סבירה – לשלם לו את שוויה.
> (ב) אחת היא אם באה הזכיה מפעולת הזוכה, מפעולת המזכה או בדרך אחרת.

*Gist:* Whoever received property, a service or another benefit, without a legal right, that came to him from another, must return it, or its value if return in kind is impossible or unreasonable, whether the benefit came from his own act, the other's act, or otherwise. [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_1)

Three elements: (1) a benefit (money, goods, services, use, saved expenses, profit); (2) that came from the plaintiff (a link between the benefit and the plaintiff's property or efforts); (3) without a legal basis (no contract, statute or gift that justifies keeping it).

### Defences and adjustments

> בית המשפט רשאי לפטור את הזוכה מחובת ההשבה לפי סעיף 1, כולה או מקצתה, אם ראה שהזכיה לא היתה כרוכה בחסרון המזכה או שראה נסיבות אחרות העושות את ההשבה בלתי צודקת.

*Gist:* The court may exempt the enriched party, wholly or partly, if the benefit involved no loss to the plaintiff, or other circumstances make restitution unjust. [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_2) The structure matters: restitution does not require the plaintiff's loss; its absence is only a ground for a discretionary exemption. A recipient who spent the money in good faith ("change of position") argues "other circumstances".

> הזוכה רשאי לנכות ממה שעליו להשיב את מה שהוציא או התחייב להוציא או השקיע באופן סביר להשגת הזכיה.

*Gist:* The enriched party may deduct what he reasonably spent or invested to obtain the benefit. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_3)

### Paying another's debt; acting for another

> מי שפרע חובו של אדם אחר בלי שהיה חייב לכך כלפיו, אינו זכאי להשבה אלא אם לא היתה לזוכה סיבה סבירה להתנגד לפרעון החוב [...], ואינו זכאי להשבה זו אלא כדי מה שנתן לפרעון החוב.

*Gist:* A volunteer who paid someone else's debt recovers only if the debtor had no reasonable ground to object, and only what he paid. [s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_4)

> (א) מי שעשה בתום לב ובסבירות פעולה לשמירה על חייו, שלמות גופו, בריאותו, כבודו או רכושו של אדם אחר, בלי שהיה חייב לכך כלפיו, והוציא או התחייב להוציא הוצאות בקשר לכך, חייב הזוכה לשפותו על הוצאותיו הסבירות [...]

*Gist:* A person who in good faith and reasonably acts to protect another's life, body, health, dignity or property recovers his reasonable expenses, and the court may award compensation for damage to his property; not if the beneficiary objected or had reason to, except when life or health was at stake. [s. 5](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_5)

### The relation to other law

> (א) הוראות חוק זה יחולו כשאין בחוק אחר הוראות מיוחדות לענין הנדון ואין הסכם אחר בין הצדדים. [...] (ג) אין בחוק זה כדי לגרוע מתרופות אחרות.

*Gist:* The law applies where no other statute has special provisions and the parties made no other agreement; it binds the State and does not detract from other remedies. [s. 6](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_עשיית_עושר_ולא_במשפט#סעיף_6)

Special restitution rules elsewhere: after cancellation for breach (Remedies Law s. 9), after cancellation for a defect in formation (Contracts Law s. 21), for illegal contracts (Contracts Law s. 31; [lesson 03.6](../module-03/lesson-06.md)), and for a tenant's unauthorised changes, which the Rental and Borrowing Law expressly leaves to unjust enrichment (s. 20א(ב)(1)).

## Leading cases

### ד״נ 20/82 אדרס חומרי בניין בע״מ נ׳ הרלו אנד ג׳ונס G.m.b.H., פ״ד מב(1) 221 (1988)

A German seller contracted to sell steel to an Israeli buyer; when prices rose, it sold the steel to someone else at a higher price. In a further hearing the Supreme Court held, by majority, that the buyer could recover the seller's profit from the breach, with the unjust-enrichment law serving as a basis for disgorging gains made by breaching a contract, where ordinary damages would leave the breaching party with an unjust benefit. Why it matters: Adras is the founding case for restitution of profits from breach of contract in Israel and for the broad reading of "came to him from another"; critics argued it blurs the line between contract and restitution and rewards efficient breach less than it should. ⚠️ Free text: search the case number on [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il); we could not open it on 17 October 2026.

### ע״א 5097/11 טלראן תקשורת (1986) בע״מ נ׳ צ׳רלטון בע״מ (2013)

Charlton held the exclusive Israeli broadcast rights to the 2006 World Cup; Telran sold decoder cards that let Israelis watch foreign channels' encrypted broadcasts. The Supreme Court held that Telran did not infringe copyright, directly or indirectly, because selling cards is not broadcasting and end-users' viewing was not infringement; but it remanded the case for the district court to decide whether Telran was unjustly enriched at Charlton's expense, a head the lower court had not considered. Why it matters: when a specific regime (copyright) does not protect a claimant, unjust enrichment can still be considered, subject to the debate whether it may give protection that intellectual-property law deliberately withheld (the "extra element" line begun in ע״א 5768/94 א.ש.י.ר., ⚠️ not opened). [Cardozo ISCP](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/382)

### ע״א 1846/92 לוי נ׳ מבט בנייה בע״מ (1993)

After cancelling an apartment purchase for persistent leaks, the buyers had to pay restitution for their years of use of the flat, but recovered the same sum as damages, since damages put them where performance would have. Why it matters: restitution after cancellation is mutual (Remedies Law s. 9), but the injured party can offset its restitution burden through damages. [Cardozo ISCP](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/237)

## How it works in practice

### The checklist

1. **Benefit:** what exactly did the defendant get (money, use of a flat, saved costs, profit)? Value it.
2. **Link:** did it come from you (your money, your property, your work)?
3. **Legal basis:** is there a contract, statute, judgment or gift that justifies it? If a contract governs, use its rules (s. 6(א)).
4. **Special regime:** cancellation (Remedies Law s. 9), formation defects (Contracts Law s. 21), illegality (s. 31), tenant changes (s. 20א).
5. **Defences:** no loss to you (s. 2), change of position, his expenses to obtain it (s. 3).
6. **Limitation:** an unjust-enrichment claim is a civil claim under the Limitation Law ([lesson 15.9](../module-15/lesson-09.md)).

### A mistaken bank transfer

The bank will usually contact the recipient's bank but cannot reverse a completed transfer without the recipient's consent. Steps: report at once to your bank in writing; ask it to request the return; send the recipient a written demand if you can identify him (the bank may not give you details; a court can order disclosure in a claim); sue in small claims for the amount. A recipient who knowingly keeps money paid by mistake may also face criminal exposure, which is a matter for the police, not for your letter.

## Worked example

Noa Ben-Haim, from a kibbutz in the Galilee, pays her accountant ₪12,000 by bank transfer but mistypes one digit; the money lands in the account of Samir Khoury in Akko. Samir sees it, assumes it is a late payment from a client, and spends ₪7,000 on a used car. Two weeks later Noa's bank contacts Samir's bank. Separately, while Noa's neighbour, Dov, is abroad, a pipe bursts in his flat; Noa calls a plumber and pays ₪1,800 to stop the flood, which also saves her own ceiling below.

1. **Transfer: elements.** Samir received money (benefit), from Noa (link), without any legal basis (no debt between them): s. 1 applies.
2. **Defences.** Samir argues he spent ₪7,000 in good faith (s. 2, "other circumstances"). The money is fungible and he bought an asset that remains with him; the court is likely to order full restitution, perhaps with instalments. He incurred no expenses to obtain the benefit (s. 3).
3. **Action.** Noa writes to Samir (through the banks if she lacks his address), demands ₪12,000 within 14 days, then sues in small claims.
4. **Plumber.** Noa acted in good faith and reasonably to protect Dov's property (s. 5(א)); Dov did not object and had no reason to. She recovers reasonable expenses. Dov may argue that she also protected her own ceiling; the court may apportion, but s. 5 does not require that the helper gain nothing.
5. **Not s. 4.** She did not pay Dov's debt; she paid her own debt to the plumber for an act that saved Dov's property.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A situation of your own where someone holds a benefit that came from you (a deposit not returned after a deal fell through, a mistaken payment, shared expenses paid by you), or Noa's facts.
- The [demand letter to a company](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md) or [pre-action letter](../../templates/pre-action-letter.md), adapted to an individual.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. Run the six-point checklist above in writing.
2. Decide whether a special regime applies (contract, cancellation, illegality); if so, name it and stop using s. 1 for that part.
3. Value the benefit and identify any s. 2–3 arguments against you.
4. Draft a restitution demand: facts, the benefit, s. 1, the sum, a deadline, and what you will do next.
5. Add one paragraph answering the strongest defence in advance.

### Template

Adapt the [pre-action letter](../../templates/pre-action-letter.md); for a company recipient, the [demand letter to a company](../../templates/demand-letter-company.md).

### How you know it worked

- The letter states the three elements in three sentences.
- It names the special regime if one applies.
- The sum is calculated and supported by a document (bank confirmation, receipt).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Restitution claims of modest size are typical small claims, which an individual brings himself; magistrates' court above the ceiling. Claims that try to use unjust enrichment to protect ideas, designs or business methods are legally complex and need a lawyer.

### Where and how

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

- Mistaken transfer: your bank first (in writing), then a demand, then a claim. Complaints about a bank's handling go to the Bank of Israel's banking supervision public enquiries (⚠️ manual check).
- Court: small claims within the ceiling, with the [small claim template](../../templates/small-claim-contract-consumer.md) adapted to restitution; fees in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The claim is about profits from someone's breach of contract (an Adras-type claim) or about commercial copying.
- The recipient is insolvent or the money has moved abroad.
- The amount is large and the recipient claims a legal basis (a gift, a set-off, a debt).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suing in unjust enrichment where a contract governs | Claim fails under s. 6(א) | Re-plead under the contract and the Remedies Law | Check for a contract first |
| Waiting for the bank to "reverse" a transfer | Months lost; limitation runs | Demand and sue | Act within days |
| Claiming your loss, not his benefit | Wrong measure | Value the defendant's gain | Measure the benefit |
| Ignoring the recipient's expenses (s. 3) | Overclaim; costs against you | Deduct reasonable expenses | Anticipate s. 3 |
| Treating every copying of an idea as unjust enrichment | Claim dismissed | Show an extra element or an IP right | Get IP advice early |

## Review

- Section 1: a benefit, from the plaintiff, without a legal basis, must be returned in kind or in value.
- The plaintiff's loss is not an element; its absence is a ground for discretionary exemption (s. 2); the defendant may deduct reasonable expenses (s. 3).
- Paying another's debt (s. 4) and protecting another's interests (s. 5) have their own conditions.
- The law is subsidiary to special statutes and agreements (s. 6); after cancellation, restitution follows the Remedies Law.
- Adras allowed restitution of profits from breach; Telran left room for unjust enrichment where copyright gives no protection.
