# 25.1 · Principles of income tax: residency and sources of income

By the end of this lesson you will be able to answer the two questions every income-tax analysis starts with: is this person an Israeli resident for this tax year, and which source of income does each receipt belong to? You will apply the centre-of-life test and its day-count presumptions, sort receipts into the ten sources of s. 2 of the Income Tax Ordinance, and separate taxable income from a capital gain, which is taxed under different rules.

## Why it matters

Israeli income tax is a tax on a *person* (the resident) and on *sources* (the categories of income the Ordinance lists). Residency decides the reach of the tax: a resident is taxed on income produced in Israel and abroad; a non-resident only on income produced in Israel (s. 2). The source decides almost everything else: the rate, the deductions, whether the income is "from personal exertion" (יגיעה אישית) and so taxed on the lower brackets, whether tax is withheld at source, and whether the person must file a return. A person who moves abroad for work, an immigrant, a student with a summer job and a part-time online trader all ask the same two questions. Getting them wrong produces either a large assessment years later or a refund that is never claimed.

## The law

### Residency: the centre of life

> ”תושב ישראל“ או ”תושב“ – (א) לגבי יחיד – מי שמרכז חייו בישראל; ולענין זה יחולו הוראות אלה: (1) לשם קביעת מקום מרכז חייו של יחיד, יובאו בחשבון מכלול קשריו המשפחתיים, הכלכליים והחברתיים [...]

*Gist:* An individual is an Israeli resident if his centre of life is in Israel, judged by all his family, economic and social ties. [Income Tax Ordinance s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה#סעיף_1)

The definition lists the ties to weigh: the permanent home; where he and his family live; his usual or permanent occupation or place of employment; his active and substantial economic interests; and his activity in organisations and institutions (s. 1, para. (א)(1)(א)–(ה)).

> חזקה היא שמרכז חייו של יחיד בשנת המס הוא בישראל – (א) אם שהה בישראל בשנת המס 183 ימים או יותר; (ב) אם שהה בישראל בשנת המס 30 ימים או יותר, וסך כל תקופת שהייתו בישראל בשנת המס ובשנתיים שקדמו לה הוא 425 ימים או יותר;

*Gist:* There are two presumptions of residency: 183 days or more in Israel in the tax year, or 30 days or more in the year and 425 days or more over that year and the two before it; part of a day counts as a day. [s. 1, para. (א)(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה#סעיף_1)

The presumptions are rebuttable "both by the individual and by the assessing officer" (para. (א)(3)). So a person who spent 200 days in Israel may still show his centre of life is abroad, and a person who spent 100 days may still be a resident because his family and business are here. The presumptions shift the burden; they do not decide the case. The Ordinance also requires an individual who meets the presumption but claims it is rebutted to file a report with the facts and documents (s. 131(א)(5ה)).

### Sources of income

> מס הכנסה יהא משתלם [...] על הכנסתו של אדם תושב ישראל שהופקה או שנצמחה בישראל או מחוץ לישראל ועל הכנסתו של אדם תושב חוץ שהופקה או שנצמחה בישראל, ממקורות אלה:

*Gist:* Income tax is charged on a resident's income produced in Israel or abroad, and on a non-resident's income produced in Israel, from the sources listed in s. 2. [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה#סעיף_2)

The sources, in order:

1. business or profession, including a one-off transaction "of a commercial character" (s. 2(1));
2. employment, including every benefit from the employer and expense payments (s. 2(2));
3. (repealed);
4. dividends, interest, linkage differentials and discounts (s. 2(4));
5. pensions and annuities (s. 2(5));
6. rent and other income from land or buildings (s. 2(6));
7. income from other property (s. 2(7));
8. agriculture (s. 2(8));
9. sale of a patent or copyright by an inventor or author outside his ordinary occupation (s. 2(9));
10. any other source not excluded or exempted (s. 2(10)).

A gain from selling an asset is not one of these sources unless it is business income: it is a *capital gain*, taxed under Part E of the Ordinance (securities, shares, a business) or, for land, under the Real Estate Taxation Law (lesson 25.7). That is why the line between "revenue" and "capital" matters.

### Rates for individuals in 2026

> המס על הכנסתו החייבת של יחיד בשנת המס יהיה כלהלן: [...]

*Gist:* Section 121 sets progressive brackets; for income from personal exertion (and income of a person aged 60 or more) the lower brackets start at 10% and rise through 14%, 20%, 31% and 35% to 47%, with an additional tax on very high incomes (s. 121ב). The 2026 bracket amounts are on the [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) page. [s. 121](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_מס_הכנסה#סעיף_121)

Income that is not from personal exertion (for example rent taxed under the regular rules) starts at 31%, which is why classification as "personal exertion" can be worth a lot. The Wikisource note records that the 2026 and 2027 brackets differ from 2025: the 20% and 31% brackets were widened.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 165/82 קיבוץ חצור נ׳ פקיד שומה רחובות (1985)

The question was whether a kibbutz that owned an approved enterprise had to pay a security loan levied on "benefited income" under the Encouragement of Capital Investments Law. Until then Israeli courts, following English law, read tax statutes strictly by their words and gave the benefit of a doubt to the taxpayer. Justice Barak (Justices Netanyahu and Goldberg concurring) held that a tax statute is interpreted like any other statute: the language is the starting point but not the end, and among the meanings the words allow the court chooses the one that serves the provision's purpose; the kibbutz was liable and its appeal was dismissed. Why it matters: since Hatzor, a taxpayer cannot rely on a literal gap in the wording if the purpose of the provision clearly covers his case, and the Tax Authority cannot rely on wording that defeats the provision's purpose; this is the method taught in [lesson 02.4](../module-02/lesson-04.md). Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia article on the ruling](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פסק_דין_קיבוץ_חצור_נגד_פקיד_השומה_רחובות) (secondary; the official text ⚠️ was not opened).

### ע״א 9187/06 רפאל מגיד נ׳ פקיד שומה פתח תקווה

An insurance company's investment manager traded shares for himself and profited by more than ₪1.8 million, partly by manipulating prices; he claimed the profit was a capital gain, then untaxed. Justice Danziger, for the court, collected ten indications for telling revenue (business) income from capital: the nature of the asset; the frequency of transactions; turnover; how the activity was financed; the holding period; the use of the proceeds; the taxpayer's expertise; an organised apparatus; improvement and marketing of the asset; and the surrounding circumstances as an overall test. Applying them, all but one pointed to business income. Why it matters: the Magid list is the standard checklist for day-traders, people who build and sell apartments, and anyone with a "hobby" that turned into a business. Source: [Hebrew Wikipedia article on the ruling](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פסק_דין_מגיד) (secondary; ⚠️ official text not opened).

## How it works in practice

### Who decides and where

The assessing officer (פקיד שומה) of the district where you live or work decides residency and classification in an assessment (lesson 25.5). You state your position in your annual return (lesson 25.2). An employer decides, in practice, how much tax to withhold from salary, using the Form 101 you give it (lesson 25.2).

### Residency in practice

1. Count days in Israel for the year and the two years before (your entry and exit stamps; the Population Authority can issue a record of border crossings ⚠️ manual check of the current gov.il service).
2. List the ties: home (owned or rented, kept or let), spouse and children, job, business, bank accounts, pension and health insurance, clubs, voting, driving licence.
3. If the day count and the ties point the same way, you have your answer. If they conflict, the ties usually decide, and you must document them.
4. A person leaving Israel who wants to stop being a resident should keep evidence of the move abroad (lease, work contract, children's school) and, where needed, file the s. 131(א)(5ה) report.
5. A new immigrant or a returning resident after a long absence has special exemptions for foreign income (s. 14); and double-tax treaties can change the result for a person resident in two countries (s. 196). Both are beyond this lesson; see an accountant.

### Classification in practice

For each receipt ask: what is it for (work, business, capital, property, a gift)? Who paid it (an employer withholding tax, a client, a bank)? Is it regular or one-off? Then check the Magid indications for anything that looks like "selling at a profit".

## Worked example

Noa Ben-David, 34, a software engineer from Haifa, moved to Berlin on 1 March 2026 for a two-year contract. Her husband Amir and their daughter stayed in Haifa until 1 July and then joined her. They kept their Haifa apartment and let it to tenants from August. Noa visited Israel for 20 days in December. During 2026 she also sold shares she had held for six years through an Israeli bank.

1. **Days.** Noa was in Israel for 59 days in January–February and 20 in December: 79 days. The 183-day presumption does not apply. In 2024 and 2025 she was in Israel almost all year, so 79 + 2 years of about 360 days each is more than 425, and 79 is more than 30: the second presumption applies (s. 1, para. (א)(2)(ב)).
2. **Ties.** For January–June her family, home and the Israeli job were in Israel. From July her family, home life and employment are in Berlin; the Haifa apartment is let, so it is an economic interest, not her home.
3. **Result.** The presumption makes her a resident for 2026 unless she rebuts it. She has a strong case that her centre of life moved during 2026, but the year as a whole is mixed. This is exactly the kind of case where a person files a return with the facts (s. 131(א)(5ה)) and takes advice, because treaty rules for the year of departure can matter.
4. **Sources.** The Berlin salary is employment income (s. 2(2)), produced abroad; if she is still a resident for part of the year it is taxable in Israel with a credit for German tax. The rent from Haifa is s. 2(6) income produced in Israel, taxable whether she is a resident or not. The share sale, held for six years with savings, has none of the Magid business indications: it is a capital gain on which the bank withholds tax.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your own tax year 2025 or 2026 (or the Ben-David case).
- Your days in Israel for three years (a diary or the border-crossing record).
- A list of everything you received during the year: salary slips, bank interest, rent, freelance invoices, sales of assets, gifts.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Day count (10 min).** Write the days in Israel for the year and the two before it. Does either presumption in s. 1 apply?
2. **Ties table (10 min).** Draw two columns, Israel and abroad, and list your family, home, work, economic and social ties.
3. **Residency decision (5 min).** One paragraph: resident or not, and why, citing s. 1.
4. **Source table (15 min).** For every receipt write the source in s. 2 (by paragraph), where it was produced, and whether tax was withheld.
5. **Capital or revenue (5 min).** For any sale of an asset, run the ten Magid indications and decide.

### Template

None.

### How you know it worked

- Your residency paragraph names both presumptions and says whether they are rebutted.
- Every receipt in your table has a paragraph of s. 2, or is marked "capital gain" or "not income" (for example a gift) with a reason.
- Your capital-or-revenue decision mentions at least four of the Magid indications.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Residency and classification are questions you answer in your own annual return and, if the assessing officer disagrees, in an objection (lessons 25.2 and 25.5). You may do both yourself; representing others before the Tax Authority is reserved to lawyers, accountants and representing tax advisers (Income Tax Ordinance s. 236; Bar Association Law s. 21; [can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md)).

### Where and how

- The Tax Authority's assessing office for your area; the Tax Authority's online personal area (⚠️ manual check of the current service on gov.il).
- Free help: the Tax Authority's call centre; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer (or accountant) when

- You lived in two countries in the same year, or you plan to move abroad or return after years away.
- You trade often (securities, crypto, apartments) and the profits are large.
- You received a letter from the assessing officer questioning your residency.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating 183 days abroad as automatic non-residency | Assessment on worldwide income | File a return with the facts; object if assessed | Weigh the ties, not just the days |
| Forgetting income from Israel after leaving (rent, pension) | Unreported Israeli-source income | Report it; non-residents are taxed on it too | List Israeli sources before you leave |
| Calling frequent trading "investment" | Reclassification as business income at higher rates | Run the Magid indications; argue the facts | Keep records of financing, holding periods and purpose |
| Relying on a literal gap in a section | Court applies the purpose (Hatzor) | Argue purpose as well as wording | Read the section's purpose before relying on it |

## Review

- An individual is a resident if his centre of life is in Israel; the 183-day and 30/425-day presumptions are rebuttable by either side (s. 1).
- Residents are taxed on worldwide income, non-residents on income produced in Israel, from the sources listed in s. 2.
- Capital gains are not a s. 2 source; Magid's ten indications decide whether a profit is business income or capital.
- Since Kibbutz Hatzor, tax statutes are read by their purpose, like any other statute.
- The 2026 brackets in s. 121 are on the current numbers page; income from personal exertion gets the lower brackets.
