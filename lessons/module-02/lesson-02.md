# 02.2 · Finding case law for free

By the end of this lesson you will be able to find Israeli rulings without a paid database, decode a case number at a glance, and check that the ruling you found has not been reversed or overtaken before you rely on it.

## Why it matters

A statute tells you the rule; rulings tell you how courts actually apply it. Under Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20 (lesson 01.5), a Supreme Court ruling binds every lower court, so a single paragraph of a Supreme Court judgment can decide your small claim. Lawyers find rulings through paid databases (Nevo, Takdin, Pador). You can do most of the same work for free, because the Judiciary itself publishes its decisions, and because a good ruling leads you to the others: every Israeli judgment cites the precedents it relies on.

The risk runs the other way too. Free search results include magistrates' decisions that were reversed on appeal, interim decisions mistaken for final judgments, and rulings the Supreme Court later departed from. Finding a ruling is half the job; checking it is the other half.

## The law

### Why Supreme Court rulings are worth finding

> (א) הלכה שנפסקה בבית משפט תנחה בית משפט של דרגה נמוכה ממנו.
> (ב) הלכה שנפסקה בבית המשפט העליון מחייבת כל בית משפט, זולת בית המשפט העליון.

*Gist:* A higher court's ruling guides lower courts; a Supreme Court ruling binds every court except the Supreme Court itself. ([Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_השפיטה))

Only the **holding** binds (lesson 02.3 shows how to separate it from the rest). A magistrates' court or small-claims ruling binds nobody but the parties; it is still useful to show how courts treat facts like yours.

### Why rulings are public, and when they are not

> (א) בית משפט ידון בפומבי.

*Gist:* Courts sit in public. ([Courts Law [Consolidated Version] 1984, s. 68(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

Section 68(ב) lets a court sit in closed session, for example to protect a minor, and s. 70 prohibits publishing what happened in a closed session without the court's permission:

> (א) לא יפרסם אדם דבר על דיון שהתנהל בבית משפט בדלתיים סגורות אלא ברשות בית המשפט.

*Gist:* No one may publish anything about a closed-door hearing without the court's permission. ([Courts Law, s. 70(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_המשפט))

This is why family, juvenile and many medical rulings appear with the parties as "פלוני נ' פלונית" (anonymous), and why some decisions are not online at all. When you share a ruling you found, keep its anonymisation; do not add names you know from elsewhere.

## Leading cases

### עע"ם 1758/10 לשכת עורכי הדין בישראל נ' שגיא (15.8.2011)

The Bar Association refused to admit a candidate who had passed the Bar exams, relying on a civil judgment that found he had taken company money, while a police investigation was still open. The Administrative Affairs Court held that civil findings were not enough. The Supreme Court (Justice Fogelman, Justices Hayut and Melcer concurring) allowed the Bar's appeal: findings in a civil judgment are admissible "other facts" under s. 44(א)(2) of the Bar Association Law, and deferring admission until the criminal investigation ends was reasonable and proportionate.

Why it matters here: it is a short, complete Supreme Court judgment you can read free on [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פסק_דין_עע%22ם_1758/10_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_בישראל_נ'_שלום_שגיא), and it shows every element you will look for in a ruling: the header with the court below (עת"מ 2622-07) and its date, the parties' arguments, the legal framework, the precedents cited with full citations (for example בג"ץ 987/94 יורונט קווי זהב (1992) בע"מ נ' שרת התקשורת, פ"ד מח(5) 412 (1994)), and the result. Each of those citations is a lead to the next ruling. Lesson 02.3 briefs it in full.

### עע"ם 9135/03 המועצה להשכלה גבוהה נ' הוצאת עיתון הארץ (19.1.2006)

A seven-justice bench held that a public authority may not refuse a freedom-of-information request merely because the material is internal deliberation: the exemptions in s. 9(ב) of the Freedom of Information Law give discretion, so the authority must weigh the public interest and the requester's interest and give reasons. Disclosure is the rule; refusal is the exception.

Why it matters here: it is the leading case for lesson 02.7, and it is one of many Israeli Supreme Court judgments available in full **English translation** on [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/council-higher-education-v-haaretz), a free project of Cardozo School of Law. Versa is the quickest way into a landmark case if your Hebrew legal reading is still slow; quote the Hebrew original in anything you file.

### ע"א 165/82 קיבוץ חצור נ' פקיד שומה רחובות, פ"ד לט(2) 70 (1985)

The leading statement of purposive interpretation in tax law (lesson 02.4). Why it matters here: it is an older ruling, published in the official Supreme Court reports (פ"ד, volume 39, part 2, page 70). Rulings of that age are often hard to find on the Judiciary's own sites. A free copy of the text is hosted by a private law office's archive ([efraty.com, Word file](https://www.efraty.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/khtsvr82.doc)). Private copies are fine for study; check the header (court, case number, judges, dates) against the citation, and in anything you file cite the official report, not the website.

## How it works in practice

### Decoding a case number

| You see | It means |
|---|---|
| ע"א 165/82 | Civil appeal (ערעור אזרחי) to the Supreme Court, file 165 of 1982 |
| ע"פ | Criminal appeal |
| בג"ץ | Petition to the High Court of Justice |
| רע"א / רע"פ | Application for leave to appeal, civil / criminal |
| דנ"א / דנ"פ | Further hearing, civil / criminal |
| עע"ם | Appeal in an administrative matter (from the Administrative Affairs Court) |
| עת"מ | Administrative petition in a District Court sitting as Administrative Affairs Court |
| ת"א / ת"ק / ת"פ / תמ"ש | Civil claim / small claim / criminal case / family-court claim |
| 23742-11-14 | Newer lower-court format: file 23742, opened in November 2014 |
| (שלום ת"א), (מחוזי חי') | Court and seat: Tel Aviv magistrates' court; Haifa District Court |

The full list of abbreviations is in appendices ד and ה of the [uniform citation rules](https://law.tau.ac.il/sites/law.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/law_heb/izkur/izkur2021pdf.pdf) (lesson 02.5).

### Where the free rulings are

| Source | What is there | How to search |
|---|---|---|
| [Supreme Court decisions site](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/) | Supreme Court judgments and decisions, including HCJ | By case number, party names and free text in the decision |
| [Net HaMishpat](https://www.court.gov.il/NGCS.Web.Site/HomePage.aspx), menu "איתור החלטות" and "חיפוש תיקים, פסקי דין והחלטות" | Decisions of the magistrates', district, family and labour courts in public cases; the site states that decisions are published "בחלוף 48 שעות מרגע חתימתם" | By court and case number; you usually need the number first |
| [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/) | English translations of leading Supreme Court judgments, with commentary | Keyword or case name in English |
| [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ויקיטקסט:ספר_החוקים_הפתוח) | A small number of rulings, and statutes that link to rulings affecting them | Search the ruling's name |
| Private free sites and law-office archives | Copies of many rulings, of uneven quality | Web search with the case number in quotation marks |
| Nevo, Takdin, Pador | Nearly everything, with citators | Paid; the course does not rely on them |

### Search strategies that work

1. **You have the case number.** Search it directly on the right site: Supreme Court numbers on the decisions site, lower-court numbers on Net HaMishpat. Lower-court searches usually need the court as well.
2. **You have a statute section.** Run a free-text search on the decisions site for the section and the law in Hebrew as courts write it, for example "סעיף 2(א) לחוק לתיקון סדרי המינהל". Then try the short form ("חוק ההחלטות וההנמקות").
3. **You have a doctrine or phrase.** Search the exact phrase in quotation marks ("ראיה מינהלית"). Courts reuse the wording of leading cases, so the leading case surfaces in many later judgments.
4. **You have one good ruling.** Read its list of cited rulings and follow the most cited ones ("snowballing back"). Then search the good ruling's own case number in free text to find later rulings that cite it ("snowballing forward").
5. **Web search as a finder.** A general search engine, with the case number in quotation marks, often finds a copy, a law-firm summary or an academic article. Use it to find the ruling, then get the text from the court's site or verify the copy.

### Is it still good law?

Before you rely on a ruling, check:

- **Finality.** Is it a judgment or an interim decision? Was it appealed? Search the case number in free text: an appeal judgment's header says "ערעור על פסק דינו של בית המשפט… בתיק…".
- **Further hearing.** Supreme Court rulings can be reheard by an expanded bench (דיון נוסף). Search for "דנ"א" or "דנ"פ" with the original number.
- **Later departures.** Snowball forward: later rulings may distinguish, narrow or overrule it. A ruling from 1985 may have been "refined" ten times since.
- **Statutory change.** A ruling interpreting a section that has since been amended (lesson 02.1) may no longer apply.
- **Level of court.** Only Supreme Court holdings bind. A district court ruling is persuasive; a small-claims ruling is an illustration.

## Worked example

Rami Khoury, a teacher in Haifa, asked the Ministry of Education for the internal minutes of a committee that rejected his request for a transfer. The Ministry refused, saying the minutes are "internal discussions". He wants to know whether that is the end of it.

1. **From statute to section.** The Freedom of Information Law on Wikisource shows that internal discussions are in s. 9(ב)(4), a section on information the authority "אינה חייבת למסור", meaning it **may** refuse, not must.
2. **Find the leading case.** A web search for "סעיף 9(ב)(4) לחוק חופש המידע דיונים פנימיים" returns many rulings and articles that cite עע"ם 9135/03 המועצה להשכלה גבוהה נ' הוצאת עיתון הארץ.
3. **Read it.** Rami reads the English translation on Versa in 40 minutes, then finds the Hebrew paragraphs he wants to quote by searching the case number on the Supreme Court decisions site.
4. **Check it is still good law.** He runs a free-text search for "9135/03" on the decisions site and skims a few recent rulings: they still cite it as the starting point for s. 9(ב) refusals. He checks the Wikisource amendment marks on s. 9: no change that affects (ב)(4) since the ruling.
5. **Result.** The Ministry may refuse only after weighing the interests and giving reasons; a bare "internal discussions" label is not enough. Rami writes back (lesson 02.7) asking the Ministry to reconsider and explain its balancing, and notes that the decision can be challenged by petition to the Administrative Affairs Court (Freedom of Information Law s. 17).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Your question and statute section from lesson 02.1.
- A browser; a document to keep notes.
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Run a free-text search on the Supreme Court decisions site for your section (strategy 2). Record how many results you get and open the three most recent.
2. In each, find the paragraph that discusses your section. Copy the case number, parties, date and paragraph number.
3. From the most useful one, follow two cited rulings back (strategy 4). Record their citations as they appear in the text.
4. Search one of them forward: free-text search its case number. Note whether later rulings follow, distinguish or criticise it.
5. Find one lower-court decision on similar facts (web search with the section and a key fact, then confirm on Net HaMishpat if you can). Record the court and level.
6. For each ruling, answer: final or interim? appealed? binding, persuasive or illustrative?
7. If any ruling has a Versa translation, read the English summary and note the Hebrew paragraph numbers you would quote.

### Template

None. Keep your notes; lesson 02.3 briefs one of the rulings with [the case brief template](../../templates/case-brief.md).

### How you know it worked

You have at least four rulings on your question, each with a complete citation, its court level, and a status check. You could hand the list to someone else and they could find every ruling from your notes alone.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can say what each abbreviation in my case numbers means.
- [ ] I separated Supreme Court rulings from lower-court decisions.
- [ ] I checked each ruling for appeals or later departures.
- [ ] I checked that the statute section the ruling interprets has not since been amended.
- [ ] I kept any anonymisation (פלוני) as published.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Court decisions in public cases are published for everyone. Researching case law for your own matter is not restricted. Doing it for others as a service is legal advice reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(4); lesson 01.1).

### Where and how

- Supreme Court: [decisions site](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il/).
- Other courts: [Net HaMishpat](https://www.court.gov.il/NGCS.Web.Site/HomePage.aspx), "איתור החלטות". The Judiciary's information centre is reachable at *3852 or 077-2703333 (numbers shown on the Net HaMishpat home page on 2026-10-16) if you cannot find a decision in a public case.
- English: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/).
- No fee for searching or reading. Copies certified by the court for filing are a separate matter (module 8).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Rulings on your question conflict and the stakes are high: lawyers know which line the courts currently follow.
- The leading ruling is under further hearing or the law changed after it.
- You need a decision from a closed-door proceeding: access needs the court's permission.
- The other side relies on a paid-database ruling you cannot read and it seems decisive.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on a magistrates' ruling as if it binds | The judge dismisses it as one colleague's view | Find the Supreme Court or district ruling it relies on and cite that | Always note the court level next to each ruling |
| Quoting a decision that was reversed on appeal | Your argument rests on a ruling with no force; your credibility drops | Cite the appeal judgment instead and explain the change | Search every case number forward before you rely on it |
| Treating an interim decision as the outcome | You misstate what the court decided | Find the final judgment in the same file | Check whether the document is titled פסק דין or החלטה |
| Citing a summary or blog post instead of the ruling | The summary is wrong or incomplete; the court cannot check it | Get the ruling's text and quote it with paragraph numbers | Use summaries only to find rulings |
| Ignoring a publication ban | You expose a party, and may break the law | Remove identifying details; follow the published anonymisation | Never add names to a ruling published as פלוני |

## Review

- Supreme Court holdings bind every other court (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 20); lower-court rulings persuade or illustrate.
- Supreme Court decisions are on the Court's decisions site; lower-court decisions in public cases are on Net HaMishpat, published 48 hours after signing.
- A case number tells you the proceeding type, the court and the year; learn the common abbreviations.
- One good ruling leads to the others: follow its citations back and search its number forward.
- Before relying on a ruling, check finality, appeals, further hearings, later departures and statutory change.
