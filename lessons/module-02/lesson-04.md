# 02.4 · Statutory interpretation: language, purpose and the Barak method

By the end of this lesson you will be able to read a statutory provision the way Israeli courts read it: check the Interpretation Law's built-in rules, map the meanings the words can bear, choose among them by purpose, and know when special rules or competing views change the answer.

## Why it matters

Almost every legal dispute you will have turns on words in a statute or a regulation: what counts as "in writing", when a period ends, whether "or other" covers your case, whether "abuse" includes a farming practice. You can lose a strong case by reading a word the obvious way when the courts read it another way, and you can win a weak-looking one by showing that the purpose of the provision covers you.

Israel has a distinctive method. Since the 1980s the Supreme Court, led by Aharon Barak, has applied **purposive interpretation** (פרשנות תכליתית): language sets the limits, purpose chooses within them. The method is settled in the courts, but it is not uncontested, and several current justices argue for giving more weight to the legislature's actual intent. You need to know both the method and the debate.

## The law

### The Interpretation Law: defaults, not dogma

> חוק זה יחול לגבי כל חיקוק והוראת מינהל, אף אם ניתנו לפני תחילתו, אם אין הוראה אחרת לענין הנדון ואם אין בענין הנדון או בהקשרו דבר שאינו מתיישב עם חוק זה…

*Gist:* The Interpretation Law applies to every enactment unless the enactment says otherwise or the context is inconsistent with it. ([Interpretation Law 1981, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הפרשנות))

Its rules are defaults. The most useful in daily practice:

> "בכתב" - לרבות בכל דרך אחרת של הצגת אותיות, ספרות או סימנים בצורה הנראית לעין או הניתנת לפענוח חזותי;

*Gist:* "In writing" includes any visible or visually decodable representation of letters, numbers or symbols. (s. 3)

> המלים "או", "אחר" או ביטוי דומה להן - להבדיל מן המפורט שלפניהן הן באות ולא להקיש לו, זולת אם יש עמן המלים "דומה" או "כיוצא בזה" או ביטוי אחר שמשמעו היקש.

*Gist:* "Or" and "other" after a list add something different from the listed items, not something of the same kind, unless the text says "similar" or "and the like". (s. 7) This reverses the common-law ejusdem generis presumption.

> (א) מקום שנקבעה תקופה קצובה במספר ימים או שבועות מיום פלוני, אותו יום לא יבוא במנין.
> (ב) תקופה קצובה במספר חדשים או שנים לאחר אירוע פלוני תסתיים בחדשה האחרון ביום שמספרו בחודש כמספר יום האירוע…
> (ג) במנין ימי תקופה יבואו גם ימי מנוחה, פגרה או שבתון שעל פי חיקוק, זולת אם הם הימים האחרונים שבתקופה.

*Gist:* For periods in days, do not count the starting day; a period in months ends on the same date in the last month; rest days and holidays count, except when they fall at the end of the period. (s. 10)

Also: "person" includes a body of persons (s. 4), singular includes plural (s. 5), masculine includes feminine and vice versa (s. 6).

### When the law is silent: the Foundations of Law Act

> ראה בית המשפט שאלה משפטית הטעונה הכרעה, ולא מצא לה תשובה בדבר חקיקה, בהלכה פסוקה או בדרך של היקש, יכריע בה לאור עקרונות החירות, הצדק, היושר והשלום של המשפט העברי ומורשת ישראל.

*Gist:* Only where legislation, precedent and analogy give no answer does the court turn to the principles of freedom, justice, equity and peace of Jewish heritage. ([Foundations of Law Act 1980, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יסודות_המשפט))

Interpretation comes first; a true gap is rare. Module 10 returns to this section.

### A special rule for criminal law

> ניתן דין לפירושים סבירים אחדים לפי תכליתו, יוכרע הענין לפי הפירוש המקל ביותר עם מי שאמור לשאת באחריות פלילית לפי אותו דין.

*Gist:* If, after purposive interpretation, several reasonable readings remain, the one most lenient to the person facing criminal liability prevails. ([Penal Law 1977, s. 34כא](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_העונשין))

Note the order: purpose first, lenity only if purpose leaves more than one reasonable reading.

## Leading cases

### ע"א 165/82 קיבוץ חצור נ' פקיד שומה רחובות, פ"ד לט(2) 70 (1985)

A kibbutz argued that a compulsory "security loan" on "benefited income" did not reach it, because of how the word "חל" (applies) linked two sections of the Capital Investments Encouragement Law. Justice Barak (with Justices Netanyahu and Goldberg) dismissed the appeal, and in doing so stated the method that Israeli courts still use:

> חייבת להיות נקודת אחיזה ארכימדית לפירוש הנכון בלשון החוק… אלא אם כן כל משמעות המעוגנת בלשון מביאה לאבסורד.

> לעולם יש לפתוח בלשון החוק, אך לעולם אין לסיים בה.

*Gist:* Every interpretation needs at least a minimal anchor in the words; start with the language, never finish with it.

He added that no wording is "clear" in itself: whether language is clear is the conclusion of interpretation, not its starting point; purpose is learned from the law itself, its structure, the problem it was meant to solve and its legislative history; and if purpose does not resolve the question, the judge interprets against the system's basic values. Tax laws are interpreted by the same method. Why it matters: it ended the earlier habit of reading tax statutes strictly literally and is the standard citation for purposive interpretation. Free copy: [efraty.com (Word file)](https://www.efraty.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/khtsvr82.doc).

### בג"ץ 9232/01 "נח" – ההתאחדות הישראלית של הארגונים להגנת בעלי חיים נ' היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (11.8.2003)

Was force-feeding geese "התעללות" (abuse) prohibited by s. 2(א) of the Cruelty to Animals Law, given that s. 19 tells the Minister of Agriculture to consider "agricultural needs" when making regulations? Justice Strasberg-Cohen read "abuse" in light of the statute's purpose of protecting animals and the system's values, balancing the purpose of the practice, its means and the suffering caused; she held that force-feeding is abuse and annulled the regulations. Justice Grunis, dissenting, gave more weight to "agricultural needs" as part of the statutory context. Why it matters: the same words, the same method, different weight to different contextual signals, different results. That is what purposive interpretation looks like in a hard case. English text: [Versa](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/%E2%80%9Cnoah%E2%80%9D-israeli-federation-animal-protection-organizations-v-attorney-general).

### עע"ם 1758/10 לשכת עורכי הדין בישראל נ' שגיא (15.8.2011)

An everyday example of the method. Section 44(א)(1) of the Bar Association Law allows refusal after a criminal conviction; (א)(2) after "עובדות אחרות" (other facts). The Court read (א)(2) by its language, contrasted with (א)(1), and by its purpose of protecting the profession's integrity, to include findings in a civil judgment. Free text: [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פסק_דין_עע%22ם_1758/10_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_בישראל_נ'_שלום_שגיא).

## How it works in practice

### The method in four steps

1. **Check the defaults.** Is the word defined in the statute itself (Interpretation Law s. 2) or in the Interpretation Law (s. 3)? Is a period, a list or a gendered term involved (ss. 5 to 10)? Does the context exclude the default (s. 1)?
2. **Map the language.** List every meaning the words can bear in ordinary and legal Hebrew. Meanings with no anchor in the words are out (Hatzor), unless every anchored meaning is absurd.
3. **Find the purpose.**
   - *Subjective purpose:* what the Knesset meant. Sources: explanatory notes (דברי הסבר) in the bill, committee protocols and plenary debates, all reachable from the law's page in the Knesset database (lesson 02.1).
   - *Objective purpose:* what a reasonable legislature in a democratic system would aim at, reflected in the statute's structure, related laws and basic values such as equality, fairness and the rights protected by the Basic Laws.
4. **Choose** the anchored meaning that best achieves the purpose. If the purposes conflict, say which you prefer and why. In criminal law, if more than one reasonable meaning survives, take the most lenient (Penal Law s. 34כא).

### The debate you should know

Barak's version of the method, developed in his writing and judgments, gave objective purpose considerable weight, especially in older statutes and in laws touching rights. Critics, including Justice Noam Sohlberg in several opinions, argue that when the legislature's actual intent can be identified from the text and its history, it should prevail; that heavy reliance on objective purpose lets judges substitute their values for the Knesset's; and that this weakens legal certainty and the separation of powers. Supporters answer that statutes must be read as part of a coherent legal system, that legislative intent is often unknowable or does not foresee new situations, and that values the Knesset itself entrenched in Basic Laws are a legitimate guide. A related doctrine, "maintaining" interpretation (פרשנות מקיימת), prefers a reading that keeps a law valid over one that would make it unconstitutional; it too is applied with different intensity by different justices. An overview with references to the leading opinions on each side: [Hebrew Wikipedia, פרשנות תכליתית](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פרשנות_תכליתית).

For your own writing the practical consequence is simple: argue both purposes. Show what the explanatory notes say (subjective) and why your reading fits the system's values (objective). An argument that wins on both is hard to answer.

### Where to find purpose quickly

| Source | Where | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| The section's own wording and neighbours | Wikisource text | Highest: always start here |
| Definitions section and the law's "purpose" section (if any, often s. 1) | Wikisource text | High |
| Explanatory notes to the bill | Knesset database, law page, bill PDF | Strong evidence of subjective purpose |
| Committee protocols | Knesset database | Useful, but one member's remarks are not the Knesset's intent |
| Leading rulings on the section | Lesson 02.2 | Binding if Supreme Court holding |

## Worked example

Olga (lesson 02.1) emailed her request for a residents' parking permit to the municipality's official address on Wednesday 4 March 2026; the city's automatic reply confirmed receipt the same day. Two questions.

**Was the request "בכתב" (in writing) under s. 2(א) of the Decisions and Reasons Law?**

1. Defaults: the Decisions and Reasons Law does not define "בכתב". The Interpretation Law s. 3 does: "לרבות בכל דרך אחרת של הצגת אותיות… בצורה הנראית לעין". An email shows letters visibly on a screen.
2. Context (s. 1): nothing in the Decisions and Reasons Law suggests paper only. Its purpose, to make officials answer written requests on time, is served by email at least as well as by paper.
3. Conclusion: yes. (Keep the automatic reply: it proves the date of receipt.)

**When did the 45 days end, and when does silence become a refusal?**

1. Interpretation Law s. 10(א): the day of receipt is not counted. Day 1 is 5 March; day 45 is Saturday 18 April 2026.
2. Section 10(ג): rest days inside the period (Saturdays, and the Passover holiday days in early April) count; but a rest day that is the **last** day does not. Saturday 18 April is the last day, so the period ends on Sunday 19 April 2026.
3. Section 6(ב) of the Decisions and Reasons Law: no answer within "שלושת החדשים" is treated as a refusal without reasons. Section 10(ב): a period in months ends on the same date: 4 June 2026.
4. Result: from 20 April Olga can write that the city has breached s. 2(א); from 5 June she can treat the silence as a refusal and consider challenging it (module 8 and year 2's administrative law module). The deadlines used here are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The provision from your lesson 02.1 research, and the rulings from 02.2.
- The Knesset database page of the law (for explanatory notes).
- 60 minutes.

### Steps

1. Copy the provision. Underline every word that could be read in more than one way.
2. For each underlined word, check the statute's own definitions, then the Interpretation Law s. 3.
3. Apply any Interpretation Law default that is relevant (periods, lists, singular/plural, gender).
4. List the possible meanings of the key word (at least two).
5. Open the original bill's explanatory notes and copy the sentence that explains why the provision exists.
6. Write two sentences on the objective purpose: which values or related laws point to one reading.
7. Check your 02.2 rulings: has a court already interpreted this word? If a Supreme Court holding exists, it decides the question.
8. Write a one-paragraph interpretation: words, purpose, conclusion. If the provision is criminal, add the lenity step.
9. Compute one real deadline from your own life using s. 10, and write it down with the date of the triggering event.

### Template

None. Your paragraph goes into the "Analysis" section of [the research memo template](../../templates/research-memo.md) in lesson 02.6.

### How you know it worked

Your interpretation names the meanings the words can bear, cites the explanatory notes or a ruling for purpose, and reaches a conclusion that someone who disagrees would have to answer on the merits. Your deadline calculation states the triggering date, the rule applied and the end date.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. Interpreting a provision for your own matter is what every self-represented party does in every letter and pleading. It shows up in practice when you argue that a deadline has passed, that a right covers you, or that an authority read its power too widely.

### Where and how

Statutes on Wikisource; explanatory notes and protocols on the Knesset database; rulings as in lesson 02.2. Courts hearing self-represented parties focus on substance, but a clear interpretation paragraph with a source for purpose makes a judge's work easier.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The provision is criminal and your liberty or record is at stake.
- Your reading departs from a Supreme Court holding, or depends on the court adopting a contested theory.
- A deadline is close and its calculation is genuinely uncertain: file early rather than argue later.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating the words as "clear" and stopping | The court adopts a purposive reading you never addressed | Add a purpose paragraph; answer the other reading | Always map at least two meanings and check purpose |
| Arguing a meaning the words cannot bear | The argument is rejected outright (no "Archimedean point") | Find a meaning anchored in the text | Test each meaning against the words before arguing it |
| Counting the first day of a period | You think a deadline passed a day early, or you file a day late | Recount under s. 10(א) | Write "day 1 = the day after" on every calculation |
| Assuming a holiday at the end does not matter | You miss the extra day, or rely on an extension that does not exist | Check whether the rest day is the last day (s. 10(ג)) | Check the weekday and holidays of the last day |
| Relying on one MK's speech as "the purpose" | The court gives it little weight | Rely on explanatory notes and the statute's structure | Treat debates as supporting, not decisive, evidence |
| Using lenity before purpose | The argument misreads s. 34כא | Do the purposive analysis first | Remember the order: purpose, then lenity |

## Review

- The Interpretation Law gives default meanings (in writing, person, or/other) and counting rules (exclude the first day; months end on the same date; rest days count unless they are the last days).
- Israeli courts use purposive interpretation: the words set the range (with an Archimedean anchor), purpose chooses within it (Kibbutz Hatzor).
- Purpose is subjective (the Knesset's intent, from explanatory notes and history) and objective (the system's values); how much weight objective purpose gets is debated on the Court.
- In criminal law, if purpose leaves several reasonable readings, the most lenient wins (Penal Law s. 34כא); only a true gap sends the court to the Foundations of Law Act.
- Argue both purposes, cite a source for each, and check for an existing Supreme Court holding first.
