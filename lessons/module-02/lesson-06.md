# 02.6 · Practice lab: a research memo

In this lab you research a real legal question from your own life from start to finish and write it up as a research memo (תזכיר משפטי) of two to four pages, the document lawyers write before they advise or act, and the one you will write before every step you take on your own.

## Why it matters

Research that stays in browser tabs is lost by next week. A memo forces you to state the question precisely, separate facts from law, deal with the arguments against you, and say how sure you are. It is also the document that tells you when to stop: if the memo's honest conclusion is "it depends on a contested question and a lot of money", you need a lawyer, and the memo will save that lawyer, and you, hours.

## The law

The memo applies whatever law your question involves. Two provisions frame the lab itself.

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין… (4) ייעוץ וחיווי דעת משפטיים.

*Gist:* Giving legal advice and legal opinions as an occupation or for payment is reserved to lawyers. ([Bar Association Law 1961, s. 20(4)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין)) A memo on your own matter is not advice to anyone else; a memo written for a friend's matter, for pay or as a habit, would be.

The worked example below uses the Decisions and Reasons Law, the duty of a public official to answer a written request:

> נתבקש עובד הציבור, בכתב, להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין, יחליט בבקשה וישיב למבקש בכתב בהקדם, אך לא יאוחר מארבעים וחמישה ימים מיום קבלת הבקשה.

*Gist:* A public servant asked in writing to use a statutory power must decide and answer in writing promptly, and within 45 days of receiving the request. ([Decisions and Reasons Law 1958, s. 2(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_סדרי_המינהל_%28החלטות_והנמקות%29); the period is listed in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

## How it works in practice

### The structure

| Part | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Heading | Your name, date, "Memo in my own matter, not legal advice to others", the question in one line | 4 lines |
| 1. Question presented | The legal question, specific to your facts, ending in a question mark | 1 to 3 sentences |
| 2. Short answer | Yes / no / probably, plus the one-sentence reason | 2 to 4 sentences |
| 3. Facts | Only facts that matter legally, with dates and documents; mark facts you have not confirmed | Half a page |
| 4. Legal framework | Each statute section quoted (Hebrew), each ruling cited and summarised | Half to one page |
| 5. Analysis | Apply the law to the facts, issue by issue; interpretation (02.4); counter-arguments and your answers | One to two pages |
| 6. Conclusion and next steps | What you will do, by when, with which document | Short list |
| 7. Open questions and risks | What you could not verify; what would change the answer; when you would get a lawyer | Short list |
| Sources | Full citations (02.5) with links and the date you checked them | List |

### Rules of good memo writing

- **Answer first.** The reader, often you in three months, should get the answer from part 2.
- **One issue at a time.** If the question has sub-questions (deadline? remedy? forum?), give each its own heading in the analysis.
- **Quote, don't paraphrase, the decisive words** of a statute or ruling, with a pinpoint.
- **Argue against yourself.** For each conclusion, write the best argument the other side would make and why it fails, or concede that it might succeed.
- **Grade your confidence.** "Clear" (statute plain, no contrary authority), "likely" (good arguments, some risk), "uncertain" (contested or no authority).
- **Date it.** Law changes (lesson 02.1); a memo is true as of the date you checked.

## Worked example

Nadia Halabi, a nurse from Daliyat al-Karmel, asked her local council in writing to fix a broken streetlight on her road and to tell her whether the council has a maintenance plan for the street. Her request was emailed on Sunday 7 June 2026; the council's system confirmed receipt the same day. On 1 September 2026 she still has no answer. Her memo, condensed:

**Heading:** Nadia Halabi, 1.9.2026. Memo in my own matter, not legal advice to others. Question: must the council answer my request, and what can I do now?

**1. Question presented.** Did the local council breach a legal duty by not answering, within 45 days, a written request emailed on 7.6.2026 to repair a streetlight and to disclose its maintenance plan; and what remedies are open to me?

**2. Short answer.** Probably yes. The council was required to answer in writing within 45 days (Decisions and Reasons Law s. 2(א)), that is by 22.7.2026; it did not, and did not notify a reason for delay under s. 2(ג). From 8.9.2026 its silence is treated as a refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)). The maintenance-plan part of the request is better made as a freedom-of-information request. Confidence: clear on the deadline, likely on the duty, uncertain on remedies.

**3. Facts.** Email of 7.6.2026 to the council's official address (copy and automatic receipt saved). No answer, no interim notice. Streetlight still broken (photos 7.6, 15.7, 1.9). Not confirmed: whether the council has a written maintenance plan.

**4. Legal framework.**
- Decisions and Reasons Law: s. 1 ("עובד הציבור" includes a local-authority employee), s. 2(א) (answer within 45 days), s. 2(ג) (notice of reason for delay), s. 2ב (transfer to the competent official), s. 6(ב) (silence for three months is a refusal without reasons).
- Interpretation Law: s. 3 ("בכתב" includes any visible representation, so email qualifies); s. 10 (counting periods).
- Freedom of Information Law: s. 7 (application in writing, no need to give reasons, decision within 30 days, extendable); FOI fees regulations (an application fee, waived for information about yourself; see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- עע"ם 9135/03: refusal of an FOI request requires weighing and reasons.

**5. Analysis.**
- *Is a council employee covered?* Yes: s. 1 covers "עובד רשות מקומית".
- *Is a request to repair a streetlight a request "להשתמש בסמכות שניתנה לו על פי דין"?* Likely yes: maintaining streets is a statutory function of local authorities. Counter-argument: this is a routine service request, not the exercise of a "power". Answer: the Law's purpose, timely and reasoned answers from public bodies, covers requests to act on statutory functions; but I have not found a ruling on this point. Confidence: likely.
- *Deadline.* Receipt 7.6.2026, not counted (Interpretation Law s. 10(א)). Day 45 is Wednesday 22.7.2026, not a rest day. Breach from 23.7.2026.
- *Deemed refusal.* Three months from 7.6.2026 ends on 7.9.2026 (s. 10(ב)); from 8.9.2026 the silence is a refusal without reasons (s. 6(ב)). In any later proceeding the council bears the burden of showing its decision was lawful (s. 6(א)).
- *Maintenance plan.* An FOI request is the proper tool: it does not require reasons, has its own deadline, and a refusal is subject to the weighing required in עע"ם 9135/03.

**6. Conclusion and next steps.**
1. Today: send a formal reminder letter citing s. 2(א) and s. 6, asking for a decision within 14 days (lesson 02.7).
2. Today: send a separate FOI request for the maintenance plan.
3. If no answer by 15.9.2026: complaint to the State Comptroller and Ombudsman (local authorities are within its remit); consider whether a court petition is proportionate (module 8; year 2 administrative law).

**7. Open questions and risks.** No ruling found on whether a service request is a "power" under s. 2. A petition to court for a streetlight would be disproportionate in cost; the realistic tools are letters and the Ombudsman. If the council answers that the repair is scheduled, the matter ends.

**Sources.** Full citations with links, checked 1.9.2026.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A real legal question from your own affairs: a deadline, a right against a company or an authority, a contract term, a benefit. Not someone else's.
- Your work from lessons 02.1 to 02.5 if it concerned the same question.
- [The research memo template](../../templates/research-memo.md).
- Three hours, in two or three sittings.

### Steps

1. **Frame the question (15 min).** Write it in one sentence with your key facts and a question mark. If it has several parts, list them.
2. **Fix the facts (20 min).** List the facts with dates. Collect the documents. Mark anything unconfirmed.
3. **Find the statute (30 min).** As in lesson 02.1: consolidated text, amendment check, version for your date. Quote the decisive sections.
4. **Find the case law (40 min).** As in lesson 02.2: at least two rulings, at least one binding if any exists. Brief the most important one (lesson 02.3).
5. **Interpret (20 min).** For each disputed word, the lesson 02.4 method: meanings, purpose, conclusion. Compute every deadline.
6. **Write the analysis (30 min).** One heading per issue. For each: rule, application, counter-argument, answer, confidence.
7. **Write the short answer and conclusion (10 min).** Then the next steps with dates.
8. **Write open questions and risks (5 min).** Include "I would get a lawyer if…".
9. **Cite and date (10 min).** Every source in the format of lesson 02.5, with the date checked.

### How you know it worked

- Someone who knows nothing about your matter reads part 2 and knows the answer, then reads the rest and can check every legal statement against a source.
- Every deadline is computed with its rule.
- Part 6 tells you exactly what to do next and by when.
- Part 7 honestly says what could make you wrong.

### Self-check

- [ ] The question is about my own matter and is specific to my facts.
- [ ] Every statute is quoted from a checked, current (or correctly dated) text.
- [ ] Every ruling is cited correctly and checked for later treatment.
- [ ] Each issue has a counter-argument and my answer to it.
- [ ] I graded my confidence for each conclusion.
- [ ] The memo is dated and says it is not advice to others.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for your own affairs. Researching and writing a memo for yourself is not regulated. Writing memos for others as a service, or for payment, is legal advice reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(4)). Do not "help" friends by writing them memos on their matters; you may share the course with them instead.

### Where and how

All sources are free (lessons 02.1, 02.2). If you want a professional check of your memo, free and low-cost options are listed in [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md): Legal Aid (subject to eligibility tests), university law clinics, and Kol-Zchut for background.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your confidence grade is "uncertain" on an issue that decides the outcome, and the stakes are significant.
- The memo shows a deadline you cannot meet, or one that has already passed.
- The question involves criminal exposure, a large sum, your home, your children, or your immigration status.
- The other side has a lawyer and has raised arguments your research cannot answer.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A vague question ("what are my rights?") | Research sprawls; no usable answer | Rewrite the question with facts and a specific legal issue | Write the question first and keep it in view |
| Facts mixed with argument | You cannot tell what you can prove | Move arguments to the analysis; mark unconfirmed facts | Write the facts section from documents only |
| No counter-arguments | You are surprised by the other side's first reply | Add the best opposing argument for each issue | Ask "how would the authority or company answer this?" |
| Overconfidence | You act on a weak position, or skip a lawyer when you need one | Grade each conclusion; reconsider next steps | Use the clear / likely / uncertain grades every time |
| No date or stale law | The memo misleads you months later | Re-check the law before acting on an old memo | Put the date checked next to every source |

## Review

- A research memo states the question, gives the short answer first, then facts, law, analysis, conclusion and open questions.
- Every legal statement needs a checked, cited source; every deadline needs a computation.
- Arguing against yourself and grading your confidence are what make a memo useful.
- The memo decides your next step, including whether to get a lawyer.
- Memos are for your own affairs; writing them for others is reserved to lawyers.
