# 37.3 · A police complaint and following it up

Lab [05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md) taught you to file a complaint and lab [05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md) to appeal a closure. Most real complaints are lost in between: evidence is not secured, the investigator never sees the video, the victim never hears what happened, and the closure notice arrives months later with a ground that does not fit the facts. In this lesson you run a complaint of your own, or Dmitri Volkov's, through that middle stretch, and choose the right step when the file is closed.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> Complain only about an offence against you (or, where an offence caused a death, as a family member). Report what you know first-hand, and say when something is hearsay. Do not confront the suspect, collect evidence by trespass or recording others' private conversations, or post accusations online: each can expose you to criminal or civil liability. In an emergency call 100 first.

A complaint is the start of a file the police control. The police must investigate a felony; for a non-felony an officer may decide not to (CPL s. 59). What you can control is the quality of what the investigator has, the record of what you asked for, and your use of the rights the Crime Victims' Rights Law gives you. A complainant who does those three things gets more files investigated, and has a strong appeal when one is closed.

## The law

### What the police must do and tell you

> נודע למשטרה על ביצוע עבירה, אם על פי תלונה ואם בכל דרך אחרת, תפתח בחקירה [...]

*Gist:* The police must investigate once they learn of an offence; for a non-felony an officer of a set rank may decide not to, for the reasons in the section. [CPL s. 59](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_59)

A decision not to investigate or not to prosecute must be sent to the complainant with the ground of closure; for a decision not to prosecute, the notice may say where to learn the reason (CPL s. 63; lesson 05.8). A victim who asks is entitled to be told of the stages of the case, including transfer to the prosecution and a decision not to prosecute (Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 8(ג) and the Second Schedule), and every station has an officer responsible for victims' rights (s. 24). ([Crime Victims' Rights Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_זכויות_נפגעי_עבירה))

### Which closures can be appealed

> (א) על החלטה שלא לחקור או שלא להעמיד לדין, משום שנסיבות העניין בכללותן אינן מתאימות לפתיחה בחקירה או להעמדה לדין, לפי העניין, שלא נמצאו ראיות מספיקות או שנקבע שאין אשמה, רשאי המתלונן לערור [...]

*Gist:* The complainant may appeal a decision not to investigate or prosecute on three grounds: the circumstances as a whole, insufficient evidence, or no guilt. [CPL s. 64(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_64) The appeal goes through the police or the prosecutor within the s. 65 period from delivery of the notice ([current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).

"Offender unknown" is not one of the three grounds. A file closed that way has not decided anything about a suspect, so the statutory appeal does not fit it. The practical route is a reasoned written request to resume the investigation (חידוש חקירה), addressed to the head of the investigations office at the station, with the identifying evidence (⚠️ manual check: the current police procedure on resuming closed files is not on a free official source). If the police then identify a suspect and close the file on a listed ground, the s. 64 appeal opens, with a new s. 63 notice and a new period.

### The fallback: a private complaint

For the offences in the Second Schedule (not against a family member), you may prosecute yourself by a private criminal complaint (קובלנה) (CPL s. 68; lesson 05.8). Damage to property is among them (Penal Law s. 452, in the list in lesson 05.8). It is a full criminal trial that you conduct, with the burden of proof beyond reasonable doubt.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 3292/07 עדאלה נ׳ היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (2011)

The Court refused to order criminal investigations into civilian deaths in military operations: a demand to investigate must rest on a suitable prima facie evidentiary foundation, and the gravity of the outcome alone is not enough. Why it matters here: every follow-up letter, request to resume and appeal you write is judged by the same question. Point to specific evidence (a video, a witness, a phone record) and a specific step the investigator has not taken. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/24)

### Key text: the follow-up log as evidence

There is no leading case on how a complainant follows a file. The texts that matter are s. 8 of the Crime Victims' Rights Law (your right to be told of the stages), s. 63 of the CPL (the closure notice and its ground) and s. 65 (the appeal period from delivery). A dated log of each request and answer is what turns those rights into a remedy: it proves when the notice reached you, that evidence was offered and not taken, and that you asked to be informed. It is also the core of an Ombudsman complaint about delay (lesson 37.6).

## How it works in practice

### The follow-up timeline

| When | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Statement at the station or online; your written complaint attached; ask for the confirmation and file number | The file number is the key to every later step |
| Day 0–2 | Written request that footage be secured (shops, buses, municipal cameras), naming each camera; deliver your own video on a USB stick and get a receipt | Most camera systems overwrite footage within days |
| Day 0 | Written request under s. 8 to be told of the stages of the case | Without a request, you may not be told |
| Week 2–4 | Short email to the investigator or the investigations office: file number, "has the video been viewed? I can identify the person" | Puts the evidence in front of a named officer |
| Week 6–8 | If no answer: letter to the station's victims'-rights officer (s. 24) asking for the stage | Creates a record of silence |
| Notice of closure | Keep the envelope; read the ground; decide the route (table below) | The appeal period runs from delivery |

### Reading the closure notice

| Ground stated | Route | Main argument |
|---|---|---|
| Offender unknown (עבריין לא נודע) | Request to resume, with identifying evidence (⚠️ procedure) | "The offender is known: here is the evidence and here is the step not taken" |
| Insufficient evidence | Appeal (ערר) within the s. 65 period, through the police or prosecutor (05.8) | Specific evidence not collected or not weighed |
| Circumstances as a whole (no public interest) | Appeal | Seriousness, repetition, ongoing danger, the harm to you |
| No guilt | Appeal | Facts showing the act and the suspect's part in it |
| Any ground, appeal refused | Private complaint for a Second Schedule offence; civil claim; oversight complaint about how the file was handled | Choose by what you need: punishment, compensation, or a corrected process |

### Civil options in parallel

For property damage, the faster remedy is often civil: a demand letter and a small claim against the person who did it (lesson [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md)), using the same video. A criminal complaint and a civil claim can run side by side. A conviction can later be used in the civil case, but you do not need one.

## Worked example

Dmitri Volkov (supplied case B) found his car scratched along one side and two tyres slashed one morning in July 2026, parked in his street in Netanya. His dashboard camera, which records when it senses movement, shows a man he recognises as a neighbour from the next building bending by the wheels at 02:40.

1. **Complaint.** He filed online the same day and was called to give a statement on 14 July. He brought a typed complaint (the [police complaint template](../../templates/police-complaint-victim.md)), the video on a USB stick, the tyre invoice and photographs. He described what the video shows, and wrote "I believe the man is my neighbour, whose name I know from the building's committee; I did not see him myself that night". He received a confirmation with the file number and a receipt for the USB stick.
2. **Rights.** In the same letter he asked under s. 8 to be informed of the stages of the case.
3. **Follow-up.** On 4 August and 1 September he emailed the investigations office asking whether the video had been viewed. No answer. On 15 September he wrote to the station's victims'-rights officer. No answer.
4. **The notice.** On 2 October a letter arrived: the file was closed, "עבריין לא נודע". He kept the envelope.
5. **Route.** The ground is not one of the three in s. 64(א), so an appeal does not fit. He writes a request to resume the investigation to the head of the investigations office: the file number; the video with its time stamp; the neighbour's name and address; the fact that his two emails and his letter got no answer; a request that the video be viewed and the neighbour questioned. He attaches his follow-up log. He diarises 60 days from 2 October anyway, in case a later notice states a listed ground.
6. **Parallel steps.** He sends the neighbour a short demand letter for the cost of repair and diarises a small claim. He prepares, but does not yet send, an Ombudsman complaint about the unanswered letters (lesson 37.6), to use if the request to resume is ignored.
7. **Outcome.** In November the police reopen the file and question the neighbour, then close it again for "insufficient evidence" with a new notice. Dmitri now has a s. 64 appeal, and files it within the period, pointing to the video and to the neighbour's own admission that he was outside at 02:40.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- An offence against you (property damage, theft, threats, harassment, fraud), with your own evidence; or Dmitri's case.
- The [police complaint and victims' rights request](../../templates/police-complaint-victim.md), the [appeal against closing a file](../../templates/appeal-against-closing-file.md) and the [private criminal complaint](../../templates/private-criminal-complaint.md).
- Your case file with the exposure check.
- About 120 minutes, spread over the life of the file.

### Steps

1. **Exposure check (5 min).** Is any act of yours in the same events possibly an offence? If yes, stop and get advice.
2. **Complaint package (40 min).** The written complaint on the template: facts in time order, first-hand versus hearsay, participants, evidence and who holds it, the likely offence; a list of cameras to secure; the s. 8 request. Copy your own video to a USB stick and keep the original.
3. **Filing (time varies).** Statement at the station or online; ask for the confirmation and file number and a receipt for every item handed over.
4. **Follow-up log (10 min, then 5 min per entry).** One line per contact: date, to whom, channel, what you asked, the answer. Set reminders at weeks 3, 6 and 9.
5. **Closure (30 min, when the notice arrives).** Record the delivery date and its proof; read the ground; choose the route from the table; write the appeal or the request to resume on the right template.
6. **Parallel remedy (20 min).** Decide whether a civil demand and small claim, a protection order or mediation solves the problem better; write one line on why.
7. **Fallback (15 min).** If the appeal fails: test the private complaint against the four questions of lesson 05.8, and write one paragraph deciding for or against it.

### Template

- [Police complaint and victims' rights request](../../templates/police-complaint-victim.md) (lesson 05.7).
- [Appeal against closing a file](../../templates/appeal-against-closing-file.md) (lesson 05.8); use its structure for a request to resume, changing the addressee and the request.
- [Private criminal complaint](../../templates/private-criminal-complaint.md) (lesson 05.8).

### How you know it worked

- You hold a complaint confirmation with a file number and a receipt for your evidence.
- Your log shows each follow-up with a date and a named addressee.
- Your response to the closure matches its ground: an appeal for a listed ground, a request to resume for "offender unknown".

### Self-check

- [ ] I separated what I saw from what I believe or heard.
- [ ] I asked in writing, on the first day, for footage to be secured and to be told of the stages.
- [ ] I counted the appeal period from delivery of the notice and kept the proof.
- [ ] I know that "offender unknown" calls for a request to resume, not a s. 64 appeal.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Anyone may complain (CPL s. 58); the complainant appeals a closure (s. 64); a request to resume is an ordinary letter; a private complaint is conducted by the complainant or his lawyer (s. 71), though the court may stay it until a lawyer is appointed if the complainant cannot manage it (s. 73). Victims of listed sexual offences and families of homicide victims have free Legal Aid (lesson 05.7). See [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- Complaint: any police station; online for some offence types on the police website (⚠️ manual check of the current list); 100 in an emergency.
- Appeal: through the police or the prosecutor named in the notice; the appeals authority is set by s. 64(א); period in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: victims' assistance centres, the prosecution's victims' assistance units, Legal Aid where eligible ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You may be a suspect yourself in the same events, or the suspect has filed a counter-complaint.
- The offence is a serious sexual or violent one: use the free Legal Aid representation if you are eligible.
- You are considering a private complaint against someone who will be represented, or a petition against an appeal decision.
- The suspect is a police officer or another public servant acting on duty (lesson 37.6; a private complaint needs the Attorney General's consent, s. 69).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handing over the only copy of your video | It may be lost; you cannot use it in a civil claim | Ask the station for a copy; check the camera's memory | Keep the original; hand over a copy with a receipt |
| No written request for footage | Cameras overwrite; key evidence lost | None for overwritten footage | Name each camera in writing on day 0 |
| Filing a s. 64 appeal against "offender unknown" | Answered that there is nothing to appeal; weeks lost | Write a request to resume with the identifying evidence | Read the ground first |
| Counting the appeal period from the date printed on the letter | You believe you are late, or file late | Use the delivery proof; ask for an extension before the deadline | Keep the envelope or email header |
| Calling the suspect a criminal online | Defamation claim; harassment complaint against you | Remove the post; apologise if needed | Let the file and the court decide |

## Review

- A complaint is won or lost in the follow-up: secure the footage, put the evidence before a named investigator, ask in writing to be told of the stages.
- The closure ground decides the route: an appeal for the three listed grounds within the s. 65 period; a request to resume for "offender unknown".
- A dated follow-up log proves your diligence and the authority's silence, for an appeal or an oversight complaint.
- Civil remedies and a private complaint run alongside or after the criminal file.
