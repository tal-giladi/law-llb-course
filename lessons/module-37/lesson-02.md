# 37.2 · An administrative petition from start to finish

Lab [14.11](../module-14/lesson-11.md) was a dry run: you wrote a petition and filed nothing. Here you run a real administrative matter of your own, or the supplied case of Ilana Mizrahi, from the decision to the point where you either file a petition in your own name or record why you did not. You get the decision and its reasons, send the reconsideration request and prior demand, re-test the threshold doctrines on the answer you receive, and, if you file, assemble the petition, affidavit, fee, service and diary so that the court will actually read the petition.

## Why it matters

Most administrative matters end before a petition: a well-built prior demand, quoting the statute and attaching the facts, often produces a new decision. The petition is the backstop, and it works only if you prepared for it from day one: the period runs from the first decision, not from the end of correspondence; the court looks at whether you asked the authority first; and it will not read a petition that was not served. The theory (jurisdiction, threshold doctrines, remedies) is in lesson [14.9](../module-14/lesson-09.md); this lesson is about running the file and the decision points.

## The law

### Jurisdiction: s. 5 and the First Schedule

> החלטה של רשות מקומית או של נושא משרה או תפקיד בה [...]

*Gist:* Decisions of a local authority and its office-holders are heard by the Administrative Affairs Court, with listed exceptions (First Schedule, item 8(א)); the court's petition jurisdiction is over decisions on First Schedule matters (s. 5(1)). [Administrative Affairs Courts Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים) Ministry and Government decisions outside the Schedule stay in the High Court of Justice (lesson [06.8](../module-06/lesson-08.md)).

### Period, delay and self-representation

The period runs from the earliest of publication, notice or knowledge (reg. 3(ב)); the court may extend it (reg. 3(ג)) and may dismiss for delay even inside it (reg. 4). An individual acts in his own name: reg. 44א applies chapter 20 of the Civil Procedure Regulations, including reg. 170. ([Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין)); [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

### The duty you hold the authority to: an example

> רשות מקומית תקצה לרכב מזכה אחד של נכה המתגורר בתחומה [...]

*Gist:* A local authority must allocate one parking space for the qualifying vehicle of a disabled resident who meets the section's conditions, as close as possible to his home and within a set distance, and must do so within a set period of the request; it does not apply if the person already has suitable exclusive parking. [Parking for Persons with Disabilities Law s. 4ד](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_חניה_לנכים) (the section's own eligibility test is stricter than the general one, s. 4ד(ז); read it before you rely on it).

A statute like this changes the petition: the question is no longer whether the authority used its discretion reasonably, but whether it performed a duty. A "policy" cannot override a statutory duty (lesson [14.1](../module-14/lesson-01.md)).

### Silence

A written request must be decided and answered within the period of Decisions and Reasons Law s. 2(א); a refusal without reasons, or silence beyond the period in s. 6(ב), is treated as a refusal without reasons, and the burden shifts to the authority to show it acted lawfully (s. 6). Silence is itself a decision you may petition against; diarise it.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 3/58 ברמן נ׳ שר הפנים (30.10.1958)

The Court held that an authority must give a person a fair opportunity to be heard before a decision that harms him, even where no statute says so, and that a person who knows a process concerning him is under way should ask to be heard. Why it matters here: in your reconsideration request, ask expressly for a hearing; if the authority refuses or ignores it, the refusal itself becomes a ground. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/93)

### Key text: the remedy fits the ground

Where the authority breached a clear statutory duty, ask the court to order it to perform the duty (צו עשה) by a date; where it used discretion badly, the usual remedy is to cancel the decision and return the matter for a new decision after a hearing (lesson 14.9). Ask for both, main and alternative, and for costs. A petition that asks the court to decide the matter itself, where the authority has discretion, usually fails on the remedy even if the ground is good.

## How it works in practice

### The six stages and their decision points

| Stage | What you do | Decision point |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The decision | Get it in writing, with reasons and the date it reached you; diarise the period | Is there a statutory objection or appeal? If yes, use it (lesson 14.10), not a petition |
| 2. Records | Reasons request; FOI request for the file and the policy relied on | None; but do not let it delay stage 3 |
| 3. Reconsideration and prior demand | One letter: facts, the statute, the defect, the remedy, a request to be heard, a short deadline for an answer, and a statement that you will petition | Send at once, so the answer arrives inside the petition period |
| 4. The answer | Compare it with your request, line by line | Full grant: close. Partial: petition on the rest, or accept. New reasons: re-test the grounds. Silence: petition on the decision and the silence |
| 5. Filing | Petition, affidavit, annexes, fee or exemption; serve; file proof of service | Interim order needed? If yes, a separate application and consider a lawyer |
| 6. After filing | Diary the respondent's answer, further particulars, skeleton arguments, the hearing | A new decision during the case: ask whether the petition is moot, and claim costs |

### Filing in practice

- **Where:** the district court for the district where the decision was made (reg. 2), at the registry. Unrepresented parties can file documents in an existing case on Net HaMishpat with the national digital login; whether a new petition can be opened online by an unrepresented petitioner should be checked with the registry before the day (⚠️ manual check). Bring the original and the copies the registry asks for.
- **Fee:** schedule item 20 of the Courts (Fees) Regulations, or a fee-exemption application with proof of means; amount in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- **Service:** directly on each respondent within the reg. 35(א) period; then file proof of service: until you do, the petition is not brought before the judge (reg. 7(ב)).
- **The answer:** if the court orders one, the respondent has the reg. 10(א) period unless the court sets another. Often the authority's lawyer first asks for more time, or announces a new decision.

### When the authority changes its mind after you file

Many petitions end with a "notice of update" from the authority: it has reconsidered, granted the request, or set a date. Read it carefully. If it gives you everything, agree that the petition has become moot (התייתרה) and ask for costs, since the petition caused the change. If it gives part, say so and ask the court to decide the rest. Do not withdraw without a written record of what the authority undertook.

## Worked example

Ilana Mizrahi (supplied case A) uses a wheelchair, holds a disabled parking card and owns a vehicle registered for her own use. Her building in Ashdod has no parking. On 10 July 2026 she asked the municipality for a reserved space near her home. On 24 September she received, by email, a letter dated 20 September: "the municipality's policy is not to mark reserved spaces in streets where buildings have private parking; your request is refused." No hearing; no reference to the statute.

1. **Stage 1.** No statutory objection route for this decision. Period: from 24 September (receipt; she checks that nothing was published earlier). Last day diarised with a reminder ten days before.
2. **Stage 2.** The same day she asks, under FOI and Decisions and Reasons, for the written policy and the inspector's report on her street. The information concerns her, so no application fee (see current numbers).
3. **Stage 3.** On 26 September she sends a reconsideration request and prior demand to the head of the traffic department, copied to the city's legal adviser: her eligibility documents; s. 4ד(א) and the period in s. 4ד(ג), which had already passed by the time of the refusal; her building has no parking, so s. 4ד(ו) does not apply; the policy cannot override the statute; a request to be heard; an answer within ten days; notice that she will petition.
4. **Stage 4.** On 8 October the legal adviser answers: the policy stands, but "the matter will be re-examined by the parking committee next month". No date, no hearing. Decision point: this is not a new decision, and waiting would eat the period. She decides to file.
5. **Stage 5.** Petition against the municipality (the only respondent: no third party holds the space). Grounds, strongest first: breach of a statutory duty; a policy that ignores the statute; no hearing. Remedies: an order to allocate a space within 30 days; alternatively, to decide within 30 days after hearing her; costs. She files on 15 October at the Be'er Sheva District Court registry (⚠️ confirm the district for Ashdod with the registry), pays the fee, serves the municipality's legal department by hand on 16 October and files proof of service the same day.
6. **Stage 6.** On 2 November the municipality files a notice: a space will be marked within 21 days. Ilana answers that she agrees the petition will become moot once the sign is installed, asks the court to order the municipality to report when it is, and asks for costs. The court gives the order and awards modest costs. She photographs the sign and closes the matter (lesson 37.8). The hearing stage is in lesson [37.7](lesson-07.md).

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- Your own decision (or silence) by a body whose decisions are in the First Schedule, or Ilana's case.
- Your threshold memo and petition draft from lab 14.11, if you did it.
- The [objection and reconsideration template](../../templates/objection-to-administrative-decision.md), the [administrative petition template](../../templates/administrative-petition.md), the [FOI request](../../templates/foi-request.md) and your case file.
- About 150 minutes over two weeks, plus waiting.

### Steps

1. **Decision sheet (15 min).** The decision, its maker, its date, how and when it reached you, the enabling statute, any statutory objection route. Period and last day, with the trigger and its proof.
2. **Records (15 min).** Send the reasons and FOI requests. Log them.
3. **Prior demand (40 min).** One letter on the objection template: facts with annexes, the statute, each defect, the remedy, a hearing request, a short answer date, notice of a petition. Send by email and registered mail, or the authority's form; keep proof.
4. **Answer review (20 min, when it arrives or when the answer date passes).** Line-by-line comparison; mark the decision point from the table.
5. **Threshold memo (20 min).** Re-run lesson 14.9's checklist against the answer: schedule item, venue, period, delay, alternative remedy, respondents, clean hands.
6. **Petition package (40 min).** Update your 14.11 draft (or write it now on the template): opening paragraph, jurisdiction and period, facts with annexes, prior demand and answer, two to four grounds, main and alternative remedy, costs; the affidavit separating knowledge from belief; fee or exemption; a service plan.
7. **File or record (10 min).** If your filters, period and exposure check all say go, file at the registry, serve and file proof. If not, write one paragraph explaining why the matter stays a dry run, and what would change that.

### Template

- [Objection or request for reconsideration](../../templates/objection-to-administrative-decision.md) (lesson 14.10), used as the prior demand.
- [Administrative petition and affidavit](../../templates/administrative-petition.md) (lesson 14.11).
- [FOI request](../../templates/foi-request.md) (lesson 14.7).

### How you know it worked

- Your prior demand was sent within days of the decision, and its answer date falls well inside the petition period.
- Your decision at stage 4 is written, with the reason.
- If you filed: the registry stamped the petition, the respondent signed for it, and proof of service is in the court file.

### Self-check

- [ ] I checked for a statutory objection or appeal before choosing a petition.
- [ ] I counted the period from the earliest trigger and did not let correspondence run it out.
- [ ] My remedy fits my ground (perform a duty, or reconsider after a hearing).
- [ ] I know the court will not read the petition until proof of service is filed.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for an individual in his own matter (reg. 44א with Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170; the petition form asks for a lawyer's details only if you are represented). A company generally needs a lawyer (unverified in a free source). No one may represent you except a lawyer (Bar Association Law s. 20). See [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The district court for the district where the decision was made (reg. 2), sitting in administrative matters; registry filing; Net HaMishpat for later documents (⚠️ manual check for opening a case online).
- Fee or exemption, the petition period, service and answer periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: university administrative-law and disability clinics; NGOs in the field of your matter; the Judiciary's information centre for registry questions ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need an interim order within days (a closure, a demolition, a revocation that takes effect soon).
- A third party with lawyers is a respondent, or the authority raises threshold objections you cannot answer.
- The case turns on expert evidence or on cross-examining officials.
- The decision is by the Government or a minister and the matter may belong in the High Court of Justice.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the "re-examination" the authority promised | Period passes; delay | File, and tell the court about the promised re-examination | Treat a promise without a date as no decision |
| Petition instead of a statutory objection | Dismissed for an alternative remedy | File the objection; ask to extend its period | Read the governing law first |
| No proof of service filed | Petition not brought before the judge (reg. 7(ב)) | Serve and file proof at once | Serve the day after filing, file proof the same day |
| Withdrawing after an oral promise | No remedy and no costs if the promise fails | Ask the court to record the undertaking | Agree to mootness only on a written, dated undertaking |
| Asking the court to decide instead of the authority | Remedy refused | Ask for a new decision after a hearing | Main and alternative remedies |

## Review

- A petition is the backstop of a prior demand, and both are planned from the day of the decision.
- The period runs from the earliest trigger; correspondence does not stop it; delay can defeat a petition filed in time.
- A statutory duty makes the strongest petition; a policy cannot override it.
- File, serve, and file proof of service; then diary the answer and watch for a new decision that makes the petition moot, with costs.
