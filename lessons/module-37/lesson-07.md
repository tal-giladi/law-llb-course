# 37.7 · The hearing in the administrative or magistrates' court

The hearing is short and decided mostly on paper you filed weeks earlier. In the Administrative Affairs Court the judge has read the petition, the answer and the skeleton arguments, and the hearing is a conversation about the weakest point of each side. In the traffic tribunal there may be no oral hearing at all. In a fine-offence trial the officer is the main witness and your absence is an admission. In this lesson you prepare for the hearing in your own matter (or for Ilana's petition and Yossi's traffic appeal), using the hearing-day kit from module 12 with the additions that public and criminal hearings need.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> A traffic or local-affairs trial is a criminal trial, even when it is about a parking or bylaw fine. You have the right to remain silent and need not testify; anything you say may be used. If at the start of the trial the prosecutor says that a prison sentence may be sought, or the charge changes to something more serious, ask the court to stop and to appoint a defender (lesson [24.6](../module-24/lesson-06.md)).

Self-represented parties lose hearings in predictable ways: they file skeleton arguments late or not at all; they try to tell the whole story when the judge wants one answer; they do not know that a respondent's affidavit stands unless they asked, in time, to cross-examine; they do not attend a traffic trial because "the ticket was obviously wrong". Each has a fix you can apply before the day.

## The law

### The administrative hearing: regs 14–16

> ארבעה ימים לפחות לפני המועד שנקבע לדיון [...]

*Gist:* Once an answer is filed and a hearing set, each party files skeleton arguments and sends them directly to the others at least a set number of days before the hearing; they summarise the main arguments and the authorities (an unpublished ruling is attached); at the hearing you argue from your skeleton and need leave for a new argument (reg. 14). [Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בתי_משפט_לענינים_מינהליים_(סדרי_דין))

> לא יאוחר מארבעה עשר ימים לפני מועד הדיון [...]

*Gist:* The court decides on the affidavits; to cross-examine the other side's affiant you must apply in writing no later than a set number of days before the hearing, stating the other side's position; if the affiant does not appear, the affidavit is evidence only with leave (reg. 15(א)–(ד)). The petitioner argues first, then the respondent; the court may allow a reply and may ask for written completion (reg. 16). Periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### The traffic tribunal: on the papers unless needed

The tribunal decides on the written pleadings unless that would harm justice or the public interest (2024 Law s. 21(א)); you may ask, with reasons in the appeal, for a particular form of hearing (reg. 2(ו)); if it holds a hearing with the parties, it is held through the online system, and each party may consult its representative privately during it (regs 12–13). The decision is given as close as possible to the end of the examination and sent through the system (regs 20, 25). An appeal from a final decision lies to the traffic magistrates' court, or to the local-affairs court for a municipal fine (s. 24). ([2026 Regulations](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_הפרות_תעבורה_מינהליות_(סדרי_הדין_והמינהל_בבית_דין_לתעבורה)))

### The fine-offence trial: s. 240

In traffic offences without injury, fine offences and administrative offences, a defendant who was summoned and does not appear is treated as admitting the facts unless a defender appears for him, and may be tried in his absence without prison; he may send written arguments on sentence (CPL s. 240(א)). A judgment in absence may be set aside on a request showing a good reason for not appearing, or a miscarriage of justice (s. 130, applied by s. 240(ב)). If convicted after asking to be tried, the fine will not be lower than the notice without special circumstances (s. 230). ([CPL](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי#סעיף_240))

## Leading cases

### Key text: the hearing rules are the leading text

There is no leading appellate ruling on how a self-represented party should conduct an administrative hearing or a fine-offence trial; the court's wide discretion is the rule. The controlling texts are regs 14–16 of the administrative procedure regulations, s. 21 of the 2024 Law with regs 2 and 12–13 of the tribunal regulations, and CPL ss. 130, 230 and 240. Read them once more the week before your hearing.

### בג״ץ 3/58 ברמן נ׳ שר הפנים (1958)

The right to be heard before a harmful decision (lesson [14.4](../module-14/lesson-04.md)). Why it matters at the hearing: when the authority's lawyer argues that "the policy" justified the refusal, the court's first question is often whether you were heard. If you were not, the most common outcome is not a win on the merits but an order to return the matter to the authority for a new decision after a hearing, by a date. Decide in advance whether that is good enough for you. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/93)

## How it works in practice

### Administrative hearing: the week before

| When | What |
|---|---|
| As soon as the answer arrives | Read it for new facts; decide whether you need further particulars (reg. 11) or cross-examination (reg. 15) |
| No later than the reg. 15(ב) period before the hearing | Written request to cross-examine, if a disputed fact in the respondent's affidavit decides the case |
| No later than the reg. 14(א) period before the hearing | Skeleton arguments filed and sent to the respondent: one to three pages, two to four grounds, the authorities attached |
| The day before | Hearing-day kit: the two-minute opening, the three questions the judge will ask, your answers, your position on a return to the authority |

### Administrative hearing: on the day

- **Opening (two minutes):** the decision, the defect, the remedy. Do not read the petition aloud.
- **The judge's questions:** usually about thresholds (delay, alternative remedy, respondents) and about what the authority would do on a return. Answer the question asked, then stop.
- **The respondent's argument:** note each point; answer only the new ones in your reply.
- **The proposal:** the court often suggests that the authority reconsider within a time, that the petition be withdrawn with or without costs, or that it be struck as moot. Compare it with your sheet (below). You may ask for a short break.

### Traffic tribunal

Most appeals are decided on the papers. Your hearing is your appeal: if you asked for an oral hearing, prepare as for a short administrative hearing, online, with the video or photographs ready to share on screen, and a one-page note of the facts the tribunal must decide. Check the online system for the respondent's answer and any decision; documents count as delivered when sent through the system (reg. 25), so check it, not only your email.

### Fine-offence trial (request to be tried)

| Stage | What you do |
|---|---|
| Before | Ask the prosecutor in writing for the evidence (the officer's report, photographs); bring three copies of each of your documents |
| Answer to the charge | Say which facts you admit and which you deny; you need not explain more at this stage |
| Prosecution witness | Cross-examine the officer with short, closed questions, one fact per question (what he saw, from where, for how long) |
| Your case | You may testify (and be cross-examined) or stay silent; call your witness; submit your documents |
| Arguments | One minute on why the prosecution did not prove the element you dispute |
| Sentence, if convicted | Personal circumstances; ask for the notice amount and, if points apply, explain your work |

### The proposal sheet (write it before the hearing)

| Outcome | What it means for you | Accept? |
|---|---|---|
| Win in full | | |
| Return to the authority for a new decision after a hearing, by a date | | |
| Withdrawal with costs to you / without costs | | |
| (Traffic) change to a lesser violation / conviction with the notice amount | | |
| Loss | | |

## Worked example

**Ilana's petition (supplied case A, continued from 37.2).** Suppose the municipality had not given way and filed an answer: Ilana's street has public parking within a short walk and her request "is under examination"; the policy is reasonable.

1. **Answer review.** The affidavit of the traffic engineer says the nearest free public space is within the statutory distance. Ilana measures it: further than stated, and up a slope. The fact decides whether the authority could refuse. She files a written request to cross-examine the engineer within the reg. 15(ב) period, attaching her measurement and photographs, and stating that she asked the municipality's lawyer for consent and was refused.
2. **Skeleton.** Two pages, filed and emailed to the municipality's lawyer within the reg. 14(א) period: (1) breach of the statutory duty (s. 4ד); (2) a policy cannot override the statute; (3) no hearing. Three authorities, Berman among them.
3. **Proposal sheet.** Win: a space within 30 days. Return to the authority after a hearing, within 30 days, with a measurement on site: acceptable. Withdrawal without costs: no.
4. **On the day.** The judge asks the municipality's lawyer whether the engineer measured on site. He did not. The judge proposes that the municipality decide again within 30 days, after hearing Ilana and measuring on site, and that the petition be struck with costs to Ilana if it does not. Ilana asks for a short break, checks her sheet, and agrees, asking that the measurement be done in her presence. The court gives a decision in those terms.

**Yossi's traffic appeal (supplied case C, continued from 37.4).** The tribunal grants Yossi's request for a hearing because the officer's view is disputed. It takes place online. Yossi shares the dashcam clip on screen, timed to the second, and asks one question through the tribunal: from which side of the van did the officer see the phone? The police representative answers from the report: from the left lane, at a red light. Yossi points out that the phone holder is on the right side of the dashboard, visible in the clip. The tribunal cancels the decision.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- Your filed petition and the answer, or your traffic appeal or summons; or the supplied cases.
- The [hearing-day kit](../../templates/hearing-day-kit.md) (module 12), the [written motion](../../templates/written-motion.md) template for a request to cross-examine, and the [witness examination plan](../../templates/witness-examination-plan.md).
- About 120 minutes in the week before the hearing.

### Steps

1. **Answer review (20 min).** List each new fact or argument in the answer; mark the one that decides the case.
2. **Evidence decision (15 min).** Do you need to cross-examine an affiant, or (in a trial) the officer? If yes, write the request or the questions now.
3. **Skeleton or written note (30 min).** For a petition: the skeleton within the reg. 14 period. For the tribunal: a one-page note if a hearing was granted. For a trial: one page of what you deny and how you will show it.
4. **Opening (15 min).** Two minutes, timed aloud.
5. **Questions (15 min).** The three questions the judge or tribunal will ask, and your one-sentence answers.
6. **Proposal sheet (10 min).** Fill the table; decide in advance which proposals you accept.
7. **Logistics (15 min).** Three copies of each document; the link or the courtroom; arrival time; for a trial, confirm you will attend.

### Template

- [Hearing-day kit](../../templates/hearing-day-kit.md) (lesson 12.6), with the proposal sheet above.
- [Written motion](../../templates/written-motion.md), for the request to cross-examine (reg. 15) or to set aside a judgment in absence (s. 130).
- [Witness examination plan](../../templates/witness-examination-plan.md), for the officer or the affiant.

### How you know it worked

- Your skeleton (or note) was filed and sent within its period, and you have the proof.
- Your opening takes two minutes or less and names the remedy.
- You know before the hearing which proposals you will accept.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know that an affidavit stands unless I asked in time to cross-examine its maker.
- [ ] I know that the tribunal usually decides on the papers and checks the online system for delivery.
- [ ] I know that missing a fine-offence trial counts as admitting the facts.
- [ ] I know the right to remain silent applies at my traffic trial.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. An individual argues his own petition (reg. 44א with Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170), his own traffic appeal (the tribunal regulations ask for a lawyer only if you have one) and his own fine-offence defence. No one but a lawyer may appear for you (Bar Association Law s. 20). See [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The district court in administrative matters; the traffic tribunal's online system; the traffic magistrates' court or the local-affairs court for trials. Dates and documents: Net HaMishpat (with the national digital login) or the tribunal's system.
- Periods for skeletons and cross-examination requests: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- Free help: the Judiciary's information centre for procedure; university clinics ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The respondent's answer raises threshold objections or new evidence you cannot answer, or experts are involved.
- At a traffic trial, the prosecutor says a prison sentence may be sought, or the charge is amended to an offence with disqualification.
- The court suggests that you are likely to lose and that you withdraw: ask for time to take advice before you decide.
- You lost and want to appeal on a point of law (administrative appeal to the Supreme Court; appeal from the tribunal to the magistrates' court).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| No skeleton, or filed the day before | The court may refuse to hear new points; poor impression | Ask leave to file late; argue from the petition | Diary the reg. 14 period |
| Disputing an affidavit at the hearing without having asked to cross-examine | The affidavit stands | Ask leave; explain why the request was late | Request within the reg. 15(ב) period |
| Rejecting a return-to-authority proposal by reflex | A dismissal instead of a new decision | Ask for a break; reconsider | Fill the proposal sheet in advance |
| Not attending the traffic trial | Treated as admitting the facts (s. 240) | Request to set aside under s. 130 at once, with reasons | Attend, or ask in advance to postpone |
| Testifying at length to "explain" | Admissions used against you | Stop; answer only what is asked | Decide in advance whether to testify |

## Review

- An administrative hearing is decided mostly on paper: skeleton arguments and any cross-examination request have deadlines before the hearing.
- The traffic tribunal usually decides on the written appeal; ask for a hearing, with reasons, only where facts are disputed, and watch the online system.
- A fine-offence trial is a criminal trial: attend, cross-examine the officer briefly, keep your right to silence in mind.
- Decide before the day which proposals (return to the authority, withdrawal with costs, a lesser violation) you will accept.
