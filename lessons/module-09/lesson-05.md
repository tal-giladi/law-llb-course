# 09.5 · Rights, justice and the rule of law

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take a legal right apart into its components, compare the main theories of justice that underlie arguments about who gets what, and explain the difference between a thin, formal rule of law and a thick, substantive one. You will read three Israeli rulings where these ideas decided the case: a pardon granted before any trial, income support for kollel students, and the deferral of military service for yeshiva students. Each is contested, and the lesson gives each side's strongest legal argument.

## Why it matters

The words "rights", "justice" and "rule of law" appear in almost every public argument in Israel, often meaning different things to different speakers. Legal theory gives them precise content:

- A **right** is not one thing: it may be a claim against someone, a liberty to act, a power to change legal relations or an immunity from such change. Confusing them leads to bad arguments (Nahmani turned partly on whether "the right to be a parent" is a claim or a liberty).
- **Justice** may mean maximising welfare, protecting the least advantaged, or respecting what people have acquired fairly. Budget, tax and benefit cases are arguments between these views.
- The **rule of law** may mean only that officials follow clear, public, prospective rules, or also that the law respects rights and equality. Israel's debates over the courts since 2023 are partly a dispute between these conceptions.

For a self-represented person the practical value is precision. A letter or claim that says "my rights were violated" is weak. One that says "the authority had a duty to decide by published, equal criteria (Foundations of Budget Law s. 3א(ד)) and decided without them" is strong.

## The law

### Rights: Hohfeld and two theories

Wesley Hohfeld (1913 and 1917, public domain) showed that "right" is used for four different relations, each with a correlative:

| If A has a… | then B has a… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| claim-right | duty | A tenant's claim that the landlord repair; the landlord's duty to repair |
| liberty (privilege) | no-right | A's liberty to criticise the government; the government has no right that A stay silent |
| power | liability | A's power to make a will; heirs' liability to have their expectations changed |
| immunity | disability | A's immunity from having property taken without a law; the authority's disability |

Two theories explain what rights are for (paraphrase of the SEP entry): the **will (choice) theory** says a right gives its holder control over another's duty (to waive or enforce it); the **interest theory** says a right protects an interest important enough to impose a duty on others. Dworkin added that rights are **trumps**: a right defeats ordinary arguments of collective welfare, though not every competing consideration.

Israeli Basic Laws contain both kinds of provision:

> אין פוגעים בחייו, בגופו, או בכבודו של אדם באשר הוא אדם.

*Gist:* No one may harm a person's life, body or dignity as such: an immunity against the State. [Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו)

> כל אדם זכאי להגנה על חייו, על גופו ועל כבודו.

*Gist:* Every person is entitled to protection of life, body and dignity: a claim-right that the State act. The Court has read it to include a right to minimal dignified subsistence. [s. 4](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו)

### Justice: three families of theory

All three are paraphrased from the SEP Justice and Rawls entries.

- **Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill; public domain).** The just policy maximises total welfare. Strength: it takes everyone's interests into account. Weakness: it may sacrifice a minority for a larger gain.
- **Justice as fairness (John Rawls, A Theory of Justice, 1971).** Imagine choosing principles behind a **veil of ignorance**, not knowing your place in society. Rawls argued you would choose (1) equal basic liberties for all, and (2) social and economic inequalities only if attached to positions open to all under fair equality of opportunity and to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged (the **difference principle**).
- **Entitlement theory (Robert Nozick, 1974).** Justice concerns how holdings were acquired and transferred, not the pattern of distribution. Redistribution beyond correcting past injustice violates rights.

Aristotle's older distinction (Nicomachean Ethics, book V) still organises Israeli law: **distributive justice** governs how the State allocates benefits and burdens (budget, land, service), and **corrective justice** governs how one party repairs a wrong done to another (torts, contracts).

The Knesset has written a distributive principle into budget law:

> הסכום שנקבע בסעיף תקציב לסוג של מוסדות ציבור יחולק בין מוסדות ציבור הנמנים עם אותו סוג לפי מבחנים שוויוניים.

*Gist:* A budget allocation for a type of public institution must be divided among institutions of that type by egalitarian criteria. [Foundations of Budget Law, s. 3א(ד)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יסודות_התקציב)

### The rule of law: thin and thick

Dicey (1885; 8th edition 1915, public domain) gave the classic English statement. Among its elements, that no one is punishable "except for a distinct breach of the law", and equality before the law:

> "With us every official, from the Prime Minister down to a constable or a collector of taxes, is under the same responsibility for every act done without legal justification as any other citizen."

*Gist:* Officials are subject to the ordinary law like everyone else. [Dicey, Law of the Constitution, ch. IV](https://archive.org/details/introductiontost00diceuoft)

Modern theories (paraphrase of the SEP entry):

- **Formal (thin).** Fuller's eight requirements: laws must be general, public, prospective, clear, non-contradictory, possible to obey, reasonably stable, and applied as written. Joseph Raz argued that the rule of law in this sense is a virtue of law, like the sharpness of a knife, and does not guarantee just content.
- **Substantive (thick).** Others (Tom Bingham, and President Barak in Israel) include the protection of fundamental rights and equality in the concept. Critics reply that this turns "rule of law" into "rule of good law" and gives courts a wide mandate.

Israeli statutes express formal rule-of-law values directly: no offence without a law and no retroactive offence (Penal Law ss. 1, 3, lesson 09.1); judges subject to no authority but the law (Basic Law: The Judiciary, s. 2, lesson 09.2); and rights limited only "by a law" or under express authorisation in a law:

> אין פוגעים בזכויות שלפי חוק־יסוד זה אלא בחוק ההולם את ערכיה של מדינת ישראל, שנועד לתכלית ראויה, ובמידה שאינה עולה על הנדרש [...]

*Gist:* Rights may be infringed only by a law that befits the State's values, for a proper purpose, and no more than required. The first condition is formal; the others are substantive. [s. 8](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק-יסוד:_כבוד_האדם_וחירותו)

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 428/86 ברזילי נ׳ ממשלת ישראל, פ״ד מ(3) 505 (1986)

In 1986 the President pardoned the head of the General Security Service and three aides for offences connected with the 1984 "Bus 300" affair (the killing of two captured hijackers and the subsequent cover-up) before any investigation or trial. Petitioners argued that a pardon before conviction violates the rule of law. The Court, 2–1, dismissed the petitions. President Shamgar held that the power to pardon under Basic Law: The President extends to the period before conviction, relying on long practice and the Anglo-American model; Deputy President Ben-Porat agreed, describing the pardon as a balance between equality before the law and a vital public interest. Justice Barak dissented: the pardon power is a residual power to be used after the police, prosecution and courts have done their work, and reading it otherwise would place the President above the authorities that administer criminal justice. "The law stands above them all", he wrote of the branches of government.

Why it matters: a clear contest between a formal reading (the text and practice allow it) and a substantive rule-of-law reading (no one escapes the ordinary process of law). Shamgar's opinion also broadened public-interest standing for petitions about the rule of law. While the petitions were pending, the Attorney General announced that the police would investigate. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/barzilai-v-government-israel-0)

### בג״ץ 4124/00 יקותיאלי נ׳ השר לענייני דתות (14.6.2010)

For years the annual budget paid income support to married kollel students, although the Income Support Law excludes students in yeshivas and other students did not receive it. A seven-justice panel, 6–1, held (President Beinisch) that the budget item violated equality: eligibility was based on economic need, and need does not distinguish kollel students from other students. It amounted to earmarked funding contrary to s. 3א of the Foundations of Budget Law, and it failed proportionality. The item was to be ended, with a transition to protect recipients' reliance. Justice Levy, dissenting, held that the line drawn was legitimate social policy for the elected branches.

Why it matters: a distributive-justice case decided through equality and a statutory principle of egalitarian allocation. Supporters of the payments argue that the State may support a community's chosen way of life, including Torah study, which the Knesset has since recognised in Basic Law: Torah Study (2026, lesson 06.1); the majority's answer was that support must be given by equal criteria. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yekutieli-v-minister-religious-affairs)

### בג״ץ 6298/07 רסלר נ׳ הכנסת (21.2.2012)

The Deferral of Service for Yeshiva Students Law 2002 (the "Tal Law") allowed full-time yeshiva students to defer military service. A nine-justice panel, 6–3, held (President Beinisch) that the law violated equality, which is part of human dignity, and failed the proportionality test because it had not achieved its own stated purpose of gradually increasing Haredi service. The law was not struck down immediately but could not be extended in its existing form. Justices Arbel and Rivlin would have given the State more time; Justice Grunis held that courts should not review laws by which the majority grants a benefit to a minority, and that the court's ability to change this reality is limited.

Why it matters: "equality in bearing the burden" is a principle of distributive justice applied to duties rather than benefits. The arguments on each side remain central to Israeli law and politics: one side stresses equal citizenship and shared obligation; the other stresses religious freedom, the value of Torah study and the limits of judicial competence. Later rulings on the same subject are covered in module 6. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/ressler-v-knesset)

## How it works in practice

### Turning theory into an argument

| Theory | Question it makes you ask | Where it helps in your own case |
|---|---|---|
| Hohfeld | Is my "right" a claim, a liberty, a power or an immunity? Who has the correlative duty? | Identifying whom to sue and for what |
| Distributive justice | Were benefits or burdens allocated by relevant, published, equal criteria? | Grants, benefits, permits, tenders |
| Corrective justice | Did the other side cause a loss it must repair? | Contract and tort claims |
| Formal rule of law | Was the rule public, prospective, clear and applied as written? | Fines, sudden changes in policy, secret criteria |
| Substantive rule of law | Does the decision respect rights and equality? | Petitions and appeals against authorities |

### Fuller's checklist for a rule applied to you

1. Was it published before it was applied to you?
2. Is it general, or made for your case alone?
3. Does it apply to conduct after it was made?
4. Is it clear enough to know what was required?
5. Does it contradict another rule that also applies?
6. Was it possible to comply?
7. Has it changed so often that no one could follow it?
8. Did the official apply it as written, or something else?

A "no" to any question is the start of a legal argument: lack of publication, retroactivity, vagueness, impossibility or a gap between the rule and its application all have recognised legal consequences.

## Worked example

The community centre in Daliyat al-Karmel, a Druze town, applied for a ministry grant for youth music programmes. The ministry rejected it, saying funds were "allocated to institutions with an established track record". The criteria were not published; the director, Wafa Halabi, later learned that most of the budget went to three institutions that had received it for years.

1. **Hohfeld.** The centre has no claim-right to a grant. It has a claim-right that the ministry decide by equal criteria (Foundations of Budget Law s. 3א(ד)); the ministry has the correlative duty.
2. **Distributive justice.** "Track record" may be a relevant criterion, but used alone it locks in past allocations. A Rawlsian would ask whether the criteria benefit the least advantaged; a utilitarian whether the money produces the most benefit; a Nozickian would see no injustice in the pattern as such but would object to arbitrary or unequal procedure.
3. **Formal rule of law.** The criteria were not published (Fuller's requirement 1) and were not general (requirement 2 is doubtful if they fit only existing recipients).
4. **The argument.** Wafa writes to the ministry, in the centre's name through its authorised director, asking for the published criteria under which the decision was made, and stating that allocation without published egalitarian criteria is contrary to s. 3א(ד).
5. **The other side.** The ministry can argue that track record is an equal criterion that protects public money. The centre answers that it is acceptable as one criterion among published ones, not as an unpublished sole test.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A decision about you by a public body that allocated a benefit or imposed a burden: a grant, a benefit, a fee, a placement, a permit.
- The decision letter and any criteria or procedure you can find.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the decision in one sentence and identify the legal source of the power.
2. Analyse your position in Hohfeld's terms: what do you have (claim, liberty, power, immunity), and who has the correlative position?
3. Run Fuller's eight questions. Mark every "no" or "unknown".
4. Ask the distributive question: were the criteria relevant, published and equal? Note what a utilitarian, a Rawlsian and a Nozickian would each say about the allocation.
5. Write a 250-word argument, using the [argument-from-principle template](../../templates/argument-from-principle.md), that relies on one formal rule-of-law defect and one equality argument.
6. Write the strongest answer the authority could give, and your reply in two sentences.

### Template

[Argument from principle (טיעון מן העקרונות)](../../templates/argument-from-principle.md).

### How you know it worked

- You named the duty-holder and the precise duty, not just "my right".
- Your formal argument rests on a fact you can prove (no publication, a date, the wording of the rule).
- You stated the authority's best justification fairly.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Not a filing lesson. The ideas are directly useful when you argue your own case against an authority: requests for criteria and reasons, objections and appeals in your own name are open to any individual. A non-profit or company usually acts through its authorised officers; when it goes to court it usually needs a lawyer.

### Where and how

- Ask the authority in writing for the criteria and the reasons for its decision (lesson 02.7 has the letter; the Freedom of Information Law and the Decisions and Reasons Law give the rights and the deadlines in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- State the formal defect first (no published criteria, retroactive change), then the equality argument.
- Free help: the Ombudsman (State Comptroller) handles complaints against public bodies without a fee (lesson 06.5); Kol-Zchut explains benefits.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You need to petition a court against the allocation: administrative petitions have short deadlines and fees, and the authority will be represented.
- The allocation involves many applicants and the remedy you want would change their share.
- Your argument is constitutional (that a law, not just a decision, is invalid).

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claiming "a right to the grant" | The authority answers that no one is entitled to a grant | Claim the right to equal, published criteria | Run the Hohfeld analysis first |
| Arguing that the result is unjust without identifying a defect | The authority repeats its discretion | Find a formal or equality defect and prove it | Use Fuller's checklist |
| Treating "rule of law" as a slogan | The argument sounds political | Name the specific requirement (publication, prospectivity, equality before the law) | Cite the statute that embodies it |
| Presenting Ressler or Yekutieli as one-sided victories | A reader from the other side stops listening | State the dissent and the competing values | Include the strongest counter-argument |
| Ignoring the ministry's legitimate interest | Your request looks unreasonable | Accept relevant criteria, object to unpublished or exclusive ones | Ask for the narrowest remedy |

## Review

- Hohfeld separates claims, liberties, powers and immunities, each with a correlative; the will and interest theories explain why rights exist.
- Justice may be utilitarian, Rawlsian (veil of ignorance, difference principle) or entitlement-based; Israeli law applies distributive justice through equality and s. 3א(ד) of the Foundations of Budget Law.
- The thin rule of law (Fuller, Raz) concerns form; the thick rule of law adds rights and equality. Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8 contains both.
- Barzilai (formal power against substantive rule of law), Yekutieli (equal criteria for support) and Ressler v. Knesset (equality in bearing burdens) show the theories deciding cases, each with a strong dissent.
- In your own matter, name the precise right, duty and defect.
