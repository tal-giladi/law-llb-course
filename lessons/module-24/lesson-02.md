# 24.2 · Police powers: search, arrest and detention

This lesson covers what the police may do to your body, your home and your liberty before any charge: stop and hold you briefly, arrest you with or without a warrant, search your home, your person and your phone, and seize things. You will learn the conditions the law sets for each power, the duties it places on the officer, and the lawful way to protect yourself in the moment: identify, ask, state your rights, do not resist, and challenge afterwards.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> This lesson is about police powers. Never physically resist, run from or obstruct a police officer, even if you think the action is unlawful: resisting or hindering an officer is itself an offence, and the law gives you ways to challenge the action afterwards (complaint, remand hearing, exclusion of evidence, compensation). Ask questions calmly, state that you do not consent if you do not, and ask for a lawyer.

Police powers are where the right to liberty (Basic Law s. 5) and the right to privacy (s. 7) are limited daily, often in seconds, on the street or at the door. The law answers three questions for each power: on what suspicion (the threshold), for what purpose, and with what safeguards (identification, explanation, witnesses, records, a judge). Knowing them matters twice: in the moment, to ask the right question and not make things worse; and later, because a power used without its conditions can lead to release, exclusion of evidence (Yissacharov, lesson [24.1](lesson-01.md)), compensation, or a complaint to the Police Investigations Department.

## The law

### Detention on the spot: Arrests Law ss. 67, 73, 75

A police officer with reasonable suspicion that a person committed an offence, or is about to commit one that may endanger a person, the public or State security, may detain him to establish his identity and address, to question him and to give him documents (s. 67(א)). He may require him to come to the station, now or later, if the identification was insufficient or questioning on the spot is not possible (s. 67(ב)).

> בכל מקרה, לא יעוכב אדם או כלי רכב לפרק זמן העולה על שלוש שעות…

*Gist:* Detention may last only as long as reasonably needed and never more than three hours, extendable by three more for many people involved (s. 73(ב)). ([Arrests Law ss. 67–73](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_67); [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)) A private person may detain someone caught committing certain offences until a police officer arrives (s. 75).

### Arrest without a warrant: s. 23

An officer may arrest without a warrant where he has reasonable suspicion of an **arrestable offence** (any offence except a contravention, s. 23(א)(7)) and one more ground: the offence was committed in his presence or recently and he fears danger; a reasonable fear that the suspect will not appear for investigation; a reasonable fear of obstruction (hiding property, influencing witnesses, harming evidence); a reasonable fear of danger to a person, the public or State security; a listed serious offence (life-imprisonment offences, security offences, drug offences other than use, serious violence or weapons, family violence); or a breach of bail. And always:

> לא ייעצר אדם לפי סעיף זה אם ניתן להסתפק בעיכוב.

*Gist:* No arrest if a detention would do (s. 23(ג)). This is proportionality written into the power.

### The officer's duties on arrest: s. 24

> העוצר אדם יזהה את עצמו תחילה בפני החשוד… יודיע לו מיד שהוא עצור ויבהיר לו את סיבת המעצר בהקדם האפשרי…

*Gist:* The arresting officer must identify himself, tell the person at once that he is under arrest and explain why as soon as possible, and show a warrant if there is one; the duties may be postponed only in listed situations (danger, frustration of the arrest), and "מילוי החובות… הוא תנאי לחוקיות המעצר" — complying is a condition of the arrest's legality (s. 24(ג)). ([Arrests Law s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_24))

### At the station: ss. 25–31

The arrested person is brought to the station and to the **officer in charge**, who reviews the arrest and may release with or without bail (s. 27). Before deciding he must let the person speak, after warning him that he need not say anything that may incriminate him (s. 28). If he keeps the person in custody, he must bring him before a judge as soon as possible and within 24 hours (s. 29; Shabbat and holiday rules in s. 29(ב)–(ה)); if that does not happen, the person must be released (s. 31(ב)). Rights on arrest (s. 32–34) are in lesson [24.3](lesson-03.md). A person released without indictment may ask the court for compensation if there was no basis for the arrest (s. 38).

### Arrest warrant before indictment: ss. 13, 17

A judge orders arrest only on reasonable suspicion of an offence that is not a contravention, plus a ground: obstruction or flight, danger, or (for up to five days) investigation steps that need custody; and not if bail and conditions would achieve the purpose with less harm (s. 13(ב)). Periods and extensions are in lesson [24.4](lesson-04.md).

### Searching a place: Search Ordinance ss. 23–28

A judge may issue a search warrant for any house or place where the search is needed to produce an object for an investigation or trial, or where there is reason to believe stolen property or an object of an offence is kept, or an offence is being committed against a person there (s. 23). Without a warrant, an officer may enter and search only if he has reason to believe a felony is being committed there or was committed recently, the occupier asks for police help, someone there asks for help and there is reason to believe an offence is being committed, or he is pursuing a fugitive (s. 25(א)(1)–(4)).

> חיפוש, בין על פי צו ובין שלא על פי צו, ייערך בפני שני עדים שאינם שוטרים, זולת אם…

*Gist:* Every search, with or without a warrant, is conducted before two witnesses who are not police officers, unless urgency prevents it (recorded in a protocol), a judge allowed it, or the occupier asked to search without them (s. 26). The occupier or someone on his behalf may be present and receives on request a signed list of seized items (s. 28). ([Search Ordinance ss. 23–28](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(מעצר_וחיפוש)#סעיף_23))

A temporary provision in force from 20 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 adds a warrantless search where there is a recent reasonable suspicion that a place holds footage or a camera that may prove a serious felony or listed offences, with a senior officer's written approval and reporting duties (s. 25(א)(5), (ג)–(ה)). Check whether it has been extended when you read this.

### Searching the body: Body Search Law ss. 2–3, 11

A suspect's body is searched only under the Body Search Law, only after his consent is requested, and in a way that preserves dignity, privacy and health as far as possible, by a person of the same sex with listed exceptions (s. 2). An external search needs reasonable suspicion that evidence is on the body; if the suspect refuses, an officer must hear his reasons and may authorise reasonable force (s. 3(ג)–(ד)); refusal of some searches may be used as evidence against him (s. 11). ([Body Search Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_חיפוש_בגוף_ונטילת_אמצעי_זיהוי)#סעיף_2)) A limited pat-down of a detained person for weapons, when he is taken into a police car or ambulance, is a separate power that needs no suspicion (Arrests Law s. 72א).

### Seizure and computers: ss. 23א, 32

An officer may seize an object if he reasonably believes an offence was or is about to be committed with it, or it may be evidence (s. 32(א)). Searching computer material ("חדירה לחומר מחשב") needs a warrant that says so (s. 23א). A seized computer that can be separated from the storage and is not needed as evidence must be returned within a period set in the law unless a magistrate orders otherwise (s. 32(ב1); [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)). The owner may ask the court to return seized property (s. 34).

## Leading cases

### רע״פ 10141/09 בן חיים נ׳ מדינת ישראל (6.3.2012)

Officers asked passers-by to "agree" to a search of their pockets or bags without any suspicion. The Supreme Court (President Beinisch) held that where there is no statutory power, consent can justify a search only if it is informed consent: the officer must tell the person that he may refuse and that refusing will not be held against him. Evidence from a search without real consent may be excluded under Evidence Ordinance s. 56א, which codified Yissacharov in 2022. Why it matters: "Can I look in your bag?" is a request, not an order; you may say no. Free source: the [Hebrew Wikipedia article](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/הלכת_בן_חיים) (secondary).

### בג״ץ 3809/08 האגודה לזכויות האזרח בישראל נ׳ משטרת ישראל (28.5.2012)

Petitions challenged the Communications Data Law 2007, which lets the police obtain traffic and location data (not content) from phone and internet companies. The Court upheld the law, reading its emergency power to obtain data without a warrant narrowly and stressing judicial warrants as the rule, data minimisation and reporting. Why it matters: your phone's location history is reachable by the police under a separate statutory regime, but within proportionality limits; content needs a wiretap or computer-search warrant. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/association-civil-rights-israel-v-israel-police)

## How it works in practice

### The ladder of powers

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A["Question on the street (no power needed)"] --> B["Detention (עיכוב): reasonable suspicion, max 3 h (s. 67, 73)"]
  B --> C["Arrest without warrant: arrestable offence + ground, not if detention suffices (s. 23)"]
  C --> D["Officer in charge: release, bail or custody (ss. 27–28)"]
  D --> E["Judge within 24 h (s. 29)"]
```

### Phrases that protect you without obstructing

| Situation | What to ask or say (Hebrew and English) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Stopped on the street | "אני מעוכב? מה החשד?" — Am I being detained, and on what suspicion? | Detention needs reasonable suspicion (s. 67) |
| Asked to come to the station | "האם אני עצור או מוזמן? אפשר לתאם מועד?" — Am I arrested or invited; can we set a time? | Invitation (s. 67(ב)) is not arrest |
| Arrested | "מה סיבת המעצר? אני מבקש להודיע לקרוב משפחה ולעורך דין" — Why am I arrested? I ask that a relative and a lawyer be told | Duties in ss. 24, 32–33 |
| Asked to consent to a search | "אני לא מסכים לחיפוש. יש צו?" — I do not consent. Is there a warrant? | Ben Haim: consent must be informed and free |
| Search of the home | "אני מבקש עדים ועותק מרשימת התפוסים" — I ask for witnesses and a copy of the seizure list | Ordinance ss. 26, 28 |
| Phone demanded | "אני לא מוסר סיסמה בלי ייעוץ עם עורך דין" — I will not give a password without a lawyer | Self-incrimination; s. 23א warrant |

If the officer proceeds anyway, do not resist. Remember names, badge numbers, time and witnesses, and write them down as soon as you can.

### Challenging afterwards

- **In the remand hearing:** argue that the arrest was unlawful (s. 24(ג)) or unnecessary (s. 23(ג)); the judge may release.
- **In the trial:** ask to exclude evidence from an unlawful search (Evidence Ordinance s. 56א; Ben Haim), usually through your lawyer.
- **Seized property:** ask the magistrates' court to return it (Ordinance s. 34).
- **Compensation:** Arrests Law s. 38 after release without indictment; a tort claim for false imprisonment (lesson [04.4](../module-04/lesson-04.md)).
- **Complaint about an officer:** the Police Investigations Department (מח"ש) at the Ministry of Justice (⚠️ check the current form on gov.il).

### Neutrality note

Warrantless powers are contested. Supporters stress that offenders destroy footage and phones within minutes; critics stress that consent searches and stops fall more often on minorities and the young. The 2026 temporary search power reflects that debate. Courts respond by tightening conditions (Ben Haim) rather than by removing powers.

## Worked example

Noam Peretz, 19, from Bat Yam, is standing with friends near a kiosk at 23:00 when two officers approach after a report of a stolen scooter nearby.

1. **Detention.** The officer asks for ID. A recent report and the group's presence near the scene may be reasonable suspicion; detention for identification is lawful (s. 67(א)). Noam asks, "מה החשד?" and shows his ID.
2. **"Can we check your bag?"** There is no specific suspicion against Noam. Under Ben Haim, the officer must tell him he may refuse. Noam says calmly, "אני לא מסכים לחיפוש". The officer writes his details and lets him go.
3. **Variation: arrest.** A witness points to Noam. The officer says he is arrested, identifies himself and states the reason (s. 24). At the station the officer in charge hears Noam after the warning (s. 28). Noam says only that he wants a lawyer (lesson 24.3). He is released on conditions at night (s. 42) or brought before a judge within 24 hours (s. 29).
4. **Variation: home search.** Two days later officers come with a warrant for the scooter. Noam's mother asks for two civilian witnesses and the seizure list (ss. 26, 28). They take his laptop; the warrant does not mention computer material. Noam's lawyer later asks for its return and for exclusion of anything found on it (ss. 23א, 32(ב1), 34).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Arrests Law ss. 23–31, 67–75, Search Ordinance ss. 23–34 and Body Search Law ss. 2–3 (links above).
- A card or note on your phone for the "phrases" table.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. **Threshold table (15 min).** For detention, arrest without warrant, arrest warrant, search with warrant, search without warrant and body search, write: the threshold (suspicion level), the purpose, the safeguards and the section.
2. **Three scenarios (15 min).** Decide lawful or not, and why: (a) an officer arrests a driver for a contravention because he "was rude"; (b) police enter a flat without a warrant after neighbours report screams; (c) police ask a teenager to open his bag "just to check" and he agrees without being told he may refuse.
3. **Your rights card (15 min).** Write a five-line card in Hebrew with the questions and statements you would use, and the three facts you would record afterwards (names, numbers, witnesses). The template [suspect's rights card](../../templates/suspect-rights-card.md) is a model.

### Template

[Suspect's rights card (כרטיס זכויות לחשוד ולעצור)](../../templates/suspect-rights-card.md).

### How you know it worked

- Your table gives a section for every threshold and safeguard.
- Scenario (a) is unlawful (no arrest for a contravention, s. 23(א)(7)); (b) is lawful if there is reason to believe an offence against a person is happening (s. 25(א)(3)); (c) is a defective consent search under Ben Haim.
- Your card has no sentence that tells you to resist or to argue on the spot.

### Self-check

- [ ] I know detention is limited to three hours and arrest is barred where detention suffices.
- [ ] I know the officer must identify himself and give the reason for arrest, and that this is a condition of legality.
- [ ] I know a search is held before two civilian witnesses unless an exception applies.
- [ ] I know I may refuse a consent search and that refusal must be explained to me.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, for asserting your rights in the moment, asking for the return of seized property in a simple case, and filing a complaint about an officer. Challenging an arrest in a remand hearing or excluding evidence is a job for a lawyer or the Public Defender, who represents detainees without means (Public Defender Law s. 18(א)(7)).

### Where and how

- Return of seized property: application to the magistrates' court under Search Ordinance s. 34 (⚠️ check the court fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Complaint about an officer: Police Investigations Department, Ministry of Justice (⚠️ manual check on gov.il).
- Compensation for arrest: Arrests Law s. 38, in the court that dealt with the arrest.
- Help: the Public Defender for detainees; Legal Aid does not cover criminal defence ([where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are arrested, or asked to give a body sample, a password or consent to a phone search.
- Police seized a phone or computer you need, or with private material on it.
- You were injured during an arrest or search.
- The search found something that may lead to charges.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulling away or arguing physically | A new offence; force may be used lawfully | Stop at once; ask for a lawyer | Ask questions, never resist |
| "Agreeing" to a search to look cooperative | Evidence found is usually admissible | Lawyer may argue the consent was not informed (Ben Haim) | Say "I do not consent" clearly |
| Not asking for witnesses or the seizure list | Hard to prove what was taken | Ask the station for the list | Ask for both at the start (ss. 26, 28) |
| Handing over a phone password on the spot | Full access to private data | Consult a lawyer before any further step | Say you will decide after consulting a lawyer |
| Waiting months to ask for seized items | Property kept or sold (s. 38 of the Ordinance) | Apply to court under s. 34 | Ask in writing soon after the seizure |

## Review

- Detention (עיכוב) needs reasonable suspicion and lasts at most three hours; arrest without warrant needs an arrestable offence plus a ground, and is barred if detention suffices (Arrests Law ss. 23, 67, 73).
- The officer's duty to identify himself and explain the arrest is a condition of its legality (s. 24(ג)); a judge must see the detainee within 24 hours (s. 29).
- Searches need a warrant or a listed exception, and two civilian witnesses (Search Ordinance ss. 23, 25, 26); computers need a specific warrant (s. 23א).
- Consent to a search counts only if informed and free (Ben Haim).
- Assert rights calmly and challenge afterwards; never resist.
