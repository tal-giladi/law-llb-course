# 24.3 · Interrogation: the warning, silence, a lawyer and recording

This lesson is about the room where most criminal cases are decided: the police interrogation. You will learn the difference between a witness and a suspect, what the caution means, the right to silence and its price, the right to a lawyer and when the police may delay it, the Public Defender for detainees, and the rules on recording. By the end you will have a written plan for what you would do if you were summoned for questioning tomorrow.

## Why it matters

> [!WARNING]
> If you are summoned for questioning as a suspect, or arrested, this lesson is not a substitute for a lawyer. Talk to a lawyer, or ask for the Public Defender, before you answer questions about the facts. Never lie to investigators, never destroy or hide evidence and never contact witnesses about the case: these are offences of their own and damage your defence. Staying silent and asking for a lawyer are lawful; obstructing is not.

Most convictions in Israel rest on what the suspect said. A statement given in the first hours, tired and without advice, is recorded and will be read to the judge years later. The rules in this lesson exist because interrogation is the stage where the power gap is widest: the investigator knows the evidence, the suspect does not; the investigator has done this many times, the suspect is frightened. The law answers with four tools: the caution and the right to silence, the right to a lawyer, the duty to record, and judicial control over confessions (exclusion, the need for "something extra", Milstein). Module 23 (Evidence) covers confession admissibility in depth; here the focus is on the suspect's rights in the room.

## The law

### The duty to answer and its limit: Testimony Ordinance s. 2

> אדם, הנחקר כך, יהיה חייב להשיב נכונה על כל השאלות… חוץ משאלות שהתשובות עליהן יהיה בהן כדי להעמידו בסכנת האשמה פלילית.

*Gist:* A person questioned by the police must answer truthfully, except questions whose answers would expose him to a criminal charge. ([Testimony Ordinance s. 2(2)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הפרוצידורה_הפלילית_(עדות))) This is the statutory root of the privilege against self-incrimination, and of the right to silence of a suspect. A witness (עדות פתוחה) must answer, except self-incriminating questions; a suspect is questioned "under caution" (באזהרה).

### The caution: Arrests Law s. 28

Before the officer in charge decides on arrest or bail he must let the person speak, "לאחר שהזהירו כי אינו חייב לומר דבר העלול להפלילו, כי כל דבר שיאמר עשוי לשמש ראיה נגדו" — after warning him that he need not say anything that may incriminate him and that anything he says may be used as evidence against him. ([Arrests Law s. 28(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_28)) The same caution, in police practice and case law, opens every questioning of a suspect. The caution form used in practice also tells the suspect that his silence may strengthen the evidence against him; whether and how silence in questioning is weighed is a matter for the trial court.

### Rights on arrest: ss. 32–33

On deciding to arrest, the officer in charge must explain at once, in a language the detainee understands, the arrest and its reason, and

> (1) את זכותו שתימסר הודעה על מעצרו, לאדם קרוב לו ולעורך־דין ואת זכותו להיפגש עם עורך־דין… וכן את זכותו להיות מיוצג על ידי סניגור…

*Gist:* the right to have a relative and a lawyer notified, to meet a lawyer, and to be represented by a defender under CPL s. 15 or the Public Defender Law; and how long he may be held before release or a judge (s. 32). A relative named by the detainee is notified without delay unless the detainee asks otherwise; the detainee receives the Bar Association's list of lawyers willing to act for detainees and signs that he was told (s. 33).

### The lawyer meeting: s. 34

> (א) עצור זכאי להיפגש עם עורך דין ולהיוועץ בו. (ב) ביקש עצור להיפגש עם עורך דין… יאפשר זאת האחראי על החקירה, ללא דיחוי.

*Gist:* A detainee is entitled to meet and consult a lawyer, without delay, in private and in conditions that ensure confidentiality (s. 34(א)–(ג)). The meeting may be delayed only: for a few hours to finish an investigation step in progress, by a senior officer's reasoned decision (s. 34(ד)); by up to 24 hours if it may frustrate the arrest of other suspects or the finding of evidence (s. 34(ה)); and by up to 48 hours from arrest to save life or prevent a felony (s. 34(ו)). Security offences have longer periods (s. 35), and emergency regulations made during the "Iron Swords" war extended some of them for security suspects; check whether they are still in force. ([Arrests Law ss. 32–35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(סמכויות_אכיפה_–_מעצרים)#סעיף_32); periods in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md))

### The Public Defender at the station: Public Defender Law ss. 18–19

> נעצר אדם והובא לתחנת משטרה… יודיע לו הממונה על התחנה או על החקירה, בהקדם האפשרי, כי באפשרותו לבקש מינוי סניגור ציבורי אם הוא זכאי לכך לפי חוק זה.

*Gist:* A person arrested and brought to a station must be told, as soon as possible, that he may ask for a Public Defender if eligible (s. 19). Eligible are, among others, defendants and suspects in the CPL s. 15(א) categories, a detainee without means under the regulations, minors in listed situations and people summoned to a preliminary hearing under s. 143א (s. 18(א)). The request goes in writing to the district Public Defender office; a refusal can be appealed to the National Public Defender (ss. 21–22). ([Public Defender Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסניגוריה_הציבורית#סעיף_18))

### Language, place and recording: Interrogation of Suspects Law 2002

- **Language:** questioning is in the suspect's language or one he understands and speaks, including sign language (s. 2).
- **Place:** at a police station, unless it cannot be held there or there is a substantive need for urgency, with the reasons recorded (s. 3).
- **What recording means:** a video or audio recording covers the whole questioning from start to finish, including body language on video (s. 4(א)); a written record covers the main exchanges, made at the same time or as close as possible (s. 4(ב)).

> (1) חקירת חשוד בעבירה מן העבירות המנויות בתוספת תתועד בתיעוד חזותי…

*Gist:* Questioning at a station about an offence in the Schedule — offences punishable by ten years or more — must be video-recorded, subject to an officer's written decision to use audio or writing because of a technical fault or substantive need (s. 7(1)–(2)); other offences may be recorded on video, audio or in writing (s. 7(3)). Questioning outside the station may be recorded in any form, but a re-enactment must be on video (s. 9); records made outside the station are shown to the suspect for approval (s. 11). Security offences are exempt from the recording duty, with random supervision instead (s. 17). From 29 July 2027 a five-year temporary provision extends video recording to minors and people with disabilities questioned in listed offences without a lawyer present. ([Interrogation Law](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_סדר_הדין_הפלילי_(חקירת_חשודים)#סעיף_7))

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 5100/94 הוועד הציבורי נגד עינויים בישראל נ׳ ממשלת ישראל (6.9.1999)

The General Security Service used physical methods ("moderate physical pressure": shaking, painful positions, sleep deprivation) under internal directives. The State argued that the methods were needed to prevent imminent terror attacks ("ticking bomb" cases) and were covered by the general power to investigate and by the necessity defence. An expanded panel held that a reasonable interrogation must be free of torture and cruel or degrading treatment, that the GSS had no statutory authority to use such methods, and that the necessity defence (Penal Law s. 34יא) cannot serve as an advance authorisation; at most it may be considered after the fact in a decision to prosecute an investigator. Why it matters: it sets the outer limit of every interrogation in Israel and grounds the exclusion of confessions obtained by improper means. You met its necessity analysis in lesson [05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md). [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/public-committee-against-torture-v-israel)

### רע״פ 4142/04 מילשטיין נ׳ התובע הצבאי הראשי (14.12.2006)

A soldier confessed in a police interrogation to using drugs, then denied the charge and did not testify. Israeli law requires "something extra" (דבר מה נוסף) to convict on a confession; the lower courts used his silence at trial as that something extra. The majority (Justice Arbel, President Barak) held that as a rule silence at trial is not something extra, but may be in exceptional cases, sparingly: the confession must be logical, consistent and detailed, the court must rule out a false confession from internal pressure, and the silence must not stem from pressure or an innocent motive. Why it matters: a confession in the room is powerful evidence, and silence later does not undo it; video recording strengthens it further. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/milstein-v-chief-military-prosecutor)

### ע״פ 5121/98 יששכרוב נ׳ התובע הצבאי הראשי (4.5.2006)

The facts are the right-to-counsel story: the soldier was not properly told of his right to consult a lawyer, and his confession was excluded. Why it matters here: the right to consult a lawyer is not a formality; failing to notify it can cost the prosecution the confession. (Lessons [08.4](../module-08/lesson-04.md), [24.1](lesson-01.md).)

## How it works in practice

### Witness or suspect?

A summons (טלפון, SMS or letter) usually says whether you are invited to give a statement as a witness or to be questioned under caution. Ask if it does not: "אני מוזמן כעד או כחשוד?". If the questioning starts as a witness statement and turns accusatory, the investigator must stop and caution you; you may then ask to consult a lawyer before continuing.

### A summons is not an arrest

A person invited to the station who is not arrested may ask to reschedule within reason to consult a lawyer first. If he refuses to come at all, the police may detain him (Arrests Law s. 67(ב)) or seek a warrant. The lawful course is: call a lawyer the same day, agree a time with the investigator, and attend.

### Before you go

1. **Call a lawyer.** If you have none, the Bar Association's on-call lists and private criminal lawyers give a short consultation, often by phone. If you cannot pay and are arrested, ask for the Public Defender at once.
2. **Write down what you know of the suspicion** from the summons, not your account of the facts.
3. **Bring:** ID, a list of medicines, a phone number of the lawyer and of a relative written on paper (your phone may be taken).
4. **Do not** delete messages, call witnesses or "prepare a story". All are dangerous and some are offences (obstruction of justice, Penal Law s. 244).

### In the room

- Listen to the caution; ask for it to be repeated if unclear.
- Say: "אני מבקש להיוועץ בעורך דין לפני שאענה" — I ask to consult a lawyer before I answer.
- Choosing silence is a right, but it has a price at trial in some situations; it is a decision to make with a lawyer, not alone.
- If you do answer, say only what you know first-hand; never guess, never lie, never sign a written statement you have not read; you may correct it before signing.
- Ask whether the questioning is being recorded; note the time it starts and ends.

### After

Write down, the same day, who questioned you, when, what was asked, and what you were told about your rights. Your lawyer will need it if the recording is missing or the record is disputed.

## Worked example

Hadas Ben-Ami, 52, a school secretary from Kiryat Gat, receives a call: "Come tomorrow at 10 to the fraud unit, under caution, about the school fund." She suspects it concerns a cash box she managed.

1. **Status.** "Under caution" means suspect. Fraud and breach of trust may be felonies.
2. **Lawyer.** She calls a criminal lawyer, who asks the investigator to move the time by a day for a consultation; the investigator agrees.
3. **Advice.** The lawyer explains the caution, the risk of silence and of answering, and that documents (receipts, the cash book) are better produced through the lawyer after the material is clearer.
4. **Questioning.** Hadas is cautioned; the questioning is on video. She answers questions about her duties, and on the disputed payments says, on advice, that she will respond through her lawyer after seeing the documents.
5. **Outcome.** She is released without conditions. Months later a s. 60א letter arrives (lesson [24.5](lesson-05.md)); her lawyer writes the request with the receipts.

Variation: if Hadas had been arrested at home, she should have asked at once for a relative and a lawyer to be notified (s. 33) and, if she could not afford one, for the Public Defender (Public Defender Law s. 19).

### Steps

1. **Recording check (15 min).** For each, decide the required form of recording: (a) suspected wounding with intent (Penal Law s. 329, max. 20 years) questioned at the station; (b) suspected simple theft (max. 3 years) at the station; (c) a re-enactment at the scene of a burglary; (d) a security offence.
2. **Delay check (10 min).** A detainee asks for a lawyer at 02:00. The officer says "after the morning shift". Under s. 34, is that a lawful delay? What would be?
3. **Your plan (25 min).** Fill in the rights card for yourself: the lawyer you would call (name, phone) or the Public Defender office in your district, a relative's number, the sentences in Hebrew you would use, and the three things you would not do. Keep it in your wallet.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

You can and should assert your rights yourself: ask whether you are a witness or a suspect, ask for a lawyer, ask for a relative to be told, ask for the Public Defender, read before signing. Deciding whether to answer or remain silent in a suspect interrogation should be done with a lawyer. Bar Association Law s. 20 means a friend who is not a lawyer cannot advise or represent you for pay or as an occupation.

### Where and how

- Public Defender: ask the station officer (s. 19); district offices and an online request form on the Public Defender's site (⚠️ manual check on gov.il); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
- Bar Association list of lawyers for detainees: given at the station (Arrests Law s. 33(ג)).
- Deadlines and lawyer-meeting delay periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Always before answering as a suspect about the facts.
- You are asked to sign a statement, take part in a re-enactment, an identity parade or a polygraph.
- You are offered to become a State witness or to "help yourself".
- You are a minor, have a disability, or do not understand Hebrew well: insist on your language and on the special protections.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Going to a "short chat" under caution without advice | A recorded statement that fixes your version | Tell the lawyer exactly what you said; ask for the recording later (s. 74) | Call a lawyer before attending |
| Lying to "get out of it" | Lies are evidence of guilt and may be an offence | Correct it through your lawyer | Silence or the truth, never a lie |
| Signing a written record unread | Errors become "your words" | Ask to correct before signing; note disputes in writing | Read every line; write corrections |
| Not asking for a relative and a lawyer to be told | Hours alone without advice | Ask again, record the time | Say it at the moment of arrest (s. 33) |
| Messaging witnesses after questioning | Obstruction suspicion, arrest, bail conditions | Stop; tell your lawyer | No contact about the case |

## Review

- A witness must answer except self-incriminating questions; a suspect is cautioned that he need not incriminate himself and that what he says may be evidence.
- A detainee may consult a lawyer without delay and in private; delays are short, reasoned and listed (Arrests Law s. 34).
- Detainees must be told they may ask for a Public Defender (Public Defender Law s. 19).
- Station interrogations for offences of ten years or more are video-recorded (Interrogation Law s. 7); re-enactments always on video.
- Interrogations must be free of torture (Public Committee Against Torture); a confession needs "something extra", and silence at trial is that only in rare cases (Milstein).
