# 35.2 · What only lawyers may do, and unauthorised practice

By the end of this lesson you will be able to look at any legal service on offer, from a "claims company" to an AI legal app, and decide whether its provider may lawfully do what it promises, what happens to a client who uses an unlicensed provider, and where the law deliberately lets non-lawyers in. Lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md) gave you the basic rule for acting in your own affairs; this lesson looks at the monopoly itself from the profession's and the market's side.

## Why it matters

Israel reserves an unusually wide area to lawyers. Not only court representation, but also drafting legal documents for others and giving legal advice are reserved when done as an occupation or for pay (s. 20). The monopoly is defended as consumer protection: lawyers are trained, examined, insured in practice, bound by confidentiality and loyalty, hold client money in trust, and answer to disciplinary tribunals. It is criticised as a barrier to access to justice: many people cannot afford a lawyer for a small dispute, and cheaper providers (claims-assistance companies, paralegals, online platforms, now AI tools) are pushed to the margins.

For you the practical questions are sharper. If you pay a non-lawyer to write your claim, can he sue you for his fee? If he files something for you, is it valid? If a lawyer works "with" a commercial platform that found you, is that lawful? This lesson answers them.

## The law

### The reserved acts: s. 20

> הפעולות המנויות להלן, לא יעשה אותן דרך עיסוק, או בתמורה אף שלא דרך עיסוק, אלא עורך דין; [...] (3) עריכת מסמכים בעלי אופי משפטי בשביל אדם אחר, לרבות ייצוג אדם אחר במשא ומתן משפטי לקראת עריכת מסמך כזה; (4) ייעוץ וחיווי דעת משפטיים.

*Gist:* As an occupation or for any consideration, only a lawyer may represent others before courts, tribunals, arbitrators and listed offices, draft legal documents for others (including negotiating them), and give legal advice or opinions. [s. 20](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_20)

Paragraph (1) covers courts, tribunals, arbitrators and any body with judicial or quasi-judicial authority; paragraph (2) lists offices: the Execution Office, the Land Registry, the condominium superintendent, the Companies, Partnerships and Cooperative Societies registrars, the patent, design and trademark registrars, the income-tax assessor, the land-appreciation tax director and the estate-tax director.

Three interpretive points carry most cases:

1. **The two triggers are alternatives.** "דרך עיסוק" catches a free but regular service (an NGO volunteer who drafts claims every week); "בתמורה" catches a single paid act (a cousin who writes one appeal for ₪500).
2. **"For another person."** Drafting your own contract or will is never caught. Drafting for your employer as part of your job is more complex: an in-house lawyer is fine; a non-lawyer employee who drafts the company's contracts as his job is, on the text, drafting for another as an occupation. In practice routine business documents are not prosecuted, but legal opinions and court filings are.
3. **"Legal advice" has no definition.** Courts and the Bar read it as advice applying the law to a specific person's facts. General legal information (this course, Kol-Zchut, a statute) is not advice; telling a particular client what to do in his case is.

### The exceptions: s. 21 and other statutes

> הוראות סעיף 20 אינן פוגעות באלה: [...] (3) ייצוג לפני בית משפט, בית דין, גוף או אדם אחר שהייצוג לפניהם מוסדר בחיקוק [...]; (6) ייצוג של ארגון עובדים או מעבידים או של חבר בהם על ידי נציגו של ארגון כזה, בבוררות לעניני עבודה או בקשר להסכם עבודה; (7) חיווי דעת משפטי על ידי אדם שנתבקש לכך על ידי עורך דין או על ידי רשות מרשויות המדינה

*Gist:* Section 20 does not apply where another enactment regulates representation, to union and employer-organisation representatives in labour matters, or to legal opinions requested by a lawyer or a state authority. [s. 21](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_21)

Paragraph (3) is the door through which the legislature lets other professions in: tax advisers and accountants before the tax authorities (Income Tax Ordinance s. 236 and the Tax Advisers Representation Law), patent agents (s. 21(4)), rabbinical pleaders in the rabbinical courts (religious courts are outside s. 20 altogether, s. 21(2)), and non-lawyers in small claims with the court's approval (Courts Law s. 63, lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)).

### The sanctions: ss. 96–98

> מי שאינו עורך דין ועושה פעולה שנתייחדה לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין, דינו – קנס [...]

*Gist:* A non-lawyer who does a reserved act commits an offence punishable by a fine. [s. 96](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_96)

> (א) לא יזדקק בית משפט לתביעת שכר בעד שירות שנתן מי שאינו עורך דין, במידה שהיה בו שירות שנתייחד לפי חוק זה לעורכי דין.

*Gist:* A court will not hear a fee claim by a non-lawyer for a service that is reserved to lawyers. [s. 98](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_98)

Impersonating a lawyer, and a suspended lawyer who keeps doing reserved acts, carry up to a year's imprisonment (s. 97).

### Keeping non-lawyers out of the business: s. 58 and rule 11ב

> עורך דין לא יעסוק במקצועו בשותפות עם אדם שאינו עורך דין [...] ולא ישתף אדם כזה בהכנסותיו – אם ברוטו ואם נטו – בתמורה לשירותים, סיוע או תועלת אחרת לעסקו

*Gist:* A lawyer may not practise in partnership with a non-lawyer or share fees with one in return for services or benefits to the practice. [s. 58](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_58)

> (א) עורך דין לא ייתן שירות משפטי ללקוח, שהפנה אליו גוף – שאינו עורך דין [...] – הפועל למטרת רווח ולמטרה זו מפרסם ברבים מתן שירותים משפטיים.

*Gist:* A lawyer may not serve a client referred by a for-profit non-lawyer body that advertises legal services; and a lawyer employed by a for-profit non-lawyer may not serve outsiders for whom the employer charges. [Rule 11ב](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

Together these rules mean that a lawful "legal platform" is either run by lawyers, or is a pure directory that does not itself advertise legal services for profit and does not take a share of fees. The Bar's central committee may allow exceptions in the public interest (r. 11ב(ג)).

### Where the legislature regulated non-lawyers instead of banning them: National Insurance

> לעניין סימן זה טיפול בתביעה הוא אחד מאלה: (1) ייצוג אדם לפני המוסד [...] לפני ועדה רפואית או ועדת עררים, או לפני בית הדין לעבודה [...]; (2) סיוע אחר בהגשת תביעה והקשור בה או הנובע ממנה.

*Gist:* The fee-cap chapter of the National Insurance Law covers both representation and "other assistance" in filing a benefit claim, with separate, lower caps for assistance that does not include a lawyer's representation. [ss. 315ב–315ג](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הביטוח_הלאומי)

The 2015 amendment accepted that commercial "claims-assistance" companies exist and capped what they may charge (lesson [35.4](lesson-04.md)). It did not make them lawyers: representation in the labour court remains reserved, and the caps for "a lawyer's handling that includes representation and appearance" before medical committees are higher than for "other assistance".

## Leading cases

### רע״א 8120/08 (recap from lesson 01.1)

Without an express provision, only a licensed lawyer may represent another adult party in court; Israeli civil procedure has no "close friend" representative. Read the summary and link in [lesson 01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md). Why it matters here: it is the court-side counterpart of s. 20. A court will not hear the unlicensed representative, so the client loses the hearing even if no one is ever prosecuted under s. 96.

### Key text: s. 98, the civil sanction

Prosecutions under s. 96 are rare; the civil rule in s. 98 is what bites in practice. A non-lawyer "consultant" who drafted a contract or a claim for a client cannot sue for his fee, to the extent the service was reserved. If the service mixed reserved and unreserved elements (bookkeeping plus a legal opinion, for example), the court can still hear a claim for the unreserved part ("במידה ש"). For a client this is leverage, not a licence to cheat: refusing to pay for honest non-legal work is not protected.

### Key text: the debate about the monopoly

There is no single leading judgment on the scope of "legal advice" that can be read in a free source, and the policy debate runs mostly in the literature, Bar enforcement and legislation. The strongest argument for a wide monopoly is **asymmetry of information**: a client cannot judge the quality of legal advice until it is too late, so the state screens providers in advance and disciplines them afterwards. The strongest argument for narrowing it is **unmet legal need**: most people with a legal problem never see a lawyer, and regulated non-lawyer providers (as in the National Insurance chapter) or technology could serve them more cheaply. The legislature's recent choices show both instincts: it regulated claims-assistance companies by capping their fees rather than banning them (2015), and in 2026 it extended fee caps to claims by disabled soldiers and hostile-action victims (lesson 35.4). Whether an AI system that gives individual legal answers for a subscription is "giving legal advice for consideration" has not been decided by a court in a published ruling found for this lesson; on the text of s. 20(4) the risk is real for the operator, and the Bar's ethics committee has warned lawyers not to let AI agents give independent legal advice (lesson [35.7](lesson-07.md)).

## How it works in practice

### A four-question test for any service

| # | Question | If yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the provider a lawyer (or a law firm or lawyers' company)? | Lawful; ethics rules apply |
| 2 | Is it a reserved act: representing before a listed body, drafting a legal document for you, or advising you on your legal position? | Go to 3 |
| 3 | Is it done as an occupation or for any consideration? | Go to 4 |
| 4 | Does s. 21 or another statute let this kind of provider do it (tax adviser, patent agent, union representative, rabbinical pleader, small-claims approval)? | Lawful within that statute; otherwise unauthorised practice |

### Typical services, classified

| Service | Lawful? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A friend helps you once, free, to read your lease | Yes | Not an occupation, no consideration |
| A "claims company" files your small claim and argues it for 20% | No | Representation for pay; s. 63 approval is unavailable to someone who represents regularly, as a business or for pay |
| An accountant represents you before the income-tax assessor | Yes | s. 21(3) with Income Tax Ordinance s. 236 |
| A benefits-assistance company fills in your NII disability claim for a capped fee | Regulated | National Insurance Law ss. 315א–315ג caps "other assistance"; labour-court representation stays reserved |
| A website sells fill-in-the-blank contract templates | Usually yes | Selling a form is not drafting "for another person"; tailoring it to your facts on request moves toward drafting and advice |
| A platform matches you to a lawyer and takes 15% of the fee | No for the lawyer | s. 58 bans fee sharing with non-lawyers; r. 11ב bans serving clients referred by for-profit advertisers of legal services |
| A union representative argues your dismissal case in labour arbitration | Yes | s. 21(6); labour court: Labour Court Law s. 34 |
| An AI app that tells you, for a monthly fee, whether to sign your settlement | Unsettled | The operator risks s. 20(4); no published ruling found |

### What happens to you, the client

- **Validity.** A court may refuse to hear an unlicensed representative, and a document filed by one in your name may need to be refiled in your own name. Keep control: sign and file documents yourself.
- **No fee claim against you** (s. 98), but money already paid is not automatically refundable; you would need a separate claim (for example in restitution or for misrepresentation).
- **No protection.** No trust account rules, no confidentiality privilege under Evidence Ordinance s. 48 (lesson [23.2](../module-23/lesson-02.md)), no disciplinary route.

## Worked example

Rina Abu-Ghosh from Abu Ghosh was injured at work. Three offers arrive:

- **"Zchuyot Plus Ltd."** will prepare and submit her National Insurance work-injury claim and "accompany" her to the medical committee, for a percentage of the benefit plus a file-opening fee.
- **Her neighbour Eli**, a retired claims adjuster, offers to "do the whole thing, including the labour court if needed", for ₪3,000.
- **A lawyer** offers representation at the medical committee and, if needed, in the labour court.

Analysis:

1. Zchuyot Plus may provide "other assistance" for a fee within the statutory cap (ss. 315ב–315ג). Rina should check that the agreement states the cap and does not exceed it, and that the company is not promising labour-court representation.
2. Eli, not a lawyer, would be acting for pay. Filing documents is drafting for another; representing her in the labour court is reserved (s. 20(1)) and no exception covers him. If she agrees and later refuses to pay, a court would not hear his fee claim (s. 98), but Rina, not Eli, bears the risk of a badly run claim.
3. The lawyer may do everything, under a higher cap if the handling includes representation at the committee.
4. Rina's choice: file the claim herself with free help (lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md)); if she wants help, use either the capped company for the paperwork or the lawyer, with a written agreement that cites the statutory cap.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- Three real offers of legal or "rights" services you have seen (ads, messages, websites), or the examples in this lesson.
- 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. For each offer, write what is actually promised in one line: represent, draft, advise, or inform.
2. Apply the four-question test. Record the answer to each question and the section that decides it.
3. For any lawful non-lawyer service, find the statute that lets it operate and the limit it sets (fee cap, forum, approval).
4. For any offer that crosses the line, write what you would do instead: act yourself, a capped provider, Legal Aid, or a lawyer.
5. Note one offer where you are unsure, and write the strongest argument on each side.

### Template

None. A table with columns: offer, promise, reserved act?, occupation or pay?, exception, verdict, alternative.

### How you know it worked

- Every verdict cites a section (s. 20 paragraph, s. 21 paragraph, or another statute).
- You distinguished information from advice in at least one case.
- Your "unsure" case has a reasoned argument both ways.

- [ ] I can recite the two triggers of s. 20 and the four kinds of reserved act.
- [ ] I know why a non-lawyer cannot sue me for a fee for reserved work.
- [ ] I can explain why a lawyer may not take clients from a platform that shares fees.
- [ ] I can name two statutes that let non-lawyers represent others.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes, in your own affairs everywhere (lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)). This lesson does not change that; it tells you which helpers are lawful. Helping another person informally, once and free, is lawful; doing it regularly or for any benefit is not.

### Where and how

- **Report unauthorised practice or impersonation:** the district ethics committee of the Bar (the Bar enforces ss. 96–97 and often sues or complains to the police) and the police for impersonation (s. 97). Address: the Bar's website (⚠️ manual check).
- **Lawful low-cost help:** Legal Aid, university clinics, the Judiciary's information centre and Kol-Zchut (lesson [12.8](../module-12/lesson-08.md)); for NII claims, the free "יד מכוונת" service (lesson 17.9).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A non-lawyer has already filed in your case and a deadline is running: have a lawyer check validity and refile if needed.
- You are being sued by a "consultant" for a fee, or you are building a business that touches legal services: the scope of s. 20 and s. 58 in your model needs a professional opinion.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paying a claims company to argue your case | The court refuses to hear the representative; the hearing may be lost | Ask for an adjournment and appear yourself | Use non-lawyers only for capped, lawful assistance |
| Assuming any "rights" company is a law firm | No privilege, no trust account, no Bar discipline | Move the file to a lawyer or to yourself | Ask: "Is the person handling my case a lawyer? Registration number?" |
| A lawyer accepting clients from a fee-sharing platform | Disciplinary offence for the lawyer (s. 58, r. 11ב); the arrangement is open to challenge | Choose a lawyer directly | Ask how the platform is paid |
| Treating a template site as advice | Template misses your facts | Adapt it yourself or get advice | Read the template's header: who it is for and its limits |

## Review

- Section 20 reserves representation, legal drafting for others and legal advice when done as an occupation or for any consideration.
- Exceptions come from s. 21 and other statutes (tax advisers, patent agents, union representatives, rabbinical pleaders, small-claims approval, capped NII assistance).
- The provider risks a fine (s. 96), imprisonment for impersonation (s. 97) and loses any fee claim (s. 98); the client loses protection and sometimes the hearing.
- Lawyers may not share fees with non-lawyers or take clients from for-profit advertisers of legal services (s. 58, r. 11ב).
- Whether AI legal advice for a fee is unauthorised practice is unsettled; the text of s. 20(4) points to risk.
