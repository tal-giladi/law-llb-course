# 35.3 · Professional conduct: loyalty, conflicts and confidentiality

By the end of this lesson you will be able to test whether a lawyer may take or keep your case when other loyalties are involved, explain exactly what your lawyer must keep secret and when a court can still hear it, and use these rules in practice: when you hire a lawyer, when your opponent's lawyer used to work for you, and when you face a represented opponent alone.

## Why it matters

Loyalty and confidentiality are the core of what you buy from a lawyer. A lawyer who is quietly loyal to someone else (your opponent, a regular corporate client, his own financial interest) can damage you more than an incompetent one, because you will not see it. The rules on conflicts are therefore strict and largely prophylactic: they forbid the situation, not only the betrayal.

These rules also shape your life as a self-represented party. A lawyer on the other side owes you courtesy and honesty (rules 23–25, 34), may not threaten you, and may not use what he learned when he once acted for you. Knowing this lets you object at the right moment and in the right forum. The evidential side of confidentiality, the lawyer–client privilege in Evidence Ordinance s. 48, was covered in lesson [23.2](../module-23/lesson-02.md); here we look at the ethical duty, which is wider.

## The law

### The basic duty: s. 54 and rule 2

> במילוי תפקידיו יפעל עורך דין לטובת שולחו בנאמנות ובמסירות, ויעזור לבית המשפט לעשות משפט.

*Gist:* A lawyer must act for the client's benefit with loyalty and devotion, and help the court do justice. [s. 54](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_54)

> עורך דין ייצג את לקוחו בנאמנות, במסירות, ללא מורא, תוך שמירה על הגינות, על כבוד המקצוע ועל יחס כבוד לבית המשפט.

*Gist:* The lawyer represents the client loyally, devotedly and fearlessly, while keeping fairness, the dignity of the profession and respect for the court. [Rule 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

The two halves of s. 54 pull in different directions. The lawyer is the client's partisan, but also an officer of the court. Rule 34 resolves the tension at its sharpest point: a lawyer may not put forward a factual or legal claim he knows to be false, though denying claims in civil pleadings and pleading not guilty in a criminal case are allowed.

### Conflicts: rule 14

> (א) לא ייצג עורך דין לקוח, לא יקבל על עצמו לייצגו ולא ימשיך בייצוגו, אם קיים חשש שלא יוכל למלא את חובתו המקצועית כלפיו, בשל עניין אישי שלו, או בשל התחייבות או חובת נאמנות שיש לו כלפי אחר [...]
> (ב) לא ייצג עורך דין צדדים בעלי אינטרסים מנוגדים באותו ענין.
> (ג) בענין שצד בו לקוח קבוע של עורך הדין לא ייצג עורך דין צד אחר [...]
> (ד) הוראות סעיפים קטנים (ב) ו־(ג) לא יחולו על עריכת הסכם ועל טיפול בענין שהצדדים הסכימו, בכתב, כי ייעשה בידי אותו עורך דין.

*Gist:* A lawyer may not take or continue a case where there is a risk ("חשש") that a personal interest, a duty to another or even work overload will prevent him from serving the client; may not act for parties with opposing interests in the same matter; and may not act against a regular client; but parties may agree in writing that one lawyer drafts an agreement or handles a matter for both. [Rule 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

Note the test in rule 14(א): a *risk*, not proof of harm. Note also what it lists: the lawyer's own interest (he is a shareholder in the other side, he wants a job there), a duty to someone else (another client, a fiduciary role), and workload. Rule 14(ה) allows lawyer and client to agree in writing to narrow the service so as to avoid a clash, if the narrowing is reasonable and does not harm loyalty. Section 60 adds that a lawyer may not engage in another occupation where a conflict may arise.

### Former clients: rules 15–17

> (א) עורך דין לא יטפל נגד לקוח – (1) בענין או בקשר לענין שטיפל בו למען אותו לקוח; (2) בענין אליו יש זיקה של ממש למידע שקיבל עורך הדין מאותו לקוח או מטעמו.

*Gist:* A lawyer may not act against a client, including a former client, in the same matter or in any matter substantially connected to information he received from that client. [Rule 16](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

Rule 15 adds that a lawyer who drafted a document or handled a transaction may not later argue that it is invalid, or act for someone whose case reasonably requires that argument. Rule 17 bars acting in a matter the lawyer decided as a judge or arbitrator. The district committee may rule in advance, at a lawyer's request, that a mere consultation was not "handling" or that particular information is not substantially connected (rule 16(ב)).

### Confidentiality: rule 19, and its limit at s. 90

> עורך דין ישמור בסוד כל דבר שיובא לידיעתו בידי לקוח או מטעמו, תוך כדי מילוי תפקידיו, זולת אם הסכים הלקוח במפורש אחרת; הוראה זו אינה חלה על גילוי בהליך משפטי, חקירה או חיפוש שאינו חסוי על פי סעיף 90 לחוק.

*Gist:* The lawyer must keep secret everything the client or someone on the client's behalf brought to his knowledge in the course of his work, unless the client expressly consents; but the ethical duty gives way to disclosure in legal proceedings, investigations or searches where the material is not privileged under s. 90. [Rule 19](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

> דברים ומסמכים שהוחלפו בין לקוח לבין עורך דין ויש להם קשר עניני לשירות המקצועי שניתן על ידי עורך הדין ללקוח, לא יגלה אותם עורך הדין בכל הליך משפטי, חקירה או חיפוש, מלבד אם ויתר הלקוח על חסינותם.

*Gist:* Communications and documents exchanged between client and lawyer that are substantively connected to the professional service may not be disclosed in any proceeding, investigation or search, unless the client waives. [s. 90](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_90)

So there are two circles. The **ethical duty** (rule 19) covers *everything* the client told the lawyer, from any source, including facts that are public elsewhere; it binds the lawyer's staff (rule 20) and forbids using the information (rule 21). The **privilege** (s. 90 and Evidence Ordinance s. 48) is narrower: only lawyer–client communications connected to the service, but it is absolute against courts and investigators unless the client waives (lesson [23.2](../module-23/lesson-02.md)). Rule 22 adds that a lawyer may not record a conversation with a client or another lawyer without their knowledge.

### Toward the other side: rules 23–25

> (א) עורך דין היודע כי הצד שכנגד מיוצג בענין פלוני בידי עורך דין – (1) לא יפנה אל הצד שכנגד אלא באמצעות עורך הדין המייצגו באותו ענין [...]

*Gist:* A lawyer who knows the other side has a lawyer must communicate through that lawyer; he must treat the other side courteously (rule 23) and may not threaten, though he may describe the lawful steps his client will take and point to the law (rule 24). [Rules 23–25](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

If you are unrepresented, the other side's lawyer may contact you directly, but must not threaten you, deceive you (rule 34 applies in court; general honesty duties apply outside) or give you advice as though he were your lawyer.

### Getting out: rule 13

A lawyer may stop acting only for listed reasons (disagreement about how to handle the case, a legal or ethical bar, unpaid fees, or another justified reason), must notify the client without delay, must try to stop in a way that does not harm the client, and in a pending court case needs the court's permission under the procedure rules.

## Leading cases

### רע״א 9930/17 אל מדאמין בע״מ נ׳ חנא (28.3.2018)

In a copyright case, the third-party defendants asked the district court to stop the claimant's lawyer from acting, because he had previously represented one of them and received information from them. The district court sent them to the Bar's ethics committee. The Supreme Court (Solberg J., with Shoham and Mintz JJ.) held that a civil court has power, under its general remedial power in Courts Law s. 75, to rule on an alleged ethics breach, but only where the breach may harm a party's legitimate interests in the case or the fairness of the proceeding. The natural forum for ethics questions is the Bar's institutions; often the court should first refer the parties for a preliminary opinion (s. 60א) and keep the last word on the remedy. Here the referral was proper because the conflict question required factual inquiry beyond the case.

Why it matters: if your former lawyer appears against you, you may ask the court to disqualify him, but you must show how the conflict harms your position in that case (use of your confidential information, rule 16), and expect the court to involve the ethics committee. [Text hosted by Reichman University](https://www.colman.ac.il/media/f3yhrn00/9930-17_al_madamin_ltd_v_hana_1.doc)

### על״ע 17/86 פלונית, עו״ד נ׳ לשכת עורכי הדין, פ״ד מא(4) 770 (as reported)

According to the account in the Reichman University ethics newsletter, the Supreme Court held that a lawyer suing a former client for fees may disclose privileged information only to the extent necessary for the claim, only what is essential, and only after exhausting ways to collect without using the information as a threat or pressure. The privilege is not a shield to avoid paying, but loyalty survives the end of the relationship. ⚠️ The judgment text itself could not be opened in a free source; the holding is cited from the newsletter.

Why it matters: it shows the duty of confidentiality outlives the retainer and limits the lawyer even when he is the one with a grievance. [Newsletter text](https://www.colman.ac.il/media/yanlrhr0/gilion_13avthat_tashlom_shar_hatrha_hafsakat_yizog_pnia_l.doc)

### Key text: the competing views on conflicts

The Bar's former ethics chair Dror Arad-Ayalon describes advocacy as a private mission with a public horizon: the lawyer's first duty is always to the client, even for a public client or a court-appointed receiver ([newsletter 48](https://www.colman.ac.il/media/xldoox2v/gilion_48_nigod_hainyanim_echel_orchei_din.doc)). Critics of strict conflict rules argue that in a small country with specialised bars, wide disqualification deprives clients of the lawyer of their choice and is used tactically to delay cases. Al Madamin reflects the balance: disqualification by a court is the exception, linked to real harm in the case.

## How it works in practice

### A conflict check you can run as a client

Before hiring, tell the lawyer the names of all parties, including the other side's companies and directors, and ask:

1. Have you or your firm ever acted for any of them? In this matter or a connected one? (rules 14(ג), 16)
2. Do you or your firm have any personal or financial interest in the outcome or in any party? (rule 14(א), s. 60)
3. Will anyone else's interests limit what you can do for me (an insurer paying your fee, a co-client)? (rule 14(א))
4. If two of us are clients (spouses, co-buyers, co-founders), what happens if we disagree? (rules 14(ב), 14(ד))
5. Who in the office will see my file, and how is it stored, including any AI tools? (rules 19–20; lesson 35.7)

Ask for the answers in the engagement letter.

### One lawyer for both sides

Rule 14(ד) allows it only for drafting an agreement or a matter the parties agreed in writing should be handled by one lawyer, typically a simple apartment sale or a consensual divorce agreement. Even then, the lawyer remains bound by rule 14(א): if a real dispute appears, he must stop. Practical rule: if the deal has a negotiable point that matters to you (price, timing, guarantees, custody), have your own lawyer.

### Raising a conflict

| Situation | Where | What to ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Your own lawyer has a conflict | The lawyer first, in writing; then the district ethics committee | Disclosure, withdrawal, file transfer, fee refund for tainted work |
| Your former lawyer acts against you in a pending case | The court (Al Madamin), usually with a referral to the Bar | Disqualification and a bar on using your information |
| You are unsure whether a step would breach the rules | Lawyers may ask the ethics committee for a preliminary opinion (s. 60א) | Not open to clients directly; ask the lawyer to seek one |

### Confidentiality in practice

- Anything you tell your lawyer for the purpose of the service is privileged; telling third parties (a friend copied on emails, a public post) can destroy the privilege (lesson 23.2).
- An insurer's lawyer who acts for you as the insured in a claim against you also has duties to the insurer; ask in writing what will be shared.
- A lawyer cannot be required to reveal your privileged communications in a search; if police search a lawyer's office, the lawyer must claim the privilege for you.

## Worked example

Shira and Omer Katz, a couple from Modi'in, are separating. Omer's cousin, attorney Gil Katz, offers to draft their divorce agreement "for both of you, half price". Gil also handled the purchase of their apartment four years ago and drafted a loan agreement between Omer and his father for part of the down payment.

1. **One lawyer for both (rule 14(ב), (ד)).** Allowed only if both agree in writing, and only while there is no real dispute. Shira wants the father's "loan" treated as a gift to both; Omer says it is a debt to be repaid from the apartment. That is a live conflict on a central point. Gil may not act for both.
2. **Acting for Omer alone (rule 14(א), 15, 16).** Gil drafted the loan agreement (rule 15: he cannot argue against its validity, but here he would defend it); he handled the apartment purchase for both spouses and received information from Shira (rule 16(א)(2)). Acting against Shira in a matter substantially connected to that information is barred. His family tie to Omer and to the father (a party with an interest in the "loan") adds a personal-interest risk (rule 14(א)).
3. **Outcome.** Gil should decline both roles. Shira writes to him, asking him to confirm that he will not act for either side and that he will not disclose what she told him in the purchase (rule 19). If Gil later appears for Omer in the family court, Shira can ask the court to disqualify him, pointing to the specific information he holds (Al Madamin), and complain to the ethics committee.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A matter you have or had with a lawyer, or the worked example's facts.
- The names of all parties connected to it.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. List every party and connected person (companies, directors, family members, insurers).
2. Write the five conflict-check questions above, adapted to your matter.
3. For each question, write which rule it tests and what answer would make you walk away.
4. Draft a short letter (Hebrew) to the lawyer asking for written answers before you sign an engagement. Include a request to confirm who will see the file and whether client data will be entered into AI tools.
5. Mark one piece of information you would share with your lawyer and decide whether it is privileged under s. 48 (lesson 23.2), confidential only under rule 19, or neither.

### Template

Use part ב of the draft [choosing and supervising a lawyer worksheet](../../templates/lawyer-engagement-worksheet.md) (new in this module) for the conflict-check letter.

### How you know it worked

- Every question is tied to a rule (14(א)–(ד), 15, 16, 19–20).
- Your walk-away answers are concrete ("you acted for my landlord's company in 2023").
- You can explain the difference between rule 19 and s. 90 for your example.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can state when one lawyer may act for both parties and when that ends.
- [ ] I know why my former lawyer may not act against me in a connected matter.
- [ ] I know the two-step test a court applies before ruling on a lawyer's conflict (Al Madamin).
- [ ] I know that the other side's lawyer must go through my lawyer, and how he must treat me if I am unrepresented.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may question a lawyer's conflicts before hiring, write to him, complain to the ethics committee, and raise a former lawyer's conflict in your own case before the court.

### Where and how

- **Before hiring:** the questions and the letter above; insist on written answers.
- **A conflict in a pending case:** a written motion in your case asking the court to rule on the opposing lawyer's representation, explaining the harm to you; expect a referral to the Bar for an opinion (Al Madamin). Motion practice: lesson [15.11](../module-15/lesson-11.md) and the [written motion template](../../templates/written-motion.md).
- **A complaint:** to the district ethics committee (lesson [35.6](lesson-06.md) and the lab in [35.8](lesson-08.md)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The conflict affects a high-stakes case (your home, custody, a business) and you need to replace a lawyer mid-case without missing deadlines.
- You believe your former lawyer is using your confidential information: the remedy (disqualification, injunction, damages) needs professional handling.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Using one lawyer for both sides when there is a real dispute | Your interests are not represented; the agreement may be attacked | Get your own lawyer before signing | Use a joint lawyer only for an agreed, simple deal, in writing |
| Raising a conflict only after losing | The court sees it as tactics; delay counts against you | Raise it as soon as you learn of it | Ask the conflict questions at the start |
| Copying friends or family on emails to your lawyer | Privilege may be lost | Stop; send future emails to the lawyer only | Talk to the lawyer alone |
| Assuming the other side's lawyer will "explain" things to you fairly | You rely on advice from someone loyal to your opponent | Get independent advice | Treat his statements as your opponent's position |

## Review

- The lawyer owes loyalty and devotion to the client and fairness to the court (s. 54, rule 2); he may not knowingly argue falsehoods (rule 34).
- Rule 14 bars acting where there is a *risk* of divided loyalty, for opposing parties, or against a regular client, with a narrow written-consent exception for drafting agreements.
- A lawyer may never act against a former client in the same or a substantially connected matter (rule 16), nor attack a document he drafted (rule 15).
- Confidentiality (rule 19) is wide but yields in proceedings; privilege (s. 90, Evidence Ordinance s. 48) is narrow but absolute unless the client waives.
- Courts may rule on ethics breaches only where they harm a party's interests in the case, usually after involving the Bar (Al Madamin, 2018).
