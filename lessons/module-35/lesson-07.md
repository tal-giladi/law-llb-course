# 35.7 · AI in legal work: duties and the courts' response

By the end of this lesson you will know what Israeli professional rules require of a lawyer who uses AI, what the Bar's ethics committee has said about it, how the courts reacted when filings with invented case law reached them, and how the same standards apply to you when you file your own documents. Lesson [21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md) taught the verification routine for academic work; this lesson applies it to practice and to your lawyer.

## Why it matters

Generative AI is now part of everyday legal work: summarising files, drafting letters, searching case law, translating. It is useful and it fails in a specific, dangerous way: it produces fluent text with citations and quotations that look right and do not exist. Between February 2025 and March 2026 Israeli courts, up to the Supreme Court, met filings built on such citations from lawyers, a public body and a self-represented party, and moved from warnings to costs. In September 2026 the Bar's National Ethics Committee published a stricter opinion. Two kinds of questions follow for you: as a client, what you should require of your lawyer's AI use and data handling; and as a self-represented party, what the court will expect of anything you file.

## The law

No Israeli statute or ethics rule mentions AI by name. The existing duties apply.

### Loyalty, devotion and helping the court: s. 54

> במילוי תפקידיו יפעל עורך דין לטובת שולחו בנאמנות ובמסירות, ויעזור לבית המשפט לעשות משפט.

*Gist:* A lawyer must act for the client loyally and devotedly and help the court do justice. [s. 54](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_54)

Filing work you did not check is neither devoted service to the client nor help to the court.

### No false claims: rule 34

> (א) לא יעלה עורך דין, בין בעל פה ובין בכתב, טענה עובדתית או משפטית ביודעו שאינה נכונה.

*Gist:* A lawyer may not put forward, orally or in writing, a factual or legal claim he knows to be untrue. [Rule 34](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/כללי_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_(אתיקה_מקצועית))

Rule 34 requires knowledge; a lawyer who did not check an AI citation may argue he did not *know* it was false. That is why the courts and the Bar treat failure to verify as a breach of the duty of care and of s. 54, and as conduct unbecoming the profession (s. 61(3)), rather than relying on rule 34 alone.

### Confidentiality: rules 19–21

The lawyer must keep secret everything the client told him (r. 19), make his staff keep it (r. 20) and not use it otherwise (r. 21). Pasting a client's file into a public chatbot that may store or train on it can breach all three (lesson [35.3](lesson-03.md)).

### The Bar's guidance

**May 2024: preliminary opinion (גילוי דעת מקדים, decision את/60/24).** The National Ethics Committee's first opinion on AI (published on gov.il and summarised on law.co.il) said, in short: AI output must be checked case by case against external sources; general tools should be used for general or theoretical queries; client personal, confidential or privileged information may not be entered into AI platforms, and in-office tools need special care and vendor contracts that bar sharing the data; client data may be used only with the client's prior, written, explicit and specific consent, which the client can easily withdraw. [Opinion (PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/ai_info_law/he/%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%99%20%D7%93%D7%A2%D7%AA%20%D7%91%D7%A2%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A9%20%D7%91%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%94%20%D7%9E%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA%20%D7%91%D7%A2%D7%91%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA%20%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%99%20%D7%94%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9F%20(2024).pdf); [summary](https://www.law.co.il/news/2024/05/08/ai-ethcis-rules-for-israeli-lawyers/). ⚠️ The PDF's Hebrew text could not be extracted automatically; the summary relies on the law.co.il report and the opinion's title page.

**17 September 2026: updated opinion.** According to Calcalist and ynet, the committee (chair: Adv. Menachem Moshkovitz) published a second, stricter opinion: AI may serve at most as a controlled technical aid and the lawyer bears full personal responsibility for any AI-assisted output; autonomous systems or AI agents may not make material legal decisions, give independent legal advice, draft pleadings or advance proceedings without close human oversight and approval (low-risk technical tasks are allowed if documented, reversible and based on a prior human decision); removing names is not enough to protect client data, which must also be walled off from staff not on the case; even closed platforms require checks of storage, permissions, logs, encryption and integrations, and vendors should commit not to train on the data or share it; disclosure to the client is required where AI materially affects the representation, where restricted information goes into open platforms (which needs prior, explicit, informed consent; a general clause in a fee agreement or power of attorney is not enough), where the client asks or has given AI instructions, or where AI affects fees. Non-compliance creates a rebuttable presumption of breach. [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/bkqvhskyfe); [ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/hkoxjhftgg). ⚠️ The opinion's own text and number were not found in a free source; cited from press reports.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 38379-12-24 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין השרעי לערעורים ירושלים (February 2025, as reported)

A lawyer's petition against a Sharia Court of Appeals decision rested mainly on judgments and quotations that do not exist; she said she had relied on a website recommended by colleagues. The Supreme Court (Kanfi-Steinitz J., with Mintz and Elron JJ.) dismissed the petition on the threshold and held that using AI does not relieve a lawyer of responsibility or professional judgment; lawyers must use these tools responsibly, carefully and critically, and filings with fabricated content breach duties to the client, the court, the other side and the profession. As the first case of its kind, it imposed no sanction, but said that in future courts should use their tools (striking pleadings, personal costs against the lawyer, and the possibility of a disciplinary offence under s. 61) and that this is "a duty, not a right". [law.co.il report](https://www.law.co.il/news/2025/02/24/israeli-supreme-court-on-attorneys-us-of-ai/). ⚠️ Case number and holding from the report; the decision text was not opened (supremedecisions.court.gov.il unreachable).

Why it matters: it set the standard every later court cites. Lesson 21.5 described the same episode from the press; here note the list of sanctions the Court put on the table.

### ת״א (שלום י-ם) 77773-12-20 זלטין נ׳ אסייג (11.7.2025, as reported)

A plaintiff who was a lawyer and accountant, representing herself in a rental dispute, filed unsigned summations citing case law and statutes that did not exist, probably AI-generated. The Jerusalem Magistrates' Court (Tcherka J.) called them a manifest contempt of the court, ordered her to pay ₪5,000 personal costs to the State Treasury and ₪20,000 costs to the defendants, relying on the Supreme Court's guidance in 38379-12-24. [law.co.il report](https://www.law.co.il/news/2025/08/10/lawyer-fined-after-submitting-ai-made-case-law/). ⚠️ From the report; the judgment text was not opened.

Why it matters: the duty to verify is not only a lawyer's. A self-represented party who files fabricated authorities can be ordered to pay costs, including to the State.

### Supreme Court 63194-08-25 בן כהן נ׳ עיריית רמת גן (22.3.2026, as reported)

A father asked Ramat Gan municipality to provide transport to school for his son with special needs. The municipality's reply relied on a Ministry of Education circular that does not exist and on fabricated quotations attributed to real case numbers, and its filings in the district court and on appeal repeated the pattern. The Supreme Court (Sohlberg D.P., with Kanfi-Steinitz and Grosskopf JJ.) allowed the father's appeal on costs, ordered the municipality to pay ₪30,000, rejected the excuse that interns made the errors (a lawyer is personally responsible for every filing, "whether produced by a person or a machine"), and warned that personal costs against lawyers may follow in future. Kanfi-Steinitz J. called for active oversight of AI tools by public bodies. [Bizportal/Takdin report](https://www.bizportal.co.il/takdin/news/article/20029328). ⚠️ The case number and date are from the decision's header and a Ministry of Justice case summary; the case-type prefix was not legible, and the text was not opened in full.

Why it matters: it extends the rule beyond court filings to an authority's letters to citizens, and shows that you, the citizen, can win costs when the other side relies on invented law.

## How it works in practice

### What the rules mean for your lawyer

| Duty | What it requires in AI use | Ask your lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| Devotion and care (s. 54, r. 2) | Every citation and quotation checked in the original | "Who checks AI-assisted drafts, and how?" |
| Honesty to the court (r. 34; s. 61) | No unchecked authorities filed | "Will any filing rely on AI-found sources you have not read?" |
| Confidentiality (rr. 19–21) | No client data in open tools; vendor terms checked; internal walls | "Which tools see my data? Is it used for training?" |
| Supervision (2026 opinion) | No autonomous AI decisions, advice or pleadings | "Does any AI agent act on my file without your approval?" |
| Disclosure and fees (2026 opinion) | Tell the client when AI materially affects the work or the fee | "Does AI change what you charge me?" |

Put the answers in the engagement agreement (lesson [35.5](lesson-05.md); the [engagement worksheet](../../templates/lawyer-engagement-worksheet.md), question 9).

### What the courts now do

- Strike or disregard the filing, or dismiss the petition at the threshold.
- Order costs against the party, at the higher end (Ramat Gan).
- Order personal costs against the lawyer, or against a self-represented party, including to the State (Zlatin).
- Point to s. 61 and a possible referral to the Bar.

### Your own filings: the routine

Use the verification routine from lesson [21.5](../module-21/lesson-05.md) and add three practice steps:

1. **Log every AI-assisted claim.** For each citation and quotation: where it came from, where you opened it (free host, URL), whether it says what you claim.
2. **Statutes from the current text.** Check the section on Wikisource or the Knesset database (lesson [02.1](../module-02/lesson-01.md)) and the date of the version.
3. **Protect personal data.** Do not paste ID numbers, medical records or the other side's personal details into public tools; summarise facts in general terms.

## Worked example

Michal Peretz from Netanya is defending a small claim by her former landlord. She asks a chatbot to "write my defence with Supreme Court precedents on deposits".

1. **Output.** The draft cites three rulings, each with a case number and a "כהן נ׳ לוי"-style name, and a quotation about deposits.
2. **Verification.** She searches the case numbers on free hosts (lesson [02.2](../module-02/lesson-02.md)). One exists but concerns a different issue; two cannot be found. The quotation appears nowhere.
3. **Decision.** She deletes all three, keeps her own facts, and cites the statute she can open (the Fair Rental provisions of the Rental and Borrowing Law, lesson [20.3](../module-20/lesson-03.md)) and one ruling she found and read herself.
4. **Data.** She had pasted the landlord's full name and ID number into the chatbot. She deletes the conversation and, next time, uses "the landlord" and no identifiers.
5. **Result.** Her defence is shorter and credible. Had she filed the fabricated citations, she risked costs like those in Zlatin, and the judge's trust in everything else she said.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- A letter or a short submission in your own matter that you drafted with AI help (or ask an AI tool to draft one for practice).
- Access to free legal sources (lessons 02.1, 02.2).
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Highlight every factual statement, statute, ruling and quotation in the draft (5 min).
2. Build a log with columns: item, source given by the tool, where you checked it, result (found and correct / found but wrong / not found), action (keep / fix / delete) (20 min).
3. Delete or replace every item you could not confirm; re-read the draft for claims that now have no support (10 min).
4. List any personal data you entered into the tool and decide how to avoid it next time (5 min).
5. Write three AI questions you will put to your lawyer, or that you would, if you hire one (5 min).

### Template

None new. Use the AI-use declaration and checks in the [paper review checklist](../../templates/paper-review-checklist.md) (from module 21) and question 9 of the [engagement worksheet](../../templates/lawyer-engagement-worksheet.md).

### How you know it worked

- Every citation in the final draft is in your log as "found and correct" with a URL.
- No personal identifiers of others went into a public tool, or you know what you will change.
- You have three concrete AI questions for a lawyer.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can name the duties that apply to a lawyer's AI use without any AI-specific rule.
- [ ] I can summarise the 2024 and 2026 Bar opinions in three points each.
- [ ] I know the sanctions courts have used or announced, including against self-represented parties.
- [ ] I verify every AI-suggested authority in the original before filing.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. You may use AI tools to help draft your own documents, but you are responsible for every word you file. Courts have ordered costs against a self-represented party for fabricated citations (Zlatin, 2025).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Where and how

- **Verification sources:** Wikisource and the Knesset database for statutes (lesson 02.1); free case-law hosts (lesson 02.2).
- **If the other side relied on invented law:** point it out in your response with the search you did, and ask for costs (Ramat Gan, 2026). If the other side is represented, you may also complain to the ethics committee (lesson [35.6](lesson-06.md)).
- **If your lawyer filed unverified AI material:** ask for an explanation in writing and consider a complaint; protect your case first (correcting the filing).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The court has already criticised your filing or ordered you to explain citations: respond carefully, and consider professional help.
- The case turns on legal research you cannot verify yourself.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filing AI-suggested rulings without opening them | Dismissal, costs, loss of credibility | File a correction and apologise | Log and verify every citation |
| Pasting the full file with personal data into a public tool | Privacy and confidentiality breach | Delete the conversation; stop | Summarise without identifiers |
| Trusting a real case number with a made-up quotation | The court finds the quote does not exist | Quote only what you read | Check the paragraph, not just the number |
| Letting your lawyer use AI on your data without terms | Data used beyond your control | Agree terms now | Question 9 in the engagement worksheet |
| Not noticing the other side's fabricated citations | You lose an easy point and costs | Search them yourself | Check every authority the other side cites |

## Review

- No AI-specific statute exists; s. 54, rules 2, 19–21 and 34, and s. 61 govern a lawyer's AI use.
- The Bar's ethics committee issued a preliminary opinion in May 2024 and a stricter one on 17 September 2026: full personal responsibility, no autonomous AI agents on material work, strict data protection and client consent.
- The Supreme Court (38379-12-24, February 2025) warned that fabricated citations call for striking, personal costs and discipline; later courts imposed costs on a self-represented party (Zlatin, 2025) and a municipality (Ramat Gan, March 2026).
- The same verification duty applies to you when you file your own documents.
