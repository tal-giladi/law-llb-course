# 35.1 · The Bar and the profession: admission, internship and the Bar exam

By the end of this lesson you will know how a person becomes an Israeli lawyer, what the Israel Bar Association is and what it controls, on what grounds it may keep someone out of the profession, and how courts review those decisions. You will also do the one check every client should do before paying anyone for legal work: confirm that the person is a licensed lawyer in good standing and look for published disciplinary rulings.

## Why it matters

Module 35 mirrors the professional-responsibility courses taught in every Israeli law faculty (TAU's המקצוע המשפטי, Bar-Ilan's אתיקה מקצועית, Haifa's course on the legal profession) and the ethics part of the Bar exam. For a lawyer it is the law of their own job. For you, as a self-represented person and a client, it is the law that protects you from the person you hire.

Lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md) taught the line between acting for yourself and acting for others, and lesson [01.6](../module-01/lesson-06.md) introduced the people of the legal system. This module goes inside the profession: who may join it, what lawyers owe their clients, how they may charge, and what you can do when a lawyer fails you. This first lesson is about the gate. The Bar Association Law makes the Israel Bar both a professional body and a statutory regulator: it registers interns, examines candidates, admits members, disciplines them and publishes the results. Understanding the gate tells you what a licence guarantees (a recognised education, supervised training, an exam, and continuing discipline) and what it does not (that this lawyer is right for your case).

## The law

### The three conditions: s. 24

> אדם כשיר להיות עורך דין אם נתקיימו בו אלה: (1) הוא בעל השכלה משפטית גבוהה; (2) עבר תקופת התמחות; (3) עמד בבחינות הלשכה

*Gist:* A person qualifies to be a lawyer after recognised legal education, an internship and the Bar exams. [s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_24)

Section 25 defines "recognised education": a degree from an Israeli law faculty or college recognised by the Council for Higher Education, a foreign law degree from an institution recognised under s. 25ו (with conversion exams), or foreign admission plus at least two years of practice or judicial office. A graduate of a foreign programme must also pass the Bar's exams in Israeli law (s. 37א).

### The internship: ss. 26–37

> (א) תקופת ההתמחות תהיה 12 חודשים.

*Gist:* The internship lasts 12 months. [s. 35](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_35)

The 12-month rule is the result of amendments in 2024–2025 (the consolidation lists amendments from תשפ״ד and תשפ״ה); from 2017 the period had been 18 months. A candidate may register as an intern on completing the degree, or with no more than two exams outstanding (s. 26(1)). The intern trains under a principal (מאמן): a judge, a registrar, or a lawyer with at least five years' seniority approved by the Bar (s. 29). The principal may employ the intern only in legal work (s. 33); the intern is the principal's employee for labour-law purposes (s. 41א). In the last six months an intern may appear for the principal's clients in the magistrates' court, with the principal present or with the court's permission (s. 37). The Commissioner for Supervision of Internships hears interns' complaints about principals and can bar a principal from training for up to 18 months (s. 41ב).

### The Bar exams: s. 38 and the exam regulations

> בתום תקופת ההתמחות על המועמד לחברות בלשכה להיבחן בבחינות הלשכה להסמכה לעריכת דין, שיהיו בין השאר בדין המהותי ובסדרי דין

*Gist:* At the end of the internship the candidate must pass the Bar's qualifying exams, which cover substantive and procedural law. [s. 38(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_38)

The written exam is set by a nine-member committee appointed by the Minister of Justice: three judges, four lawyers (two from the public legal service) and two law academics (s. 40(ב)). Under the exam regulations (regs 18–18ג) the written exam has three parts: multiple-choice questions on procedure and practical subjects, multiple-choice questions on substantive law (with statute excerpts), and a drafting task. The subject list includes **professional ethics** on the substantive side and **disciplinary procedure** on the procedural side, which is why this module ends with Bar-style questions. Sittings are held twice a year and announced at least 90 days ahead (reg. 15). The pass mark is set in reg. 18ג, but it has been lowered for particular sittings: see [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) for the figure and the most recent results.

### The disciplinary reach starts early: s. 41

> בתקופת התמחותו ועד להכרעה הסופית בדבר קבלתו כחבר הלשכה נתון המועמד למרותה של הלשכה ולשיפוטה המשמעתי, והלכות האתיקה המקצועית חלות, בשינויים המחוייבים, גם עליו

*Gist:* From the start of the internship until admission, the candidate is subject to the Bar's authority, its disciplinary jurisdiction and the ethics rules. [s. 41](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_41)

A disciplinary tribunal may disqualify a candidate from admission for up to three years or permanently, and cancel an internship or an exam where the offence concerned the internship or exam integrity.

### Refusing an intern or a member: ss. 27, 43, 44

> (א) הלשכה רשאית, לאחר שנתנה למועמד הזדמנות לטעון טענותיו לפניה, שלא לקבלו כחבר הלשכה, על אף כשירותו – (1) אם המועמד הורשע בעבירה פלילית שיש בה, בנסיבות העניין, משום קלון [...]; (2) אם נתגלו עובדות אחרות [...] והלשכה סבורה שלאור עובדות אלה אין המועמד ראוי לשמש עורך דין

*Gist:* Even a formally qualified candidate may be refused, after a hearing, for a conviction involving moral turpitude or for other facts showing they are unfit, and the Bar must give written reasons. [s. 44](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לשכת_עורכי_הדין#סעיף_44)

Section 27 gives the Bar the same power when an intern applies to register. Section 43 lets **any person** object to a candidate's admission after the names are published: the public is part of the gatekeeping. Once admitted, the member is entered in the register and may practise from the date of registration (s. 46).

### What the Bar is, and who controls it

The Bar is a statutory corporation (s. 4) with a National Council, district committees, disciplinary tribunals (ss. 14–18) and ethics committees (s. 18ב). The ethics committees, which receive complaints and act as prosecutors, include public representatives (jurists who are not Bar members): at least one, or two in a committee of 11 or more, and no more than a quarter of the members (s. 18ב(ג)–(ד)). The Bar's rules (כללים) need the Minister of Justice's approval, and some also the Knesset Constitution Committee's (ss. 55, 60, 109). The Attorney General may appear in any disciplinary case and file complaints (ss. 63, 79). This mix of self-regulation and state oversight is a recurring theme in the academic debate: supporters of self-regulation stress the profession's independence from the government it often litigates against; critics point to the Bar's interest in limiting competition and argue for more public control.

## Leading cases

### עע״ם 1758/10 לשכת עורכי הדין בישראל נ׳ שלום שגיא (15.8.2011)

A candidate passed the Bar exams in 2007, but a civil court had found that he took funds from companies that employed him, and a police investigation was pending. The Bar's Central Committee decided not to admit him until the criminal proceedings ended. The administrative court ordered his admission; the Supreme Court (Fogelman J., with Hayut and Melcer JJ.) reversed. It held that the Bar is an administrative authority whose admission decisions are reviewed for reasonableness and proportionality, that a refusal under s. 44(א)(2) may rest on "other facts" proved by administrative evidence such as a civil court's findings, and that an interim refusal pending the criminal process was proportionate given the purpose of protecting the public and the profession.

Why it matters: it shows both sides of the gate. The candidate's freedom of occupation is engaged, so the Bar must hear him, give reasons and act proportionately; but the court accepted that integrity concerns may delay admission even without a conviction. [Full text on Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פסק_דין_עע%22ם_1758/10_לשכת_עורכי_הדין_בישראל_נ%27_שלום_שגיא)

### עת״מ (ת״א) 43946-03-11 רביבו אלבז נ׳ לשכת עורכי הדין (5.10.2011)

A law graduate applied to register as an intern while complaints and a police investigation alleged she had presented herself as a lawyer. The Bar deferred her registration pending the investigation. The Tel Aviv administrative court (Ronen J.) dismissed her petition: s. 27 lets the Bar refuse registration of someone it considers unfit, the ethical standard demanded of an intern is the same as that demanded of a lawyer, and impersonating a lawyer goes to the heart of fitness. The decision was proportionate because it was temporary.

Why it matters: impersonating a lawyer is a criminal offence (s. 97), and for a would-be lawyer it can also block the road to the profession. For a client, it is a reminder that a business card or a website is not proof of a licence. [Text hosted by Reichman University](https://www.colman.ac.il/media/4vgf5xk1/43946-03-11_margalit_revivo_v_lishcat_orchei_hadin.doc)

## How it works in practice

### The path, step by step

| Stage | What happens | Legal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Degree | LL.B. at a recognised faculty or college (about 3.5–4 years) | s. 25 |
| Registration as intern | Application to the Bar; may be refused for unfitness | ss. 26, 27 |
| Internship | 12 months with an approved principal; may appear in the magistrates' court in the last six months | ss. 29, 35, 37 |
| Exams | Written exam: procedure MCQ, substantive MCQ, drafting task | s. 38; exam regulations |
| Publication and objections | Candidates' names published; anyone may object | s. 43 |
| Admission | Decision of the Bar; entry in the register; right to practise | ss. 42, 44, 46 |
| Continuing discipline | Ethics committees, disciplinary tribunals, appeals | ss. 61–80 |

### What a licence tells you, and what it does not

A licence tells you the person passed the gate and is subject to discipline, carries the Bar's ethical duties (loyalty, confidentiality, trust-account rules), and may lawfully do the reserved acts (lesson [35.2](lesson-02.md)). It does not tell you whether the lawyer knows your field, has time for your case, or charges fairly. That is lesson [35.5](lesson-05.md).

### Checking a lawyer

1. **Name and licence number.** Every lawyer must state their Bar registration number on the first document filed for a client, on affidavits they certify, and on documents they authenticate (Bar Advertising Rules, r. 2(ג)). Ask for it.
2. **The Bar's register.** The Bar runs an online search of its members. When this lesson was checked, the Bar's website redirected automated visitors to another domain, so the exact address is marked ⚠️ manual check: open the Bar's official site in your browser and look for the lawyer search (איתור עורך דין).
3. **Disciplinary rulings.** The Bar must make every disciplinary judgment available to the public with the lawyer's name, unless the tribunal ordered otherwise (s. 69ב), and suspensions and expulsions are published in Reshumot, in a Bar publication, on the Bar's website and in a newspaper (s. 69(ג)). Search the lawyer's name with "בית הדין המשמעתי" on the Bar's site and in the courts' public decisions (appeals to the Jerusalem District Court under s. 71 are court judgments).
4. **Interns and impostors.** An intern may not run your case on their own. A person who is not a lawyer and does reserved acts commits an offence (s. 96); one who pretends to be a lawyer, or a suspended lawyer who keeps practising, faces up to a year's imprisonment (s. 97).

## Worked example

Avital Mizrahi from Be'er Sheva received a WhatsApp message from "עו״ד דניאל לוי – מחלקת תביעות", offering to "release your blocked National Insurance pension" for 30% of the arrears. She is not sure he is a lawyer.

1. **Ask for the licence number.** She replies asking for his Bar registration number and office address. He answers that he is "a legal consultant working with lawyers" and that the number "is not needed".
2. **Check the register.** She searches the Bar's member search for "דניאל לוי" and finds several lawyers with that name, none with the phone number or the office he named. She calls the office of one of them, who knows nothing about her.
3. **Analysis.** Calling himself "עו״ד" without being a lawyer is impersonation (s. 97). If he is not a lawyer and represents her for pay before the National Insurance Institute's medical committee or the labour court, that is unauthorised practice (s. 20, lesson 35.2). Even a lawyer could not charge 30%: National Insurance fees are capped by statute (lesson [35.4](lesson-04.md)).
4. **Decision.** Avital declines, keeps the messages, and handles the claim herself with the free help described in lesson [17.9](../module-17/lesson-09.md). She may report the impersonation to the police and to the Bar's ethics committee (lesson [35.6](lesson-06.md)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The name of a lawyer you have used, were referred to, or saw advertised (or a lawyer named in any public judgment, for practice).
- A browser and about 40 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write the path to the Bar as a timeline for a hypothetical friend who starts an LL.B. this year: degree, intern registration, internship, exams, admission. Mark the section of the Bar Association Law for each step.
2. Find your lawyer in the Bar's member search (⚠️ manual check of the address). Record: full name, registration number, district, and whether the listing shows the person as an active member.
3. Search for disciplinary rulings: the lawyer's name with "בית הדין המשמעתי של לשכת עורכי הדין" on the Bar's website and in a general search engine. Record any ruling, its date and the penalty.
4. Check one document the lawyer signed for you (a letter, a statement of claim, an affidavit confirmation): does it show the registration number where the Advertising Rules require it?
5. Write three lines: what the licence check tells you, what it does not, and what you would ask the lawyer at a first meeting (bring this to lesson 35.5).

### Template

None. A one-page table (name, number, district, status, rulings found, date checked) is enough. You will use it again in the [get-a-lawyer checklist](../../templates/get-a-lawyer-checklist.md) and in lesson 35.5.

### How you know it worked

- You found the lawyer's registration number in the register or know why you could not.
- You recorded whether any public disciplinary ruling exists and its date.
- Your timeline cites ss. 24, 26, 35, 38, 43 and 46.

### Self-check

- [ ] I can state the three conditions of s. 24 and the length of the internship.
- [ ] I know who may object to a candidate's admission and on what grounds the Bar may refuse.
- [ ] I can explain why a court reviews an admission refusal for proportionality.
- [ ] I checked a real lawyer's status and recorded the date I checked.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Checking a lawyer's status, reading published disciplinary rulings and objecting to a candidate's admission under s. 43 are open to everyone. Becoming a lawyer is not something this course can give you: it requires recognised education, an internship and the exams (lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)).

### Where and how

- **Lawyer search and disciplinary rulings:** the Bar's official website (⚠️ manual check: open it in your browser; do not follow redirects to unfamiliar domains).
- **Objection to admission (s. 43):** in writing to the Bar within the period set in its rules after the names are published; state facts, not opinions, and attach evidence.
- **Intern complaints about a principal:** the Commissioner for Supervision of Internships (s. 41ב); complaints about an intern's principal who is a judge go to the Judicial Ombudsman (s. 41ב(ג)).
- **Impersonation:** a police complaint (lesson [05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md)) and a report to the district ethics committee.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are the candidate and the Bar refuses to register or admit you: the decision is reviewable in the administrative affairs court, and deadlines are short.
- Someone who presented himself as a lawyer has already filed documents or taken money in your case: get a real lawyer to check what was filed and protect your deadlines.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hiring "a legal consultant" without checking the licence | No ethical duties, no trust account, possibly invalid filings; recovering fees is hard | Stop payments; get a licensed lawyer to review the file; report | Ask for the registration number and check the register first |
| Assuming an intern can run your case | The intern may only appear with the principal or the court's leave in the last six months (s. 37) | Insist that the principal is responsible and present | Ask who will actually handle the file |
| Treating a licence as proof of competence in your field | Poor advice in a specialised area | Get a second opinion | Ask about experience in your exact type of case (lesson 35.5) |
| Missing the objection window under s. 43 | The objection is not considered as such | Write to the ethics committee instead | Watch the Bar's publication and act promptly |

## Review

- A lawyer needs recognised legal education, a 12-month internship and the Bar exams (ss. 24, 35, 38); the Bar may still refuse registration or admission for unfitness after a hearing (ss. 27, 44).
- The Bar is a statutory regulator whose admission decisions are administrative and reviewed for reasonableness and proportionality (Sagi).
- Candidates are under the Bar's disciplinary jurisdiction from the start of the internship (s. 41); anyone may object to admission (s. 43).
- Disciplinary judgments are public with the lawyer's name unless ordered otherwise (s. 69ב): check before you hire.
- Ethics and disciplinary procedure are Bar-exam subjects; the rest of this module covers them.
