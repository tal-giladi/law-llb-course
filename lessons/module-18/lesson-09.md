# 18.9 · Practice lab: the request to settle a dispute and the assistance-unit meetings

In this lab you prepare, as a dry run, everything a spouse or parent does in the first two months of a family dispute in the Family Court: the request to settle the dispute, its service, the timeline, the preparation for the information, acquaintance and coordination (mahut) meetings, and any urgent or interim request needed while claims are frozen. Lesson [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md) explained the law and the race of jurisdiction, and lesson [10.6](../module-10/lesson-06.md) did the same filing for the rabbinical court; here the focus is the Family Court and the meetings themselves.

## Why it matters

Since 17 July 2016 no claim in a family dispute between spouses, or between parents and children, can be filed in any court (civil or religious) before a request to settle the dispute (s. 3(א)). The meetings that follow are often the best chance a family has to agree on the children and on money without litigation; they are also the point where a party who arrives unprepared signs something they regret, or misses the short filing window that decides the forum.

## The law

### What the request is, and what it is not

> (א) המבקש להגיש לערכאה שיפוטית תובענה בעניין של סכסוך משפחתי, יגיש תחילה לערכאה השיפוטית בקשה ליישוב סכסוך; הבקשה לא תכלול טענות או עובדות בקשר לסכסוך או בקשר לסמכות השיפוט של הערכאה השיפוטית.

*Gist:* Before filing a claim in a family dispute you file a request to settle it, with no allegations and no facts about the dispute or jurisdiction. [Dispute Resolution Law, s. 3(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה#סעיף_3)

It covers disputes between spouses, between parents, or between parents and children about: marriage and divorce (except divorce filed by agreement); property between spouses (not inheritance); maintenance and housing of a spouse or child; any matter concerning a child under the Capacity Law (except Hague abduction claims and an exit ban sought with them); parenthood (except by agreement); and maintenance or disputes of young adults up to 21 (s. 2). It does not apply where a party lives abroad, where the State is a party (save contested parentage), or to proceedings under the Prevention of Family Violence Law or the Threatening Harassment Law (s. 4א).

### Filing and service: regs 2–3

The request is filed with the court secretariat by the applicant or their representative on **Form 1**, under the ordinary local-venue rules (reg. 2). The secretariat refuses a claim, including an interim request, not preceded by a request, except urgent requests listed in reg. 12(א)(1), (2), (3ב), (4) and an exit ban for a minor (reg. 2א(א)); and refuses a second request while the first stay is running (reg. 2א(ב)). The secretariat sends the applicant an invitation to the first meeting (Form 2) and an explanation sheet, and serves the request, the invitation and the explanation on the other party by registered mail, secure email, personal delivery, or through the applicant's lawyer (reg. 3).

### The meetings: s. 3(ב)–(ד), regs 4–8

- Up to four meetings, all within 45 days of the request (extendable once by 15 days); the parties attend **in person**, and the unit may meet each separately (s. 3(ב)(1)–(3)).
- An invitation has the force of a court summons; non-attendance is reported to the court, which may impose costs, extend the stay or strike out the absent party's request or claim (reg. 4).
- The first meeting is for acquaintance and assessing the dispute; the parties attend alone, and no attempt is made to settle the legal dispute, except interim arrangements if both wish and after they could consult their lawyers (reg. 5(ב)). Lawyers may join later meetings devoted to settling the dispute (reg. 5(ג)).
- **Nothing is signed** at the meetings, and no legal commitment is undertaken, including interim arrangements, until each party has had an opportunity to consult a lawyer or any other person (reg. 5(ה)).
- The meetings cover information about procedures and consequences, routes to agreement (counselling, mediation, collaborative divorce, therapy), the children's interests and wishes, and interim arrangements for maintenance, residence and parenting time (s. 3(ג)).
- What is said is privileged and inadmissible (Family Court Law s. 5א; reg. 6), but the unit reports attendance and may recommend protective steps for a child at risk (s. 3(ח1)).
- Within five days of the last meeting each party tells the unit and the other party, on **Form 3**, whether they will continue an alternative process and for how long (reg. 8).

### During the stay

> (1) על אף האמור בסעיף קטן (ה), צד לבקשה ליישוב סכסוך רשאי להגיש, בכל עת, לערכאה שיפוטית המוסמכת לכך לפי דין, בקשה לסעד דחוף או זמני [...], לסעד זמני לשמירת המצב הקיים או לעיכוב יציאה מן הארץ.

*Gist:* During the stay a party may still ask any competent court for urgent or interim relief, to preserve the status quo, or for an exit ban; such a request is short and contains only the essential facts, and deciding it does not decide jurisdiction. [s. 3(ז)–(ח)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה#סעיף_3)

| Need | Form and regulation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the status quo, attachment, exit ban | Form 5, reg. 10 | Affidavit and documents; answer within 10 days (reg. 11) |
| Urgent medical decision for a child; passport or unplanned trip within 30 days; school registration near term start; woman in a shelter keeping her children; urgent divorce when a foreign-resident spouse is in Israel | Form 6, reg. 12(א) | Interim maintenance and contact during the stay go only through reg. 12 (reg. 12(ה)) |
| Shorten the stay (e.g. indictment for violence or sexual offences against the spouse or child, shelter, unknown address, insolvency) or extend it (bad-faith non-attendance, illness, a party abroad) | Form 7, reg. 16 | The unit gives its view; a party who did not attend needs special leave |

### After the stay: s. 4, reg. 9

When the periods end with no agreement, the party who filed the request may, within 15 days, file a claim in any competent forum; if they do not, or file only some issues, the other party may file the rest anywhere competent (s. 4). Every claim is filed with an accompanying notice on **Form 4** giving the request's court, number and date and whether the claimant attended the meetings, with any interim decisions attached (reg. 9). A maintenance claim filed after the stay is treated as filed on the date of the request (reg. 15(ב)). If the parties reach an agreement, a court may give it the force of a judgment (s. 3(ט)).

## Leading cases

### Key text: the 2016 regulations and their forms

The procedure is almost entirely regulatory, and the forms are mandatory: the secretariat will not accept an interim request not on Form 5 (reg. 10(א)), and courts may strike out requests that are not short or that include unnecessary facts or offensive language, with costs (regs 11(ב), 13(ב), 16(ד)). Read regs 2–16 in full before filing: [Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_להסדר_התדיינויות_בסכסוכי_משפחה). The forms themselves are on the Judiciary's forms site (⚠️ check the current version on the day).

## How it works in practice

- **Fee:** a small fee for the request in the Family Court (Fees Regulations item 13) and in the rabbinical court; exemptions and waivers as usual. See [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).
- **Where:** the Family Court under the ordinary venue rules (reg. 6 of the Family Court Procedure Regulations), or the competent religious court. The forum of the request does not bind the forum of the claims (lesson 10.3).
- **Counting days:** certain festival days are not counted (s. 6א: for Family and rabbinical courts, Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, 14–22 Tishrei and 14–21 Nisan; for Sharia courts, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha periods); but they *are* counted in the 15-day filing window unless its last day falls on them (s. 6א(ג)).
- **What the unit is:** social workers employed by the Welfare Ministry, with a family lawyer who may join (s. 3(ב)(4)). It does not represent anyone and does not decide.

## Worked example

Lior and Shani Avraham, secular Jews from Netanya, separated in September 2026. They have twins aged 7. Shani wants to keep the apartment and have the twins live mainly with her; Lior wants equal time and to sell the apartment. Lior also worries Shani will take the twins to her parents in Canada for Hanukkah.

1. **Request.** Lior files a request to settle in the Netanya Family Court on Form 1 on 1 November 2026, with the fee. It contains names, ID numbers, addresses, the twins' details and a list of the *subjects* (property, children, maintenance), with no facts.
2. **Exit ban.** Lior also files Form 5 for an exit ban for the twins, attaching an affidavit stating only why he fears removal (tickets, messages). The secretariat accepts it (reg. 2א(א)).
3. **Service.** The court serves Shani by registered mail with the Form 2 invitation and explanation sheet.
4. **First meeting.** Both attend alone. They agree in principle on an interim schedule (alternate weekends plus two afternoons for Lior), but the social worker reminds them not to sign before consulting a lawyer (reg. 5(ה)). They sign it after consultation at the second meeting.
5. **Later meetings.** The unit recommends mediation with a family mediator from the official list (reg. 7(א1)); Shani agrees, Lior does not, and each says so on Form 3 within five days.
6. **Window.** At the end of the periods Lior, as the first filer, has 15 days to file claims in any competent forum, each with Form 4. He files custody and property claims in the Family Court; Shani then files child maintenance in the same court (s. 4), and her maintenance claim is treated as filed on 1 November (reg. 15(ב)).
7. **Shani's side.** Had Shani filed the request first, the choice of forum and the first filing window would have been hers.

## Do it yourself

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### You need

- Your own situation (only with the consent of the other people involved, and without identifying children in shared work), or the Avraham case.
- The [request-to-settle template](../../templates/request-to-settle-family-dispute.md) (draft) and, for comparison, part A of the [rabbinical-court filing file](../../templates/rabbinical-court-filing-file.md).
- The Dispute Resolution Law and the 2016 Regulations (links above); a calendar with Hebrew dates (for s. 6א).
- 150 minutes.

### Steps

**Part 1: the request (30 minutes)**

1. Decide the forum where you would file the request and write one sentence explaining why (venue under reg. 6; any reason to prefer the Family Court or a religious court, lesson 18.1).
2. Fill in the template: parties, children, subjects to be settled (tick list), and nothing else. Re-read it and delete any sentence that states a fact about the dispute.
3. Note the fee or the exemption you would claim, and how service will be made (reg. 3).

**Part 2: the timeline (30 minutes)**

4. Take a filing date of today. Mark: the 45-day period (and 60 if extended); the five-day notice after the last meeting; the further periods in s. 3(ה); the 15-day window under s. 4 for the first filer. Remove festival days under s. 6א, then check the rule for the 15-day window (s. 6א(ג)).
5. Mark which claims you would file in the window, in which forum, and what you would attach (Form 4, interim decisions).

**Part 3: the meetings (45 minutes)**

6. Write a one-page preparation sheet for the first meeting: your goals for the children; what interim arrangement you could live with for two months; your monthly budget and the children's needs (for interim maintenance discussions); questions about mediation and collaborative divorce.
7. Write the three things you will not agree to without legal advice, and the sentence you will say if pressed to sign (reg. 5(ה)).
8. Draft your Form 3 notice for both outcomes: continuing mediation for 60 days, or not continuing.

**Part 4: a request during the stay (45 minutes)**

9. Decide whether your case needs a Form 5, 6 or 7 request. If yes, draft it: one page, facts needed for the decision only, a list of documents, a draft affidavit. If no, draft Lior's exit-ban request from the worked example.
10. Check it against regs 10–13 and 16: is it on the right form, short, without offensive language, with documents?

### How you know it worked

- Your request contains no facts or arguments; a stranger reading it learns only who the parties are and which subjects they need to settle.
- Your timeline has real dates for every period and the 15-day window.
- Your meeting sheet distinguishes what you can agree today from what needs advice.
- Your stay-period request fits a listed category and is on the correct form.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. The request and the meetings are designed for parties acting in person; the parties attend the meetings themselves even when they have lawyers (s. 3(ב)(2)). The unit's lawyer and social workers give information and help negotiate but do not represent either side. In the rabbinical and Sharia courts, licensed pleaders may also act (reg. 1 of the 2016 Regulations). See [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The secretariat of the Family Court (or religious court), by hand or through the court's online services where available for unrepresented parties (check on the day).
- The assistance unit beside that court (Welfare Ministry); the unit also informs you about Legal Aid (reg. 5(ה)).
- Fees, exemptions and periods: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other side is represented and you expect a filing in a religious court in the window.
- There is violence or a threat: protection orders are outside this process (lesson 18.7) and the stay can be shortened (reg. 16(א)(6), (7)).
- A child may be taken abroad, or the other party lives abroad (the law does not apply, s. 4א(1)).
- You are asked at the meetings to agree on property, pensions or a get.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Writing the story of the marriage in the request | Request struck or ordered amended; tone set badly | Refile in the bare form | Use the template; facts go only in later claims |
| Missing a mahut meeting | Reported to the court; costs; stay extended or your request struck (reg. 4) | Ask the unit for a new date at once, with reasons | Diarise every meeting; attend in person |
| Signing an interim agreement at the first meeting without advice | A commitment you did not understand | Ask the court not to give it effect; renegotiate | Rely on reg. 5(ה): take time to consult |
| Filing a full maintenance claim during the stay | Refused by the secretariat | Use the urgent route (reg. 12(ה)) | Know what can and cannot be filed during the stay |
| Letting the 15-day window pass | The other spouse chooses the forum for the open issues | File promptly for remaining issues | Count the window on the day you file the request |

## Review

- Every family dispute between spouses or parents and children starts with a bare request to settle (Form 1); claims in any forum are frozen during the stay (s. 3).
- Up to four mahut meetings within 45 days (60 if extended); attend in person; the first meeting is with the parties alone; sign nothing without an opportunity to consult (reg. 5).
- Urgent, interim, exit-ban and shortening requests remain available in short, form-based applications (s. 3(ז); regs 10–16).
- After the periods, the first filer has 15 days to file anywhere competent, with Form 4; the other party then files the rest (s. 4; reg. 9).
- Protection-order proceedings and cases with a party abroad are outside this law (s. 4א).
