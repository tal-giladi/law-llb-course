# 18.1 · The structure of Israeli family law: religious and civil law, dual jurisdiction

By the end of this lesson you will be able to take any family dispute, whether the family is Jewish, Muslim, Christian, Druze, of mixed communities or of no religion, and say which law governs each question (religious personal law or a civil statute), which court may hear it, and what the Family Court's special procedure means for a party who acts without a lawyer.

## Why it matters

Israeli family law is a layered system. At the bottom lies the Ottoman and Mandate arrangement that left marriage and divorce to the religious communities and their courts (lessons [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md) and [10.4](../module-10/lesson-04.md)). On top of it the Knesset has built a body of civil family law: property between spouses, maintenance for those without a personal law, guardianship and custody, adoption, protection from violence. In 1995 it created one specialised civil court, the Family Affairs Court (בית המשפט לענייני משפחה), to hear almost all of it.

The result is a system with two questions for every issue: *which law applies?* and *which court decides?* The answers are not always the same. A rabbinical court may decide property under a civil statute; a Family Court may decide a Jewish wife's maintenance under Jewish law. Getting the two questions confused is the most common error of people who represent themselves.

People hold sharply different views about this structure. One view sees the religious layer as the guarantee of shared personal status within each community and of communal autonomy, which the State of Israel inherited and kept for all communities, not only Jews. Another view sees it as subjecting citizens to religious law they do not hold and as a source of gender inequality, with the civil layer as a partial corrective. Each side relies on legal texts: the first on the 1953 Rabbinical Courts Jurisdiction Law and art. 51–54 of the Order in Council; the second on the Women's Equal Rights Law and Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty. This module teaches the law as it stands in October 2026 and presents each position where the law is contested.

## The law

### What a "family matter" is: s. 1

> בחוק זה, ”עניני משפחה“ – אחת מאלה: (1) תובענה בעניני המעמד האישי [...]; (2) תובענה אזרחית בין אדם או עזבונו לבין בן משפחתו, או עזבונו, שעילתה סכסוך בתוך המשפחה, יהא נושאה או שוויה אשר יהא;

*Gist:* Family matters are personal-status claims and any civil claim between family members that arises from a dispute inside the family, whatever its subject or value. [Family Court Law, s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_1)

The definition of "family member" in s. 1(2) includes a spouse "לרבות הידועה בציבור כאישתו", a former spouse (where the claim arises from the former relationship), children and step-children, parents and parents-in-law, grandchildren, grandparents and siblings. Paragraphs (3)–(8) add maintenance, paternity and maternity, child abduction, and claims under a list of statutes: Marriage Age, Names, Capacity and Guardianship, Succession, Spouses' Property Relations, Adoption, Prevention of Family Violence, and Embryo Carrying Agreements.

### One court for all family matters: s. 3, with two exceptions

> (א) עניני משפחה לפי חוק זה יידונו בבית המשפט לעניני משפחה.

*Gist:* Family matters are heard by the Family Court. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_3)

Section 3(ג) adds that the Family Court may approve an agreement on any matter within its jurisdiction, even with no claim pending, and give it the force of a judgment. Section 3(ב1) gives the Family Court jurisdiction in the family matters of Muslims and Christians (art. 52 and 54 of the Order in Council) "למעט עניני נישואין וגירושין" (lesson 10.4). Section 3(ד) lets a minor sue alone, or through a "ידיד קרוב", where their rights may be seriously harmed.

The two exceptions that keep other courts in the picture:

1. **Religious courts with exclusive or parallel jurisdiction.** Section 25 preserves the religious courts' powers; where jurisdiction is parallel, the Family Court may hear a matter "כל עוד אין בית הדין הדתי דן בו" (s. 25(ב); lesson 10.3).
2. **Protection orders.** Under s. 4(ג), an order under the Prevention of Family Violence Law may also be given by any magistrates' court, not only a Family Court (lesson 18.7).

### Special procedure: ss. 5, 5א, 8

> (א) בכל ענין של דיני ראיות וסדרי דין, שאין עליו הוראה אחרת, לפי חוק זה, ינהג בית המשפט בדרך הנראית לו הטובה ביותר לעשיית משפט צדק;

*Gist:* Where no specific rule applies, the Family Court may follow the evidence and procedure it considers best for doing justice. [s. 8(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_8)

Section 8(ב) lets the court hear a child in the absence of others or stop a child's testimony to prevent harm; s. 8(ג) bars a party's own expert where the court has appointed one, unless the court permits. Section 5 sets up the assistance unit (diagnosis, counselling, treatment and mediation); s. 5א makes what is said there privileged and inadmissible, with exceptions for suspected offences against a child or a vulnerable person and for grounds for a protection order (s. 5א(ה)).

The 2020 procedure regulations state the court's mission:

> (א) בית המשפט לענייני משפחה אחראי על ניהול ההליך השיפוטי גם לשם צמצום ההתדיינות השיפוטית בין בעלי דין שהם בני משפחה במטרה להגיע לסיום הסכסוך ביניהם וכן ליישובו בדרכי שלום אם ניתן [...]

*Gist:* The Family Court manages the case so as to reduce litigation between family members and, where possible, end the dispute peacefully. [Family Court Procedure Regulations 2020, reg. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בית_המשפט_לענייני_משפחה_(סדרי_דין)#תקנה_2)

Regulation 2(ב) requires the court, before any decision about a child, to weigh the child's rights, needs and interests, and the child's wishes according to age and maturity. Regulation 49 gives the child a right to be heard in matters concerning them.

### Which law decides: personal law or civil statute

Maintenance shows the pattern. For a spouse:

> (א) אדם חייב במזונות בן־זוגו לפי הוראות הדין האישי החל עליו, והוראות חוק זה לא יחולו על מזונות אלה.

*Gist:* A person owes spousal maintenance under their own personal (religious) law; the Maintenance Law applies only to those with no personal law or of a community not listed. [Maintenance Law, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לתיקון_דיני_המשפחה_(מזונות)#סעיף_2)

Section 3 says the same about children's maintenance, and s. 19 makes "בית משפט" in the law include a religious court wherever it has jurisdiction. So the *Family Court* applies *Jewish law* to a Jewish father's child-maintenance duty, *Sharia* to a Muslim husband's duty to his wife, and the civil rules of the Maintenance Law to a couple of no recognised religion.

Other questions are governed by civil statutes that bind religious courts too:

- **Equality:** Women's Equal Rights Law s. 7(ב): every religious court applies it unless all adult parties freely agree before the court to their community's law. [s. 7](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_שיווי_זכויות_האשה#סעיף_7)
- **Property:** Spouses (Property Relations) Law s. 13(ב), with the same consent exception (lesson 18.4). [s. 13](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_יחסי_ממון_בין_בני_זוג#סעיף_13)
- **Children:** the Capacity and Guardianship Law (lesson 18.6), the Prevention of Family Violence Law (18.7), the Adoption of Children Law (18.8).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 1000/92 בבלי נ׳ בית הדין הרבני הגדול, פ״ד מח(2) 221 (1994)

A rabbinical court divided a couple's property under its own rules, refusing the wife half of the property accumulated during the marriage. The High Court of Justice set aside the decision: a rabbinical court deciding property is bound by the civil law that the Supreme Court has developed, including the presumption of community of property between spouses (הלכת השיתוף) and the principle of equality between the sexes in the Women's Equal Rights Law.

Why it matters: it is the classic statement that religious jurisdiction does not mean religious *substantive* law in civil matters. Supporters see it as protecting equality; critics, including many dayanim, argue that it interferes with the courts' religious character, and in later years some rabbinical-court decisions questioned how far it reaches. Free source: the full text was not reached from the research environment; the citation and holding are as summarised in [Hebrew Wikipedia: הלכת השיתוף](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/הלכת_השיתוף) (⚠️ secondary).

### בג״ץ 4602/13 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי חיפה (18.11.2018)

The Great Rabbinical Court denied a wife's claim to a share in a house registered in the husband's name; she argued it had applied religious law (giving weight to alleged infidelity) to a civil property question. The HCJ (2–1) declined to intervene: the decision rested on a factual finding that a specific partnership had not been proved, and the HCJ intervenes in religious courts only for lack of jurisdiction, breach of natural justice, or departure from a statute that binds them.

Why it matters: read with Bavli, it shows both sides of the structure. Civil law binds the religious court, but review is narrow, so the choice of forum still matters in practice. [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/3)

### רע״א 3927/15 פלונית נ׳ פלוני (26.11.2015)

The Supreme Court held that a dispute between former cohabitants arising from their relationship belongs to the Family Court, because cohabitants are "family members" within s. 1 of the Family Court Law. Why it matters: it confirms that the Family Court's jurisdiction follows the family relationship, not the marriage certificate (lesson 18.3). Citation and holding as reported in [Hebrew Wikipedia: ידוע בציבור](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/ידוע_בציבור) (⚠️ secondary; full text on the Judiciary's site not reached).

## How it works in practice

### The map

| Issue | Law that decides it | Forum or forums |
|---|---|---|
| Marriage and divorce | Personal (religious) law; the 1969 Law for mixed or no-religion couples | The community's religious court; Family Court only in the 1969 cases or for couples of no recognised community (lesson 10.4) |
| Spousal maintenance | Personal law (Maintenance Law s. 2); civil law if none | Family Court; religious court where it has jurisdiction (Jewish wife: s. 4 of the 1953 Law) |
| Child maintenance | Personal law (s. 3), as interpreted in בע״מ 919/15 (lesson 18.5) | Family Court; rabbinical court if joined to divorce (Amendment 6 temporary order, lesson 10.3) |
| Property between spouses | Property Relations Law; for marriages before 1974, the presumption of community | Family Court; religious court if properly joined, applying civil law |
| Custody, contact, guardianship | Capacity and Guardianship Law: best interest of the child | Family Court; religious court if properly joined |
| Protection orders | Prevention of Family Violence Law | Any magistrates' court, Family Court, or a religious court for its parties |
| Adoption, surrogacy, parentage | Adoption Law; Embryo Carrying Agreements Law; case law | Family Court |
| Inheritance between family members | Succession Law | Family Court (or Registrar of Inheritance for uncontested orders) |

### What is special about the Family Court

- **Closed doors.** Family hearings are held in private and publication of identifying details is restricted; parties appear as "פלוני" and "פלונית" in published rulings.
- **Forms and particulars.** A claim between spouses is filed with a statement of particulars (Form 3); a maintenance claim with Form 4, including the claimant parent's income in the 12 months before filing and the estimated value of their assets (reg. 12(ב)(1)). Failing to state a fact within your knowledge can be treated as admitting the other side's version (reg. 12(ב)(2)).
- **Defence period.** 30 days from service (reg. 13(א)).
- **Venue.** The first claim between spouses or parents and children goes to the court of their last joint residence if one of them still lives there; special rules apply to a child's matters and to a woman in a shelter or a person protected by a protection order (reg. 6).
- **The assistance unit** can be ordered to meet the parties, report urgently, or mediate (s. 5(ג)); a party who refuses must notify the court in writing within 15 days.
- **Consolidation.** All family claims between the same parties are heard by one Family Court judge (s. 6(ד)): "one family, one judge".
- **Before any claim:** the request to settle the dispute and the mahut meetings (lessons 10.3 and 18.9).

### Costs

Family Court fees are set by the Family Court (Fees) Regulations 1995 and updated twice a year by the CPI. A protection-order application is exempt, as are interim requests in a maintenance claim (reg. 7). A fee waiver may be requested on an affidavit of means (reg. 9). Current figures are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

## Worked example

Rania and Fadi Haddad, Greek Catholic Christians from Haifa, married in church 14 years ago and have three children. Fadi has left the home. Rania wants maintenance for herself and the children, sole physical custody, and half of the apartment registered in Fadi's name. Fadi wants shared parenting and to keep the apartment.

1. **Classify.** Every issue is a "family matter" (s. 1): personal status, maintenance, custody under the Capacity Law, property under the Property Relations Law.
2. **Marriage and divorce.** For members of a recognised Christian community these stay with the community's ecclesiastical court (art. 54 of the Order in Council; Family Court Law s. 3(ב1) excludes "נישואין וגירושין"). For Greek Catholics this usually means separation or annulment rather than divorce; the details are for the church court (lesson 10.4).
3. **Maintenance, custody, property.** Under s. 3(ב1) the Family Court may hear all of them. Maintenance is decided under the couple's personal law (Maintenance Law ss. 2–3, 19), custody under the best interest of the child (lesson 18.6), property under the Property Relations Law (lesson 18.4).
4. **First step.** Whoever moves first files a request to settle the dispute (lessons 10.3, 18.9); the Family Dispute Resolution Law applies to Christian ecclesiastical courts as well as to the Family Court (s. 2).
5. **Forum choice.** Rania may prefer the Family Court for its assistance unit and its familiarity with civil property law; Fadi may prefer the ecclesiastical court for issues it can hear. The race of jurisdiction for Christians works differently from the Jewish case (lesson 10.4). Neither preference is "right"; each is a legal and practical judgment.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Any individual may appear in person in the Family Court and in the religious courts (Civil Procedure Regulations reg. 170 as applied; for the religious courts the Bar Association Law monopoly does not apply, s. 21; lesson 10.6). No one may represent you for pay unless they are a lawyer (or, in the rabbinical and Sharia courts, a licensed pleader). See [Can I act without a lawyer?](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

### Where and how

- The Family Court sits in designated magistrates' courts; filing for unrepresented parties is through the court secretariat or the Judiciary's online services (check the current options on the day).
- Free help: the assistance unit at the court (information, mediation; not representation); Legal Aid for eligible applicants in family matters; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).
- Fees and waivers: [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The other spouse has a lawyer and has already filed, or you suspect a filing in a religious court.
- There is a business, real estate abroad, a pension in payment, or significant debts.
- One spouse lives abroad, or a child may be taken abroad.
- There is violence: go first to lesson 18.7 and the emergency numbers there.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating "which court" and "which law" as the same question | You argue civil-law rules on spousal maintenance, or religious rules on property | Re-check the governing statute for each issue | Fill both columns of the map for every issue |
| Assuming Christians or Muslims have no Family Court option | You lose a forum you could have chosen | Check s. 3(ב1) | Read lesson 10.4 before choosing |
| Filing a claim before the request to settle | The claim is refused or stayed | File the request first | Start every family dispute with lesson 18.9 |
| Leaving out income or assets in the particulars form | Deemed admission of the other side's figures (reg. 12) | Ask leave to amend promptly | Fill Form 3/4 fully, with documents |
| Consenting before a religious court to litigate property "according to religious law" without understanding it | You waive the protection of the Property Relations Law (s. 13(ב)) | Ask the court to record that you do not consent | Never sign such consent without advice |

## Review

- Family matters (s. 1) include personal status and any civil claim between family members arising from a family dispute; the Family Court hears them (s. 3), subject to religious courts' powers (s. 25) and the magistrates' power to give protection orders (s. 4(ג)).
- Spousal and child maintenance follow the parties' personal law (Maintenance Law ss. 2–3); equality, property, children's welfare, violence and adoption follow civil statutes that bind every court.
- The Family Court has flexible procedure (s. 8), an assistance unit with privileged sessions (ss. 5, 5א), mandatory particulars forms, and a duty to weigh the child's interests and hear the child.
- The structure itself is contested: communal autonomy and shared status on one side, individual choice and equality on the other; the law described here is the law as of October 2026.
