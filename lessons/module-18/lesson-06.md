# 18.6 · Parental responsibility, custody and the best interest of the child

By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain who has legal authority over a child, how decisions are taken when parents disagree, how courts decide residence and contact after separation, and how to write a parenting plan that both parents can sign and a court can approve.

## Why it matters

When parents separate, the questions that matter most are about the children: where they sleep, who decides on school and health, how much time each parent has. Israeli law answers them through the Capacity and Guardianship Law 1962, a civil statute that applies to every family whatever its religion, and through the overriding principle of the child's best interest (טובת הילד). Religious courts that hear custody joined to a divorce must also act for the child's good.

The field has moved from "custody" (משמורת) to "parental responsibility" in the vocabulary of courts and committees, though the statute still speaks of guardianship and holding the child. Its most contested rule is the *tender-years presumption*: children up to age 6 stay with their mother unless there are special reasons (s. 25). The Schnitt Committee (2005–2011) recommended abolishing it by majority; the minority, and many women's organisations, argued that it protects young children and mothers who are usually the primary carers; fathers' organisations argue that it entrenches inequality and turns fathers into visitors. A government bill based on the committee's work was not completed. As of October 2026 s. 25 is in force.

## The law

### Parents as guardians: ss. 14–17

> ההורים הם האפוטרופסים הטבעיים של ילדיהם הקַטינים.

*Gist:* Both parents are the natural guardians of their minor children. [s. 14](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הכשרות_המשפטית_והאפוטרופסות#סעיף_14)

> אפוטרופסות ההורים כוללת את החובה והזכות לדאוג לצרכי הקטין, לרבות חינוכו, לימודיו, הכשרתו לעבודה ולמשלח־יד ועבודתו, וכן שמירת נכסיו, ניהולם ופיתוחם; וצמודה לה הרשות להחזיק בקטין ולקבוע את מקום מגוריו, והסמכות לייצגו.

*Gist:* Guardianship is both a duty and a right to care for the child's needs, education and property, with the authority to hold the child, decide where they live and represent them. [s. 15](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הכשרות_המשפטית_והאפוטרופסות#סעיף_15)

Parents must act "לטובת הקטין כדרך שהורים מסורים היו נוהגים בנסיבות הענין" (s. 17).

### Joint decisions and disputes: ss. 18–19

Both parents must act by agreement in every guardianship matter; consent may be given in advance or after, expressly or by implication, and is presumed until the contrary is shown; in a matter that cannot wait, either parent may act alone (s. 18(א)). Separation does not end the other parent's guardianship: schools, clinics and other listed bodies must send information to both parents on request (s. 18(ב)–(ד)). Where parents disagree about the child's *property*, either may apply to the court; on other matters they apply *together*, and the court, if it cannot bring them to agree, decides or appoints someone to decide (s. 19). In practice, after separation the Family Court decides disputes on school, health and moving home on one parent's application within custody proceedings.

### After separation: ss. 24–25

> היו הורי הקטין חיים בנפרד [...] רשאים הם להסכים ביניהם על מי מהם תהיה האפוטרופסות לקטין, כולה או מקצתה, מי מהם יחזיק בקטין, ומה יהיו זכויות ההורה שלא יחזיק בקטין לבוא עמו במגע; הסכם כזה טעון אישור בית המשפט, והוא יאשרו לאחר שנוכח כי ההסכם הוא לטובת הקטין [...]

*Gist:* Separated parents, married or not, may agree on guardianship, residence and contact; the agreement needs court approval, given only if it is in the child's best interest, and then has the force of a court decision. [s. 24](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הכשרות_המשפטית_והאפוטרופסות#סעיף_24)

> [...] רשאי בית המשפט לקבוע את הענינים האמורים בסעיף 24 כפי שייראה לו לטובת הקטין, ובלבד שילדים עד גיל 6 יהיו אצל אמם אם אין סיבות מיוחדות להורות אחרת.

*Gist:* Without an agreement, the court decides by the child's best interest, provided that children up to age 6 stay with their mother unless there are special reasons. [s. 25](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הכשרות_המשפטית_והאפוטרופסות#סעיף_25)

Section 28 allows the court to appoint an additional guardian where a parent has died, is unable to fulfil their duties or has failed to do so without reasonable cause; s. 68 lets the court take any temporary or permanent measure to protect a minor, including appointing a guardian ad litem.

### Hearing the child

> בית משפט הדן בתובענה [...] הנוגעת לילד, ייתן לילד הזדמנות להביע את רגשותיו, את דעותיו ואת רצונותיו בעניין הנדון לפניו [...] וייתן להם משקל ראוי בהחלטתו, לפי גילו ולמידת בגרותו [...]

*Gist:* In proceedings concerning a child, the court gives the child an opportunity to express feelings, views and wishes, and gives them due weight according to age and maturity, unless it decides otherwise for special reasons. [Family Court Procedure Regulations, reg. 49](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/תקנות_בית_המשפט_לענייני_משפחה_(סדרי_דין)#תקנה_49)

The child may be heard by the judge or through the assistance unit, and what the child says is privileged (regs 50–55). Even when approving an agreement, the court examines the child's interest and may hear the child (reg. 57). The court may also hear a child's testimony without others present (Family Court Law s. 8(ב)).

## Leading cases

### בע״מ 1858/14 פלוני נ׳ פלונית (3.4.2014)

The Supreme Court held that as long as the statute is unchanged, the tender-years presumption applies: for a child under six, rebutting it for "special reasons" under s. 25 requires weighty evidence. Why it matters: it is the Supreme Court's answer to the argument that the Schnitt recommendations and the trend towards shared parenting had made the presumption obsolete. Lower courts in practice award extensive and often equal parenting time to fathers, but the starting point for very young children remains statutory. Citation and quotation as reported in [Hebrew Wikipedia: משמורת ילדים](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/משמורת_ילדים) (⚠️ secondary; full text not reached).

### Key text: the Schnitt Committee and the Knesset legal survey

The Public Committee on the Legal Aspects of Parental Responsibility in Divorce (Schnitt Committee), appointed in 2005, submitted its final report in December 2011. The majority recommended replacing custody and the tender-years presumption with shared parental responsibility and a list of best-interest criteria; a minority (Prof. Daphna Hacker and others) argued that the presumption does not prevent fathers' involvement and that abolishing it without an alternative would increase litigation and uncertainty. The Ministry of Justice published a memorandum (2012) and a government bill was tabled (2014), but it was not enacted. The Knesset Legal Department's 2012 survey compares the presumption in Israel and abroad. [Knesset survey (PDF)](https://main.knesset.gov.il/Activity/Info/LegalDepartmentSurveys/Survey200512.pdf); committee history as summarised in [Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/משמורת_ילדים) (⚠️ secondary).

## How it works in practice

### What courts weigh

There is no statutory list, but Family Court practice weighs: the child's age and needs; continuity of care and who has been the primary carer; each parent's capacity and willingness to support the child's relationship with the other parent; siblings staying together; the child's wishes according to maturity; distance between homes and the child's school and community; any violence or risk (lesson 18.7). The assistance unit or a welfare-office social worker often prepares a report (תסקיר), and the court may appoint an expert psychologist; a party may then bring their own expert only with leave (Family Court Law s. 8(ג)).

### Common arrangements

| Arrangement | What it means | When courts use it |
|---|---|---|
| Joint custody with equal time | Two homes, roughly half the time each, shared decisions | Parents live near each other and can cooperate |
| Primary residence with one parent, extensive time with the other | Midweek visits and alternate weekends, half of holidays | Frequent default, especially for young children (s. 25) |
| Supervised contact | Meetings at a contact centre (מרכז קשר) | Risk, long estrangement, or allegations under investigation |
| Sole decision-making on a topic | One parent decides, for example, on medical treatment | High conflict over that topic |

### Moving abroad or to another city

Relocation needs both parents' consent or a court order. Where removal is feared, either parent may seek an exit-ban order for the child; this is allowed even before a request to settle the dispute (Dispute Resolution Regulations reg. 2א(א)). Wrongful removal from or to Israel triggers the Hague Convention return procedure in the Family Court (Family Court Law s. 1(5); Procedure Regulations regs 97–119).

### Cost and timing

The Family Court fee for a custody claim and the free status of interim requests are in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md). Reports and expert opinions take months; interim arrangements are usually agreed at the mahut meetings or ordered early.

## Worked example

Eitan and Adi Biton, secular Jews from Rishon LeZion, separate. Their children are 3 and 8. Adi has been the main carer for the toddler; Eitan has taken the older child to school every day for years. Eitan wants equal time; Adi wants the children with her, with Eitan having two afternoons a week and alternate weekends.

1. **Guardianship.** Both remain guardians (s. 14) and must agree on major decisions (s. 18).
2. **First step.** A request to settle the dispute; at the mahut meetings they agree an interim schedule (Dispute Resolution Law s. 3(ג)(4)).
3. **The 3-year-old.** Under s. 25 and 1858/14, the child stays with the mother unless Eitan shows special reasons with weighty evidence. Extensive time with Eitan is still possible within that framework.
4. **The 8-year-old.** No presumption: the court decides by best interest, including the sibling bond and Eitan's established role. The court may hear the child (reg. 49).
5. **Outcome options.** A parenting plan with primary residence at Adi's for the toddler, a graduated increase in Eitan's time, near-equal time for the older child, and a review date when the younger child turns six.
6. **Each side's best legal argument.** Adi: continuity of primary care and s. 25. Eitan: the child's interest in a full relationship with both parents, his established role, and the trend in Family Court practice.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The Biton case or your own (with the other parent's consent; no identifying details of children).
- A calendar for two months.
- 55 minutes.

### Steps

1. Write each child's age, school, activities and needs.
2. Draft a parenting plan with these headings: guardianship decisions (education, health, religion, passports); regular schedule; holidays (Jewish, Muslim, Christian or Druze holidays as relevant, and school breaks); handovers; communication between the parents and with the children; moving home; travel abroad; resolving disagreements (mediation first); review date.
3. Draw the two-month calendar and count each parent's nights.
4. Write one paragraph explaining why the plan is in each child's best interest, using the factors above.
5. Write one paragraph answering the strongest objection the other parent would raise.

### Template

[Written motion](../../templates/written-motion.md) for a request to approve the plan as an agreement under s. 24 (reg. 43).

### How you know it worked

- Every decision area has a rule, including what happens when you disagree.
- The calendar matches the text.
- Your best-interest paragraph speaks about the children, not about the parents' rights.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Parents commonly appear in person in custody and contact proceedings, and approval of an agreed parenting plan is designed to be done without lawyers (the judge explains the agreement and checks consent, reg. 43). You may not draft the plan "for" the other parent as their representative; each parent can sign the jointly written plan.

### Where and how

- The Family Court under the venue rules (reg. 6), after the request to settle (lesson 18.9); or the competent religious court if custody is properly joined to a divorce.
- Exit-ban orders: the Family Court, urgently, even before the request to settle.
- Free help: the assistance unit (information, mediation, interim arrangements); welfare-office family services; Legal Aid for eligible applicants; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- There are allegations of violence or abuse, or a criminal case (lesson 18.7).
- A parent plans to move abroad, or has taken the child abroad.
- The court appoints an expert whose opinion you dispute.
- The other parent is represented and the case concerns a change of the child's residence.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moving with the children without the other parent's consent | Court orders return; credibility damaged | Return and apply to the court | Agree in writing or get an order first |
| Using the children as messengers or witnesses | The court sees harm to the children; weight against you | Stop and use a parenting app or email | Keep the children out of the dispute |
| Withholding contact because maintenance is unpaid | Contempt and loss of credibility | Restore contact; enforce maintenance separately | Treat contact and maintenance separately |
| Agreeing on schedule but not on decisions | Disputes over school or health later | Add decision clauses and approve them | Use the full parenting-plan headings |
| Ignoring the assistance unit's invitations | Reported to the court; costs and adverse inferences | Attend and explain | Attend every meeting in person |

## Review

- Both parents are natural guardians (s. 14); guardianship is a duty and a right, exercised for the child's good (ss. 15, 17) and by agreement (s. 18).
- Separated parents may agree on guardianship, residence and contact, subject to court approval in the child's best interest (s. 24); otherwise the court decides by best interest (s. 25).
- Children up to six stay with their mother unless special reasons are shown with weighty evidence (s. 25; 1858/14); reform has been recommended and debated but not enacted as of October 2026.
- Children have a right to be heard (regs 49–57); the assistance unit, social workers and experts inform the court.
- Relocation and travel need consent or an order; exit-ban orders and Hague return proceedings protect against removal.
