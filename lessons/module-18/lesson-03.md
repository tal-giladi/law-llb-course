# 18.3 · Cohabitants and same-sex couples: recognition and rights

By the end of this lesson you will be able to say, for an unmarried or same-sex couple, which rights the law gives them, which it withholds, what they must prove to claim them, and how a written agreement can protect both partners.

## Why it matters

Many Israeli couples are not married under Israeli law: some cannot marry here (lesson 18.2), some choose not to. Israeli law answered early with a pragmatic category, the couple "known in public" as husband and wife (ידועים בציבור), first in benefits and pension statutes from the 1950s and later in inheritance, family-court jurisdiction and protection from violence. Same-sex couples were recognised gradually, mainly through the courts: registration of marriages abroad, second-parent adoption, and in 2021–2022 access to surrogacy.

Positions differ sharply. Religious parties have objected since the 1950s that recognising cohabitants undermines marriage and the status of the lawful wife, and that the courts, not the Knesset, drove the recognition of same-sex couples. LGBTQ organisations and civil-rights jurists argue that the law still discriminates, since same-sex couples cannot marry in Israel and some statutes speak only of "a man and a woman". A third view, often called the civil theory of family law (Prof. Shahar Lifshitz), cautions against treating cohabitants automatically like spouses, because some couples chose not to marry precisely to avoid those obligations. The Supreme Court has adopted that distinction in property cases.

## The law

### The statutory test: Succession Law s. 55

> איש ואשה החיים חיי משפחה במשק בית משותף אך אינם נשואים זה לזה, ומת אחד מהם ובשעת מותו אף אחד מהם לא היה נשוי לאדם אחר, רואים את הנשאר בחיים כאילו המוריש ציווה לו מה שהנשאר בחיים היה מקבל בירושה על־פי דין אילו היו נשואים זה לזה [...]

*Gist:* If an unmarried man and woman lived family life in a joint household and neither was married to someone else at the death, the survivor takes what a spouse would inherit, as if under a will, unless the deceased's will provides otherwise. [Succession Law, s. 55](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הירושה#סעיף_55)

The two elements, *family life* (intimacy, commitment) and a *joint household*, come from the classic case law (Justice Berinson's formulation), and appear in different words across many statutes. Note two limits in s. 55 itself: the text speaks of "איש ואשה", and it requires that *neither* partner was married to another person at the death. Whether s. 55 extends to same-sex partners has been argued in lower courts; check current case law before relying on it.

### Cohabitants in other statutes

- **Family Court jurisdiction:** "בן זוגו, לרבות הידועה בציבור כאישתו" (Family Court Law s. 1(2)(א)). [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_בית_המשפט_לעניני_משפחה#סעיף_1)
- **Protection from violence:** "”בן זוג“ – לרבות ידוע בציבור" (Prevention of Family Violence Law s. 1). [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה#סעיף_1)
- **Pension division on separation:** "”בן זוג“, של חוסך – לרבות הידוע בציבור כבן זוגו" (Pension Savings Division Law 2014, s. 2). [s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_לחלוקת_חיסכון_פנסיוני_בין_בני_זוג_שנפרדו#סעיף_2)
- **Couples Covenant partners** are treated as spouses (Couples Covenant Law s. 13; lesson 18.2).

What is *missing* matters as much. There is no statute giving cohabitants maintenance from each other during the relationship or after it ends; the Spouses (Property Relations) Law applies to married couples (and Couples Covenant partners), not to cohabitants. For these, cohabitants depend on contract law and on the case law below.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 3045/05 בן ארי נ׳ מנהל מינהל האוכלוסין (21.11.2006)

Five same-sex couples who married in Canada asked to be registered as married. The majority applied Funk-Schlesinger (lesson 18.2): the registration official's role is statistical, and he must register a marriage shown by a public foreign document. The Court stressed that it decided only the registration question and expressed no view on the recognition of same-sex marriage in Israel. Justice Rubinstein, dissenting, held that registration amounts in practice to recognition and should be left to the Knesset.

Why it matters: same-sex couples married abroad (or, since 2023, online) are registered as married, which opens many benefits; but the validity of the marriage and its dissolution remain unresolved at Supreme Court level. [Cardozo summary and translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/node/212)

### ע״א 10280/01 ירוס-חקק נ׳ היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (10.1.2005)

Two women, each the biological mother of children conceived by anonymous sperm donation, each asked to adopt the other's children. The majority held that the Adoption Law's exception for a single adopter could be applied under s. 25, where adoption is in the child's best interest and there are special circumstances, and returned the case to the Family Court, which granted the adoptions. The Court emphasised that it created no marital status for the couple.

Why it matters: second-parent adoption became the standard route for a non-biological parent in a same-sex couple, alongside parentage orders in surrogacy (lesson 18.8). [Cardozo](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yaros-hakak-v-attorney-general)

### Cohabitants' property and proof: בע״מ 2478/14 and רע״א 3323/23

In בע״מ 2478/14 פלוני נ׳ פלוני the Supreme Court held that the presumption of community does not apply automatically to an asset one cohabitant bought separately; an express intention to share must be shown for each asset. The ruling draws on the distinction between couples who live together because they cannot marry and those who chose not to. In רע״א 3323/23 פלוני נ׳ מגדל חברה לביטוח בע״מ (12.5.2024) the Court held that living together and even planning a wedding do not by themselves prove the intention required for recognition as cohabitants for an insurance benefit.

Why it matters: cohabitants who want shared ownership or support must *document* their intentions. Citations and holdings as reported in [Hebrew Wikipedia: ידוע בציבור](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/ידוע_בציבור) (⚠️ secondary; full texts on the Judiciary's site not reached).

## How it works in practice

### Rights compared

| Question | Married (or Couples Covenant) | Cohabitants | Same-sex couple married abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registered as married | Yes | No | Yes (Ben-Ari) |
| Property on separation | Property Relations Law (lesson 18.4) | Contract, partnership proved per asset (2478/14) | Courts generally apply the rules for spouses; ⚠️ check current case law |
| Maintenance between partners | Personal law or Maintenance Law | Only by agreement (express or implied) | ⚠️ unsettled; by agreement is safest |
| Inheritance without a will | Spouse's statutory share | s. 55, if neither was married to another | As spouse if the marriage is recognised; ⚠️ |
| Pension division | Yes | Yes (2014 Law, s. 2) | Yes |
| Protection order | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Family Court jurisdiction | Yes | Yes (3927/15) | Yes |
| Parenthood of partner's child | Presumption for a husband under personal law; otherwise adoption or order | Paternity acknowledgment, adoption | Second-parent adoption (Yaros-Hakak) or parentage order |

### Proving you are cohabitants

Evidence typically used: a joint address in the Population Registry, joint lease or mortgage, joint bank accounts, a cohabitation affidavit (signed before a lawyer under the Evidence Ordinance), joint children, testimony of friends and family, photos and correspondence over time. Many authorities accept a signed affidavit as the first step; a court will look at the whole picture.

### The cohabitation agreement

A written agreement is the main tool cohabitants have. It typically covers: the date the relationship began; ownership of each existing asset; how new assets and debts are held; contributions to the home; what happens to the home and to pension rights on separation; maintenance, if any; and a mutual will or a waiver. Unlike a spouses' property agreement (lesson 18.4), it does not *need* court approval, but approval by the Family Court under s. 3(ג) gives it the force of a judgment. Drafting such an agreement for someone else is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20); you may draft your own.

## Worked example

Tamar and Liat, from Jerusalem, married online through Utah County in 2024 and are registered as married. Tamar gave birth to their son in 2025 through sperm donation. Liat has no legal tie to the child. Ofer and Dana, from Kiryat Bialik, have lived together for nine years; the apartment is registered in Ofer's name.

1. **Tamar and Liat: status.** Registered as married under Brill and Ben-Ari. Registration does not prove validity, so for the child they should not rely on it.
2. **Tamar and Liat: the child.** Liat applies to the Family Court for second-parent adoption (Yaros-Hakak; Adoption Law s. 25). Until the order, Liat has no guardianship and no inheritance right from the child, and the child none from her.
3. **Ofer and Dana: the apartment.** No automatic sharing (2478/14). Dana must show an intention to share the apartment specifically: payments to the mortgage, statements, a joint plan. A written agreement now would avoid the question.
4. **Ofer and Dana: death.** If Ofer dies without a will, Dana inherits under s. 55 provided neither was married to someone else. If Ofer is still technically married to a former wife who never received a get, s. 55 does not apply and Dana needs a will.
5. **Both couples:** each partner writes a will; each couple signs an agreement on property; Ofer and Dana keep evidence of their household.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The rights table above and s. 55 of the Succession Law.
- 50 minutes.

### Steps

1. Choose a couple: your own (with your partner's consent), or Ofer and Dana above.
2. List the evidence that you are "known in public" and rate each item strong, medium or weak.
3. Write the outline of a cohabitation agreement with headings: parties and start date; existing assets (list each, owner, value); new assets and debts; the home; pensions; maintenance; separation procedure (including a request to settle the dispute, lesson 18.9); death (wills); approval by the Family Court (yes or no and why).
4. For each heading, note which rule applies if you sign nothing.
5. Mark which clauses you would want a lawyer to review before signing.

### How you know it worked

- For every heading you know both the default rule and what your clause changes.
- Your evidence list would satisfy a sceptical insurer or court.
- You did not assume that s. 55 or the Property Relations Law apply automatically.

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may write and sign your own cohabitation agreement and will, apply to the Family Court in your own name for approval of an agreement or for adoption, and claim inheritance under s. 55 before the Registrar of Inheritance. A cohabitation affidavit must be signed before a person authorised to take affidavits (often a lawyer, who certifies the warning but does not represent you). Drafting the agreement for your partner, or for anyone else, is reserved to lawyers.

### Where and how

- Approval of an agreement: the Family Court (fee in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md)).
- Second-parent adoption: the Family Court, with a social worker's report.
- Inheritance under s. 55: the Registrar of Inheritance; contested cases go to the Family Court.
- Free help: LGBTQ and family-rights organisations publish guides; Kol-Zchut (⚠️ manual check); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- One partner is still married to someone else, or a former partner contests the relationship.
- A home, a business or significant pension rights are at stake.
- A child's parentage or a foreign surrogacy is involved.
- An insurer or pension fund has rejected a claim based on cohabitation.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assuming cohabitants share all property like spouses | Losing a claim to an asset in the other partner's name | Gather evidence of intention for that asset | Sign a cohabitation agreement that lists assets |
| Relying on s. 55 while a partner is still married to someone else | No inheritance | A will (cannot be fixed after death) | Both partners write wills |
| Relying on registration abroad as proof of a child's parentage | Non-biological parent has no legal status | Apply for adoption or a parentage order | Start the process after the birth |
| No evidence of the joint household | Insurer or court denies cohabitant status (3323/23) | Collect documents and affidavits | Keep joint records and an affidavit up to date |
| Drafting an agreement "for" a friend or relative | Unauthorised practice of law (s. 20) | Each person drafts their own or uses a lawyer | Act only in your own name |

## Review

- Cohabitants are recognised in many statutes through two elements: family life and a joint household; the wording varies, so read each statute.
- Succession Law s. 55 gives a surviving cohabitant a spouse's share, but only if neither partner was married to another person at the death.
- There is no statutory property regime or maintenance duty between cohabitants; the Supreme Court requires proof of intention to share each separately acquired asset (2478/14).
- Same-sex couples married abroad are registered as married (Ben-Ari); a non-biological parent becomes a parent through second-parent adoption (Yaros-Hakak) or a parentage order.
- Recognition of cohabitants and same-sex couples is contested, from religious, civil-libertarian and "civil theory" positions; a written agreement and wills protect both partners whichever view prevails.
