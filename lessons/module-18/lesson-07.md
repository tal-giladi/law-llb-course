# 18.7 · Domestic violence and protection orders

By the end of this lesson you will know what a protection order (צו הגנה) can do, who can ask for one and on what grounds, how fast it can be obtained, how it fits with criminal proceedings and other family cases, and where to get help immediately.

> [!WARNING]
> If you or someone else is in danger now, call the police on **100**. The Welfare Ministry's domestic-violence hotline is **118** (confidential advice and referral to shelters and treatment centres). A protection order is a civil court order; it does not replace calling the police, and a court may take hours or days. This lesson teaches the law for your own situation and is not a substitute for emergency help, a social worker or a lawyer.

## Why it matters

Violence in the family crosses every community and class. The Prevention of Family Violence Law 1991 gives a fast civil remedy that does not depend on a criminal complaint: a court order that bars a family member from the home, from approaching, from harassing, and from holding weapons. It is available in any magistrates' court, in the Family Court and in the religious courts, and it is excluded from the mandatory dispute-resolution stage, so a request can be filed at once.

The law has been strengthened repeatedly: weapons bans, treatment of the person bound by the order, and since 2024 a temporary order allowing electronic monitoring. Two concerns pull in different directions, and both appear in the statute. Victims' organisations stress under-reporting, the danger period around separation, and the need for fast protection. Others, including some fathers' organisations and defence lawyers, warn that protection orders are sometimes sought tactically in custody and property disputes, and that ex parte orders remove people from their homes before they are heard. The statute answers with speed and ex parte orders on one side, and a hearing within seven days, limits on ex parte orders for psychological abuse, and costs and compensation for vexatious applications on the other.

## The law

### Who is protected: s. 1

"Family member" includes current and *former* family members: a spouse (including a partner known in public), parent or parent's spouse, parent-in-law, grandparent, descendant or step-descendant, sibling, brother- or sister-in-law, uncle, aunt, nephew or niece; and a person responsible for a minor or a vulnerable person who lives with them, and that minor or vulnerable person. [s. 1](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה#סעיף_1)

### The grounds: s. 3

> [...] רשאי בית המשפט לתת צו הגנה מפני אדם אם ראה כי נתקיים אחד מאלה: (1) בסמוך לפני הגשת הבקשה נהג באלימות בבן משפחתו, ביצע בו עבירת מין או כלא אותו שלא כדין; (2) התנהגותו נותנת בסיס סביר להניח כי הוא מהווה סכנה גופנית ממשית לבן משפחתו או שהוא עלול לבצע בו עבירת מין; (3) התעלל בבן משפחתו התעללות נפשית מתמשכת, או התנהג באופן שאינו מאפשר לבן משפחתו ניהול סביר ותקין של חייו;

*Gist:* A court may give a protection order if, shortly before the application, the person was violent, committed a sexual offence or unlawfully confined a family member; or their conduct gives reasonable grounds to fear real physical danger or a sexual offence; or they subjected the family member to continuing psychological abuse or made a reasonable normal life impossible. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה#סעיף_3)

The application may be made by the family member, the Attorney General, a police prosecutor, or a social worker appointed under the Youth (Care and Supervision) Law or the Protection of Vulnerable Persons Law.

### What the order can do: s. 2

The court may forbid the person, wholly or in part or on conditions: (1) to enter the home where the family member lives or come within a set distance of it, *even if he has a right in it*, or to enter or approach another place the family member regularly attends, such as work; (2) to harass the family member in any way and anywhere; (3) to prevent or hinder the family member's lawful use of property, even property in which he has a right; (4) to be within a set distance of the family member's location, knowing they are there (s. 2(א)). The order may require a bond for keeping it and for good behaviour, and any other provision needed for safety (s. 2(ב)). The court may also give a threatening-harassment order in the same proceeding (s. 2(ח1)).

**Children.** An order to protect a minor is notified to a youth social worker; a report must be filed within 30 days where the order removes a person responsible for the child from the home, and contact with that person is decided on the report (s. 2(ז)). Where the order protects the other parent, the court may regulate the bound parent's contact with the child only if satisfied it will not harm the child (s. 2(ח)).

**Weapons.** A protection order bans the bound person from holding or carrying any weapon, including one issued by the security forces, and the court orders immediate seizure (s. 2ב); the court may allow continued holding only in narrow cases and after a hearing with both sides (ss. 2ג–2ה). The ban continues after the order expires until the court cancels it (s. 5(ב)).

### Procedure and duration: ss. 4–5

> (א) בית המשפט רשאי לתת צו הגנה במעמד צד אחד; ניתן צו במעמד צד אחד יתקיים הדיון בנוכחות שני הצדדים בהקדם האפשרי ולא יאוחר משבעה ימים מיום מתן הצו.

*Gist:* The court may give an order ex parte, but then a hearing with both sides must be held as soon as possible and within seven days. [s. 4(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה#סעיף_4)

An order on the psychological-abuse ground (s. 3(3)), and an order with electronic monitoring, are given only after a hearing with both parties, unless the respondent was duly summoned and did not appear (s. 4(ג)). The court may not reject an application without letting the applicant argue orally, save in exceptional circumstances (s. 4(ד)).

> תקפו של צו הגנה לא יעלה על שלושה חדשים; בית המשפט רשאי להאריך את תקפו של הצו מפעם לפעם, ובלבד שהתקופה הכוללת לא תעלה על ששה חדשים; ואולם, מנימוקים מיוחדים [...] לתקופה כוללת שלא תעלה על שנה אחת; וכן, מנימוקים מיוחדים כאמור, [...] לתקופה כוללת שלא תעלה על שנה אחת נוספת.

*Gist:* An order lasts up to three months, extendable to six months in total, and for special reasons to one year and then one more year. [s. 5(א)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה#סעיף_5) (Periods are also listed in [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).)

A court will not refuse to hear an application because the matter is before another court, because of local jurisdiction, or because a person waived their rights (s. 10). The ordinary rules on spouses testifying against each other do not apply (s. 8(א)), and the Family Court's flexible procedure applies in the magistrates' court too (s. 8(ב)). A vexatious application can lead to costs and compensation (s. 11).

### Electronic monitoring, risk assessment and treatment (temporary orders)

Under Amendment 19 (a temporary order for three years from 6 August 2024, per the Wikisource consolidation; [Reshumot](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lsr_3056750.pdf)), the court may attach electronic monitoring to an order that keeps a person away from the home or from the protected person, with the protected person's consent, where it is needed because of a real fear of breach or high danger, and the bound person has a record or pending charge for a violence offence or breached a previous order, and a risk assessment by a state assessor has been received (s. 3ב(ג)). In cases of high and real danger to life, the court may order monitoring for a short period before the assessment (s. 3ב(ה)). The court must weigh the bound person's privacy and freedom of movement against the danger (s. 3ב(ד)). Failure to appear for the risk assessment creates a presumption of high danger (s. 6א(ד)).

Under Amendment 18 (a five-year temporary order from its commencement), the court orders an opinion by a welfare social worker on the bound person's suitability for treatment and may order treatment in a state programme (s. 2א(ד)). Check whether both temporary orders have been extended before relying on them.

### Breach: s. 7

A complaint to the police of a breach of an order that bars the home or proximity, or a monitoring alert, allows a police officer to arrest the person; a decision *not* to arrest must be made in writing with reasons by the officer in charge of investigations (s. 7(ב)). It is no defence that the protected family members did not insist on the order (s. 7(ג)). Breach is also a criminal offence under the Penal Law.

### Links to other family proceedings

- **No dispute-resolution stage:** the Dispute Resolution Law does not apply to proceedings under this Law (s. 4א(3)).
- **No fee:** applications and appeals under this Law are exempt (Family Court Fees Regulations reg. 7(1)).
- **Property:** a protection order given after a hearing with both parties is a ground to bring forward the balance of resources (Property Relations Law s. 5א(ב)(1); lesson 18.4).
- **Venue:** a Family Court may hear claims of a woman in a shelter or a person protected by an order even outside the usual venue (Procedure Regulations reg. 6(ה)).
- **Assistance-unit privilege** does not cover information indicating grounds for an order under s. 3(2) (Family Court Law s. 5א(ה)).

## Leading cases

### Key text: the statute and its amendments

No single appellate ruling defines this field for a self-represented applicant; the statute is detailed and most decisions are fact-specific and unpublished for privacy. The key texts are the consolidated Law ([Wikisource](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_למניעת_אלימות_במשפחה)) and Amendment 19 on electronic monitoring ([Reshumot](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/law/25_lsr_3056750.pdf)). Read s. 3 (grounds), s. 4 (procedure) and s. 5 (duration) before any hearing.

## How it works in practice

### Applying

1. **Where:** the nearest magistrates' court or Family Court; a religious court only for parties it has jurisdiction over (s. 1, "בית משפט"). Courts have duty arrangements for urgent applications outside working hours (check the court's information line).
2. **What:** an application on the court's form with an affidavit describing the most recent incidents (dates, what happened, injuries, threats, weapons), children in the home, and the orders requested; attach medical records, photos, messages and police complaint numbers if any.
3. **The ex parte decision:** often the same day. If granted, the order is served on the respondent (police often serve), and a hearing is fixed within seven days.
4. **The hearing:** both sides; the court may extend, change or cancel the order even if the respondent does not appear (s. 4(ב)).
5. **After:** keep a copy with you; report every breach to the police (100).

### Safety planning

Shelters, emergency apartments and treatment centres for violence in the family operate through the Welfare Ministry and local welfare offices; the hotline 118 refers callers. A woman in a shelter has special routes: urgent applications about the children during the stay (Dispute Resolution Regulations reg. 12(א)(3א)) and a shortened stay period (reg. 16(א)(7)).

## Worked example

Hila, from a moshav in the south, has been married for six years. Over the last months her husband has smashed objects, threatened to kill her during arguments, checked her phone and taken her bank card. Last night he pushed her against a wall. He holds a licensed pistol. They have a daughter, 5.

1. **Immediate safety.** Hila calls 100 last night. Today she calls 118 for advice and a safety plan.
2. **Grounds.** Last night's assault is violence "בסמוך" (s. 3(1)); the threats and the pistol support a reasonable fear of real physical danger (s. 3(2)); the control over money and phone may amount to continuing psychological abuse (s. 3(3)).
3. **Application.** At the magistrates' court in the nearest town she applies for an order barring him from the home and from approaching her and her workplace, a ban on harassment, and the seizure of the pistol (s. 2ב). No fee; no request to settle needed.
4. **Ex parte.** The court grants an order for the home, proximity and weapon now; the s. 3(3) ground will be examined at the hearing within seven days with both present.
5. **The daughter.** The court regulates the father's contact only if satisfied it will not harm the child, usually after a social worker's report (s. 2(ח)).
6. **Afterwards.** Hila may file a request to settle the dispute and family claims later; the order is also a ground for an early balance of resources (s. 5א(ב)(1)).

## Do it yourself

### You need

- The statute (link above) and the Hila case. Do not use your own situation in a class or study group if it involves current danger; seek help first.
- 45 minutes.

### Steps

1. Underline in s. 3 the ground or grounds that apply to Hila's facts, and write one sentence per ground explaining why.
2. List the orders under s. 2 you would ask for, with the subsection, and the distance or places to include.
3. Draft a one-page chronological affidavit outline: dates, incidents, threats, weapons, children, previous complaints. Facts only, in short numbered paragraphs.
4. Write the questions the judge is likely to ask at the hearing within seven days, and your answers.
5. List the evidence to bring: medical records, photos, messages, witnesses, police reference numbers.

## Without a lawyer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Protection-order applications are designed for applicants without lawyers: forms are available at the court, the fee is waived, and urgent applications are heard quickly. The respondent may also appear in person. A social worker, a police prosecutor or the Attorney General may apply on behalf of a family member (s. 3).

### Where and how

- Any magistrates' court or Family Court; the court's secretariat helps with the form.
- Police: 100. Domestic-violence hotline: 118. Shelters and treatment centres through welfare offices.
- Legal Aid: check eligibility for representation in protection-order and related family proceedings; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Criminal charges are pending or likely (for either side): lesson [05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md) covers the victim's side of the criminal process.
- The respondent is represented, or disputes the facts with witnesses and experts.
- Children's contact, the home and property are being decided at the same time.
- You are the respondent and the order affects your home, children or firearm licence.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for a court instead of calling the police in an emergency | Exposure to immediate danger | Call 100 now | Police first, court second |
| A vague affidavit ("he is always violent") | The court cannot find a ground | Add dates, events and evidence at the hearing | Write a dated chronology |
| Inviting the bound person home "to talk" | Breach risk and confusion about the order; it is not a defence for him (s. 7(ג)) | Ask the court to vary the order if circumstances change | Treat the order as binding on both of you in practice |
| Not attending the hearing within seven days | The order may be cancelled | Ask for a new date at once | Diarise the hearing on the day of the ex parte order |
| Using an application tactically in a custody dispute | Rejection, costs and compensation (s. 11), loss of credibility | Withdraw; deal with custody in the proper proceeding | Apply only on real grounds |

## Review

- A protection order protects current and former family members, including cohabitants, on three grounds: recent violence or sexual offence or confinement, reasonable fear of real physical danger, or continuing psychological abuse (s. 3).
- It can bar the home, proximity, harassment and interference with property, and always bans weapons (ss. 2, 2ב).
- Ex parte orders are possible, with a hearing within seven days; psychological-abuse and monitoring orders need a hearing with both sides (s. 4). Duration: up to three months, extendable to six, then one year and one more for special reasons (s. 5).
- No fee, no dispute-resolution stage, available in any magistrates' court; electronic monitoring and treatment run under temporary orders to be checked.
- Emergency first: police 100, hotline 118.
