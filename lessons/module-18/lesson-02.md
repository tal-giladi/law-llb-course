# 18.2 · Marriage and divorce: who may marry, marriages abroad and registration

By the end of this lesson you will be able to tell a couple, whatever their communities, how they can marry, what will be written in the Population Registry, what that registration does and does not prove, and where and on what ground the marriage can later be dissolved.

## Why it matters

There is no civil marriage ceremony inside Israel. Members of a recognised community marry through its religious authority; couples who cannot or will not do so travel abroad, marry online under a foreign law, register a Couples Covenant if both are of no religion, or live together unmarried. Each route has different consequences for registration, for property and maintenance, and above all for how the relationship ends.

The issue is among the most contested in Israeli public life. Supporters of the current arrangement argue that a single religious track in each community preserves shared personal status, prevents doubts about lineage (for Jews, especially mamzerut), and respects the communities' autonomy. Opponents argue that it leaves interfaith couples, same-sex couples and people of no recognised religion without a way to marry at home, and imposes a religious ceremony on secular citizens. Bills for civil marriage or civil unions have been tabled repeatedly; none has passed as of October 2026. Meanwhile the courts have built a path through registration, which supporters call a pragmatic recognition of reality and critics call a back door that should have been left to the Knesset.

## The law

### Who marries whom, and how it is registered

Marriage and divorce of Jewish citizens and residents are performed in Israel "על פי דין תורה" (1953 Law, s. 2; lesson [10.3](../module-10/lesson-03.md)). Muslims, Christians and Druze marry under the law of their community (lesson [10.4](../module-10/lesson-04.md)). The religious authority that performs the marriage registers it:

> ”הרשות הרושמת“ פירושה הפקיד המסדר את הקידושין, אם היו הנשואין נשואין אזרחיים, האימם, בנשואין לפי דת האיסלם, הכומר בנשואין לפי דת הנצרות, הרבי, בנשואין לפי דיני ישראל והשיך של השבט, בנשואין לפי דת הדרוזים.

*Gist:* The registering authority is the person who performs the ceremony: the imam, the priest, the rabbi or the Druze sheikh. [Marriage and Divorce (Registration) Ordinance, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/פקודת_הנשואין_והגירושין_(רישום)#סעיף_2)

Section 2א lets a Jewish couple register with any rabbi appointed as marriage registrar, regardless of where they live. Section 7 makes it an offence, punishable by two years' imprisonment, for a person not to ensure the registration of their own marriage or divorce or one they performed.

### Minimum age

> מי שעשה אחד מאלה – (א) נשא קטינה או נשאה קטין; (ב) ערך נישואין [...] של קטינה או של קטין; [...] דינו – מאסר שנתיים [...]

*Gist:* Marrying a minor (under 18), performing such a marriage or marrying off one's child under 18 is an offence. [Marriage Age Law, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_גיל_הנישואין#סעיף_2)

The Family Court may permit the marriage of a minor aged 16 or over for special reasons connected with the minor's welfare, after hearing the minor; under 17 only after a social worker's report (s. 5). An under-age marriage that is valid under the personal law stays valid but gives a ground to dissolve it (s. 3). The law applies equally to all communities.

### The Population Registry and what it proves

Section 2(א)(7)–(8) of the Population Registry Law records each resident's "המצב האישי (רווק, נשוי, גרוש או אלמן)" and the spouse's name. But:

> הרישום במרשם [...] יהיו ראיה לכאורה לנכונות פרטי הרישום המפורטים בפסקאות (1) עד (4) ו־(9) עד (13) לסעיף 2.

*Gist:* Registration is prima facie evidence only of items (1)–(4) and (9)–(13), which do not include religion, nationality or marital status. [Population Registry Law, s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_מרשם_האוכלוסין#סעיף_3)

That gap is the key to the case law below: registration of marital status does not decide whether the marriage is valid.

### The Couples Covenant for persons of no religion (2010)

> בני זוג חסרי דת, המסכימים לבוא בברית הזוגיות, רשאים לערוך ברית זוגיות בפני רשם הזוגיות ולהירשם במרשם הזוגיות [...]

*Gist:* A man and a woman (s. 1 defines "בני זוג" as "איש ואישה") who are both registered as of no religion, aged 18 or over, residents and not related, may enter a Couples Covenant before the Couples Registrar. [Couples Covenant Law, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_ברית_הזוגיות_לחסרי_דת#סעיף_2)

A religious court may object that one partner belongs to its community (s. 6); if it does not answer within three months, it is treated as not objecting. Registered partners are treated like a married couple (s. 13), including under the Property Relations Law, with exceptions for citizenship and entry. Dissolution is by agreement before the registrar, or through a request to settle the dispute in the Family Court; if no settlement is reached within six months, the court may dissolve the covenant at one partner's request (s. 11(ב)(2)). The Family Court has exclusive jurisdiction (s. 3).

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 143/62 פונק־שלזינגר נ׳ שר הפנים, פ״ד יז 225 (1963)

A Christian woman and a Jewish man married in Cyprus and asked to be registered as married. The Supreme Court held that the registration official performs a statistical function: on production of a public document showing the marriage, he must register it, and he may not examine its validity. Validity, if disputed, is for a court in proceedings that require it.

Why it matters: Funk-Schlesinger is the foundation of every later registration case, including same-sex marriages (lesson 18.3) and online marriages. Critics say that its "statistical" view has turned registration into recognition in practice; supporters say it simply keeps a clerk from deciding questions of status. [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/160)

### בג״ץ 2232/03 פלונית נ׳ בית הדין הרבני האזורי תל אביב-יפו (21.11.2006)

Two Jews who could have married religiously chose a civil wedding in Cyprus. The HCJ held that the rabbinical courts may dissolve such a marriage; it has "external" validity even if not valid under Jewish law, and it may be dissolved because it has irretrievably broken down, without fault and without both parties' consent.

Why it matters: it answers the question every couple marrying abroad should ask first, how the marriage ends. For Jewish couples, the rabbinical court remains the forum for divorce, even of a civil marriage. [Cardozo translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/11)

### עע״ם 7368/22 שר הפנים נ׳ בריל (7.3.2023): online marriages in Utah

From 2020 Utah County allowed weddings by video link, with the officiant in Utah and the couple anywhere. Hundreds of Israeli couples married this way while physically in Israel. The Population and Immigration Authority refused to register them, arguing that the marriage was in substance celebrated in Israel, where no civil marriage exists. The Lod District Court (sitting as an administrative court) ordered registration; the state appealed. On 7 March 2023 the Supreme Court (a panel of three, unanimously) dismissed the appeal: under Funk-Schlesinger the official registers on the basis of a public foreign certificate and may not decide where the ceremony "really" took place.

**The state's legal argument:** the Funk-Schlesinger rule concerned couples who travelled abroad; a ceremony with both spouses in Israel is an Israeli marriage in disguise; registration in practice confers status, so the question belongs to the Knesset; and online procedures raise authentication concerns.

**The couples' legal argument:** registration is statistical and the certificate is a valid public document of a foreign state; the official has no power to examine the place of the ceremony; and the alternative leaves couples with no lawful way to marry.

**The current state of the law:** registration of online marriages on production of a certified (apostilled) certificate continues, and Kol-Zchut describes the procedure as current (page updated December 2025, ⚠️ manual check). The ruling decided registration only; the *validity* of such a marriage for divorce, inheritance or other purposes can still be raised in a proceeding where it matters, and how such marriages are dissolved remains a developing question. No law reversing the ruling was found as of October 2026; check the Knesset site before relying on this. Sources: [Globes report](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001440499); case number and date as cited in [Hebrew Wikipedia: נישואי יוטה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/נישואי_יוטה) (⚠️ secondary; full text on the Judiciary's site not reached).

## How it works in practice

### The routes

| Couple | Can they marry in Israel? | Usual alternatives | Who dissolves the relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two Jews | Yes, through the Rabbinate (any registrar, s. 2א) | Civil marriage abroad or online; cohabitation | Rabbinical court (s. 1 of the 1953 Law), including civil marriages abroad (2232/03) |
| Two Muslims, two Druze, two members of the same Christian community | Yes, through the community's authority | As above | The community's religious court (lesson 10.4) |
| Spouses of different religions | No | Marriage abroad or online | 1969 Law: the President of the Supreme Court decides the forum (lesson 10.4) |
| Two people registered as of no religion (man and woman) | Couples Covenant | Marriage abroad or online | Family Court or the Couples Registrar (s. 11) |
| Same-sex couple | No | Marriage abroad or online; registered under Ben-Ari (lesson 18.3) | Family Court, in lower-court practice (lesson 18.3) |
| A Jew barred by Jewish law from a particular marriage (e.g. a kohen and a divorcee) | Not through the Rabbinate | Abroad or online | Rabbinical court |

### Steps to register a marriage made abroad or online

1. Obtain the marriage certificate from the foreign authority and an **apostille** (Hague Convention certification) or consular certification.
2. Apply to the Population and Immigration Authority to update marital status; bring identity cards and the certified certificate (with a translation if required). Check the current requirements on gov.il (⚠️ manual check: gov.il blocks automated access).
3. Check that the registry entry and both spouses' identity-card appendix show the change.

### Divorce and get refusal in brief

For Jewish couples, the get requires both spouses' consent; a spouse who refuses can be subjected to restriction orders by the rabbinical court (lesson 10.3). Get refusal is debated: dayanim emphasise the limits Jewish law places on coercion; women's organisations and some academic jurists press for stronger sanctions or civil solutions. Muslim, Christian and Druze divorce follows each community's law (lesson 10.4).

## Worked example

Noam (Jewish, registered as Jewish) and Katya (an immigrant under the Law of Return, registered as of no religion) live in Haifa and want to marry.

1. **In Israel?** No: they are not of the same community, and the Couples Covenant requires *both* to be of no religion (s. 2(3)).
2. **Options.** A civil wedding abroad (Cyprus, for example) or online through Utah County. Either way they present the certificate with an apostille and are registered as married (Funk-Schlesinger; Brill).
3. **What registration gives them.** The registry shows them married; most public bodies act on it. It is not proof of validity (s. 3), so a dispute over status could still be litigated.
4. **If they separate.** Because they are of different religions, dissolution goes through the 1969 Law (lesson 10.4); property is decided under the Property Relations Law (lesson 18.4), which applies to married couples whatever the form of the marriage.
5. **Alternative.** If they prefer not to marry, they can live as cohabitants with a written cohabitation agreement (lesson 18.3).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Registering a marriage, applying for a Couples Covenant and a Family Court request for a marriage permit for a minor are all done in your own name. Divorce proceedings may be conducted in person in every forum. In the rabbinical courts you may also be represented by a licensed rabbinical pleader (lesson 10.6).

### Where and how

- Marriage registration with the Rabbinate: any appointed rabbinical registrar (Ordinance s. 2א). Muslims, Christians and Druze: the community's authority.
- Registration of a foreign or online marriage, and Couples Covenant applications: the Population and Immigration Authority (⚠️ manual check of current procedure on gov.il).
- Free help: Kol-Zchut guides (⚠️ manual check), Legal Aid for divorce proceedings for eligible applicants; [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Your spouse refuses a get or a religious divorce, or is abroad.
- The validity of a foreign or online marriage is disputed in a divorce, inheritance or benefits case.
- One partner's immigration status depends on the marriage.
- A marriage involving a minor is planned or has happened.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treating a Population Registry entry as proof of marriage | Surprise when validity is challenged in a later case | Collect the original certificate and evidence of the ceremony | Remember s. 3: marital status is not prima facie evidence |
| Marrying abroad without asking how the marriage will end | A divorce process you did not expect (e.g. a rabbinical court for two Jews) | Take advice before filing | Read 2232/03 and lesson 10.4 before the wedding |
| Not obtaining an apostille | Registration refused | Order the apostille from the issuing authority | Request it with the certificate |
| Assuming the Couples Covenant is open to any couple | Application rejected or objected to | Consider marriage abroad or a cohabitation agreement | Check s. 2: both of no religion, a man and a woman |
| A religious marriage ceremony outside the Rabbinate that is never registered | Possible offence under s. 7 and doubt about status | Take advice; register if possible | Know the registration duty before the ceremony |

## Review

- In Israel each recognised community marries its members under its own law; there is no civil ceremony. The Couples Covenant (2010) is open only to a man and a woman both registered as of no religion.
- The minimum age is 18 for everyone; the Family Court may permit marriage from 16 in special circumstances.
- Registration is statistical (Funk-Schlesinger): the official registers a foreign marriage on a public certificate, and since Brill (2023) also an online Utah marriage; registration does not prove validity (Population Registry Law s. 3).
- Dissolution depends on community: the rabbinical court even for Jews married abroad (2232/03); the 1969 Law for mixed couples; the Family Court for the Couples Covenant.
- The question of civil marriage remains politically and legally contested; this lesson states the law as of October 2026.
