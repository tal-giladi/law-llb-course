# 18.8 · Parentage: adoption, surrogacy and fertility treatment (overview)

By the end of this lesson you will know the main legal routes by which a child gets a legal parent other than through birth to married parents: paternity and maternity claims, adoption, surrogacy under the 1996 Law and foreign surrogacy, and the role of the Family Court in each. This is an overview; the details change often and most cases need professional help.

## Why it matters

Legal parenthood decides guardianship, maintenance, inheritance, citizenship and the right to decide for the child. For most children it follows birth. For a growing number of families, adopted children, children born through surrogacy in Israel or abroad, and children of same-sex couples, it requires a court order. Each route is governed by a statute that sets strict conditions, and each has been reshaped by litigation.

The values in tension are serious and widely held. Religious communities and their courts care about lineage and personal status, and some religious leaders object to surrogacy or adoption by same-sex couples. Feminist critics, from a different direction, warn that surrogacy can commodify women's bodies, especially poorer women. LGBTQ organisations and single parents argue for equal access to parenthood. Children's-rights advocates insist that every rule be tested by the child's interest. The law today reflects compromises among all of these.

## The law

### Adoption: best interest, who may adopt, consent

> (ב) צו אימוץ וכל החלטה אחרת בהליכי אימוץ יינתנו אם נוכח בית־המשפט שהם לטובת המאומץ.

*Gist:* Every adoption order and decision is given only if the court is satisfied it is for the adoptee's good. [Adoption Law, s. 1(ב)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אימוץ_ילדים#סעיף_1)

The adoptee must be under 18 (s. 2), and the adopter at least 18 years older, with exceptions (s. 4). On who may adopt:

> אין אימוץ אלא על ידי איש ואשתו יחד; ואולם רשאי בית משפט ליתן צו אימוץ למאמץ יחיד – (1) אם בן זוגו הוא הורה המאומץ או אימץ אותו לפני כן; (2) אם הורי המאומץ נפטרו והמאמץ הוא מקרובי המאומץ ובלתי נשוי.

*Gist:* Adoption is by a married man and woman together; a single adopter only if their spouse is the child's parent or adoptive parent, or the parents have died and the adopter is an unmarried relative. [s. 3](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_אימוץ_ילדים#סעיף_3)

Section 25 allows the court, for the child's good and in special circumstances, to depart from the age rules, from the conditions of s. 3(2) and from the age gap; this was the basis of second-parent adoption in Yaros-Hakak (lesson 18.3). An adoption requires the parents' consent, or a declaration that the child is adoptable (s. 8). Without consent, the Attorney General may ask the court to declare the child adoptable on listed grounds, including abandonment, failure to keep contact or fulfil duties for six consecutive months without reasonable cause, or inability to care for the child that is not expected to change despite reasonable help (s. 13(א)).

### Surrogacy in Israel: the 1996 Law

> לא יבצע אדם הפריה חוץ־גופית לשם השתלת ביצית מופרית באם נושאת [...] אלא על פי הסכם לנשיאת עוברים שאישרה ועדת האישורים לפי סעיף 5 [...]

*Gist:* Embryo transfer to a surrogate is lawful only under an agreement approved by the Ministry of Health's approvals committee, using an egg that is not the surrogate's, the intended father's sperm (or, for a single intended mother, her own egg), in a recognised unit. [Surrogacy Law, s. 2](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסכמים_לנשיאת_עוברים_(אישור_הסכם_ומעמד_היילוד)#סעיף_2)

The approvals committee (two gynaecologists, an internist, a clinical psychologist, a social worker, a jurist and a cleric of the parties' religion; s. 3) approves an agreement only if, among other conditions, all parties are Israeli residents; the surrogate is usually unmarried, not a relative, aged 22 to 39, has given birth before and has not been a surrogate more than twice; the intended parents meet age and number-of-children limits; the surrogate is of the intended mother's religion (with an exception when all parties are not Jewish); and the agreement does not harm the child's or a party's rights (s. 5(א)). After the birth, the Family Court gives a parentage order (צו הורות) to the intended parents.

**Who may be intended parents.** The original definitions limited the route to a man and a woman who were a couple, and later also a single intended mother. In HCJ 781/15 (Arad-Pinkas), following petitions by same-sex couples and single men, the Supreme Court held the exclusion unconstitutional; according to the Wikisource editorial note, the definitions of "intended parents" were struck out with effect from 11 January 2022 by the Court's completing judgment, and the Ministry of Health issued implementation guidance in January 2022. [Wikisource note](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסכמים_לנשיאת_עוברים_(אישור_הסכם_ומעמד_היילוד)) (⚠️ the judgment itself on the Judiciary's site was not reached).

### The Family Court's role

Paternity and maternity claims, adoption, and proceedings under the Surrogacy Law and the Egg Donation Law are family matters (Family Court Law s. 1(4), (6)(ח), (6)(י), (8)). Adoption hearings are closed and the procedure regulations (regs 60–96) set out the steps, including social workers' reports and intercountry adoption.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 10280/01 ירוס-חקק נ׳ היועץ המשפטי לממשלה (10.1.2005)

Held that s. 25 lets a court approve the adoption of a partner's child by a single adopter in a same-sex couple, where it serves the child's best interest. Why it matters: it opened second-parent adoption as a general route without changing the marriage-based rule in s. 3. [Cardozo](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/yaros-hakak-v-attorney-general)

### בג״ץ 566/11 ממט-מגד נ׳ משרד הפנים (January 2014)

The Supreme Court decided for the first time how Israeli law should treat the registration and status of children born through surrogacy abroad, holding that the parent who is not genetically related should establish parenthood through a judicial process (adoption or a parentage order) rather than through registration alone. Why it matters: it set the path that families with foreign surrogacy follow: genetic parenthood is proved (often by a court-approved DNA test) and the other parent applies to the Family Court. Description as summarised in [Hebrew Wikipedia: פונדקאות](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פונדקאות) (⚠️ secondary; check the full text before relying on details).

### בג״ץ 781/15 ארד-פנקס (completing judgment in force from 11.1.2022)

Petition by a male couple (2015, following an earlier petition from 2010) against the exclusion of same-sex couples and single men from surrogacy in Israel. The Court held the exclusion unconstitutional and, after giving the Knesset time, struck the restrictive definitions (in force from 11.1.2022 per Wikisource). Why it matters: since 2022 same-sex couples and single men may apply to the approvals committee. Religious parties criticised the ruling as judicial legislation in a matter of family values; LGBTQ organisations saw it as overdue equality. Sources: [Wikisource note](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הסכמים_לנשיאת_עוברים_(אישור_הסכם_ומעמד_היילוד)), [Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פונדקאות) (⚠️ secondary).

## How it works in practice

### Which route establishes parenthood

| Situation | Route | Forum |
|---|---|---|
| Unmarried father wants recognition | Acknowledgment of paternity, or paternity claim (often with a genetic test) | Family Court |
| Step-parent wants to adopt spouse's child | Adoption under s. 3(1) with the other parent's consent or an adoptability declaration | Family Court |
| Same-sex partner of the birth mother | Second-parent adoption (Yaros-Hakak) | Family Court |
| Surrogacy in Israel | Approved agreement, then parentage order | Approvals committee, then Family Court |
| Surrogacy abroad | Genetic parenthood proved; adoption or order for the other parent (Mamet-Meged) | Family Court; Population Registry after the order |
| Child in care, parents unable to care | Adoptability declaration on the Attorney General's request | Family Court |

### Timelines and costs

Adoption through the Child Adoption Service involves eligibility checks and waiting periods measured in years. Surrogacy in Israel requires medical and psychological opinions, a committee hearing, and a parentage order after the birth. Family Court fees apply to private applications; adoption proceedings initiated by the state are fee-free for the state. Check [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md) and the Ministry of Welfare and Ministry of Health sites (⚠️ manual check).

## Worked example

Yonatan and Amir, a male couple from Haifa, and Salam, a single woman from Shfar'am, each want to become parents.

1. **Yonatan and Amir.** Since 2022 they can apply to the approvals committee for surrogacy in Israel: residents; a surrogate meeting s. 5(א)(1א); one of them the genetic father (s. 2(2)); a donor egg. After the birth they apply for a parentage order. If instead they use surrogacy abroad, the genetic father proves paternity and the other applies for an adoption or parentage order.
2. **Salam.** As a single woman she can apply as a single intended mother, using her own egg (s. 2(2)), or pursue fertility treatment with donor sperm. Adoption by a single person is limited by s. 3; the court may use s. 25 in special circumstances, but the Child Adoption Service decides eligibility first.
3. **Religion.** The surrogate must be of the intended mother's religion (s. 5(א)(1ד)); where all parties are not Jewish, the committee may depart from this on the cleric's opinion. For a male couple without an intended mother, check how the committee applies this condition after 2022 (⚠️ check current guidance).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. Acknowledgment of paternity, applications for parentage orders and second-parent adoption can be filed in your own name, and many families do so with help from support organisations. Surrogacy approval and intercountry adoption involve formal applications, medical and psychological documents and contracts; most families use lawyers and agencies. A lawyer is strongly advised wherever another parent's consent is missing or an adoptability declaration is sought.

### Where and how

- Family Court for all parentage, adoption and parentage-order proceedings.
- Ministry of Health approvals committee for surrogacy in Israel; Child Adoption Service (Welfare Ministry) for adoption eligibility (⚠️ manual check of current procedures on gov.il).
- Free help: LGBTQ family organisations, single-parent organisations, Kol-Zchut (⚠️ manual check); [where to get help](../../references/where-to-get-help.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The surrogacy is abroad, or the surrogate or donor is foreign.
- A biological parent opposes the adoption.
- There is a dispute between intended parents or with the surrogate.
- Citizenship or entry of the child depends on the order.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relying on a foreign birth certificate for the non-genetic parent | No legal parenthood in Israel | Apply for adoption or a parentage order | Plan the Israeli court step before the birth |
| Starting treatment before committee approval | The procedure is unlawful in Israel (s. 2) | Stop and apply | Get approval first |
| Delaying second-parent adoption | The child has no legal tie to one parent if the other dies | File promptly | File soon after the birth |
| Assuming any couple may adopt jointly | Application rejected under s. 3 | Use s. 25 or the relevant route | Check eligibility with the Child Adoption Service |

## Review

- Adoption is decided by the child's best interest (s. 1); joint adoption is by a married couple, with exceptions and a court discretion under s. 25 used for second-parent adoption.
- Without parental consent, a child can be declared adoptable only on statutory grounds on the Attorney General's request (s. 13).
- Surrogacy in Israel requires an agreement approved by the approvals committee under strict conditions (ss. 2–5) and a parentage order after birth; since 2022 same-sex couples and single men may apply (HCJ 781/15).
- For foreign surrogacy, the non-genetic parent establishes parenthood through the Family Court (Mamet-Meged).
- These routes balance religious, feminist, equality and child-welfare concerns; details change often, so check current guidance.
