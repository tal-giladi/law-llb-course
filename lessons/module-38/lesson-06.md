# 38.6 · Substantive law review III: criminal law, Basic Laws and professional ethics

This lesson reviews three substantive subjects with very different textures: the general part of the Penal Law and the main offences and sentencing rules; the Basic Laws, their limitation clause, institutions and the procedure for challenging state action; and the professional ethics of lawyers. Compact tables point to the teaching lessons; five worked items and a timed drill follow.

## Why it matters

Reg. 18א(ב) lists "criminal law, including sentencing", "Basic Laws" and "professional ethics relating to lawyers"; the procedural aspects of the Basic Laws sit on the procedural paper (reg. 18א(א)(5); lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md)). The criminal general part is a closed system of definitions in which one word changes the answer ("מטרה", "אדישות", "פשע"). The Basic Laws combine short texts with long case law and an ongoing public dispute, which this course presents from each side (plan §9). Ethics is the subject candidates underrate: its rules are specific, examinable and directly relevant to every client.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 6821/93 בנק המזרחי המאוחד בע״מ נ׳ מגדל כפר שיתופי (1995)

The Court held that the 1992 Basic Laws on rights are superior to ordinary laws, and that an ordinary law infringing a protected right without meeting the limitation clause may be declared void; on the facts the challenged amendment met the clause and stood. Bar point: **supremacy plus judicial review**, and the four conditions of the limitation clause. The justices differed on the theory (constituent authority), a difference that still shapes the debate. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/391)

### בג״ץ 5658/23 התנועה למען איכות השלטון בישראל נ׳ הכנסת (1.1.2024)

All fifteen justices sat. Twelve held that the Court may review Basic Laws in extreme cases; eight voided the 2023 amendment barring reasonableness review of Government and ministerial decisions. The seven who did not void it took three different positions: a narrow reading leaving review of extreme decisions, a finding that the harm did not reach democracy's core, or no power to review Basic Laws at all. Supporters of the ruling stress the protection of the separation of powers and the rule of law; critics argue the Court lacks authority to review Basic Laws and that the Knesset, as constituent authority, has the last word. Bar point: the **first voiding of a Basic Law provision**; reasonableness review of ministers continues. [Official abstract](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/movement-quality-government-v-knesset); lesson [06.6](../module-06/lesson-06.md).

### ע״פ 746/14 ימר נ׳ מדינת ישראל (2016)

The Supreme Court rejected self-defence and putative self-defence for a man who killed his abuser, because immediacy and necessity were missing, but accepted cumulative provocation and reduced the conviction. Bar point: **each condition of s. 34י is separate**; mitigation of a killing now lives in s. 301ב. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/400)

### רע״א 9930/17 אל מדאמין בע״מ נ׳ חנא (28.3.2018)

A civil court has power to rule on an alleged ethics breach (here a lawyer acting against a former client) only where it may harm a party's legitimate interests in the case or the fairness of the proceeding; the Bar's institutions are the natural forum. Bar point: **disqualification by a court is the exception**, linked to rule 16. [Text hosted by Reichman University](https://www.colman.ac.il/media/f3yhrn00/9930-17_al_madamin_ltd_v_hana_1.doc)

## How it works in practice

### Criminal questions: the order of analysis

1. Classify the offence by its maximum penalty (s. 24): it decides negligence (s. 21), conspiracy (s. 499), complicity (s. 34ג) and limitation.
2. Facts: conduct, circumstances, result (s. 18).
3. Mental element: what the offence requires and what the accused was aware of (ss. 19–22).
4. Extensions: attempt, complicity, conspiracy.
5. Defences, with the reasonable-doubt rule (s. 34כב(ב)).
6. Sentence: range, then position in it.

### Basic Law questions: the three stages

Infringement of a protected right (challenger's burden) → limitation clause (State's burden) → remedy. Most wrong answers skip a stage, for example jumping to proportionality without asking whether the measure is "by law or under express authorisation".

### Ethics questions: who is the client, what is the risk?

Name the client, the other parties, and what the lawyer knows. Then ask: conflict (r. 14), former client (r. 16), confidentiality (r. 19), money (rr. 39–42, s. 88), fees (ss. 84–87), advertising (s. 55–56). The test in r. 14 is a *risk*, not proven harm.

## Worked example

**Item 1.** Eyal drives at high speed through a crowded market, hoping no one is hurt but believing he can avoid people; he kills a pedestrian. Which mental element fits best?

- (A) Intent.
- (B) Indifference.
- (C) Rashness.
- (D) Negligence.

*Reasoning:* Rashness is taking an unreasonable risk of the result while hoping to prevent it (s. 20(א)(2)(ב)); indifference is equanimity towards it. He was aware of the risk, so not negligence. **Answer: C.** The offence is killing by rashness (s. 301ג).

**Item 2.** Rina lends her car to a friend who says he will use it to break into a shop that night; he does. Rina's liability?

- (A) Accessory: she acted before the offence to enable it.
- (B) Joint principal: she took part in the plan.
- (C) None: she did nothing at the scene.
- (D) Accessory after the fact.

*Reasoning:* An accessory acts before or during the offence to enable or ease it (s. 31); joint principals do acts towards the offence as part of its commission (s. 29(ב)). Lending the tool knowingly is classic aiding. **Answer: A.** The penalty is half the maximum (s. 32).

**Item 3.** A minister, without any statutory provision, issues a directive barring members of a certain profession from working at night. Which limitation-clause condition fails first?

- (A) Proper purpose.
- (B) Least harmful means.
- (C) Proportionality in the narrow sense.
- (D) "By law or under express authorisation in it".

*Reasoning:* Freedom of occupation may be infringed only by a law or under express authorisation in a law (Freedom of Occupation s. 4); residual or general powers do not authorise infringing rights (Basic Law: The Government s. 32; lesson 06.5). **Answer: D.**

**Item 4.** A lawyer represented a buyer in an apartment purchase and drafted the contract. A year later the seller asks him to sue the buyer to cancel the contract on the ground that the contract is void. May he?

- (A) Yes, with the seller's written consent.
- (B) No: he may not act against a former client in the same matter, nor argue that a document he drafted is invalid.
- (C) Yes, if a year has passed.
- (D) Yes, if he did not receive confidential information.

*Reasoning:* Rule 16 bars acting against a client (including a former one) in the same matter; rule 15 bars arguing that a document he drafted is invalid. **Answer: B.**

**Item 5.** A lawyer agrees with a client accused of fraud that she will be paid ₪50,000 if he is acquitted and nothing otherwise. Lawful?

- (A) Yes, success fees are lawful.
- (B) Yes, with written consent.
- (C) No: a fee dependent on the result is forbidden in a criminal case.
- (D) No, unless the Bar approves.

*Reasoning:* Bar Association Law s. 84(א) forbids a fee dependent on the result in a criminal case; s. 84(ב) allows it in non-criminal cases, subject to the Bar's power to reduce an excessive fee. **Answer: C.**

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Partly. You may file a police complaint or a private criminal complaint as a victim (lessons [05.7](../module-05/lesson-07.md), [05.8](../module-05/lesson-08.md)), a self-represented HCJ or administrative petition (lessons [06.8](../module-06/lesson-08.md), [14.11](../module-14/lesson-11.md)), and an ethics complaint (lesson [35.8](../module-35/lesson-08.md)). As a suspect or defendant, see lesson [38.3](lesson-03.md): the law protects you with appointed counsel in serious cases.

> [!WARNING]
> Criminal law questions in this lesson are exam practice. If you are suspected of an offence, do not use them to decide what to say to the police; consult a lawyer or ask for the Public Defender.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You are a suspect or defendant in anything above a minor offence.
- Your petition challenges a statute or a Government decision with wide effect: threshold doctrines and costs to the State are real risks.
- Your lawyer holds your money or documents and the three-month lien period is running.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classifying by the sentence given, not the maximum | Wrong rules on negligence, conspiracy, limitation | Re-read s. 24 | Always look up the maximum |
| Treating rashness as indifference | Wrong offence (s. 301ג vs s. 300) | Ask: did he hope to avoid the result? | Learn the two definitions word for word |
| Skipping "by law or under express authorisation" | Wrong limitation-clause answer | Check the legal basis first | Run the three stages in order |
| Assuming a single paid act is not "occupation" | Unauthorised practice (s. 20) | Stop; refer to a lawyer | Remember: occupation **or** consideration |
| A criminal success fee | Forbidden (s. 84(א)) | Replace with a fixed or hourly fee | Separate criminal and civil fee models |

## Review

- Classify by the maximum penalty; intent is the default mental element; negligence never for felonies.
- Complicity and attempt rules do not reach contraventions; voluntary withdrawal exempts from attempt.
- Self-defence needs an unlawful attack, real danger, immediacy, necessity and reasonableness; mistake of law rarely helps.
- Basic Laws: infringement, then the four-part limitation clause, then remedy; review of statutes since Bank Mizrahi, and of a Basic Law provision for the first time in HCJ 5658/23, a ruling still disputed.
- Ethics: reserved acts by occupation or consideration; conflict on a mere risk; no criminal success fees; client money in trust; s. 88 lien with a three-month window.
