# 38.8 · Practice lab: a full mock exam

This practice lab is a complete mock of the Bar exam in the format reported for recent sittings: a 45-minute writing task, a 40-question procedural paper in 100 minutes, and a 40-question substantive paper in 160 minutes. Every question is new, every answer key cites the section it rests on and the lesson that teaches it, and a marking sheet turns your result into a weighted score and a revision plan.

## Why it matters

Knowing the rules (lessons [38.2](lesson-02.md)–[38.6](lesson-06.md)) and producing them for five hours under the clock are different things. A full mock shows your stamina, your pacing and the subjects that still leak points, while there is time to fix them. Remember the limits: the mock is a self-test of the format described in lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md); the binding format of any real sitting is the committee's notice (reg. 18(ד)), and only a person with a recognised LL.B. and a completed internship may sit the real exam (lesson [35.1](../module-35/lesson-01.md)).

## The law

The rules for the real exam are in lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md): three parts (reg. 18(א)), at least 40% for each questionnaire and 15% for the writing task (reg. 18(ג)), the subjects of reg. 18א, and a pass mark of 60% of the total grade (reg. 18ג), lowered by temporary order for specific sittings. This mock uses the weights reported for recent sittings (writing task 20%, each questionnaire 40%; Raisman, ⚠️ unofficial). Each answer below states the section it rests on; on the real substantive paper you would have the statutes in a booklet, so practise Part C with the statutes open on Wikisource.

## Leading cases

### Key text: the answer keys below

This lab has no new ruling. The cases you need were briefed in lessons 38.2–38.6 (Amir, Hotels.com, Haniyon Haifa, Yissacharov, Peretz, Apropim, Kal Binyan, Malul, Ganz, Aharonov, Tal Trading, Bank Mizrahi, HCJ 5658/23, Yamar, Al Madamin). Several mock questions test them; the answer key names the case where it decides the answer.

## How it works in practice

### Exam-day rules for the mock

| Part | Time | Questions | Weight | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: writing task | 45 minutes | 1 | 20% | None |
| Break | 45 minutes | | | |
| B: procedural | 100 minutes | 40 | 40% | None |
| Break | 80 minutes | | | |
| C: substantive | 160 minutes | 40 | 40% | Statutes open (Wikisource), no notes |

Sit it in one day if you can, in the order above, with breaks as reported for recent sittings. Use paper, mark your answers on a separate sheet, and do not open any answer until the whole part is finished.

### Scoring

- Part B: correct answers ÷ 40 × 40.
- Part C: correct answers ÷ 40 × 40.
- Part A: mark yourself 0–20 with the rubric in lesson [38.7](lesson-07.md) (content 0–8, organisation 0–6, language 0–6).
- Total out of 100. Compare with 60 (reg. 18ג).

## Worked example

Daniel sits the mock on a Friday. Results: writing task 13/20; procedural 27/40 (27 points); substantive 29/40 (29 points); total 69. He passes the mock, but his error log shows 6 of his 13 procedural errors in criminal procedure and execution, and 4 of his 11 substantive errors in real-estate taxation and succession. He also finished Part B with four questions unanswered.

His plan: re-sit the drills of lessons 38.3 and 38.5 within a week, rewrite his "numbers by stage" card, and in his next mock set a checkpoint every 10 questions in Part B (25 minutes each). Two weeks later, on the second sitting of this mock (with the answers forgotten as far as possible, or with the drills instead), he finishes Part B with five minutes to spare.

## Do it yourself

### You need

- About six hours on one day, a quiet room, a timer, paper.
- For Part C, the statutes on Wikisource open in a browser (no notes, no course pages).
- The [module project](../../projects/m38-bar-review-file.md) error log.

### Steps

1. Part A: the writing task below, 45 minutes.
2. Break, then Part B: questions P1–P40, 100 minutes.
3. Break, then Part C: questions S1–S40, 160 minutes.
4. Mark with the keys; compute the weighted score.
5. Log every error by subject, rule and lesson; plan your revision by the subjects with most errors.

### Part A: writing task (45 minutes, 20%)

Omri Ben-David (ID 0XXXXXXXX), of 8 HaGefen Street, Modi'in, was sued in the Rishon LeZion Magistrates' Court by נוי שיווק בע״מ for ₪26,000, an alleged unpaid invoice for office furniture. The statement of claim was sent by registered mail to an address in Rishon LeZion where Omri has not lived for two years; the Population Registry shows his Modi'in address. No defence was filed and on 1.9.2026 the court gave a judgment in his absence. Omri learned of it only on 5.10.2026, when he was served with an Execution Office warning at his Modi'in home. He has a signed delivery note showing that he returned the furniture to the company on the day it arrived because it was damaged, and an email from the company's manager confirming the return. Today is 12.10.2026.

Draft Omri's motion to set aside the judgment (he acts for himself). Use placeholders for unknown details.

### Part B: procedural law (40 questions, 100 minutes)

**P1.** An employee files a claim for unpaid severance pay against her former employer in the magistrates' court. The amount is within that court's ceiling. What is correct?

- (A) The magistrates' court hears it, because the amount is within its ceiling.
- (B) The small claims court must hear it, because she is an individual.
- (C) The magistrates' court has no jurisdiction; the regional labour court does, and the case may be transferred.
- (D) The district court hears it, because labour matters are of public importance.

<details><summary>Answer P1</summary>

**C.** Claims between employee and employer arising from employment are in the regional labour court's exclusive jurisdiction (Labour Court Law s. 24(א)(1)); a court without jurisdiction may transfer the case (Courts Law s. 79). Lessons 01.4, 15.1, 17.9.

</details>

**P2.** A contractor sues a developer for ₪10 million for unpaid construction work. Which court has subject-matter jurisdiction?

- (A) The district court.
- (B) The magistrates' court, because the claim is contractual.
- (C) The administrative affairs court, because the developer won a tender.
- (D) The magistrates' court, if the parties agree in writing.

<details><summary>Answer P2</summary>

**A.** The sum is above the magistrates' court's ceiling (Courts Law s. 51(א)(2)), so the district court hears it (s. 40(1)); consent cannot create subject-matter jurisdiction (Amir). Lesson 15.1.

</details>

**P3.** Two brothers dispute who owns a plot worth ₪400,000. One sues for a declaration that he is the sole owner. Which court?

- (A) The magistrates' court, because the value is low.
- (B) The Family Court, because the parties are brothers.
- (C) The condominium superintendent.
- (D) The district court, because a claim concerning land other than possession or use falls outside the magistrates' court.

<details><summary>Answer P3</summary>

**D.** The magistrates' court hears civil claims "למעט תביעות הנוגעות למקרקעין" up to its ceiling, and claims about possession or use of land (s. 51(א)(2)–(3)); an ownership claim goes to the district court (s. 40(1)). Lessons 01.4, 15.1.

</details>

**P4.** A Muslim mother, an Israeli resident, claims child maintenance from the father. May she file in the Family Court?

- (A) No, the Sharia court has exclusive jurisdiction over all family matters of Muslims.
- (B) Yes, the Family Court may hear Muslims' family matters other than marriage and divorce.
- (C) No, maintenance claims go to the magistrates' court.
- (D) Only if the father consents in writing.

<details><summary>Answer P4</summary>

**B.** Since the 2001 amendment, Family Court Law s. 3(ב1) allows the Family Court to hear the family matters of Muslims and Christians other than marriage and divorce. Lessons 01.4, 10.4.

</details>

**P5.** A losing bidder wants to challenge a municipality's tender decision. Where should the petition be filed?

- (A) The administrative affairs court, since tenders are in the First Schedule.
- (B) The magistrates' court, as a civil claim for damages.
- (C) The High Court of Justice, because it reviews all public authorities.
- (D) The State Comptroller, who decides tender disputes.

<details><summary>Answer P5</summary>

**A.** Tenders are a First Schedule matter (Administrative Affairs Courts Law s. 5(1); item 5); the HCJ is residual (Basic Law: The Judiciary s. 15(ג)) and will refer the petitioner to the alternative forum. Lessons 06.8, 14.9.

</details>

**P6.** In a registered condominium, an owner refuses to pay his share of a lift repair approved under the bylaws. Who decides the dispute?

- (A) The small claims court only.
- (B) The magistrates' court only.
- (C) The condominium superintendent.
- (D) The district court, because common property is land.

<details><summary>Answer P6</summary>

**C.** Disputes between owners about rights and duties under the bylaws and the expense sections (including s. 58) go to the superintendent (Land Law s. 72(א)). Lesson 13.6.

</details>

**P7.** Yossi, 80, is sued in small claims. His daughter, who is not a lawyer, wants to represent him for free. What is correct?

- (A) She may not: only lawyers represent others in court.
- (B) She may, without any approval, because she is a relative.
- (C) She may only if the court finds special reasons for a lawyer to be absent.
- (D) She may, with the court's approval and a filed power of attorney, since she does not represent for pay or as a business.

<details><summary>Answer P7</summary>

**D.** In small claims a non-lawyer may represent a party with the court's approval and a power of attorney, unless he represents regularly, in the course of business or for pay (Courts Law s. 63). Lesson 08.3.

</details>

**P8.** After a road collision damaging only his car, Avi sued the other driver for the repair costs and won. He now sues the same driver for the cost of a rental car during the repairs. What is the likely result?

- (A) The new claim proceeds, because it is a different head of damage.
- (B) The new claim is barred: a plaintiff must claim the full remedy for a cause of action.
- (C) The new claim proceeds in small claims only.
- (D) The new claim proceeds if filed within seven years.

<details><summary>Answer P8</summary>

**B.** A plaintiff must include the full remedy for the cause of action and cannot later sue for the part not claimed (CPR reg. 25(א)); splitting needs the court's leave. Lesson 15.1.

</details>

**P9.** A defendant is served with a claim for ₪120,000 in the magistrates' court (ordinary track, not medical negligence). Within what period must the defence be filed?

- (A) 60 days from service.
- (B) 30 days from service.
- (C) 45 days from service.
- (D) 14 days from service.

<details><summary>Answer P9</summary>

**A.** CPR reg. 9(ב): 60 days on the ordinary track (120 in medical-negligence claims); a claim above the fast-track ceiling is not on the fast track (reg. 78). Lesson 08.2; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

</details>

**P10.** A defendant is served with a ₪50,000 contract claim in the magistrates' court. Within what period must the defence be filed?

- (A) 60 days, as in every magistrates' court claim.
- (B) 30 days, as in small claims.
- (C) 14 days, because it is a money claim.
- (D) 45 days, because the claim runs on the fast track.

<details><summary>Answer P10</summary>

**D.** Claims up to the fast-track ceiling run on the fast track (reg. 78(א)), with a 45-day defence period (reg. 80(א)). Lesson 15.6; [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

</details>

**P11.** In a magistrates' court claim, the plaintiff sends the defendant an interrogatory with 40 questions. The defendant objects. What is the rule?

- (A) There is no cap; relevance is the only test.
- (B) The cap is 25 questions including sub-questions in this case.
- (C) The cap is 50 questions in every court.
- (D) Interrogatories are not allowed in the magistrates' court.

<details><summary>Answer P11</summary>

**B.** Reg. 56(ב): 25 questions including sub-questions, or 50 in large district-court money claims and district-court personal-injury and road-accident claims. Lesson 15.4.

</details>

**P12.** A defendant did not list in its disclosure affidavit an email that supports its version. At trial it wants to submit it. What is the rule?

- (A) It may submit it freely; disclosure rules bind only the plaintiff.
- (B) It may submit it, but must pay a fee.
- (C) It may not use it without leave, given only for a reasonable excuse.
- (D) The court must strike out the defence at once.

<details><summary>Answer P12</summary>

**C.** A party that did not disclose a document may not use it as its own evidence without leave, given only for a reasonable excuse (reg. 60(ג)); strike-out is reserved for a hidden document that harms the hiding party, or special cases. Lesson 15.4.

</details>

**P13.** A neighbour plans to cut down a shared tree tomorrow morning. Ruth applies today for a temporary injunction without notice to him. What must she show to get it ex parte?

- (A) Special reasons and a reasonable fear that delay or notice would defeat the order or cause serious harm.
- (B) Only a prima facie cause of action.
- (C) That the neighbour has a criminal record.
- (D) That she has already filed a full statement of claim.

<details><summary>Answer P13</summary>

**A.** The rule is an inter partes hearing; an ex parte temporary order needs special reasons and a reasonable fear that the delay or notice would defeat the order or cause serious harm (reg. 97(א)). A pre-claim order requires filing the claim within 7 days (reg. 95(ג)). Lesson 15.5.

</details>

**P14.** The court grants an interim injunction conditioned on a ₪20,000 bank guarantee. The applicant has signed the undertaking but not yet deposited the guarantee. Is the injunction in force?

- (A) Yes, from the moment it was signed by the judge.
- (B) Yes, because the undertaking is enough.
- (C) No, it takes effect only when all the security is deposited.
- (D) No, it lapses permanently.

<details><summary>Answer P14</summary>

**C.** Every interim order is conditional on an undertaking and on security; it takes effect only when all security is deposited (reg. 96). Lesson 15.5.

</details>

**P15.** The plaintiff filed his witness's affidavit in lieu of testimony. The witness does not come to the hearing for cross-examination, and no special leave is given. What is the status of the affidavit?

- (A) It is evidence if the other side did not object in writing.
- (B) It is evidence with reduced weight.
- (C) It is evidence if notarised.
- (D) It is not evidence.

<details><summary>Answer P15</summary>

**D.** An affidavit in lieu of testimony counts as evidence only if the deponent came for cross-examination (reg. 178(ב)). Lesson 15.7.

</details>

**P16.** At the trial the plaintiff appears and the defendant, who filed a defence, does not, without excuse. What may the court do?

- (A) Strike out the claim.
- (B) Let the plaintiff prove his claim and grant the relief the evidence supports.
- (C) Adjourn automatically.
- (D) Give judgment for the full sum without any evidence.

<details><summary>Answer P16</summary>

**B.** reg. 75(2). An absent plaintiff's claim is struck out (reg. 75(1)). Lesson 15.7.

</details>

**P17.** During a magistrates' court case the judge orders the defendant to disclose a specific internal report. The defendant wants to challenge the order now. What is the route?

- (A) An application for leave to appeal to the district court, granted only on the s. 52(ב) test.
- (B) Appeal as of right within 60 days.
- (C) A petition to the High Court of Justice.
- (D) No route until the final judgment, ever.

<details><summary>Answer P17</summary>

**A.** Decisions other than a judgment are appealable only by leave of a district court judge, given where waiting for the final appeal would substantially affect the parties' rights, cause real harm or lead to an unnecessary or wrong proceeding (Courts Law s. 52(ב)); decisions on disclosure of a particular document are generally not within the no-leave list (lesson 15.4). Lesson 15.8.

</details>

**P18.** A defendant lost in the magistrates' court and filed an appeal. The plaintiff opens an execution file on the judgment. The defendant says the appeal stops execution. Is he right?

- (A) Yes, filing an appeal stays execution automatically.
- (B) Yes, if the appeal was filed within 60 days.
- (C) No, but the registrar must wait 60 days.
- (D) No, an appeal does not stay execution; he must apply for a stay.

<details><summary>Answer P18</summary>

**D.** "הגשת ערעור לא תעכב את ביצוע ההחלטה" (reg. 145(א)); a stay is requested from the lower court before the appeal or from the appeal court after it, usually against security. Lesson 15.8.

</details>

**P19.** In a court-referred mediation, the defendant's manager said, "We knew the product was defective." The mediation failed. Can the plaintiff prove the statement at the trial?

- (A) Yes, as an admission of a party.
- (B) Yes, if the mediator testifies.
- (C) No, statements made in mediation are not evidence in civil proceedings.
- (D) No, unless the court finds special reasons.

<details><summary>Answer P19</summary>

**C.** Courts Law s. 79ג(ד). Lessons 08.6, 23.2.

</details>

**P20.** Police stop a man near the scene of a burglary, on reasonable suspicion, to establish his identity and question him. They keep him at the scene for four hours. Only he was involved. What is correct?

- (A) Lawful, because detention may last up to 24 hours.
- (B) Unlawful: detention may not exceed three hours (six only where many people are involved).
- (C) Lawful, if he was told the reason.
- (D) Unlawful only if no burglary took place.

<details><summary>Answer P20</summary>

**B.** Arrests Law s. 73(ב): no more than three hours, extendable by three only where many people are involved. Lesson 24.2.

</details>

**P21.** A suspect in a misdemeanour (not a felony) complains that his file went to the prosecution without a notice inviting him to argue against indictment. What is the legal position?

- (A) The indictment is void without such a notice.
- (B) He may petition the HCJ, which always cancels such indictments.
- (C) The statutory notice applies to felonies; in a misdemeanour he may still write to the prosecution, at its discretion.
- (D) The prosecution must hold an oral hearing in every case.

<details><summary>Answer P21</summary>

- (D) He is exempt under s. 49ב(5), and it does not use up his own single-apartment exemption.

<details><summary>Answer S31</summary>

**D.** RETL s. 49ב(5): the seller is a descendant, the deceased owned one apartment and would have been exempt. Lesson 25.7.

</details>

**S32.** A minister's regulation, approved by the Knesset committee, makes breaching it punishable by one year's imprisonment. What is the legal problem?

- (A) Regulations may not create offences at all.
- (B) Only the President may set penalties.
- (C) Imprisonment set in a regulation may not exceed six months.
- (D) There is none, since the committee approved it.

<details><summary>Answer S32</summary>

**C.** Penal Law s. 2(א). Lesson 05.1.

</details>

**S33.** After Noam committed an offence but before final judgment, the Knesset reduced its maximum penalty. Which law applies?

- (A) The more lenient law, since final judgment had not yet been given.
- (B) The law at the time of the indictment.
- (C) The law at the time of the offence.
- (D) Whichever the prosecution chooses.

<details><summary>Answer S33</summary>

**A.** Penal Law s. 5(א). Lesson 05.1.

</details>

**S34.** Avner bought a new laptop for a quarter of its price from a stranger in a car park. He suspected it was stolen but chose not to ask. Is he aware of the circumstance "stolen" for a receiving offence?

- (A) No, he did not know for certain.
- (B) No, unless he admits he knew.
- (C) Yes, a person who suspected and chose not to check is treated as aware.
- (D) Only if the price was below a statutory threshold.

<details><summary>Answer S34</summary>

**C.** Wilful blindness: Penal Law s. 20(ג)(1). Lesson 05.2.

</details>

**S35.** Armed robbers order a taxi driver at gunpoint to drive them from the bank. He does. Which exemption fits best?

- (A) Necessity arising from a natural danger.
- (B) Self-defence.
- (C) De minimis.
- (D) Duress: an act he was ordered to do under a threat of serious harm.

<details><summary>Answer S35</summary>

**D.** Penal Law s. 34יב; all such exemptions fail if the act was not reasonable (s. 34טז). Lesson 05.4.

</details>

**S36.** A statute limits demonstrations near hospitals to protect patients. The court finds that a rule limiting only noise levels would protect patients equally with less harm. Which stage does the statute fail?

- (A) Proper purpose.
- (B) Least harmful means.
- (C) Rational connection.
- (D) Values of the State.

<details><summary>Answer S36</summary>

**B.** The second proportionality sub-test asks whether another means achieves the purpose to the same degree with less harm (Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty s. 8 as interpreted; lesson 06.3).

</details>

**S37.** The President signs a pardon. What else is required for it to be valid?

- (A) The countersignature of the Prime Minister or another minister.
- (B) Knesset approval.
- (C) Nothing; the pardon power is personal and absolute.
- (D) A Supreme Court ruling.

<details><summary>Answer S37</summary>

**A.** Basic Law: The President of the State s. 12: the President's signature on an official document requires a minister's countersignature, except for documents relating to forming a government or dissolving the Knesset. Lesson 06.5.

</details>

**S38.** In a dispute, lawyer Tali knows the other side is represented. She calls the other side directly to propose a settlement. What is correct?

- (A) Allowed, if the proposal is fair.
- (B) Allowed, if she records the call.
- (C) A breach: she must approach a represented party only through that party's lawyer.
- (D) Allowed in civil matters only.

<details><summary>Answer S38</summary>

**C.** Professional Conduct Rules r. 23(א)(1). Lesson 35.3.

</details>

**S39.** A client's power of attorney allows "transfer to another lawyer". The lawyer passes the whole file to a colleague without asking the client. What is correct?

- (A) Allowed, because the power of attorney says so.
- (B) Allowed, if the colleague is in the same firm.
- (C) Allowed, if the client is told within 30 days.
- (D) A breach: transfer needs the client's separate, express written consent.

<details><summary>Answer S39</summary>

**D.** Professional Conduct Rules r. 18; the lawyer may delegate tasks or use another lawyer's help. Lesson 35.5.

</details>

**S40.** A lawyer charged "₪15,000, all inclusive" without separating fee and expenses. The client refuses to pay anything. What is the consequence of the breach of s. 85?

- (A) The lawyer loses all fees.
- (B) The lawyer is still entitled to a reasonable fee; the client may recover any excess paid above what is due.
- (C) The agreement is valid; s. 85 is a recommendation.
- (D) The client must pay double expenses.

<details><summary>Answer S40</summary>

**B.** Bar Association Law ss. 85–87: a breach does not deprive the lawyer of a reasonable fee (s. 86), but the client may recover what he paid beyond what is due (s. 87). Lesson 35.4.

</details>

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes: this is self-study. The mock gives no qualification. Sitting the real exam requires a recognised LL.B. and a completed internship (lesson [35.1](../module-35/lesson-01.md)). The Part A motion is the kind of document you may file in your own case; filing for others is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- Statutes for Part C: [Wikisource, the Open Law Book](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/ויקיטקסט:ספר_החוקים_הפתוח).
- Exam registration, notices and results: the Bar's official website (⚠️ manual check).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- You face a real default judgment like Omri's and the 30-day window or an execution file is running: a short consultation is worth it.
- You are a candidate whose admission or grade is disputed (lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md)).
