# 38.4 · Substantive law review I: contracts, torts and limitation

This lesson reviews the law of obligations and limitation, the two subjects that fill the largest share of the substantive paper: forming a contract, defects in consent, standard contracts, breach and remedies; negligence and the other torts, defences and the special regimes for the State, employers and road accidents; and when a claim is barred by time. Compact tables point to the lessons that teach each rule; then you work five items and a timed drill.

## Why it matters

"Obligations, including contracts and torts" heads the substantive list in reg. 18א(ב), and limitation has its own line in it (lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md)). On the substantive paper you get the statutes, so the questions are about applying them: which section, which condition is missing, what remedy follows, and how the courts read the open words ("good faith", "reasonable time", "foreseeable"). These are also the rules you use most in your own affairs: almost every dispute in modules 3, 4, 12 and 20 runs through them.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 4628/93 מדינת ישראל נ׳ אפרופים שיכון ויזום (1991) בע״מ (1995)

The majority held that a contract is read in light of its purpose and context even where the words seem clear, rejecting a two-stage approach that looks at the circumstances only after finding ambiguity. Bar point: a question on s. 25 may turn on whether you read the text alone or with the circumstances; the Knesset has since amended s. 25 (lesson [03.7](../module-03/lesson-07.md)), so read the section in the booklet together with the case. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/360)

### ע״א 6370/00 קל בנין בע״מ נ׳ ע.ר.מ. רעננה לבניה והשכרה בע״מ (2002)

A developer negotiated to full agreement with a bidder and then signed with a contractor who never bid. The Supreme Court held that damages for bad-faith negotiation under s. 12 may, where the contract would otherwise have been concluded, cover the expected profit, not only wasted costs. Bar point: s. 12 gives **damages only**, but their measure can reach expectation. [English translation (portions)](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/219)

### דנ״א 4693/05 בית חולים כרמל חיפה נ׳ מלול (29.8.2010)

A further hearing of nine justices held, five to four, that causation is proved on the balance of probabilities, all or nothing; proportional liability is allowed only in a narrow class of "recurring distortion" cases. Bar point: a single plaintiff with a below-50% chance that the fault caused the harm recovers nothing. [English summary](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/carmel-haifa-hospital-v-malul-summary)

## How it works in practice

### Contract questions: the five-step path

1. **Is there a contract?** Offer, acceptance, certainty, form (ss. 2–11, Land Law s. 8).
2. **Is it valid?** Defects (ss. 14–18), illegality (s. 30), standard-contract terms.
3. **What does it say?** Interpretation (s. 25), gap-filling (s. 26), good faith (s. 39).
4. **Was it breached, and how badly?** Fundamental or not (s. 6).
5. **What remedy, and was it exercised properly?** Enforcement, cancellation with notice in a reasonable time (ss. 7–8), damages (ss. 10–15).

Wrong answers usually jump a step: they award damages for a breach of a contract that was never formed, or cancel for a non-fundamental breach without an extension.

### Tort questions: the five elements and the regime

Ask first which regime applies: road accident (exclusive, no fault), the State in combat (no liability), a public servant (immunity, the body pays), an employee (employer liable). Then run duty, breach, damage, causation, defences.

### Limitation questions: the four numbers

Every limitation question needs four facts: the period (s. 5 or a special law), the start (s. 6, s. 8, s. 89), any suspension (ss. 7–18ב) and any acknowledgment (s. 9). Count only when you have all four.

## Worked example

**Item 1.** On Sunday Avi offers to sell his car to Rina for ₪60,000, "valid until Thursday". On Tuesday he emails a withdrawal, which reaches Rina before she replies. On Wednesday she accepts. Is there a contract?

- (A) Yes, an offer with a fixed acceptance period cannot be withdrawn once received.
- (B) No, the withdrawal reached her before she accepted.
- (C) No, a car sale needs a written contract.
- (D) Yes, but Avi may cancel for mistake.

*Reasoning:* Normally a withdrawal that reaches the offeree before acceptance is sent is effective (s. 3(א)), but an offer stating a period for acceptance cannot be withdrawn after it reached the offeree (s. 3(ב)). No form is required for a car. **Answer: A.**

**Item 2.** A buyer of a flat discovers after signing that the seller hid a demolition order on an illegal extension. What may he do, and when?

- (A) Only sue for damages; deceit does not allow cancellation.
- (B) Cancel only if the court finds it just.
- (C) Cancel for deceit, by notice within a reasonable time after learning of it.
- (D) Cancel only after giving the seller an extension.

*Reasoning:* Non-disclosure of a fact the circumstances required to be disclosed is deceit (s. 15); cancellation is by notice within a reasonable time after learning of the ground (s. 20). The extension rule belongs to non-fundamental breach (Remedies Law s. 7(ב)), not to defects in formation. **Answer: C.**

**Item 3.** A contract provides ₪200,000 agreed damages for a week's delay in delivering office furniture worth ₪50,000; the buyer's foreseeable loss was small. The buyer claims the full sum.

- (A) He gets it: agreed damages need no proof.
- (B) He gets nothing: agreed damages are void.
- (C) He gets it only if he proves the loss.
- (D) The court may reduce it, since it bears no reasonable relation to the foreseeable loss.

*Reasoning:* Agreed damages are payable without proof of loss, but the court may reduce them if they bear no reasonable relation to the loss foreseeable at contracting (s. 15(א)). **Answer: D.**

**Item 4.** A municipal worker repairing a pavement left a hole unmarked; a pedestrian fell. The pedestrian sues the worker personally. What is the likely answer?

- (A) The worker is personally liable as the direct tortfeasor.
- (B) The worker has immunity for a governmental act; the claim lies against the municipality.
- (C) Neither is liable: statutory authority is a defence to negligence.
- (D) The worker is liable only if the municipality is insolvent.

*Reasoning:* A public servant is immune for acts in performing a governmental function unless done intentionally or with indifference (s. 7א(א)); the public authority remains liable (s. 7א(ב)). Statutory authority is not a defence to negligence (s. 6). **Answer: B.**

**Item 5.** A debt fell due on 1 March 2018. On 1 February 2025 the debtor writes: "I know I owe you the money; give me time." The creditor sues on 1 March 2026. Is the claim time-barred?

- (A) Yes, seven years passed on 1 March 2025.
- (B) No, because limitation applies only to land.
- (C) Yes, an acknowledgment made after six years has no effect.
- (D) No, a written acknowledgment restarted the seven years on 1 February 2025.

*Reasoning:* A written acknowledgment of the plaintiff's right, made within the period or after it, starts the period afresh from the acknowledgment (s. 9). (The s. 15א war-period suspension would also apply to periods running on 25.1.2024; you do not need it here.) **Answer: D.**

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes. Contract and tort claims of individuals are the core of self-representation in this course: demand letters, notices of breach, small claims and magistrates' court claims (lessons [03.10](../module-03/lesson-10.md), [04.8](../module-04/lesson-08.md), [12.5](../module-12/lesson-05.md)). Representing others for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- **Notice of breach and cancellation:** the [notice template](../../templates/notice-of-breach-and-cancellation.md).
- **Demand letter and claim:** [pre-action letter](../../templates/pre-action-letter.md), [small claim (contract or consumer)](../../templates/small-claim-contract-consumer.md).
- **Limitation dates:** [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md); count with the four numbers above.

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- The claim is near the end of its limitation period and the facts are complex.
- The injury is serious (bodily injury, medical negligence, road accident): the special regimes and fee caps (lesson [35.4](../module-35/lesson-04.md)) make a lawyer both useful and affordable.
- The other side relies on a standard-contract clause about forum or arbitration that you must challenge at once.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cancelling for a non-fundamental breach without an extension | Your cancellation is itself a breach | Withdraw it and send an extension | Classify the breach first (s. 6) |
| Waiting months to cancel for deceit | Notice not within a reasonable time (s. 20) | Act at once when you learn | Diarise the day you learned |
| Claiming full agreed damages without thinking | Court reduces (s. 15) | Prepare evidence of foreseeable loss | Check the reasonable-relation test |
| Suing the public servant personally | Claim against the wrong defendant | Amend to the authority | Check s. 7א first |
| Counting limitation from the wrong day | Barred claim or wasted panic | Re-count with s. 8 and s. 9 | Use the four numbers |

## Review

- Contracts: formation, validity, content, breach, remedy, in that order; cancellation needs notice in reasonable time, and for a non-fundamental breach an extension first.
- Standard contracts: depriving terms are cancelled; barring court access and exempting bodily injury or malice are void outright.
- Torts: choose the regime first (road accident, State, public servant, employer); causation on the balance of probabilities (Malul).
- Limitation is procedural, must be pleaded at once, and depends on period, start, suspension and acknowledgment.
- With the statutes in front of you, the exam tests conditions and case-law readings, not memory of section numbers.
