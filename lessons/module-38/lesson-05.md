# 38.5 · Substantive law review II: property, commercial and corporate law, real-estate taxation

This lesson reviews four substantive subjects that the exam often combines in one scenario: property in land and movables, succession, commercial law (bills of exchange and pledges) and company law, and real-estate taxation. A typical question sells an inherited flat, secured by a cheque, through a company, and asks who owns what and who pays which tax. Compact tables point to the teaching lessons; five worked items and a timed drill follow.

## Why it matters

Reg. 18א(ב) lists "property, including succession", "commercial law, including corporations" and "real-estate taxation" (lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md)). These are statute-heavy subjects in which the booklet helps only if you know where to look: the Land Law's numbering, the Succession Law's forms, the Bills Ordinance's definitions, the Companies Law's chapters and the Real Estate Taxation Law's exemptions. They are also the subjects behind the largest transactions in an ordinary person's life: buying a flat, inheriting one, guaranteeing a loan, running a small company.

## Leading cases

### ע״א 2643/97 גנז נ׳ בריטיש וקולוניאל חברה בע״מ (2003)

A first buyer who did not register a caveat for years lost priority under s. 9 to a later good-faith buyer for value who had not completed registration either. The Court read good faith (s. 39 through s. 61(ב)) into s. 9: the first buyer's long failure to protect himself can breach a duty to the world of buyers. Bar point: **register a caveat; "first in time" is not absolute**. ⚠️ Text not opened; search the case number on [the Judiciary's decision search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il) (lesson [13.3](../module-13/lesson-03.md)).

### ע״א 189/95 בנק אוצר החייל בע״מ נ׳ אהרונוב (1999)

Buyers who paid and took possession without a caveat prevailed over a creditor who later attached the flat, since the creditor takes the debtor's property subject to existing obligations; good faith remains relevant. Bar point: **buyer versus attaching creditor is not a s. 9 contest**. ⚠️ Text not opened ([Judiciary's search](https://supremedecisions.court.gov.il); lesson [13.3](../module-13/lesson-03.md)).

### דנ״א 8447/15 בנק לאומי לישראל בע״מ נ׳ טל טרייד (11.9.2017)

A bank took a cheque whose endorsement did not match the payee's name, so it was not a holder in due course. In 2015 the Supreme Court overruled the 1962 Guisky rule and held that a holder for value who is not in due course is subject to the drawer's defences, including total failure of consideration; a seven-judge further hearing in 2017 left that in place. Bar point: **only holder-in-due-course status cuts off personal defences**. [English translation](https://versa.cardozo.yu.edu/opinions/bank-leumi-le-israel-v-tal-trading-corp)

### ד״נ 40/80 קניג נ׳ כהן

A handwritten document without date or signature was not upheld as a will, though no one doubted it reflected the deceased's wishes. The case drove the reforms that produced today's s. 25 "basic elements" test. Bar point: **today the whole text in the testator's hand is the basic element; date and signature can be cured**. Summary: [Hebrew Wikipedia, צוואה](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/צוואה) (⚠️ secondary source; lesson [26.2](../module-26/lesson-02.md)).

## How it works in practice

### Priority contests: which rule decides?

| Contest | Rule |
|---|---|
| Buyer v. later buyer of land | Land Law s. 9 (good faith, value, registration), as read in Ganz |
| Buyer v. attaching creditor | Case law (Aharonov); a caveat (s. 127(ב)) settles it |
| Buyer v. later buyer of goods | Movable Property Law s. 12 (delivery replaces registration) |
| Owner v. good-faith buyer from a dealer | Sale Law s. 34 (market overt) |
| Pledgee v. other creditors | Pledges Law s. 4 (deposit or registration) |
| Holder v. drawer | Bills Ordinance ss. 28, 37 (in due course or not) |

### Tax questions: who, which tax, which rule

Buyer pays purchase tax; seller pays appreciation tax. Then ask: is it a residential apartment? Is it the only one of the buyer (or seller) unit, counting spouse and minor children? Is there a replacement window (24 months) or an inheritance route? Finally, the periods: 30 days to declare, 30 to object, 30 to appeal to the committee.

## Worked example

**Item 1.** On 1 March Moshe signs to sell his flat to Lea; no caveat. On 1 April he signs to sell it to Dan, who does not know of Lea and pays in full. On 1 May Dan registers, still not knowing. Who owns the flat?

- (A) Lea, first in time always wins.
- (B) Lea, because Dan paid after her.
- (C) Neither until a court decides.
- (D) Dan: second buyer in good faith, for value, registered while in good faith.

*Reasoning:* Land Law s. 9: the second transaction prevails if made in good faith and for value and registered while still in good faith. **Answer: D.** Lea's remedy is against Moshe.

**Item 2.** Rachel died intestate, leaving a husband, two children and her mother. The estate is a car, household furniture and a ₪900,000 savings account. What does the husband take?

- (A) The car and household movables, plus half the savings.
- (B) Everything.
- (C) One third of everything.
- (D) Half of everything, with the mother taking a quarter.

*Reasoning:* The spouse takes household movables including a passenger car, plus half of the rest when there are children or parents (s. 11(א)(1)). The children take the other half equally; the mother takes nothing because children exclude parents (s. 12). **Answer: A.**

**Item 3.** A witnessed will leaves a flat to Yossi, one of the two witnesses. The rest goes to the testator's daughter. What happens to the gift to Yossi?

- (A) Valid, because the will is otherwise valid.
- (B) Void, and the whole will falls.
- (C) Void, but only that provision; the rest stands.
- (D) Valid if Yossi renounces his status as witness.

*Reasoning:* A provision benefiting a witness is void (s. 35); a void part does not void the rest unless inseparable (s. 38(ב)). **Answer: C.**

**Item 4.** Gadi gave a post-dated cheque to a supplier for goods that never arrived. The supplier endorsed it, after its date, to a discount company that knew the goods had not been delivered. Must Gadi pay the discount company?

- (A) Yes, every holder is presumed to be in due course.
- (B) No, the company is not a holder in due course and takes subject to Gadi's defence of failure of consideration.
- (C) Yes, a cheque is an unconditional order.
- (D) No, because post-dated cheques are void.

*Reasoning:* A holder in due course must take the bill before it is overdue, in good faith, for value and without notice of a defect (s. 28); the company knew. A holder for value who is not in due course is subject to total failure of consideration (Tal Trading). A post-dated cheque is valid (s. 73). **Answer: B.**

**Item 5.** Noa owns one flat (held for five years). She buys a second flat on 1 January and sells the first on 1 October of the next year. Is the sale of the first flat exempt from appreciation tax?

- (A) No, she owned two apartments at the time of sale.
- (B) Yes, but only if she sold within 12 months.
- (C) Yes, the new flat was bought as a replacement within the 24 months before the sale.
- (D) No, the exemption applies only to inherited flats.

*Reasoning:* The sold flat is still treated as the only one if the other was bought as its replacement in the 24 months before the sale (s. 49ג(1)); she held it at least 18 months (s. 49ב(2)). **Answer: C.** The exemption is subject to the ceiling (s. 49א(א1)).

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Largely yes for your own affairs: a caveat ([13.4](../module-13/lesson-04.md)), a handwritten will ([26.8](../module-26/lesson-08.md)), a succession order ([26.9](../module-26/lesson-09.md)), a purchase-tax self-assessment and objection ([25.11](../module-25/lesson-11.md)), a response to a bounced-cheque demand ([29.8](../module-29/lesson-08.md)). Some offices accept filings only with a lawyer's verification of signatures (a caveat request: a lawyer or the registrar); representing others for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20(2)).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- Two buyers or a buyer and a creditor compete for the same flat.
- A will is contested for capacity or undue influence.
- You are a director or controlling shareholder facing a veil, distribution or oppression claim.
- The tax at stake is large or the transaction is a combination or urban-renewal deal.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not registering a caveat | Loss to a later good-faith buyer (Ganz) | Register at once; sue the seller | Caveat on the day of signing |
| Typing a "handwritten" will | No basic element; cannot be cured | Make a new will in a valid form | Write it wholly by hand, or use two witnesses |
| Taking a cheque with a mismatched endorsement | Not a holder in due course (Tal Trading) | Sue on the underlying debt | Check the endorsement chain |
| Paying a dividend without the solvency check | Directors deemed in breach (s. 311) | Recover from shareholders who knew (s. 310) | Minute a real solvency assessment |
| Selling the old flat after 24 months | Loss of the single-apartment exemption | Check other exemptions | Diarise the replacement window |

## Review

- Land: registration completes the deal; s. 9 decides double sales, read with good faith (Ganz); a caveat protects against later transactions and attachments.
- Movables: delivery decides double sales; a good-faith buyer from a dealer takes clean title (s. 34).
- Succession: the spouse's share depends on who else survives; a will must have its form's basic element; a witness's gift is void alone.
- Bills and companies: holder in due course or not (Tal Trading); no exemption for disloyalty; dividends need both tests.
- Real-estate tax: buyer pays purchase tax, seller appreciation tax; single-apartment rules count the family as one; 30-day periods throughout.
