# 38.2 · Procedural law review I: jurisdiction and civil procedure

This lesson reviews the first and largest block of the procedural paper: which court or tribunal hears what, and the life of a civil case under the 2018 Civil Procedure Regulations, from venue and pleadings to default judgment, interim relief and appeal, with arbitration, mediation and foreign judgments at the edges. It does not re-teach: each table points to the lesson where the rule is explained. You will work five Bar-style items step by step and then sit a 15-question timed drill.

## Why it matters

Jurisdiction and civil procedure are the backbone of the procedural paper (reg. 18א(א)(1) and (3); lesson [38.1](lesson-01.md)). They are also where self-represented litigants lose cases without ever reaching the merits: the wrong court, a missed 30-day window, a defence that admits by silence. The rules are numerous, precise and full of exceptions, which is exactly what a multiple-choice paper tests. If you learn the tables below so well that you can spot the one condition a wrong answer adds or drops, you have most of this block.

## Leading cases

### בג״ץ 8638/03 סימה אמיר נ׳ בית הדין הרבני הגדול בירושלים (2006)

A divorcing couple agreed to let the rabbinical court decide a property claim between them. The Supreme Court held that a religious court has only the jurisdiction the law gives it; consent cannot expand it, and it may not act as an arbitrator in civil matters outside it. Its decisions were void. Bar point: **subject-matter jurisdiction cannot be created by agreement** (contrast venue, where an agreed venue governs under reg. 7(א)). [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/49)

### רע״א 4716/04 Hotels.com נ׳ Zuz Tourism Ltd. (7.9.2005)

The district court refused to stay a claim against a foreign party with a New York Convention arbitration clause, because another defendant was not bound by it. The Supreme Court reversed: under s. 6 of the Arbitration Law the stay is mandatory unless the agreement is void, inoperative or incapable of being performed; joining a non-party does not make it so. Bar point: **domestic stay is discretionary (s. 5), international stay is not (s. 6)**. [English translation](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/196/)

### ר״ע 103/82 חניון חיפה בע״מ נ׳ מצת אור (הדר חיפה) בע״מ

Leave to appeal in a third instance is granted only where the case raises a question of legal or public importance beyond the parties' interest. Reg. 148א now reflects it. Bar point: a wrong decision is not enough for a third instance. Free summary: [Hebrew Wikipedia](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/פסק_דין_חניון_חיפה_בע%22מ_נגד_מצת_אור_(הדר_חיפה)_בע%22מ) (⚠️ secondary source).

### Issue estoppel and default judgments: אליהו נ׳ יחזקאל and its critics

The old rule (ע״א 202/70) was that a default judgment does not create issue estoppel. Later decisions (רע״א 2237/06 וינשטיין; רע״א 1170/08 עיריית חולון) criticised it and applied issue estoppel to judgments given in a party's absence, including small-claims judgments. Bar point: skipping a small case can cost you the facts in a large one (lesson [15.3](../module-15/lesson-03.md)). Free summary: [Hebrew Wikipedia, מעשה בית דין](https://he.wikipedia.org/wiki/מעשה_בית_דין) (⚠️ secondary source).

## How it works in practice

### How the procedural paper asks these rules

Bar questions in this block come in five shapes. Learn to recognise them:

1. **Which forum?** A dispute with one decisive fact (an employee, a married couple, land, an individual plaintiff, a company plaintiff).
2. **Which period, from when?** A date and an event; the trap is "from the judgment" versus "from service".
3. **As of right or by leave?** The kind of decision and the court that gave it.
4. **What is the effect?** Silence in a defence, absence at a hearing, an unserved ex parte order.
5. **Which remedy?** Set aside versus appeal, stay versus strike-out, attachment versus injunction.

### A 40-minute method for this block

Read the last line of the question first (what is asked), then the facts, then the options. Mark the decisive fact. For a "which forum" question, run the table from the top: is there an exclusive forum (labour, rabbinical, administrative, superintendent)? If not, value and land decide magistrates' or district court; then ask whether small claims is open (individual plaintiff, value, not assigned).

## Worked example

Five Bar-style items, solved step by step. Cover the reasoning, try each, then read on.

**Item 1.** Tamar, a nurse, claims overtime pay from a private hospital and, in the same claim, damages for defamation by her ward manager. Where does the overtime claim go?

- (A) Magistrates' court, because the amount is below its ceiling.
- (B) District court, because two causes of action are joined.
- (C) Small claims, because Tamar is an individual.
- (D) Regional labour court, which has exclusive jurisdiction.

*Reasoning:* An employee–employer claim from employment is in the labour court's exclusive jurisdiction (Labour Court Law s. 24(א)(1)). Value and the plaintiff's status are irrelevant once an exclusive jurisdiction applies. **Answer: D.** The defamation claim against the manager personally raises a separate question; the exam asks only about the overtime claim.

**Item 2.** A judgment was given in Gil's absence on 1 March and served on him on 20 March. On 15 April he applies to set it aside. On 10 May the application is dismissed and the decision is served the same day. Until when may he appeal the judgment?

- (A) 19 May, 60 days from service of the judgment.
- (B) 30 April, 60 days from the judgment.
- (C) 9 July, 60 days from service of the decision on the set-aside request.
- (D) He cannot appeal; a judgment in absence is challenged only by setting aside.

*Reasoning:* The set-aside request was within 30 days of service (reg. 131). The appeal period then runs from service of the decision on that request (reg. 137(ג)), and the period is 60 days (reg. 137(א)). **Answer: C** (recess days aside, reg. 179).

**Item 3.** Plaintiff obtained an ex parte temporary attachment of the defendant's bank account but served the order and the guarantee eight days later. What is the effect?

- (A) The order stays in force but the plaintiff pays costs.
- (B) The order lapses for failure to serve it in time.
- (C) The order stays in force until the inter partes hearing.
- (D) The bank may decide whether to honour the order.

*Reasoning:* After an ex parte order the applicant must serve it, with the request and guarantee, by personal delivery promptly, no later than 3 days (reg. 97(ד)); otherwise it lapses (reg. 100(2)). **Answer: C.**

**Item 4.** Hila and Ron signed a domestic arbitration clause. Ron sues in the magistrates' court. Hila files a full defence on the merits and, two months later, asks for a stay. What is the likely ruling?

- (A) Stay granted, because a stay under s. 5 is mandatory.
- (B) Stay refused, because the request had to come in the defence or before arguing the merits.
- (C) Stay granted, because 60 days have not passed since the last pleading.
- (D) Stay refused, because domestic arbitration clauses are void in consumer cases.

*Reasoning:* Under Arbitration Law s. 5 the stay request must come in the defence or before the applicant first argues the merits. Hila argued the merits first. **Answer: B.** Contrast an international clause, where s. 6 applies.

**Item 5.** A company sues an individual in small claims for an unpaid invoice of a modest amount. What should the court do?

- (A) Decline it: only an individual may file a small claim.
- (B) Hear it only if the company is represented by its director.
- (C) Hear it, since the amount is within the ceiling.
- (D) Hear it as a counterclaim against the consumer.

*Reasoning:* Courts Law s. 60(א) gives jurisdiction over a claim "שהגיש יחיד", filed by an individual. A company cannot file a small claim; it sues in the magistrates' court. **Answer: A.**

## Without a lawyer

### Can you do this yourself?

Yes for the drill. In real cases: an individual may act for himself in every civil court and tribunal in this lesson (CPR reg. 170 and the parallel rules; lesson [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md)); a company usually needs a lawyer outside small claims, where it may not sue at all. Representing others for pay is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law s. 20).

### Where and how

- **Forms and filing:** Net HaMishpat and the court information centres (lesson [08.8](../module-08/lesson-08.md)).
- **Forum check:** the [forum check worksheet](../../templates/forum-check-worksheet.md) and the table in [can I act without a lawyer](../../references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).
- **Periods and fees:** [current numbers](../../references/current-numbers.md).

### Stop and get a lawyer when

- A jurisdiction or venue objection must be raised now (reg. 29) and you are not sure which court is right.
- You are served with an ex parte attachment or injunction: the hearing comes within days.
- An appeal or set-aside deadline is running and the case is worth more than a small claim.

## What goes wrong

| Mistake | Consequence | How to fix | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choosing a court by value when an exclusive forum applies | The court has no jurisdiction; time is lost | Ask for transfer (s. 79) | Check exclusive jurisdictions first |
| Counting an appeal period from the judgment date | You file late | Apply for an extension with reasons | Count from service (reg. 137(א)) or from the set-aside decision (137(ג)) |
| A defence that denies "everything" in general words | Facts not denied in detail are admitted (reg. 14(ב)) | Apply to amend early (reg. 46) | Answer each paragraph expressly |
| Arguing the merits before asking for an arbitration stay | Domestic stay lost (s. 5) | Argue a special reason, rarely successful | Raise the clause in the defence |
| Not serving an ex parte order within 3 days | The order lapses (reg. 100(2)) | Apply again if still justified | Arrange personal service before filing |

## Review

- Start every forum question with exclusive jurisdictions; then value on the day of filing and land; then small claims (individuals only).
- Consent creates venue but never subject-matter jurisdiction (Amir).
- Know the periods that run from service (appeal 60 days, set-aside 30 days) and the one that runs from the decision on a set-aside request.
- Leave to appeal is the rule for interim decisions and second appeals; some decisions get no leave at all; venue and arbitration stays must be challenged at once.
- Domestic arbitration stays are discretionary and must be asked early; international ones are mandatory unless the agreement fails (Hotels.com).
