# Project: your IP file, from inventory to registration

Build the intellectual-property file a careful creator or small business keeps: an inventory of what you own, a protected work with a ready notice-and-response pack, an employment and secrecy check, and a trade-mark clearance search with a complete application. It uses every lesson of module 32 and both practice labs ([32.8](../lessons/module-32/lesson-08.md), [32.9](../lessons/module-32/lesson-09.md)).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. The project is a dry run unless you decide to send a notice about your own work or file your own trade-mark application.

## Brief

Choose either your own facts (say which parts) or the supplied case.

**Supplied case.** Liat and Ahmad run "Olive & Thread", a two-person business in Akko, as a registered partnership. They sell embroidered tote bags online and at markets.

1. **The assets.** Liat draws the embroidery patterns; Ahmad, who also works three days a week as a product designer for a bag manufacturer, designed the tote's distinctive curved handle. A freelancer drew their logo (an olive branch threaded through a needle) in 2024 for ₪1,500; nothing was signed. They photograph their own products for the shop.
2. **The infringement.** In September 2026 they find their product photos on a marketplace listing by an unknown seller offering "Olive Thread" bags at half price, and one of Liat's patterns printed on cheap bags sold by a Haifa gift shop.
3. **The employment question.** Ahmad's employment contract says that "all inventions and designs made by the employee during the employment period belong to the company".
4. **The brand.** They have never registered anything. They plan to expand to cushions and to sell in Europe next year.

Time: about 6 hours.

## Steps

1. **Inventory (45 minutes).** Use the [IP asset inventory](../templates/ip-asset-inventory.md) for every asset: regime, owner, registration, term, next action (lessons [32.1](../lessons/module-32/lesson-01.md), [32.2](../lessons/module-32/lesson-02.md)).
2. **Ownership problems (45 minutes).** The logo (s. 35(א), s. 37(ג)); Liat's patterns and photos; Ahmad's handle (Designs Law ss. 12–13; his employment contract) (lessons 32.2, [32.6](../lessons/module-32/lesson-06.md), [32.7](../lessons/module-32/lesson-07.md)). Draft the logo assignment.
3. **Infringement analysis (45 minutes).** The marketplace listing (photos, the name "Olive Thread") and the Haifa shop (the pattern): which rights, which acts, which defences, which remedies (lessons [32.3](../lessons/module-32/lesson-03.md), [32.5](../lessons/module-32/lesson-05.md)).
4. **Notice-and-response pack (75 minutes).** Using the [copyright notice and response kit](../templates/copyright-notice-and-response-kit.md): a marketplace report for the photos (part A), a demand letter to the Haifa shop (part C), and a short plan for the anonymous seller (lesson [32.4](../lessons/module-32/lesson-04.md)).
5. **Trade-mark clearance and application (90 minutes).** Using the [trade-mark search and application worksheet](../templates/trademark-search-and-application-worksheet.md): search "Olive & Thread" and the logo, decide, choose classes for bags, cushions and online retail, write the specification, calculate the fee, and note the six-month priority window for Europe (Ordinance s. 55) (lesson [32.9](../lessons/module-32/lesson-09.md)).
6. **Stop signals (20 minutes).** For each part, when you would get a lawyer or a patent attorney, and why.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 10 or more (out of 12) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory | Missing assets or regimes | All assets, some wrong regimes | Every asset with the right regime, owner, term and action |
| Ownership | "We paid, so we own" | Right rules, gaps | Logo: freelancer owns, written assignment drafted; handle: employer risk under s. 12 and the contract analysed |
| Infringement | Copying = infringement, no analysis | Right rights, weak defences | Acts, rights, s. 56א and s. 58, passing off for "Olive Thread", realistic remedies |
| Notice pack | Threats, no evidence | Complete but generic | Evidence first; platform report complete; reasoned demand letter; realistic plan for the anonymous seller |
| Trade mark | No search or wrong classes | Search done, weak specification | Variants and related classes searched; triple test; classes 18, 24 and 35 (or justified alternatives); fee and calendar right |
| Stop signals | None | Generic | Specific to each part |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**Inventory.** Embroidery patterns: artistic works, copyright, owned by Liat (s. 33), life + 70; but once used for industrial production, copyright ends (s. 7) and design law applies. Product photos: copyright, owned by whoever took them. Logo: copyright owned by the freelancer (s. 35(א)); also a potential trade mark. Name "Olive & Thread": no copyright; trade mark if registered; passing off once goodwill exists. Tote handle: design (registered or unregistered, three years from first publication if sold in Israel within six months), possibly a patent only if it has a new and inventive function. Customer list: trade secret if kept confidential.

**Ownership.** The logo belongs to the freelancer until a signed assignment (s. 37(ג)); draft one with the work, all rights, worldwide, for the full term, the consideration already paid, and a moral-right reservation. The handle: Designs Law s. 12 gives the employer designs made for the job or with substantial use of its resources; Ahmad's contract goes further ("all designs during the employment period"). If he designed the handle at home, for his own business, with no use of the employer's resources, he has arguments that the clause is too broad (a restraint on his freedom of occupation, AES), but the risk is real; he should get labour-law advice before registering or expanding the handle, and should not rely on it as the business's key asset until the question is settled.

**Infringement.** Marketplace seller: copying and making the photos available (ss. 11, 47); "Olive Thread" on similar goods is likely passing off (CTL s. 1) once they show goodwill; no trade-mark claim, as nothing is registered. Haifa shop: copying Liat's pattern onto bags: copyright infringement if the pattern is not an industrial design (s. 7); if the shop says it bought from a supplier, it may still be an indirect infringer if it knew or should have known (s. 48); s. 58 is unlikely, since it should know designs are protected. Remedies: removal, accounts of sales, statutory damages per infringement within s. 56, considering the shop's size and good faith.

**Notice pack.** Save the listing and the shop's display first. Marketplace report: owner, works, URLs, statements. Demand letter to the shop: removal of stock, details of the supplier, a reasoned payment, seven-day deadline. Anonymous seller: report to the marketplace (copyright and, if the marketplace allows, name confusion); a disclosure application under s. 60ב only if the sales are large enough to justify a District Court proceeding.

**Trade mark.** Search "Olive & Thread", "Olive Thread", "אוליב", the olive-and-needle logo, in classes 18 (bags), 24 (textiles, cushion covers), 20 (cushions) and 35 (online retail); also WIPO's database for European marks. If clear, file a combined word-and-logo application in the partners' names (or the partnership's, as appropriate), after the logo assignment is signed. Fee: first class plus each additional class (current numbers). Europe: file in Israel first, then a Madrid extension or an EU application within six months to claim priority (s. 55 for incoming priority; the Paris Convention for outgoing).

**Stop signals.** Ownership of the handle: a labour lawyer before any registration or expansion. Disclosure application: a lawyer. Opposition or a close prior mark: a patent attorney or IP lawyer. The Haifa shop: none unless it refuses and the amounts justify more than a small claim.

</details>
