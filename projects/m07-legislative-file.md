# Project: your legislative file

Build a complete file on one rule that affects you, a statute, a pending bill or a regulation: where it came from, who made it and under what power, whether it is valid and how it is read, what the regulator does with it, and what you have done (or will do) to change it. The result is a short, organised file with a source map, a validity memo, a submitted (or ready) public comment, a committee letter and position paper, and a tracking log. You will reuse it in module 12 (clinic) and in year 2 (administrative law).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run unless you choose to submit your comment or send your committee letter in your own matter; both are low-risk acts in your own name.

## Brief

Choose one rule that affects your daily life or work: a fee, a licensing condition, a consumer rule, a benefit criterion, a municipal by-law, or a pending bill or draft regulation in your field. If you have none, use the supplied case below and say so.

**Supplied case.** Shira Cohen-Nasser, 41, from Lod, runs a home day-care for five toddlers. In 2026 the ministry responsible published draft regulations requiring every home day-care to employ "a second carer with a recognised certificate whenever more than three children are present", from the day of publication, with a fine and up to six months' imprisonment for a breach. The recital cites a section of the relevant statute authorising the minister "to set conditions for the safety of children in day-care", "with the approval of the Labour and Welfare Committee of the Knesset". The comment period on the government legislation site closes in 21 days. Separately, a private bill to subsidise certificate courses for home carers passed first reading in the 25th Knesset and is in the Labour and Welfare Committee.

Time: about 4 hours.

## Steps

1. **Source map (25 minutes).** For the rule: its full title, type (statute, regulation, order, by-law, directive, bill), Reshumot reference or bill number, maker, enabling section (copy it), and the regulator that enforces it (lessons 07.1–07.3).
2. **Process timeline (20 minutes).** Every stage so far with its date and document: memorandum or draft, comment period, tabling, readings, committee sessions, approval, publication (lesson 07.1).
3. **Interpretation (25 minutes).** Apply the Interpretation Law to the key words and any periods; calculate one real deadline (lesson 07.4).
4. **Validity memo (45 minutes).** Run the eight-step checklist: source, authority, procedure (approval, consultation, publication), primary arrangement, rights and express authorisation, consistency with statute, administrative norms, time. One page, with sources (lessons 07.2, 07.4).
5. **Regulator and routes (15 minutes).** Who enforces the rule, what tools it uses, and which route fits your goal: complaint, comment, committee, claim, or indirect challenge if enforced against you (lesson 07.3).
6. **Public comment (45 minutes).** Section-by-section comments with alternative wording, using the [comment template](../templates/comment-on-draft-legislation.md). Submit if a comment period is open and you choose to (lesson 07.5).
7. **Committee letter and position paper (45 minutes).** Using the [committee letter template](../templates/letter-to-knesset-committee.md), for the committee that will approve the regulation or prepare the bill (lesson 07.6).
8. **Election and tracking plan (20 minutes).** What the 27 October 2026 elections mean for any pending bill or approval; a watch list with sources and frequency; a dated log.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 14 or more (out of 18) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source map | Rule named only | Type and maker given, no enabling section | Full reference, enabling section quoted, regulator named |
| Timeline | None | Stages listed without documents | Each stage dated with a document |
| Interpretation | Not done | Words discussed without the Interpretation Law | Sections of the Interpretation Law applied; deadline calculated correctly |
| Validity memo | Opinion without law | Some steps, sources missing | All eight steps with sources; interpretive solution considered |
| Regulator and routes | Missing | Regulator named, route unclear | Regulator, tools and route with reasons |
| Public comment | Missing or general | Section numbers but no wording | Section-specific, factual, alternative wording, submitted or ready |
| Committee letter | Missing | Letter without paper or vice versa | Request plus a two-page paper with a section table and questions |
| Election and tracking | Missing | Election effect stated, no plan | Correct continuity analysis and a watch list with sources |
| Presentation | Disorganised | Complete but long | Short, ordered, every claim traceable to a source |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This model uses the supplied case.

**Source map.** Draft regulations (not yet in force), with legislative effect; maker: the minister; enabling section: "to set conditions for the safety of children in day-care", with committee approval required by the enabling law. Enforcer: the ministry's supervision unit. Pending private bill on course subsidies: passed first reading, in the Labour and Welfare Committee.

**Timeline.** Draft published on the government legislation site; 21-day comment period; then committee approval needed before signature and publication in קובץ התקנות. The bill: tabled, preliminary reading, first reading, committee; Knesset dissolved 17.7.2026.

**Interpretation.** "Whenever more than three children are present" means four or more at the same time (not per day). "Recognised certificate" is undefined: under Interpretation Law s. 2, look for a definition in the enabling statute; if none, the regulation is unclear. Comment deadline: 21 days from publication, the publication day not counted (s. 10(א)); rest days count unless the last day is one (s. 10(ג)).

**Validity memo.**
1. Source: draft regulations.
2. Authority: child-safety conditions plausibly include staff ratios; doubtful whether they include certification requirements for a whole occupation.
3. Procedure: committee approval is required by the enabling law and, because the draft creates an offence, by Penal Law s. 2(ב); imprisonment of six months is the maximum (s. 2(א)). Publication in Reshumot required (Ordinance s. 17).
4. Primary arrangement: setting a new certification condition for an entire occupation, affecting thousands of carers, is arguably a basic policy choice for the Knesset (Rubinstein).
5. Rights: freedom of occupation; the restriction needs express authorisation in a statute (Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation; the s. 8 logic). "Conditions for safety" may not be express enough for a licensing-type requirement.
6. Consistency: no contradiction found.
7. Administrative norms: immediate commencement with no transition is likely disproportionate; no data shown.
8. Time: not yet in force.
Conclusion: doubtful on authority and primary arrangement; clearly problematic on commencement.

**Regulator and routes.** Comment now; ask the committee to condition approval on changes; if the regulations come into force unchanged and are enforced, raise an indirect challenge.

**Public comment.** (1) Reg. on staffing: define "present" and "recognised certificate"; allow an existing carer with five years' experience to qualify by a short course. (2) Commencement: 12-month transition; link to the subsidy bill. (3) Penalty: replace imprisonment with an administrative fine; note Penal Law s. 2(ב). (4) Authority: ask the ministry to state whether the enabling section covers certification; suggest placing the core in the statute.

**Committee letter.** To the Labour and Welfare Committee manager, copying its legal adviser: request to be heard when the regulations come for approval; position paper with the table of sections and questions for the ministry (cost, number of carers affected, availability of courses).

**Election and tracking.** The draft regulations need the 26th Knesset's committee; nothing can be approved until committees are formed. The subsidy bill passed first reading: continuity possible if the competent committee asks, with the sponsor's consent if re-elected, and the Knesset approves (Continuity Law ss. 2–6). Watch: the government legislation site for a revised draft; the committee's agenda weekly once formed; Reshumot for publication.

Self-score: source map 2, timeline 2, interpretation 2, validity 2, routes 2, comment 2, committee letter 2, tracking 2, presentation 1 = 17.
