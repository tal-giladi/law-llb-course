# Project: an international-law file, a treaty argument in your own matter

Build the file a careful person keeps when a treaty Israel has ratified bears on their own affairs: the treaty's status and Israel's reservations, its interpretation, the channel through which it reaches Israeli law, a written argument section in Hebrew, and a neutral status note on one pending international case. It extends the module 14 administrative file and the module 18 family file, and you will reuse it in the year-2 clinic and in module 30 (private international law).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run: nothing is filed. If you later use the letter in your own matter, check every date and status again on the day.

## Brief

Choose one matter in your own or your household's life where a treaty Israel has ratified could matter: a child's schooling or disability accommodation (CRC, CRPD), a cross-border family issue (Hague Abduction or Apostille Conventions), a pension or social-security period abroad (a bilateral agreement), a dispute with a foreign embassy or State-owned company (Foreign States Immunity Law and custom), or equality at work (CEDAW, CERD). If you have nothing suitable, use the supplied case and say so.

**Supplied case.** Samira and Tomer Avraham-Khoury, a mixed Arab-Jewish couple in Haifa, have a daughter, Lina, 11, who is hard of hearing. In September 2026 her state school refused to provide a speech-to-text transcriber in class, saying the ministry's budget covers only special-education classes, and proposed moving her to a special class in another neighbourhood. When Lina asked to speak at the meeting, the principal said "the parents will speak for you". The decision was given orally; the parents asked for it in writing and received a two-line email. Assume the Special Education Law and the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law govern the entitlement, and that no statute expressly excludes transcription in regular classes.

Time: about 6 hours.

## Steps

1. **Facts and documents (30 minutes).** A dated, numbered chronology and every document as an annex.
2. **Source test (20 minutes, lesson 19.1).** For each international claim you will make, name the source under art. 38 and who it binds.
3. **Treaty status (40 minutes, lesson 19.2).** For each treaty: signature, ratification, entry into force for Israel, reservations, Israeli incorporating or purpose-clause law. Part A of the [treaty argument worksheet](../templates/treaty-argument-worksheet.md).
4. **Custom (20 minutes, lesson 19.3).** Is any rule you need customary? If so, two items of practice and one of opinio juris; if not, say so.
5. **Parties and immunity (15 minutes, lesson 19.4).** Is any party a foreign State, agency or international body? If so, the immunity analysis; if not, one line.
6. **Interpretation (40 minutes, lessons 19.2 and 19.6).** The articles, a general comment or authoritative interpretation, Israel's own report and the latest concluding observations on the topic, each dated.
7. **Channel and presumption (30 minutes, lesson 19.7).** The four-channel test; the two readings of the Israeli provision; the rebuttal test (part C of the worksheet).
8. **The argument section (70 minutes, lesson 19.8).** In Hebrew, 1–2 pages, eight-part structure, inside the right document (letter, appeal or petition).
9. **Rebuttal and oral version (30 minutes).** The other side's best paragraph and your reply; a two-minute oral version.
10. **Neutral status note (30 minutes, lesson 19.5).** One page on one pending ICJ or ICC case (any case, Israel-related or not): court, jurisdiction, decisions, attributed positions, stage and next date.
11. **Stop signals (10 minutes).** Every deadline with its source, and the red flags that would send you to a lawyer or a clinic.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 16 or more (out of 20) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Vague, undated | Mostly dated | Dated, numbered, every document annexed |
| Sources | "International law" in general | Instruments named | Each claim classified under art. 38 and checked for whom it binds |
| Treaty status | Missing | Ratification only | Ratification, entry into force, reservations and Israeli law, with dates and links |
| Custom and immunity | Not considered | Mentioned | Correctly found relevant or irrelevant, with reasons |
| Interpretation | None | Treaty text only | Text, purpose, authoritative interpretation and Israel's own statements, dated |
| Channel | Treaty treated as directly binding | Channel named | Correct channel; two readings; rebuttal test applied |
| Argument section | Missing | Present but treaty-led | Israeli-law-led, short quotes, applied to each fact, clear relief |
| Rebuttal | Missing | Token | The other side's best argument, fairly stated and answered |
| Status note | Missing or opinionated | Mostly factual | Attributed positions, operative words quoted, stage and next date sourced |
| Neutrality and tone | Political or angry | Mostly legal | Strictly legal; the authority's case stated at its strongest |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**1. Facts.** 1.9.2026 meeting (annex 1: parents' notes); 3.9.2026 request for written decision (annex 2); 10.9.2026 two-line email (annex 3); audiologist's report (annex 4); Lina's own written statement (annex 5).

**2. Source test.** CRPD art. 24 and CRC arts 3, 12, 28: treaties (art. 38(1)(a)), binding on Israel. CRPD Committee general comment on inclusive education: interpretation (soft law). No custom needed.

**3. Treaty status.** CRPD: signed 30.3.2007, ratified 28.9.2012 (OHCHR), no reservation on art. 24; not incorporated; the Equal Rights Law preceded it. CRC: signed 3.7.1990, ratified 3.10.1991 (OHCHR); no reservation on arts 3, 12, 28; not incorporated, but the Pupils' Rights Law s. 1 states its purpose in the CRC's spirit.

**4. Custom.** Not relied on. **5. Immunity.** No foreign party.

**6. Interpretation.** CRPD art. 24(2)(a) and (c): no exclusion from general education on the basis of disability; reasonable accommodation. The Committee's general comment treats moving a child to a segregated setting for lack of support as inconsistent with art. 24 (⚠️ cite paragraph after checking). CRC art. 12(2): the child's opportunity to be heard in administrative proceedings affecting her. Israel's periodic report describes the integration chapter of the Special Education Law as implementing inclusion (check date and paragraph).

**7. Channel.** Unincorporated treaties: presumption of compatibility (Yated para. 8). Reading A (ministry): integration support means only what the ministry's budget formula funds. Reading B (family): the entitlement in the integration chapter includes the accommodation Lina needs to learn in a regular class. Reading B fits art. 24(2)(c) and Yated, where the Court read the Special Education Law to require free special education for integrated children. Nothing in the statute's words or specific purpose excludes transcription; the presumption is not rebutted.

**8. Argument section (outline, Hebrew in the actual letter).** (1) The Special Education Law's integration chapter and the Equal Rights Law govern; (2) Israel ratified the CRPD in 2012 and the CRC in 1991 without relevant reservations; Israeli law is read in harmony with them (Yated para. 8); (3) art. 24(2)(c) quoted; (4) Yated held that integrated children are entitled to free special education; (5) applied: transcription is the accommodation Lina needs; moving her is exclusion on the basis of disability; Lina was not heard herself, contrary to CRC art. 12 and the spirit of the Pupils' Rights Law; the email is not a reasoned decision (lesson 14.4); (6) the ministry's best argument is budget; reply: budget is a relevant consideration but cannot empty a statutory entitlement, and the cost of transcription is modest compared with a separate placement; (7) relief: provide transcription within 14 days, cancel the transfer proposal, and give a reasoned written decision after hearing Lina.

**9. Oral version.** Statute; treaties and Yated; transcription is reasonable accommodation; Lina was not heard; please assign a transcriber.

**10. Status note.** For example, South Africa v. Israel: ICJ, Genocide Convention art. IX; provisional measures 26.1, 28.3, 24.5.2024; Counter-Memorial due 12.3.2026; Order of 21.5.2026: Reply 22.11.2027, Rejoinder 22.5.2029; Israel's position (self-defence, no intent, case baseless) and South Africa's (violations of the Convention) stated with attribution; no merits hearing scheduled.

**11. Stop signals.** If the school moves Lina before the decision is put in writing, or refuses to hear her, consider an administrative petition with an interim order (lesson 14.11) and contact a disability-rights clinic.
