# Module 10 project: Religious law and personal status file

Build a two-part file. Part 1 is a short research note on how Jewish law was used in one Supreme Court ruling. Part 2 is a personal-status forum and filing plan for a family (your own, with no identifying details of others, or a supplied case), ending in a dry-run rabbinical-court or other religious-court file. It applies every lesson in module 10. Nothing is filed; if this is your own matter, you decide separately, in your own name, whether to act. Time: about 6 hours.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.

## Brief

**Part 1 — research note.** Choose one Supreme Court ruling tagged "Jewish Law" on the [Cardozo Israeli Supreme Court Project](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/iscp-opinions/) other than Roker v. Solomon (lesson 10.5). Suggested: Kal Binyan (CA 6370/00), Shavit (CA 6024/97), Nahmani (CFH 2401/95), or A v. Tel-Aviv-Jaffa Regional Rabbinical Court (HCJ 2232/03).

**Part 2 — forum and filing plan.** Use your own family situation if it fits, or the supplied case.

**Supplied case.** Rami Halabi (Druze, from Usfiya) and Shira Levi-Halabi (Jewish, from Haifa) married civilly in Cyprus in 2018 and were registered as married. They have one child (6). They separated in 2026; Shira lives in their Haifa flat, registered in both names. Shira wants to end the marriage, primary custody, child maintenance and to keep the flat with a payment to Rami. Separately, Shira's sister Noa Levi (Jewish, married by the Rabbinate, living in Kiryat Motzkin) wants a divorce from her husband, who says he will "never give a get"; they have two children and a jointly owned flat in Kiryat Motzkin.

## Steps

1. **Research note: map the ruling (60 min).** Facts, question, result, who joined which opinion; the Israeli legal basis; every Jewish-law reference with its route (gap-filling, interpretation, values, comparison) and layer, on the [Jewish-law reasoning worksheet](../templates/jewish-law-reasoning-worksheet.md) (lessons 10.1, 10.2, 10.5).
2. **Research note: trace and weigh (45 min).** Trace the main Jewish-law source on Sefaria to at least the Talmud and one code; check the judge's description; write the weight paragraph and a neutral close setting out the strongest argument for a strong role and for a limited role of Jewish law (lesson 10.2).
3. **Family 1 (Rami and Shira): forum (45 min).** Which court dissolves the marriage and why (1969 Law ss. 1, 3); where custody, child maintenance and the flat go; whether any religious court must be asked about a religious divorce, and which (lesson 10.4). Cite each section.
4. **Family 2 (Noa): forum and race (45 min).** Divorce in the rabbinical court (s. 1); which issues she could join (s. 3, including the 2025 temporary order on child maintenance) and which she could file in the Family Court; the request to settle and the filing window (lesson 10.3). Write one balanced paragraph on each forum, with legal and procedural reasons only.
5. **Family 2: protections (30 min).** The civil statutes the rabbinical court must apply to her (Women's Equal Rights Law s. 7, Property Relations Law s. 13) and how she avoids waiving them; what the Rabbinical Courts (Enforcement of Divorce Judgments) Law allows if a judgment orders a get and the husband refuses (restriction orders, s. 2); when she needs a lawyer or pleader.
6. **Family 2: the dry-run file (75 min).** On the [rabbinical-court filing file template](../templates/rabbinical-court-filing-file.md): the request to settle; a divorce claim meeting reg. כו with each joined issue set out separately; the documents list; the fees or exemptions; the representation decision; the deadlines table (lesson 10.6).
7. **Decision memo (20 min).** One page: what each family should do first, the dates to diarise, and the red flags that require professional help.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weak (1) | Adequate (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruling map | Summary only | Israeli basis and some references | Every Jewish-law reference with route and layer; who joined which opinion |
| Source tracing | Quotes the judge only | One source opened | Talmud and code traced, accuracy checked, open points marked |
| Neutrality | Takes a side | Both sides named | Each side's strongest legal argument; no verdict |
| Mixed-couple forum | Wrong forum | Family Court named | 1969 Law ss. 1 and 3 applied; inquiries to the right religious courts; each issue placed |
| Rabbinical-court forum and race | Everything "to the rabbinate" | Divorce and joinder explained | Request to settle, stay, filing window, joinder per issue, 2025 temporary order, s. 25(ב) |
| Protections | Not addressed | Statutes named | Statutes applied, consent trap explained, enforcement options and red flags |
| Dry-run file | Missing parts | Claim drafted | Request bare; claim meets reg. כו and כז; documents, fees, representation and deadlines complete |
| Decision memo | Vague | First steps listed | Dated plan per family with clear points for professional help |

A strong file scores at least 20 of 24, with no criterion at 1.

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**Part 1 (Kal Binyan, outline).** Question: are expectation damages available for bad faith in negotiations (Contracts Law s. 12(ב))? Result: yes. Basis: ss. 12, 39 and 61(ב) as fundamental norms (Barak P.). Jewish law: Englard J., concurring, on the assumption that s. 12 does not itself yield expectation damages, applies the Foundations of Law Act route (gap-filling) and surveys Jewish law on withdrawing before a kinyan: reliance-type relief has support; expectation loss has only the moral sanction of "מי שפרע" (Mishnah Bava Metzia 4:2). Weight: the result follows from the Contracts Law; Jewish law is an alternative path that the concurrence explores and qualifies. Neutral close: a strong-role reading sees a model of integration that tests Jewish law honestly; a limited-role reading notes that the gap was hypothetical and the statute sufficed.

**Family 1.** Mixed couple (Druze and Jewish): no religious court has exclusive jurisdiction; the Family Court dissolves the marriage (1969 Law s. 1(א)). Because each spouse belongs to a recognised community, the court sends inquiries to the heads of the rabbinical courts and of the Druze court on whether a religious divorce is needed (s. 3(א)); if a head says yes, that part is transferred to the religious court (s. 3(ג)), otherwise the Family Court decides (s. 3(ד)). Custody, child maintenance and the flat: Family Court (family matters, Family Court Law ss. 1, 3). The request to settle applies before contested claims (Dispute Resolution Law s. 3).

**Family 2.** Divorce: rabbinical court only (Rabbinical Courts Jurisdiction Law s. 1). First, a bare request to settle; after mahut and the stay, as first filer Noa chooses the forum for each issue within the window (s. 4). In the rabbinical court she can properly join custody, the flat and, during the temporary order, child maintenance in full (s. 3 as amended); otherwise she files those in the Family Court, which hears them as long as the rabbinical court is not (Family Court Law s. 25(ב)). Either forum applies the Property Relations Law to the flat; in the rabbinical court she must not consent to religious law (s. 13(ב)) or waive the Women's Equal Rights Law (s. 7(ב)). If the court rules that the husband must give a get and he refuses, it may impose restriction orders (Enforcement of Divorce Judgments Law ss. 1–2: leaving the country, passport, driving licence, bank accounts and more). Red flags: get refusal, threats, children's safety: get a lawyer or licensed pleader; Legal Aid if eligible. File: the request names the Haifa regional rabbinical court (last joint residence Kiryat Motzkin; check the annex to reg. ו), no facts; the claim lists the marriage, last joint residence, children, the breakdown in neutral terms, then separate sections for custody, child maintenance (with the maintenance form, reg. לג; fee exemption reg. 14א) and the flat (land-registry extract; Property Relations Law); deadlines 30/10/30 days.
