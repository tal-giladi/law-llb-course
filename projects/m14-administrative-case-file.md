# Project: an administrative case file, from FOI request to petition

Build the file a careful person keeps when a public authority decides against them: the decision analysed ground by ground, the information obtained, the objection or prior demand, the choice of forum, and a dry-run petition ready if the authority refuses. This file extends the module 6 public-law file to the full toolkit of administrative law, and you will reuse it in the year-2 clinic and in module 27 (planning and local government).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run except for the FOI request and, if low-risk and in time, the objection, which you may send in your own matter. Nothing is filed in court.

## Brief

Choose one decision (or failure to decide) of a public authority that affects you or your household: a municipality, a ministry unit, a planning or licensing committee, a government company, a health fund. If you have nothing suitable, use the supplied case and say so.

**Supplied case.** Rivka and Moshe Friedman, a Haredi couple in Bnei Brak with six children, applied in May 2026 to their municipality for a disabled-parking space next to their building for their son Eli, 9, who uses a wheelchair. They attached a disabled-parking badge issued by the Ministry of Transport. On 20 August 2026 they received a two-line letter from the municipal traffic engineer: "The application is rejected. Policy: no reserved spaces on streets narrower than 8 metres." No reasons beyond that, no hearing, nothing about objection rights. Their street is 7.5 metres wide; a neighbour two streets away, on a 7-metre street, received a reserved space in 2024. The couple believe a reserved space at the corner of their street, where it widens, would solve the problem. Assume the governing by-law gives the traffic engineer power to designate reserved disabled-parking spaces and says nothing about street width.

Time: about 6 hours.

## Steps

1. **Facts and documents (30 minutes).** A dated, numbered chronology; the decision in full; every letter and answer as a numbered annex.
2. **Authority (30 minutes, lesson 14.1).** Who decided; under which provision; does it cover this subject; is any third party enlisted; is the power-holder the person who signed (delegation, lesson 14.5)?
3. **Discretion (45 minutes, lessons 14.2 and 14.5).** Purpose of the power; considerations used and missing; the street-width policy: does it leave room for exceptions (fettering)? Equality: the comparator and any data; extreme unreasonableness.
4. **Proportionality (30 minutes, lesson 14.3).** The three subtests, with the authority's best case first; a concrete less harmful alternative.
5. **Procedure (20 minutes, lesson 14.4).** Hearing, reasons (s. 2א), notice of objection rights (s. 5), conflict of interest; compute the Decisions and Reasons periods.
6. **FOI request (40 minutes, lesson 14.7).** A real request (or a dry run for the supplied case) for the policy, the file and comparator data, with fee status and the decision dates; use the [FOI request template](../templates/foi-request.md).
7. **Objection or prior demand (60 minutes, lesson 14.10).** Route and deadline; the document on the [objection template](../templates/objection-to-administrative-decision.md), two to four grounds, evidence, remedy and a date.
8. **Other tools (15 minutes, lesson 14.8).** Would an Ombudsman complaint help, and at what stage? What deadlines would it not stop?
9. **Forum and threshold (30 minutes, lesson 14.9).** Schedule item, venue, period and last day, delay, exhaustion, ripeness, respondents, clean hands, standing; each rated low, medium or high risk.
10. **Dry-run petition (70 minutes, lesson 14.11).** A petition and affidavit on the [administrative petition template](../templates/administrative-petition.md), with remedy and alternative; a one-paragraph interim-order assessment.
11. **Stop signals (10 minutes).** Every date with its source; the red flags that would send you to a lawyer or a clinic.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 16 or more (out of 20) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Vague, undated | Mostly dated | Dated, numbered, every document annexed |
| Authority | Not considered | Source named | Source quoted; subject, person and delegation checked |
| Discretion and equality | Assertion only | Grounds named | Considerations tied to purpose; comparator; fettering addressed |
| Proportionality | Missing | Tests named | Each subtest reasoned, concrete alternative with evidence |
| Procedure | Missing | Some defects noted | Hearing, reasons, s. 5 notice and periods computed |
| FOI request | Missing or "all information" | Specific but incomplete | Specific items, format, partial disclosure, fee and dates |
| Objection or demand | Missing | Present but vague | Right route, in time, focused grounds, remedy and date |
| Forum and threshold | Court by default | Forum named | Schedule item, venue, every threshold doctrine rated |
| Petition | Missing | Outline | Every reg. 5(ב) item, affidavit, realistic remedy |
| Neutrality and tone | Political or angry | Mostly legal | Strictly legal; authority's case stated at its strongest |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**1. Facts.** 5.2026 application with badge (annex 1); 20.8.2026 rejection (annex 2); 2024 reserved space on a 7-metre street nearby (annex 3, photo and street measurement); site photos of the corner (annex 4).

**2. Authority.** The by-law empowers the traffic engineer to designate reserved disabled-parking spaces; the engineer signed. No lack of authority. The street-width rule is not in the by-law: it is an internal policy, so its legality is a discretion question.

**3. Discretion.** Purpose: access for people with disabilities, balanced with traffic flow and safety. The decision considered only width and ignored relevant considerations: Eli's needs, the badge, the possibility of a space at the corner. The policy was applied as an absolute rule with no exception: fettering (lesson 14.5; Gas Outline on the duty to exercise discretion). Equality: a space was given on a narrower street two years ago; unless there is a relevant difference, the refusal is unequal (Open House). Reasonableness: a rule that excludes almost every street in a dense city from reserved spaces may be extremely unreasonable in its outcome.

**4. Proportionality.** Authority's case: narrow streets must stay passable for emergency vehicles; a proper purpose, and refusing reserved spaces advances it. Least harmful means: a space at the corner, where the street widens, or a space with time limits, achieves the safety purpose with far less harm. Narrow sense: the harm to a child who cannot reach his home is severe; the benefit of refusing compared with a corner space is small.

**5. Procedure.** No hearing (Berman), no real reasons beyond quoting the policy (s. 2א), no notice of rights (s. 5). The application was decided in about three months, so no s. 6(ב) issue, but reasons are missing, so in court the burden of showing legality shifts (s. 6(א)).

**6. FOI request.** To the municipality's FOI officer: the written policy on reserved disabled-parking spaces and its date; the professional opinion in the Friedman file; the list (by street, without names) of reserved spaces approved and refused in 2023–2026 with street widths. Electronic format; partial disclosure under s. 11; application fee paid, but the file documents are about the family (reg. 6(א)).

**7. Prior demand / request for reconsideration.** No statutory objection route is stated or found, so a request for reconsideration to the traffic engineer, copied to the legal adviser, sent on 25.8.2026. Grounds: fettering and missing considerations; inequality; proportionality with the corner alternative; no hearing or reasons. Remedy: cancel and decide again after a hearing within 21 days, considering the corner space. Notes that an FOI request has been filed and that a petition is being considered.

**8. Other tools.** An Ombudsman complaint is possible but adds little where a quick demand and petition are available; it would not stop the petition period. Useful later if the municipality agrees but does not implement.

**9. Forum and threshold.** First Schedule item 8 (local authorities; also item 14(ו) for disabled badges, but the badge is not in dispute); Tel Aviv District court sitting in administrative matters (decision made in Bnei Brak; ⚠️ confirm the district); period from 20.8.2026, the date of notice, with the last day diaried; delay risk low if filed promptly; exhaustion: no statutory route found (medium: check the by-law again); ripeness: a final decision exists; respondents: the municipality and the traffic engineer, no third party; clean hands: full disclosure of the badge and family details; standing: direct personal interest (Eli, through his parents as guardians).

**10. Petition.** Remedy: annul the decision; alternatively return it for a new decision after a hearing within 30 days, considering a space at the corner; costs. Grounds in order: fettering and missing considerations; inequality; disproportionality; procedure. Affidavit by Rivka, separating what she saw (measurements, photos, letters) from what she was told (the 2024 neighbour's space, source: the neighbour). Interim order not needed: no imminent irreversible act.

**11. Stop signals.** If the municipality answers with a traffic-engineering opinion the family cannot assess, consult an accessibility NGO or clinic; if a lawyer for the municipality raises threshold objections the family cannot answer, get advice before the hearing.
