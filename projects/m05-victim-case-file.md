# Project: a victim's case file

Build the complete file a careful victim keeps for one criminal incident: the legal analysis of what happened, the police complaint and rights request, the victim statement, and the plan for what to do if the file is closed, including an appeal and a reasoned decision on a private criminal complaint. You will reuse the file in module 8 (criminal procedure in brief) and in the year-4 clinic.

> [!WARNING]
> This project is written from the victim's side only. Do not use it if you are a suspect or fear a counter-complaint: consult a defence lawyer. If you are in danger, call the police on 100.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run: nothing is filed unless you decide to file your own complaint about a real incident.

## Brief

Choose one incident in which you were the victim of an offence: threats, an assault, damage to your property, a burglary, an online fraud, harassment. It may be recent or past (check the limitation period). If you have no suitable incident, or it is too painful to use, take the supplied case and say so.

**Supplied case.** Avi and Meital Gabay, a couple in their 30s from Modi'in, have a dispute with the owner of the flat above them over water leaks. On 3 September 2026 the neighbour, Ronen, shouted at Meital in the stairwell, "אם תגישו תביעה אני אשבור לכם את הרכב ואת הראש", and pushed her against the wall; she has a bruise on her arm, photographed the same evening, and went to the emergency clinic the next day. A resident, Mrs Halabi, saw the push. On 10 September the side mirror of their car, parked in the building's car park, was found broken; the building's camera covers the car park. Meital complained at the Modi'in station on 11 September. On 15 November she received a notice that the file had been closed for "insufficient evidence". The camera footage was never collected, and Mrs Halabi was never questioned.

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Facts (30 minutes).** A dated, numbered chronology; exact words in quotation marks; what you saw vs. what you were told.
2. **Offences (40 minutes).** Each candidate offence with section, maximum penalty and class (lessons 05.1, 05.5); a neighbouring offence you rejected and why.
3. **Elements table (40 minutes).** For each offence, conduct, circumstances, result and mental element, with evidence and gaps (lesson 05.2).
4. **Participants and defences (20 minutes).** Every participant and role (lesson 05.3); any defence the suspect might raise and why it fails or succeeds (lesson 05.4).
5. **Complaint and rights request (45 minutes).** Fill the [police complaint and victims' rights request](../templates/police-complaint-victim.md) template; request only the rights that apply (lesson 05.7).
6. **Victim statement (30 minutes).** One page under Crime Victims' Rights Law s. 18, with a request for compensation under Penal Law s. 77 (lesson 05.6).
7. **Closure plan (60 minutes).** Assume the file is closed. Calculate the appeal deadline from the date of receipt; write the appeal with the [appeal against closing a file](../templates/appeal-against-closing-file.md) template (lesson 05.8).
8. **Private complaint decision (30 minutes).** Eligibility (Second Schedule, family exception, State employee, limitation), proof, risks, alternatives; if you would file, a draft with the [private criminal complaint](../templates/private-criminal-complaint.md) template.
9. **Deadlines and stop signals (15 minutes).** Every date with its source and check date; the red flags that would send you to a lawyer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 14 or more (out of 18) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Vague, undated | Mostly dated | Dated, numbered, exact words, sources of knowledge marked |
| Offences | Wrong or none | Right offence without class or section | Offences with sections, class and a rejected alternative |
| Elements | Not analysed | Elements listed without evidence | Every element matched to evidence, gaps marked |
| Participants and defences | Ignored | Mentioned generally | Each role and each possible defence tested |
| Complaint | Missing | Present but story-like | Chronology, evidence list, request to secure evidence |
| Rights request | Missing | Generic | Only applicable rights, with sections |
| Victim statement | Argues guilt or sentence | Describes harm without documents | Documented harm and s. 77 request |
| Appeal | Repeats the complaint | Some gaps named | Deadline from receipt, specific steps per element, precise remedy |
| Private complaint decision | Not considered | Eligibility only | Eligibility, proof, risks and alternatives weighed |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This model uses the supplied case.

**Offences.** Threats (Penal Law s. 192): threatening unlawful harm to their property and bodies to deter a lawsuit, with apparent intent to frighten; maximum three years, a misdemeanour. Assault causing actual bodily harm (s. 380): the push and the bruise ("חבלה" includes passing injury, s. 34כד); maximum three years, a misdemeanour; simple assault (s. 379) is the fallback if the bruise is not proved. Malicious damage (s. 452) for the mirror, misdemeanour, but the link to Ronen depends on the footage. Rejected: insult in a public place (s. 194(ב)): the stairwell is probably not a public place and the threat is the stronger charge.

**Elements (excerpt).** Threat: the words (Meital's testimony; Mrs Halabi may have heard them); unlawful harm (to body and property); intent to frighten (context: "אם תגישו תביעה"). Assault with harm: the push (Mrs Halabi saw it); no consent; the bruise (photo of 3 September, clinic record of 4 September). Mirror: damage (photos, repair invoice); identity (gap: the footage).

**Defences.** Ronen may say Meital pushed first or that the words were a figure of speech. Self-defence needs an unlawful attack and immediate necessity (s. 34י); nothing suggests that. "A figure of speech" goes to intent, which context answers.

**Complaint and rights.** Chronology as above; Mrs Halabi named with phone; a written request on 11 September to secure the car-park footage from the building committee. Rights: stage notices (s. 8(ג)(1)), a copy of any indictment (s. 9), support services (s. 11), the victim statement (s. 18), information on protection options (s. 6) given that they share a building; an accompanying person at questioning (s. 14) because threats and assault are listed sexual or violent offences. Personal details: they live in the same building, so withholding the address is pointless; she may still ask that her phone number not be passed on (s. 7(ה), the prosecutor's discretion).

**Victim statement.** The bruise and clinic visit, fear in the stairwell, Avi now escorting Meital, ₪650 for the mirror, a day's lost work; request for compensation under s. 77.

**Closure plan.** Notice received 15 November 2026: the appeal period runs from that date (CPL s. 65; the length is in current numbers). Appeal through the Modi'in station or the police prosecution unit to the district attorney / head of appeals (s. 64(א)(1)). Grounds: (1) the footage was never requested although she asked in writing; (2) the eyewitness, Mrs Halabi, was never questioned; (3) the clinic record and photos establish the injury; (4) the threat's words and timing show intent. Remedy: cancel the closure and complete the investigation (footage, Mrs Halabi), or prosecute for threats and assault.

**Private complaint.** Threats (s. 192), assault (ss. 379–380) and malicious damage (s. 452) are all in the Second Schedule, item (1); Ronen is a neighbour, not a family member; not a State employee acting in office; well within limitation. Proof: threats and assault are provable with Meital and Mrs Halabi, if Mrs Halabi is willing to testify; the mirror is not provable without footage. Decision: appeal first; if rejected, consider a private complaint for threats and assault only. Risks: an ongoing neighbour relationship, Meital's cross-examination, attendance at every hearing, the possibility of a counter-complaint. Alternatives: a civil claim over the leaks (the underlying dispute), a small claim for the mirror if the footage turns up, mediation through the municipality's community mediation centre.

**Deadlines.** Appeal period from 15 November 2026 (s. 65); limitation for misdemeanours (s. 9, see current numbers); the civil limitation period for the leaks and the mirror (Prescription Law, see current numbers).

**Stop signals.** Further violence or threats (ask for police protection or a restraining order and a lawyer); a counter-complaint against Meital; Ronen hiring a lawyer and threatening a defamation claim.

Self-score: facts 2, offences 2, elements 2, participants and defences 1, complaint 2, rights 2, statement 2, appeal 2, private complaint 2 = 17.
