# Project: a cross-border file, from a foreign judgment to a foreign website

Build the file a careful person keeps when life crosses a border: a forum-and-law analysis, an enforcement-or-defence plan for a foreign judgment, a family-and-estate memo with foreign elements, and a complete dispute kit for a failed international purchase. It uses every lesson of module 30 and the practice lab ([30.7](../lessons/module-30/lesson-07.md)).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. The project is a dry run unless you have your own matter and decide to send the seller and issuer letters in your own name.

## Brief

Choose either your own facts for any part (say which parts), or the supplied case.

**Supplied case.** Tamar and Eli Rosen live in Modi'in. They married in Toronto in 2011 while both lived there, and moved to Israel in 2015.

1. **The judgment.** In 2021 Eli personally guaranteed a Canadian company's supply contract; the guarantee chose "the courts of Ontario" and gave his Toronto address, which he had already left. In June 2023 the Ontario Superior Court gave judgment against him for CAD 40,000 by default. In August 2026 the creditor's Israeli lawyer writes demanding payment. Eli says he never received the Ontario claim.
2. **The estate.** Tamar's mother died in May 2026, domiciled in Netanya since 2018. She left a flat in Netanya, a bank account in Toronto and a will typed and signed in Toronto in 2016 before one witness, leaving everything to Tamar; Tamar's brother in Haifa contests it.
3. **The purchase.** In September 2026 Tamar ordered a ₪2,400 standing desk from a website in Hebrew with shekel prices, run by a company in Lithuania; she paid by credit card in four instalments. Delivery was promised by 10 October; on 25 October nothing has come and the company answers only with automatic replies. Its terms say disputes go "exclusively to the courts of Vilnius under Lithuanian law".

Time: about 6 hours.

## Steps

1. **Three questions (30 minutes).** For each part, write jurisdiction, applicable law and recognition/enforcement in one table, with the source for each (lesson [30.1](../lessons/module-30/lesson-01.md)).
2. **The judgment plan (90 minutes).** Use the [foreign judgment worksheet](../templates/foreign-judgment-worksheet.md): conditions (s. 3), reciprocity (s. 4), the five-year date (s. 5), defences (ss. 6–7), evidence list, Eli's next step and a reply to the lawyer's letter without admissions (lesson [30.5](../lessons/module-30/lesson-05.md)).
3. **The estate memo (60 minutes).** Jurisdiction (s. 136), the law of succession (s. 137), the form of the will (s. 140), the Toronto account, and the forum (Registrar or Family Court) (lesson [30.4](../lessons/module-30/lesson-04.md)). Add one paragraph on Tamar and Eli's own matrimonial property regime (s. 15).
4. **The purchase kit (90 minutes).** Use the [international purchase dispute kit](../templates/international-purchase-dispute-kit.md): the seller letter, the s. 17 notice to the issuer, a chargeback request for any instalment already debited, and a draft small-claim statement with a request for service abroad and a paragraph on the Vilnius clause (lessons [30.2](../lessons/module-30/lesson-02.md), [30.6](../lessons/module-30/lesson-06.md)).
5. **Stop signals (30 minutes).** For each part, when you would get a lawyer or Legal Aid, and why.

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

**Three questions.** Judgment: the Ontario court had jurisdiction under its law (forum clause); in Israel the question is enforcement under the 1958 Law (s. 2). Estate: Israeli jurisdiction (domicile at death and assets in Israel, s. 136); Israeli succession law (s. 137); recognition of the Israeli order in Ontario is for Ontario law. Purchase: Israeli small claims court with service abroad (reg. 166(4)(א): performance in Israel prevented; possibly (5)); applicable law: Lithuanian law by the clause, but in practice Israeli law unless someone proves Lithuanian law; enforcement in Lithuania would be a separate problem, so the money route matters most.

**Judgment.** Given June 2023: the five years run to June 2028 (s. 5). Conditions: the creditor must prove Ontario jurisdiction, finality, enforceability in Ontario, and reciprocity (a foreign-law opinion that Ontario courts would enforce an Israeli money judgment). Defences: s. 6(א)(2): the claim went to the Toronto address Eli himself wrote in the guarantee, so under VTB his defence is weak, unless he can show the creditor knew his Israeli address and chose not to use it. s. 6(א)(3): he consented by the forum clause; no jurisdiction defence. Advice: reply without admissions, ask for the judgment and the proof of service, check with an Ontario lawyer whether the default judgment can still be set aside there (where his non-receipt may count more), and consider a negotiated settlement.

**Estate.** Israeli succession law governs (domicile at death in Netanya). The will is not valid under Israeli form rules (one witness), but under s. 140(א) it is valid if valid under Ontario law (place of making, domicile in 2016). Tamar needs an Ontario-law expert opinion on the form of wills. A contested will goes from the Registrar to the Family Court. The Toronto account may need an Ontario procedure. Tamar and Eli: domiciled in Ontario at marriage, so Ontario matrimonial property law governs their property relations (s. 15) unless they made an agreement in Israel.

**Purchase.** Seller letter: cancellation for non-delivery, refund within 14 days (CPL ss. 14ג, 14ה). Issuer: s. 17 notice (non-supply after 10 October and cancellation sent), asking it to stop the remaining instalments from the notice and to open a "goods not received" chargeback for those already debited. Never cite the repealed Debit Cards Law. If the issuer refuses or the seller later proves delivery, a small claim: the Vilnius clause is a foreign-court clause in a consumer standard contract, presumed depriving (SCL s. 4(9); Ben Hamo); the Lithuanian-law clause may stand, but she can argue it deprives her of specific claims, or simply note that no one proved Lithuanian law. Service abroad: reg. 167 request, e-mail plus registered post, or the Hague Service Convention route if the court prefers it (check Lithuania's status in the HCCH table).

**Stop signals.** Judgment: a lawyer before answering in court if the creditor files; an Ontario lawyer on setting aside. Estate: a lawyer once the brother files an objection, because Ontario law must be proved. Purchase: none unless the amount rises or a counterclaim appears.

</details>
