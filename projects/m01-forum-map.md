# Project: your personal forum map

Build a forum map for three matters in your own life: for each one, the law that governs it, the forum with jurisdiction and the section that gives it, the venue, the first step, who may act, the free help available, every running deadline, and the signals that would send you to a lawyer. The result is a short case-file document you will keep updating throughout the course.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run: nothing is sent or filed.

## Brief

Choose three matters from the personal inventory you built in lesson 01.1. They should be of different types, for example one consumer or contract matter, one involving an employer or National Insurance, and one involving an authority or a family matter. If you have fewer than three real matters, use the supplied case from lesson 01.7 for the rest and say so.

For each matter, produce a one-page forum decision memo (format in lesson 01.7), supported by a filled-in [forum check worksheet](../templates/forum-check-worksheet.md). Then write a one-page overview that ranks the three by urgency and says what you will do first.

Time: about 3 hours.

## Steps

1. **Select and describe (20 minutes).** Pick the three matters. For each, write five lines of facts: parties, dates, place, amount or remedy, documents you hold.
2. **Governing law (30 minutes).** Find the main statute for each matter in its current consolidated text. Note its layer (Ottoman, Mandate ordinance, Israeli law) and copy one key sentence in Hebrew with a link.
3. **Hierarchy check (15 minutes).** If any matter involves an authority's rule (a bylaw, a regulation, a procedure), trace it to the authorising statute and write one line on whether it stays within that authority.
4. **Jurisdiction and venue (40 minutes).** Use the worksheet. Test exclusive forums first, then the general courts by value and subject, then reg. 7 for venue. Write the section that gives jurisdiction, in Hebrew.
5. **Precedent (20 minutes).** For at least one matter, find one ruling relevant to it (a Supreme Court ruling if possible). Record the holding in one sentence and say whether it binds the forum you chose.
6. **Who may act and free help (20 minutes).** For each forum, state whether you may act alone, who else may represent you and on what basis, and whether Legal Aid, the Public Defender or another free service could help.
7. **Deadlines and stop signals (15 minutes).** List every running deadline with its source and the date you checked it. List the red flags for each matter.
8. **Overview (20 minutes).** Rank the matters by urgency. Write the first action for each, with a date.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 14 or more (out of 18) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Vague or missing dates and amounts | Mostly complete | Complete, dated, with documents listed |
| Governing law | No statute or wrong statute | Statute named, no quote or link | Statute, layer, Hebrew quote and link |
| Hierarchy | Not considered where relevant | Rule identified but not traced | Rule traced to its authorising section with a conclusion |
| Jurisdiction | Forum wrong or unsupported | Forum right, section missing | Forum right with the Hebrew section quoted; exclusive forums tested |
| Venue | Missing | Named without a reason | Named with the reg. 7 connection |
| Precedent | None | Ruling cited, holding unclear | Holding stated; binding effect explained under s. 20 |
| Who may act | Missing or wrong (for example a relative as advocate) | Correct answer, no basis | Correct answer with s. 20, reg. 170 or the forum's own section |
| Deadlines | None | Listed without sources | Listed with sources and check dates; most urgent marked |
| Overview | Missing | Ranked without actions | Ranked with a dated first action for each matter |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This model uses three invented matters for "Shira Ben-David", 41, a freelance graphic designer from Rehovot.

**Matter 1: a client has not paid ₪12,500 for a branding project.**
Facts: written quote accepted by email on 3 February 2026; work delivered 30 March; invoice sent 1 April; client, a company in Modi'in, has not paid. Law: Contracts (General Part) Law 1973 and Contracts (Remedies for Breach) Law 1970, Israeli codes. Jurisdiction: Shira is an individual, the amount is below the small-claims ceiling (Courts Law s. 60: "תביעה אזרחית שהגיש יחיד – לתשלום סכום שאינו עולה על..."), so the small claims court is available; the magistrates' court is an alternative (s. 61). Venue: reg. 7(א), the defendant's place of business (Modi'in's court area) or where the obligation was to be performed. First step: a demand letter with a 14-day deadline (module 2 lab). Who may act: Shira herself; the client company may need to act through its manager or a lawyer depending on the forum. Precedent: none needed for a simple debt; if the client claims the work was defective, a ruling on the burden of proof in service contracts would help. Deadline: the general limitation period (Prescription Law s. 5; see current numbers), not urgent. Stop signals: a counterclaim for damages above the ceiling.

**Matter 2: National Insurance assessed her self-employed contributions on an income figure she says is wrong.**
Law: National Insurance Law [consolidated version] (Israeli law). Jurisdiction: matters listed in the Labour Court Law's first schedule go to the regional labour court (s. 24(א)(5)); before that, a written request to the Institute's branch to correct the assessment. Venue: the regional labour court for her area (check which region covers Rehovot). Who may act: Shira herself; Legal Aid represents National Insurance matters without a means test (check the current schedule). Deadline: the period for a claim against an Institute decision (see current numbers); urgent. Stop signals: if the assessment leads to a debt in collection proceedings.

**Matter 3: the municipality fined her for a sign on her home studio's balcony under a signage bylaw.**
Hierarchy check: the bylaw cites the Municipalities Ordinance as its authority; the fine section applies to "commercial signs". Shira's sign is a small nameplate. Her argument is scope: whether a nameplate is a "commercial sign" under the bylaw's own definition. Forum: a request to cancel to the municipality's prosecutor, then, if refused, a request to be tried in the local affairs court (Courts Law s. 55). Who may act: Shira herself. Deadline: the period for requesting cancellation or trial printed on the notice (see current numbers); most urgent. Stop signals: none at this level unless the municipality claims an unlicensed business use.

**Overview.** (1) Matter 3: file the cancellation request this week. (2) Matter 2: write to the Institute this month and apply to Legal Aid in parallel. (3) Matter 1: send the demand letter now; file in small claims if no payment within 14 days.

Self-score: facts 2, law 2, hierarchy 2, jurisdiction 2, venue 1 (the labour court region needs checking), precedent 1, who may act 2, deadlines 2, overview 2 = 16.
