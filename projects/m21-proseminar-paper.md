# Module 21 project: Proseminar paper: peer review, revision and submission

Take the draft paper you wrote in lab 21.6 through a real peer review, revise it, and produce a final 15-page proseminar paper with a source list, an AI-use declaration and a short revision memo. Time: about 6 hours.

## Brief

Your paper must answer one legal research question on Israeli law, in about 15 pages of text plus footnotes, in Hebrew or English, footnoted in the format of the uniform citation rules (third edition, 2021). Use your own question from lesson 21.1, or one of the two supplied questions in lesson [21.6](../lessons/module-21/lesson-06.md):

- **A.** Should Israeli universities' disciplinary rules treat undisclosed use of generative AI in a paper as cheating or as breach of instructions?
- **B.** Is the purpose test in Copyright Law s. 19(a) an independent precondition for fair use, and should it be?

You need a reviewer: a classmate, a study partner or anyone who reads Hebrew legal writing carefully. You review their paper in return. If you have no reviewer, do the peer review on your own draft after at least three days away from it, using the same form.

## Steps

1. **Swap drafts (10 min).** Exchange drafts with your reviewer, with the [paper review checklist](../templates/paper-review-checklist.md), part 3. Agree a date to return comments, and keep each other's drafts confidential.
2. **Review their paper (60 min).** Read it once without notes, then answer the ten peer-review questions. Comment; do not rewrite.
3. **Read their review of yours (20 min).** Compare their one-sentence statements of your question and thesis with yours. If they differ, your page 1 is not yet clear.
4. **Revision plan (20 min).** List every comment; mark each "accept", "accept in part" or "reject, because ___". Decide the three changes that matter most.
5. **Revise (150 min).** Make the three main changes first (usually: the thesis on page 1, a weak step in the argument, the counter-arguments), then the smaller ones.
6. **Final checks (45 min).** Repeat the footnote pass (lesson [21.4](../lessons/module-21/lesson-04.md)) and the integrity check, part 1 of the checklist (lesson [21.5](../lessons/module-21/lesson-05.md)); rescore the self-review rubric, part 2.
7. **Package (20 min).** Title page (name, course, supervisor, date, word count); the paper; the source list in citation format (outline part D); the AI-use declaration.
8. **Revision memo (15 min).** Half a page: what the reviewer said, what you changed, what you rejected and why, your rubric score before and after.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weak (1) | Adequate (2) | Strong (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question and thesis | Topic, or thesis only at the end | Question and thesis present but vague | One-sentence question and a contestable thesis on page 1; the conclusion answers it |
| Structure | Survey; topic headings | Logical order, some sections do not advance the thesis | Every heading states a claim; roadmap matches; within length |
| Legal framework | Literature before law; missing binding rulings | Correct sources, too long | Statute, binding rulings, then literature; only what the argument uses |
| Method | Not stated | Named, limits not stated | Named, fits the question, limits stated |
| Literature | Rulings only, or secondary web sources | Some Israeli journal articles | Israeli journals plus English sources; strongest opposing source used |
| Counter-arguments | Absent or straw man | Present, weakly answered | Strongest objection, cited, fairly stated and answered |
| Footnotes | Unfindable or inconsistent | Mostly correct format | Uniform rules throughout; correct signals; pinpoints; Bluebook for English |
| Integrity | Unchecked AI sources, close paraphrase | Checked, minor gaps | Every source read or cited "מובא אצל"; declaration accurate |
| Use of peer review | Ignored | Small edits only | Main comments addressed or reasoned rejection; revision memo |

A strong paper scores at least 23 of 27, with no criterion at 1.

**Supplied question B: outline of a strong paper**

- **כותרת:** "כגון אלה": מבחן המטרה בשימוש הוגן בין לשון החוק לפסיקה
- **א. מבוא.** Hook: s. 19(a) says "למטרות כגון אלה", yet, as Elkin-Koren and Netanel report, only a minority of rulings in the first decade expressly acknowledged that the list is open. Question: is the purpose test an independent precondition, and should it be? Thesis: it is, after Nestlé, but it should be read as a light filter ("resemblance to a listed purpose"), with the weight of the analysis in the four factors. Method: doctrinal analysis of the Supreme Court rulings plus the published empirical study; limits: reported rulings only.
- **ב. המסגרת הנורמטיבית.** s. 19(a) and (b); the 1911 fair-dealing exception it replaced; Safecom (open list, four factors, para. 37); Premier League and Nestlé as reported in the literature.
- **ג. The Supreme Court treats the purpose test as a separate threshold.** Nestlé, as reported; how Safecom and Premier League apply the factors.
- **ד. Lower courts use the threshold to close the list again.** The empirical finding: rulings that found no listed purpose almost always rejected fair use, often without asking whether the use was "such as" a listed one.
- **ה. Counter-arguments.** Certainty for rights holders and users (a clear threshold is predictable); the text ("למטרות") does impose a purpose element; Knesset history. Replies: certainty is served by a structured factor analysis; the "such as" wording requires analogy, not a closed list.
- **ו. Proposal.** A two-sentence test courts could adopt: a use passes s. 19(a) if its purpose is of the same kind as a listed one; fairness is then decided by s. 19(b).
- **ז. סיכום.**
