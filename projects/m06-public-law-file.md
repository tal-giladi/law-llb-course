# Project: a public-law case file

Build the complete file a careful person keeps when a public body makes a decision that limits one of their rights: the constitutional analysis, the choice of institution and forum, the written demand to the authority, and a dry-run petition ready to file if the authority refuses. You will reuse the file in module 7 (legislation and regulation), in the year-2 administrative law module and in the year-1 clinic.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing. This project is a dry run: you may send the letter to the authority about your own matter if you choose, but nothing is filed in court.

## Brief

Choose one decision, rule or failure to act by a public body that affects you: a ministry refusing a permit, a local authority by-law, a condition on a benefit, a refusal to give reasons, a delay. If you have nothing suitable, use the supplied case and say so.

**Supplied case.** Tamar Levi-Nasser, 52, from Be'er Sheva, is a licensed tour guide. In August 2026 the Ministry of Tourism announced a new rule (an invented one for this project): from 1 January 2027, guides must renew their licence every two years by passing a written exam in Hebrew, with no option to take it in Arabic, English or Russian, and with no exception for guides licensed more than 20 years. Tamar, who guides mainly in Arabic and English, wrote to the ministry on 1 September asking for an exam in English or an exception; on 20 September she received a reply saying "the decision is final and applies to all guides equally". Assume the rule was made under a general power in the licensing statute that does not mention exams or languages.

Time: about 5 hours.

## Steps

1. **Facts and documents (30 minutes).** Dated, numbered chronology; the rule or decision in full; every letter and answer as a numbered attachment.
2. **Reviewability and source (20 minutes).** What kind of norm it is (statute, regulation, decision); the statute it relies on; its date; any override (lesson 06.2).
3. **Rights (30 minutes).** Every right affected, listed or derived, with its source and a leading case (lesson 06.4). Say how strong the infringement is.
4. **Limitation clause (60 minutes).** Fill the [proportionality worksheet](../templates/proportionality-worksheet.md): legality, values, purpose stated at its strongest, the three sub-tests with concrete alternatives, and a remedy (lesson 06.3, 06.7).
5. **Institutions (20 minutes).** Which institution can fix this most cheaply: the authority itself, the Ombudsman, a minister, a Knesset committee, a court (lesson 06.5). Choose and explain.
6. **The 2023–2026 context (20 minutes).** Which grounds you rely on and their current status as of the day you write (reasonableness, proportionality); one paragraph stating each side's strongest argument on the ground most relevant to you, with no political verdict (lesson 06.6).
7. **Written demand (40 minutes).** A letter with the [letter to a public authority](../templates/letter-to-authority.md) template: facts, why the decision is unlawful, the remedy you ask for, and a date by which you expect an answer.
8. **Dry-run petition (70 minutes).** Forum check (First Schedule of the Administrative Affairs Courts Law), threshold table, and a petition with the [HCJ petition](../templates/hcj-petition.md) template, or an outline for the Administrative Affairs Court if that is the forum (lesson 06.8).
9. **Stop signals (10 minutes).** Every date with its source; the red flags that would send you to a lawyer.

## Self-assessment rubric

Score each criterion 0–2. A total of 14 or more (out of 18) means the project meets the standard.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | Vague, undated | Mostly dated | Dated, numbered, every document attached |
| Reviewability | Not considered | Type of norm named | Type, source statute, date and override checked |
| Rights | None or wrong | Right named without source | Listed or derived right, source and case, strength of infringement |
| Limitation clause | Conclusion only | Stages named, alternatives vague | Every condition reasoned, concrete alternatives, remedy |
| Institution choice | Court by default | A choice without reasons | Cheapest effective route chosen and explained |
| Neutrality | One-sided or political | Both sides, one weakly | Current law stated; each side at its strongest |
| Written demand | Missing | Present but vague | Facts, legal reason, specific remedy, deadline |
| Forum and threshold | Not checked | Forum named only | Schedule checked; every threshold row met or fixed |
| Petition | Missing | Orders broad or facts unattached | Narrow orders, attached facts, affidavit, red-team answers |

## Model answer

<details><summary>Model answer</summary>

This model uses the supplied case.

**Reviewability and source.** A ministerial rule (secondary norm), made in 2026 under a general licensing power. Post-1992; no override. The key weakness is legality: a general power may not be enough to limit freedom of occupation.

**Rights.** Freedom of occupation (Basic Law: Freedom of Occupation s. 3): a renewal exam is a condition on continuing to work, a strong infringement for a guide who may lose her licence. Equality (derived from dignity, Miller): a Hebrew-only exam burdens guides whose working languages are others, though the rule is formally the same for all.

**Limitation clause.**
- *Legality:* s. 4 requires infringement "by a law or under a law by express authorisation". A general power that does not mention renewal exams is doubtful authority for a new periodic exam; the primary-arrangements doctrine (Rubinstein) points the same way. Possibly fails here.
- *Values:* met.
- *Purpose, at its strongest:* keeping guides' knowledge current and protecting tourists and the image of the country. Proper.
- *Rational connection:* an exam tests current knowledge. Met.
- *Least harmful means:* an exam in the guide's working language, or continuing-education courses, achieve the same purpose with less harm; Hebrew proficiency is not what the purpose requires. Likely fails.
- *Narrow sense:* modest gain against the risk of losing a livelihood after decades.
- *Remedy:* the narrowest: allow the exam in the guide's working languages, or an exception for long-licensed guides; not voiding the whole renewal scheme.

**Institution.** First the ministry (done; refused). Then a renewed demand with the legal argument, copied to the ministry's legal adviser; an Ombudsman complaint is less suitable because the issue is a general rule, not a failure in her own case. If refused, court.

**2023–2026 context.** The argument rests on legality and proportionality, not reasonableness, so the reasonableness dispute matters little here. If reasonableness were added: for limiting it, judges should not rebalance a minister's policy; for keeping it, it reaches arbitrary decisions other grounds miss. As of October 2026 the 2023 amendment is void and reasonableness remains available.

**Written demand (excerpt).** "הכלל החדש פוגע בחופש העיסוק שלי ואינו מעוגן בהסמכה מפורשת בחוק. מבחן בשפת ההדרכה שלי או השתלמות מקצועית יגשימו את התכלית באותה מידה. אבקש לאפשר לי להיבחן באנגלית או לפטור מדריכים בעלי ותק, ולהשיב לי בתוך 30 יום."

**Forum.** Check the First Schedule item on tourism (תיירות, item 9): if the item covers decisions under the tour-guide licensing provisions, the Administrative Affairs Court in Be'er Sheva is the forum, not the HCJ. The model answer therefore prepares an Administrative Affairs Court outline and notes that an HCJ petition would be sent there. If the item did not cover it, the HCJ.

**Threshold.** Standing: own licence. Prior demand: 1.9.2026 and the renewed legal demand; final answer 20.9.2026. Delay: file within weeks of the final answer to the renewed demand. Alternative remedy: none in the rule. Clean hands: disclose that she passed the original exam in Hebrew in 1998.

**Orders sought.** Why the rule should not be cancelled insofar as it requires an exam in Hebrew only; alternatively, why an exam in the guide's working language should not be allowed.

**Stop signals.** The rule takes effect 1.1.2027: if no answer arrives by early December, she needs an interim order and should consult a lawyer or a clinic; also if the ministry relies on a regulation she has not seen.
