# Israeli Law (LL.B. Level): Self-Study with Practice

A self-study course that follows the Israeli law degree (LL.B.) course by course, from contracts, torts and criminal law to procedure, property, family and tax law, and adds a practice track so you can handle your own legal affairs without a lawyer wherever Israeli law allows it. Every lesson ends with something you do, and every course ends with practice labs.

This is not a law degree. It does not qualify you to sit the Bar exam or to practise law, and it is not legal advice.

## How it is built

- **Four years, like the LL.B.** One module is one university course. The spine is Tel Aviv University's LL.B. program, checked against the other Israeli faculties and the Bar exam subjects.
- **University depth.** Each lesson quotes the statute in Hebrew and explains the leading cases.
- **Practice everywhere.** Every lesson has a "Without a lawyer" section: can you do this yourself, where and how, and when to stop and get a lawyer. Every module ends with practice labs, and each year ends with a clinic module of real tasks in your own affairs.
- **Language.** Explanations are in English. Israeli laws, rulings, forms and templates are in Hebrew, because that is what you read and file.
- **Numbers** (fees, ceilings, deadlines) change every year and are kept in one place: [current numbers](references/current-numbers.md).

The course is condensed: a real LL.B. is about 140 credits of full-time study. Expect roughly 250–300 hours per year of the course.

## The one legal limit to remember

You may always act for yourself. Representing other people, drafting legal documents for them and giving them legal advice, as an occupation or for payment, are reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law 1961, s. 20). See [can I act without a lawyer?](references/can-i-act-without-a-lawyer.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Acting on your own can carry risk: losing a case, paying the other side's costs, missing a deadline. Check current rules, act only in your own name, and get help when the stakes are high. See [where to get help](references/where-to-get-help.md).
