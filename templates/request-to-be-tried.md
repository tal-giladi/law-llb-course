# Request to cancel a fine notice and request to be tried (בקשה לביטול הודעת תשלום קנס / הודעה על בקשה להישפט)

**What it is for:** answering a fine-payment notice (הודעת תשלום קנס) for a fine offence: a parking or traffic ticket, a municipal bylaw fine, a littering or noise fine. Part א asks the prosecutor to cancel the notice (Criminal Procedure Law [Consolidated] 1982, s. 229(א)(1), (ג)); part ב gives notice that you wish to be tried (s. 229(א)(2); Criminal Procedure Regulations 1974, reg. 42); part ג asks for relief from surcharges (s. 229(ו)–(ז)). Used in lesson 24.9 and the module 24 project.
**Who may file it:** the person who received the notice, in his own name (for a vehicle, the registered owner or holder). Filing for others as an occupation or for payment is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law 1961, s. 20).
**Where:** the prosecutor and address printed on the notice (police traffic prosecution, municipal prosecution, another authority), or its online service. The request to be tried goes on the slip attached to the notice (Form 8א) if there is one. Keep a registered-mail receipt or online confirmation (regs 42(ב), 42א(ג)).
**Deadlines (from service of the notice):** cancellation request within 30 days (90 days for a vehicle owner who proves someone else drove); request to be tried within 90 days, or within 30 days of a refused cancellation (s. 229(א)). Late requests need reasons (s. 229(ה)). See `references/current-numbers.md`.
**Remember:** paying counts as a conviction (s. 229(ח)); not answering is treated as a conviction when the periods pass (s. 229(ח2)); after a trial the fine will not be lower than the notice unless the court finds special circumstances (s. 230). In a fine-offence trial, if you do not attend you are treated as admitting the facts (s. 240).
**Disclaimer:** act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules before filing.
