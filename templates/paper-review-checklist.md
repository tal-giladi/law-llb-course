# Paper review checklist (רשימת בדיקה לעבודה אקדמית)

**What it is for:** checking a draft legal paper before submission: an integrity check, a scored self-review rubric, a peer-review form for a classmate, and an AI-use declaration. Used in lessons 21.5 and 21.6 and the module 21 project.
**Who uses it:** you on your own draft, and a classmate reviewing yours (you review theirs). Reviewing a classmate's paper is academic feedback, not writing it for them: comment, do not rewrite.
**Where:** keep the completed copies with your draft. Attach the AI-use declaration to the paper if your course requires one.
**Disclaimer:** this checklist is a study aid. Your course's instructions and your university's rules (citation style, length, AI policy) take precedence.

## Part 1: Integrity check (בדיקת יושרה)

- [ ] I know the course's AI policy: ____ (allowed / allowed for ____ / not allowed / must be declared).
- [ ] I opened and read every source I cite; sources I know only from another author are cited "מובא אצל".
- [ ] Every quotation is in quotation marks, word for word, with a pinpoint.
- [ ] My three longest paraphrases were compared line by line with the source; none keeps its structure or phrases.
- [ ] Every original idea taken from someone else is footnoted, even when not quoted.
- [ ] Every ruling: number, parties, date and paragraph checked on a free host.
- [ ] Every statute quoted from the current text.
- [ ] Every AI-suggested source, quotation or page number was verified in the original, or deleted.
- [ ] No data, quotation or source is invented or "approximately right".
- [ ] Any reuse of my own earlier work is disclosed in a footnote and was approved.
- [ ] No long quotations that I do not analyse.

## Part 3: Peer-review checklist (ביקורת עמיתים)

Reviewer: ____ Paper author: ____ Date: ____

Read the paper once without notes. Then answer:

1. In one sentence, what is the paper's question? ____
2. In one sentence, what is its thesis? ____
3. Did you find both on page 1? Yes / No. If not, where? ____
4. Which section is the strongest, and why? ____
5. Which step of the argument did not convince you, and what would? ____
6. What is the best objection the paper does not answer? ____
7. Name one claim that needs a source, or a source that does not seem to say what the paper says. ____
8. Pick three footnotes at random. Could you find each source from the citation alone? ____
9. One sentence or paragraph you had to read twice: ____
10. One change that would most improve the paper: ____

Rules for reviewers: be specific (page and paragraph); describe the problem, do not rewrite the text; start with what works; keep the paper confidential.
