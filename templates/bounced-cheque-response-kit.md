# Bounced-cheque response kit (מענה לדרישה על שיק שחזר וערעור על הגבלת חשבון)

**What it is for:** two documents for the drawer of a cheque that was refused. Part A answers a holder's demand letter about a bounced cheque (pay, propose an arrangement, or state your defence). Part B is an outline of an appeal to the magistrates' court under s. 10 of the Cheques Without Cover Law 1981, asking to remove a cheque from the count of refused cheques (form 1 of the Cheques Without Cover (Procedure) Regulations). Used in lessons 29.3 and 29.8 and the module 29 project.
**Who may file it:** the drawer or account owner, in his own name. A company normally needs a lawyer in court. Drafting these for others as an occupation or for payment is reserved to lawyers (Bar Association Law 1961, s. 20).
**Where:** Part A is sent to the holder (registered mail or email with read receipt; keep a copy). Part B is filed in the magistrates' court nearest the bank branch on which the cheque was drawn (reg. 1), against the bank (reg. 3), within the period from service of the bank's restriction notice (reg. 5), with a detailed affidavit, copies of evidence, a copy of your prior request to the bank and its answer (reg. 4), the deposit (reg. 17) and the court fee. Periods, deposit and fees: `references/current-numbers.md`.
**Important:** the appeal grounds are a closed list (s. 10(א)); a defect in, or non-receipt of, the bank's warning is not a ground (s. 10(ב)). Removing a cheque from the count does not discharge your liability on it; a defence on the cheque does not remove it from the count. If the holder has already opened an execution file, use the opposition in the execution debtor response kit instead of Part A.
**Disclaimer:** act in your own name only. This is not legal advice — check current rules and the court's current form before filing.

Notes in English in brackets are guidance; delete them.
