## For Tal to review personally

1. **06.4, "Current disputes", media measures**: the petitions against the foreign-broadcaster law and the decisions on Army Radio and advertising are listed with no argument from either side. I could not verify a free source, so I did not add the arguments. Add one sentence per side (security and the regulation of a military-run station, against press freedom and pluralism) with a source, or drop the bullet.
2. **10.3, the new paragraph**: its source is Hebrew Wikipedia (the course already uses Wikipedia in 02.4). Swap it for an IDI or Knesset Research Center paper if you prefer a primary source.
3. **06.6, the respondents' argument in 2412/23**: this was written from memory of the case, and the Hebrew judgment was not re-read. Check it against the judgment's summary of the respondents' position.
4. **Coverage gaps (not bias)**: year 1 barely covers LGBT family law (a single factual mention of Ben-Ari in 10.4), policing and protest (only doctrinal quiz items) and the territories (only the State Liability Law). That is acceptable for year 1, but years 2–4 (administrative, criminal procedure, family) should present both sides there.
5. **Territory terminology**: the course mixes "Judea and Samaria" (statutory, 04.7, question bank m04) and "West Bank" (06.3). Decide on a house style, such as the statutory term at first use with the alternative in brackets.
6. **06.1, "the most recent one shows how light the procedure is"** (Basic Law: Torah Study, and m06-006): this is factual, but it uses a politically charged law as the example of the light procedure. Consider also citing a less charged example, such as the temporary budget Basic Laws in Bar-On.
7. **Accuracy (not neutrality), 01.3**: the 5658/23 summary calls the review ground "abuse of the Knesset's constituent power". The majority's ground was deviation from (exceeding) constituent authority; "abuse" is the Shafir/Bar-On doctrine (see 06.1).
8. **Character pattern**: older Russian-speaking women recur as vulnerable parties (01.6 Olga, 05.3 Yulia, 12.8 Galina). This is offset by FSU engineers elsewhere, but worth varying in years 2–4.
