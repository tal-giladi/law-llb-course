**Corrections to the brief:**
- **Debit Cards Law 1986 s. 8** is not the chargeback provision, and the whole Law was **repealed on 14.10.2020** by s. 55 of the **Payment Services Law 2019** (in force the same day, s. 77(א)). Under the old Law, s. 8 was the transaction document (prima facie evidence), s. 9 the card-not-present ("מסמך חסר") refund and s. 10 the non-supply stop. Their successors are Payment Services Law **s. 29** (payment without a document: 30-day notice, refund in 8 business days) and **s. 17** (stop of a guaranteed payment for non-supply after cancellation notice to the seller). Module 30 teaches the 2019 Law. Earlier modules that mention a chargeback (03.10) do not cite the old Law, but any reference elsewhere to "חוק כרטיסי חיוב" should be updated.
- **"Gorman / ע"א 1432/03 Yinon"**: ע"א 1432/03 is **ינון יצור ושיווק מוצרי מזון בע"מ נ' קרעאן**, judgment **1.9.2004** (hearing 23.12.2003), Rivlin J., Naor and Hayut JJ. It abandoned double actionability and adopted the law of the place of the tort with a narrow exception, applied there. No case named "Gorman" could be identified; it is not cited.
- **Sale (International Sale of Goods) Law**: the official year is **התש"ס–1999**; Israel acceded to the CISG on 22.1.2002 (in force 1.2.2003). Section 3 extends the Convention to parties in non-party States.
- **CPR regs 166–168** are the current numbering in the 2018 Regulations (plus reg. 169, protection of a defendant who contests jurisdiction; reg. 163(ג), service on a permanent representative).
- **Succession Law choice-of-law chapter** is ss. **135–144** (the brief said 136–142): s. 135 domicile, 136 jurisdiction, 137 domicile at death, 138 lex situs assets, 139 capacity, 140 form, 141 characterization, 142 renvoi, 143 public policy/discrimination, 144 foreign statutory heirs.
- **2023+ consumer/import reforms**: the relevant one found is the 2024 "aggravated-circumstances breacher" regime (Consumer Protection Law ss. 22כז–22לח; Payment Services Law s. 18א; Execution Law s. 78ב), commencement noted on Wikisource as 4.10.2024. The personal-import VAT threshold change (2025–2026) could not be confirmed from an official source (⚠️) and is mentioned only as "check the Tax Authority".
- The Consumer Protection Law has **no territorial-application clause** for foreign online sellers (s. 17ז(א)(1) exempts only the price-display chapter for goods sold outside Israel; s. 14ג2 deals with tourism services abroad). Applying it to a foreign seller is a choice-of-law question; no Supreme Court holding on its mandatory force was found (⚠️ flagged in 30.3, 30.6).
